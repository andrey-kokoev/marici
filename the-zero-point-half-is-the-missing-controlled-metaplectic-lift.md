# The Zero-Point Half Is the Missing Controlled Metaplectic Lift

## Generator quotient

Unitary channel dynamics generated by a self-adjoint operator \(H\) is

\[
\dot\rho=-i[H,\rho].
\]

It is unchanged under every scalar shift

\[
H\longmapsto H+cI.
\]

Therefore the channel-level generator is not \(H\), but its class modulo
\(\mathbb RI\).

For the endpoint oscillator rotation, compare

\[
N_u=u\partial_u
\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad
H_u=N_u+\frac12 I.
\]

They generate exactly the same conjugation-channel path. Their linear lifts at
angle \(2\pi\) differ:

\[
e^{-2\pi iN_u}=I,
\qquad
e^{-2\pi iH_u}=-I.
\]

The entire metaplectic sign is carried by the scalar zero-point half that the
channel generator quotients out.

## Why a channel path still does not suffice

Supplying the full continuous channel path does not repair the loss. At every
parameter value, adding a scalar function \(c(t)I\) leaves the commutator
generator unchanged while changing the accumulated linear phase by

\[
\exp\left(-i\int c(t)\,dt\right).
\]

Thus initial-point data and continuity do not select a unique linear lift of a
projective path. A connection, scalar generator, or equivalent phase convention
must be supplied.

## Controlled consequence

On an uncontrolled endpoint, the scalar term has no channel effect. Under
conditionalization it becomes

\[
|1\rangle\langle1|\otimes\frac12 I_H,
\]

which is not scalar on the joint control-endpoint system. It produces the
relative control phase detected in Entry 3703.

Hence controlled construction promotes a quotient-invisible scalar into an
observable relational generator. This explains why channel access cannot be
lifted to controlled access functorially.
