What exactly is being drawn?

Step 1. A flag is a nested choice of vertex, edge, face, and body. The barycenters of those four objects form one small tetrahedron. There are 4 × 3 × 2 = 24 flags. This is a subdivision count.

Step 2. If b is the body center and v is a boundary point, the visible point is y = (1 − t)b + tv. Sending t to zero erases v from the visible point. The right panel explicitly stores v separately. It does not claim that an ordinary geometric point contains a visible miniature tetrahedron. The direction boundary is a tetrahedral surface, topologically a sphere.

Step 3. The right triangle is a coordinate chart for the selected coefficient image, not a host spatial triangle. For tetrahedron barycentric coordinates v, its coordinates are u = (vA + vD/3, vB + vD/3, vC + vD/3). This represents the kernel quotient of the retained projector in the declared ABCD alignment. It is not a display of the three complex coefficients themselves.

For m = min(uA,uB,uC), the two boundary preimages are upper = (uA,uB,uC,0) and lower = (uA−m,uB−m,uC−m,3m). On the boundary m = 0 and the points coincide. The dashed segment joins equal-readout endpoints through the solid interior; only its endpoints belong to the direction boundary.

The animated loop follows these explicit global lifts. It does not demonstrate every possible transport law. The host's six-panel belt is deliberately omitted: it is a different object.
