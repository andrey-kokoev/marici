# Fourier–Tate Sewing Reverses Boundary Grade Rather Than Preserving It

## Homogeneous-distribution law

On the real line, Fourier transform sends a homogeneous distribution of
Mellin degree \(s\) to the complementary degree. With a fixed Fourier
normalization, the model identity has the form

\[
\mathcal F\bigl(|x|^{s-1}\bigr)
=
\gamma_\infty(s)|\xi|^{-s}.
\]

The scalar \(\gamma_\infty(s)\) is meromorphic and nonzero at generic
points of the critical strip. Thus Fourier transform is an invertible
correspondence between the two complementary homogeneous lines.

The exponent exchange is

\[
s-1
\longleftrightarrow
-s.
\]

At the level of Euler boundary magnitudes, this is exactly the exchange

\[
N^{1-s}
\longleftrightarrow
N^s.
\]

## Consequence for the boundary comparator

The reciprocal boundary comparator

\[
R_s(N)
=
\frac{s}{1-s}N^{1-2s}
\]

is singular off the seam only when both boundary classes are measured as
though they belonged to the same graded fiber.

But source-native Fourier--Tate sewing does not preserve that fiber. It maps
the direct grade to the complementary reciprocal grade. As a morphism between
those differently graded spaces, it can remain perfectly invertible for
off-seam \(s\).

Therefore uniform invertibility in a common unshifted cutoff norm is not yet a
source requirement. Imposing it would suppress the actual homogeneity shift
of Fourier transform.

## What the adelic-height theorem still proves

Adelic height canonically synchronizes the regulator clocks:

\[
H(r^{-1})=H(r).
\]

That result remains exact and useful. It prevents arbitrary independent
reparameterization of the two sectors.

What it does not do is identify their associated graded objects without a
degree shift. Shared resolution and equal grade are different claims.

## Route disposition

The following inference is invalid without another constructor:

1. reciprocal zero-germs use a common adelic cutoff;
2. Fourier--Tate sewing is invertible;
3. therefore their boundary grades agree.

Step 3 fails because the sewing morphism is grade-reversing.

The boundary-condition-number route does not prove RH in its current form.
Its remaining possible repair is substantially stronger:

> A source-admissible zero event carries an additional comparison that
> identifies the two complementary Fourier grades inside one common graded
> object.

That comparison cannot be the ordinary Fourier--Tate transform itself.

## Hostile interpretation

An off-line symmetric zero quartet is compatible with:

- common adelic height;
- complementary boundary grades;
- invertible Fourier--Tate sewing;
- the scalar functional equation.

Its off-seam condition-number growth is then expected transport between
different grades, not a contradiction.

A successful selector must distinguish an actual theta zero from this hostile
packet through a second coherence cell beyond Fourier reciprocity.

## New frontier

The plausible remaining object is a pullback or equalizer of two maps:

- Fourier--Tate transport, which reverses grade;
- source completion or self-duality, which may compare both presentations
  inside a common completed object.

Only their higher coherence could force a zero event to be fixed rather than
merely transported to a complementary grade.

This recovers the multi-tower intuition. The in and out towers are connected
by a legitimate grade-changing morphism. RH would require a control tower
whose coherence cell says that the particular null event descends to their
grade-zero equalizer.

## Explanatory gain

The critical line is still the fixed-grade locus of reciprocal homogeneity.
But being a fixed locus does not imply that every transported event is fixed.

The missing theorem is no longer “Fourier sewing is reversible.” That is true
everywhere. It is “source-authorized null events must be fixed objects of the
grade-reversing sewing.” That statement is both sharper and currently
unproved.
