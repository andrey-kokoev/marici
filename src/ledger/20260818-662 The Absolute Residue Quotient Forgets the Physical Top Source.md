# 662 — The Absolute Residue Quotient Forgets the Physical Top Source

## Hard-to-vary claim

The literal rank-twenty four-mark residue presentation from Entry 661 does
not retain a relative top class after passage to its absolute quotient.
The constant physical source column survives, but it lies in the span of
classes represented on proper marked faces.

## Quotient-coordinate audit

The reducer now retains normalized relation pivots and projects every
low-space labelled column to quotient coordinates.  For each occurrence
family the physical source is the constant numerator with all four marked
pole levels equal to one.

At ambient homotopy degree ten, cutoff degree five, and generic Kummer
weight five, both occurrence presentations give

\[
\dim M_{123,23}=\dim M_{123,31}=20.
\]

In both cases the physical source has a nonzero quotient representative
(with support on two free coordinates).  However, the span of all columns
having at least one marked pole level zero has rank

\[
\boxed{20}.
\]

Consequently the physical source vanishes after quotienting by the
proper-face span.  The same result holds for the \(q_{g_{23}}\) family at
Kummer weight seven and at ambient homotopy degree eleven.

## Consequence

The rank-twenty absolute residue quotient is a valid coefficient-space
census, but it has forgotten which lift came from the complete physical
five-pole source.  Differentiating its reduced source vector and taking an
ordinary linear saturation cannot produce a canonical relative extension:
every resulting vector is already typed only in a space generated by
proper faces.

Thus Entry 659's missing off-diagonal block cannot be recovered by
horizontal saturation *after* the absolute rank-twenty reduction.  The
source label and its boundary homotopy must be retained first, for example
in the relative localization fiber of Entry 658, and the connection must
be constructed on that complex before taking absolute homology.

This is the residue-side analogue of Entry 513's deformation-linearity
correction:

\[
\boxed{
\text{specialize or quotient only after the structure-defining map acts.}
}
\]

## Relation to the algebraic quartic

Entry 660's Källén double cover may still describe a coefficient cover or
physical endpoint collision.  It cannot repair the loss found here: a
branch polynomial on the coefficient base does not restore the forgotten
relative source label.
