# Affine G2 Selects a Mark Ray Only After a Pairing Choice

WP797 tests the asymmetric triple bond of \(G_2\) as a possible source for the
relative flavor portal found conditionally in compact clockwork.

With the declared Cartan convention, finite \(G_2\) has determinant one and no
null mode. The untwisted affine matrix has primitive Kac marks
\((1,3,2)\) on its left null ray and comarks \((1,1,2)\) on its right null ray.
The distinction survives physical symmetrization. The root Gram form is
positive semidefinite with kernel \((1,3,2)\) and nonzero eigenvalues
\(7\pm\sqrt7\); the coroot Gram form is positive semidefinite with kernel
\((1,1,2)\) and nonzero eigenvalues \(5\pm\sqrt7\).

Therefore the triple is genuine source geometry, but it is not yet the
uniquely executable flavor coupling. A source-derived representation and
pairing functor must choose root rather than coroot data and map its oriented
nodes to physical species. Neither the quadratic-form sign, overall
coefficient, completed matter RG, finite thresholds, nor a calibrated
`physical16` detector response follows from the affine root datum.

The smallest exact falsifier is the root/coroot pair itself: the same affine
Cartan packet yields two canonical, gapped, inequivalent null profiles. The
first nonfaithful arrow is the missing executable-pairing interface. Affine
\(G_2\) is consequently a conditional relative-profile selector and
rigidifier, not yet a Deutschian explanation of the flavor portal.
