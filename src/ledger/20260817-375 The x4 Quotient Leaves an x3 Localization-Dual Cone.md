# The x4 Quotient Leaves an x3 Localization-Dual Cone

## Result

The support reduction isolated in Entry 370 is necessary but not sufficient.
After quotienting the unsupported \(x_4\)-branch, there is indeed a canonical
map in the direction required by the Entry-176 cap.  However, that map is not
an equivalence over the unlocalized physical coefficient ring.  Its cone is
the dual of the additional \(x_3\)-localization quotient and is nonzero.

Consequently Entry 176's primitive cellular coefficient \(k=1\) cannot by
itself make the reduced exceptional packet perfect.  The remaining problem
is not to manufacture a scalar.  It is to construct a physical coefficient
functor—such as a justified residue, nearby-cycle, or relative-boundary
operation—that kills or evaluates the \(x_3\)-localization cone.

## The reduced coefficient types

Write

\[
R=A[(D_{03}x_1)^{-1}],\qquad x=x_3.
\]

This is the base change that makes the two common normal factors units.  It
is a valid obstruction test: an equivalence over the original coefficient
ring would remain an equivalence after this base change.

After the \(x_4\)-support quotient, the center and physical branch
coefficients are

\[
\mathsf T_{\mathrm{ctr}}=R\operatorname{Hom}_R(R,R),
\qquad
\mathsf T_{x_3}=R\operatorname{Hom}_R(R[x^{-1}],R),
\]

up to the already recorded common incidence and Cartier shifts.  The
localization map

\[
\iota:R\longrightarrow R[x^{-1}]
\]

therefore gives, by contravariance, a canonical typed arrow

\[
\phi_{\mathrm{loc}}
=R\operatorname{Hom}_R(\iota,R):
\mathsf T_{x_3}\longrightarrow\mathsf T_{\mathrm{ctr}}.
\]

This closes the bare existence and arrow-direction question left open in
Entry 370.  Entry 176's positive normal orientation can multiply
\(\phi_{\mathrm{loc}}\) by \(+1\); it does not change its cone.

## Exact cone calculation

Let

\[
Q_x=R[x^{-1}]/R.
\]

The localization triangle

\[
R\longrightarrow R[x^{-1}]\longrightarrow Q_x\longrightarrow R[1]
\]

dualizes to

\[
R\operatorname{Hom}_R(Q_x,R)
\longrightarrow
\mathsf T_{x_3}
\xrightarrow{\phi_{\mathrm{loc}}}
\mathsf T_{\mathrm{ctr}}
\longrightarrow
R\operatorname{Hom}_R(Q_x,R)[1].
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Cone}(\phi_{\mathrm{loc}})
\simeq R\operatorname{Hom}_R(Q_x,R)[1].}
\]

This cone is nonzero.  For the normal parameter \(x\), the standard telescope
resolution identifies the relevant derived dual with the \(x\)-adic
completion sector.  After localization at the height-one prime \((x)\), its
cohomology contains the nonzero completion contribution (equivalently the
extension generated by the tower \(x^{-1},x^{-2},\ldots\)).  In particular,
the map is not a quasi-isomorphism and the cone is not removed by multiplying
the incidence generator by the unit \(+1\).

This is the same coefficient mechanism detected for one-normal sectors in
Entry 367, now applied to the exact center-to-\(x_3\) localization arrow of
the reduced exceptional packet.

## Meaning of the physical quotient

A quotient that merely deletes the \(x_4\) incidence summand leaves
\(Q_x\) untouched.  Therefore it produces the two-term shape

\[
\mathsf T_{x_3}\xrightarrow{\phi_{\mathrm{loc}}}\mathsf T_{\mathrm{ctr}},
\]

but not the scalar complex \(R\xrightarrow{1}R\).  Replacing the former by
the latter without an additional coefficient functor would conflate a
primitive cellular coefficient with an invertible map of localization-dual
modules.

The minimal viable physical reduction must do two distinct things:

1. remove the nonphysical \(x_4\)-support summand;
2. kill, take a residue of, or otherwise canonically evaluate
   \(R\operatorname{Hom}_R(Q_x,R)\).

Only after the second operation can Entry 176's \(k=1\) become the unit
matrix \([1]\) on the resulting rank-one coefficient lines.

## Correct next experiment

Construct the coefficient part of the physical-support functor on the
localization triangle

\[
R\to R[x_3^{-1}]\to Q_{x_3}.
\]

The acceptance test is binary:

- if the functor canonically sends the dual \(Q_{x_3}\)-term to zero and
  carries \(\phi_{\mathrm{loc}}\) to the identity of the retained residue
  line, the Entry-176 cap gives an acyclic reduced cone;
- if the dual \(Q_{x_3}\)-term survives, the exceptional correction remains
  nonperfect despite the \(x_4\) quotient.

No perfectness claim is made before this coefficient functor is constructed.
