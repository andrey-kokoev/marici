# 1487 — The Causal Boundary Ideal Is the Deck-Anti-Invariant Conormal Module

## Status

Exact algebraic identification of the single-field local-potential sector of
the doubled initial boundary.

## Deck algebra

Work over a characteristic-zero coefficient ring and set

\[
\phi_c=\frac12(\phi_++\phi_-),
\qquad
\phi_q=\phi_+-\phi_-.
\]

The contour deck involution exchanges the two branches:

\[
\tau(\phi_+,\phi_-)=(\phi_-,\phi_+),
\]

and therefore

\[
\tau(\phi_c)=\phi_c,
\qquad
\tau(\phi_q)=-\phi_q.
\]

For

\[
R=\mathbb Q[\phi_c,\phi_q],
\]

the invariant and anti-invariant pieces are

\[
R^+=\mathbb Q[\phi_c,\phi_q^2],
\]

\[
\boxed{
R^-=\phi_q\,\mathbb Q[\phi_c,\phi_q^2].
}
\]

Thus the anti-invariant sector is a free rank-one module over the invariant
sector, generated by the labelled contour conormal \(\phi_q\).

## Relation to normalization

The diagonal ideal is

\[
\mathcal I_\Delta=(\phi_q).
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
R^-=\mathcal I_\Delta\cap R^{\tau=-1}.
}
\]

Every unitary local boundary action difference lies canonically in this
deck-anti-invariant conormal module. Entry 1485's strict trace vanishing is
therefore an algebraic consequence of deck character and diagonal support,
not an accident of the three computed operators.

## Polarization map

For a local potential \(V\), define

\[
\operatorname{Pol}(V)
=V\!\left(\phi_c+\frac12\phi_q\right)
-V\!\left(\phi_c-\frac12\phi_q\right).
\]

Its exact expansion is

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Pol}(V)
=
\sum_{m\ge0}
\frac{V^{(2m+1)}(\phi_c)}
{2^{2m}(2m+1)!}
\phi_q^{2m+1}.
}
\]

Only odd contour-conormal grades occur. Moreover, the coefficients at
different odd grades are derivatives of one source potential; they are not
independent contact terms.

For an independently supplied \(V(\phi)=\phi^4\), the formula gives

\[
\operatorname{Pol}(V)
=4\phi_c^3\phi_q+\phi_c\phi_q^3.
\]

This example is not implied by Collins--Holman Eq. (6.37), which fixes only
the term linear in the fluctuation around the classical zero mode.

## Necessary qualification

Although \(R^-\) is free of rank one over \(R^+\), not every arbitrary
anti-invariant polynomial is the polarization of a one-variable potential.
The Taylor-derivative relations above define the source-compatible
polarization subspace. Deck parity alone does not prove integrability to a
single undoubled action.

## Carrier interpretation

The doubled carrier contributes:

- the two labelled contour occurrences;
- their deck involution;
- the diagonal Cartier divisor;
- its conormal generator.

The potential and its linked odd Taylor grades are coefficient data. This is
a precise instance of

\[
\text{shared labelled carrier}
+
\text{sector-specific coefficient module}.
\]
