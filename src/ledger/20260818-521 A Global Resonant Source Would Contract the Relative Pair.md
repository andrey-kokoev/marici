# A Global Resonant Source Would Contract the Relative Pair

Entry 520 constructs the formal mapping-fiber pair

\[
\bigl(r_7;\operatorname{res}q_7\bigr).
\]

There is a prior nontriviality gate.  For a restriction map of complexes
`rho:C->E`, the homotopy fiber has differential

\[
D_{\mathrm{fib}}(x,h)=(Dx,\rho x-Dh).
\]

If `q_7` is a global section of the normalized source lattice and
`r_7=Dq_7`, then

\[
D_{\mathrm{fib}}(q_7,0)
=\bigl(r_7;\operatorname{res}q_7\bigr).
\]

The relative pair is therefore a boundary.  Source provenance supplies a
nullhomotopy, but by itself it does not supply a nonzero relative class.

Consequently the odd resonance can survive only through a mismatch of
weighted lattices: the normalized exceptional target must glue globally
while the corresponding normalized `q_7` source fails to glue as a global
source section.  Equivalently, the three Rees shifts and one incidence
shift of Entry 519 must appear in the target/source transition ratio, not
as a common twist of both.

The next calculation is now exact and local.  Compute the normalized source
frames `q_{7,u}` and `q_{7,a^2}` on the two weighted charts and compare them
on `s=t^{-1}`.  If their transition is the same as that of `r_7`, the
candidate contracts.  If their ratio has the divisor
`3[1]+4[-1]`, that Čech defect is the obstruction class supporting the
relative resonance.

Entry 520 establishes the cone cycle equation only.  Nontriviality remains
open pending this source-versus-target transition audit.
