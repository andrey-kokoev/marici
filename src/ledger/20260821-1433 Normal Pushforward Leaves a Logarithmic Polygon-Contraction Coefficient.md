# 1433 — Normal Pushforward Leaves a Logarithmic Polygon-Contraction Coefficient

Sequence claim: seqclaim-f7b2059b5918fa7e8c452f81.

## Question

Entry 1432 shows that endpoint residues on the contracted-edge divisor give
either a one-sided unit map or an exchange-resolved factor two. Could
pushforward in the contracted-edge normal canonically supply the missing
normalization?

## Local normal model

Write

\[
y=y_{\{n,1\}},
\qquad
q_{\{1\}}=A+y,
\qquad
q_{\{n\}}=B+y.
\]

On \(y=0\), the merged target wall is

\[
q_{\{n,1\}}=A+B.
\]

The source normal factor is

\[
\omega_y=\frac{dy}{(A+y)(B+y)}.
\]

## Ordinary operations

There is no logarithmic pole at \(y=0\), so

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Res}_{y=0}\omega_y=0.}
\]

Ordinary restriction gives

\[
\left.\frac{1}{(A+y)(B+y)}\right|_{y=0}
=
\frac1{AB},
\]

not the target pole \(1/(A+B)\).

## Normal pushforward

The exact partial fraction identity is

\[
\frac1{(A+y)(B+y)}
=
\frac1{B-A}
\left(
\frac1{A+y}-\frac1{B+y}
\right).
\]

For the positive normal ray,

\[
\boxed{
\int_0^\infty
\frac{dy}{(A+y)(B+y)}
=
\frac{\log(B/A)}{B-A}.
}
\]

The diagonal limit is \(1/A\). This is exchange-symmetric, but it is not a
constant multiple of \(1/(A+B)\): it retains the relative endpoint ratio
\(B/A\).

## Consequence

Normal pushforward supplies neither of Entry 1432's permitted normalizations:

- it does not select one endpoint occurrence;
- it does not produce a universal factor \(1/2\);
- it does not collapse the two source poles to the merged rational pole.

Instead it produces a relative logarithmic coefficient. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{Carrier contraction}
+
\text{normal pushforward}
\ne
\text{target positive exceptional cell}.
}
\]

A physical one-sided current, an independently normalized counit, or retention
of the relative coefficient remains necessary.

## Architectural meaning

The failure is not missing carrier incidence. It is a coefficient/readout
obstruction generated by combining two labelled occurrences. Carrier
functoriality at the alphabet level does not imply period functoriality.
