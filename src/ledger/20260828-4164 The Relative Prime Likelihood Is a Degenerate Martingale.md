# 4164 — The Relative Prime Likelihood Is a Degenerate Martingale

Finite prime cutoffs admit the ordinary density

$$
L_X=\prod_{p\le X}P_{p^{-1/2}}(\theta_p)
$$

of the arithmetic Poisson state relative to the Haar product state. These
densities form a nonnegative mean-one martingale. Nevertheless,

$$
\frac{\log L_X}{\sum_{p\le X}p^{-1}}\longrightarrow-1
$$

almost surely under Haar measure, so $L_X\to0$ almost surely and the
martingale is not uniformly integrable.

The exact logarithmic expansion shows why. The primitive character has
divergent quadratic variation $2\sum_{p\le X}p^{-1}$, while the square
current supplies the matching deterministic drift
$-\sum_{p\le X}p^{-1}$. Higher fluctuating grades are summable. Thus the
nonsummable interface is a coherent primitive-fluctuation/square-compensator
pair, not a square correction that can be subtracted independently.

Consequently the infinite Haar-to-arithmetic interface is not an ordinary
Radon--Nikodym density, an $L^1$ limit of finite relative modular operators,
or a bounded vacuum change. A completed construction must retain both grades
in a correspondence, distributional logarithmic cocycle, or source-derived
noise extension before exponentiation.
