# 2006 — Two Generic Late-Time Samples Separate the Generated Initial-State Pair

## Question

Entry 1501 proved that the one-loop generated Gaussian pair

\[
(\beta_k,B_k)
\]

is visible in two independent bilocal statistical time structures. Does a finite late-time readout still separate the pair?

## Frozen linearized source map

At equal times, Entry 1501's source formula reduces to

\[
2\omega_k^2\Delta G_k^{(1)}(t,t)
=
\beta_k\cos\!\bigl(2\omega_k(t-t_0)\bigr)+B_k.
\]

No coefficient or normalization is changed in the audit below; the common nonzero factor \(2\omega_k^2\) is merely cleared.

## One time is insufficient

At one observation time \(t_1\), with

\[
c_1=\cos\!\bigl(2\omega_k(t_1-t_0)\bigr),
\]

the readout matrix is

\[
\begin{pmatrix}c_1&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

It has rank one and kernel generated by \((1,-c_1)\). A single equal-time power measurement cannot reconstruct both coefficients.

## Two generic times are sufficient

For two times, the matrix is

\[
M_{12}=
\begin{pmatrix}
c_1&1\\
c_2&1
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det M_{12}=c_1-c_2.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rank}M_{12}=2
\quad\text{when}\quad
c_1\ne c_2.
}
\]

The failure locus is exactly the phase-degenerate sampling condition \(c_1=c_2\).

## One first jet is also sufficient

At one time, use the value and its time derivative. After clearing the same common factor, the matrix is

\[
M_{\rm jet}=
\begin{pmatrix}
c_1&1\\
-2\omega_k s_1&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
s_1=\sin\!\bigl(2\omega_k(t_1-t_0)\bigr).
\]

Hence

\[
\det M_{\rm jet}=2\omega_k s_1.
\]

It has rank two away from the discrete sampling locus \(s_1=0\).

## Time averaging loses one direction

An ideal infinite-time average kills the oscillatory \(\beta_k\) term and retains only \(B_k\). Its readout matrix is \((0,1)\), again rank one.

## Narrow result

\[
\boxed{
\text{two generic equal-time samples}
\;\simeq\;
\text{one generic first time jet}
\;\Rightarrow\;
\text{faithful readout of }(\beta_k,B_k).
}
\]

Thus the generated pair does not require an infinite bilocal observable for separation. The smallest generic finite readout has two scalar components. Rank loss in a one-time power spectrum or a time average is an observational projection kernel, not collapse of the underlying coefficient object.

This result does not explain numerical flavor constants and makes no cross-sector constant-value claim.

## Carrier classification

The carrier remains the finite initial hypersurface with doubled contour occurrences. The distinction occurs entirely at the coefficient-to-readout map.

## Next falsifier
