# 1625 — The First Quartic Gaussian Covariance Variation Is a Hartree Hamiltonian Tangent

## Hostile parity-even test

Entry 1624 used odd cubic parity to force the first statistical covariance correction to second order.  A quartic interaction is parity-even and can interfere linearly with a Gaussian state.  Test whether that interference supplies a non-Cut statistical direction.

Freeze the local Hermitian representative

\[
H_4=\lambda q^4
\]

and a centered Gaussian covariance

\[
V=
\begin{pmatrix}
V_{qq}&V_{qp}\\
V_{qp}&V_{pp}
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

## Direct first variation

The quartic contribution to the covariance equations is

\[
\dot V_{qq}=0,
\qquad
\dot V_{qp}=-4\lambda\langle q^4\rangle,
\qquad
\dot V_{pp}=-4\lambda\langle\{p,q^3\}\rangle.
\]

Gaussian Wick reduction gives

\[
\langle q^4\rangle=3V_{qq}^2,
\qquad
\langle\{p,q^3\}\rangle=6V_{qp}V_{qq}.
\]

Thus

\[
\dot V_{qp}=-12\lambda V_{qq}^2,
\qquad
\dot V_{pp}=-24\lambda V_{qq}V_{qp}.
\]

## Hartree typing

These are exactly the covariance equations generated by the state-dependent quadratic Hessian

\[
h_{qq}=12\lambda V_{qq}.
\]

Therefore the linear quartic variation is symplectic:

\[
\dot V=A_HV+VA_H^T
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\frac{d}{dt}\det V=0.
}
\]
