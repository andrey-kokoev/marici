For

\[
\gamma_p(z)=\frac{1-p^{-1/2}e^{-z\log p}}
{1-p^{-1/2}e^{z\log p}},
\qquad z=\sigma+it,
\]

the exact local defect is

\[
1-|\gamma_p(z)|^2
=
\frac{4p^{-1/2}\sinh(\sigma\log p)
\left(p^{-1/2}\cosh(\sigma\log p)-\cos(t\log p)\right)}
{|1-p^{-1/2}e^{z\log p}|^2}.
\]

It vanishes on the critical seam but is generally nonzero off seam.  Near the
seam its sign flips between heights \(t=0\) and
\(t=\pi/\log p\) while \(\sigma\) remains fixed.

Therefore local theta/Tate incidence does authorize off-seam defect channels,
and it does not give either open half-sector a uniform contractive variance.
A seam-only support theorem cannot descend factor by factor.  Universal Julia
dilation is too weak, while single-sector coisometry is too strong.

Any surviving zero-confinement law must assemble signed reciprocal incidence,
primitive and square boundary currents, and the archimedean channel before
positive defect-space formation.  The phase/positivity tradeoff is structural:
positive metricization loses the vertical variance that the ordered skew port
retains.
