# The Odd Exceptional Residue Is a First Filtration Differential

Let `F` be the weighted exceptional filtration induced by
`nu(a)=1`, `nu(u)=2`.  At a generic `q` source degree the labelled
differential has its expected filtration order.  At `s_b=1`, `i=7`, Entry
517 proves cancellation of that leading symbol:

\[
\operatorname{gr}_F^w(D)(q_7)=0.
\]

The exact formula leaves the next term

\[
D(q_7)=\frac{1}{8}u a^7(b+1)+\text{other labelled components},
\]

one exceptional step deeper.  Therefore the odd residue may appear as a
class on the leading associated-graded page, but the filtered complex
supplies the next differential

\[
d_1[q_7]=[u a^7(b+1)].
\]

Thus the source is absent from the deeper grade only because it lies in the
preceding filtration piece; it has not disappeared from the filtered or
nearby-cycle complex.  The exceptional residue is killed by the first
filtration/Bockstein differential unless an independently defined support
or perverse truncation removes the preceding source grade.

This explains simultaneously why the resonance is visible in the Euler
associated quotient and why it contracts in the full weighted mapping
fiber of Entry 522.  A nonzero associated grade is not yet a nearby-cycle
class.

The remaining admissible gate is geometric strictness: derive a support
functor whose V-filtration excludes `q_7` while retaining its deeper target
residue.  Without such an independently specified functor, selecting the
deeper grade alone would be a post hoc truncation.
