# 3484 — Rank Two Permits Primitive Pairing Three but Not a Unique Flux Orbit

## Claim

In signature \((1,1)\), the even unimodular hyperbolic plane has no primitive
characteristic vector. In the odd lattice \(I_{1,1}\), the primitive
characteristic vector \(b=(1,1)\) admits both

\[
f_1=(2,-1),
\qquad
f_2=(4,1),
\]

with

\[
b\cdot f_1=b\cdot f_2=3.
\]

Their invariant norms are \(3\) and \(15\), so no integral lattice isometry
can identify them. Pairing three therefore has a non-singleton physical
fiber.

Two exact positive metrics compatible with \(I_{1,1}\) give one flux vector
distinct kinetic energies \(5\) and \(13/4\). The integral lattice does not stabilize
the physical threshold normalization.

## Classification

Rank two repairs the primitive-pairing obstruction but remains neither a
unique flux selector nor a metric selector. A source-derived secondary
invariant or potential must select one orbit and stabilize one metric before
orientation, threshold survival, or detector instrumentation can be claimed.
