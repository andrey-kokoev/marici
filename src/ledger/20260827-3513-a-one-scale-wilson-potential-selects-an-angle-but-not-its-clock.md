# 3513 — A One-Scale Wilson Potential Selects an Angle but Not Its Clock

## Claim

For every one-scale compactification potential

\[
V(\theta,R)=\frac{C}{R^4}F(\theta),
\]

joint stationarity requires \(F'=F=0\). At such a point the mixed and radial
Hessian entries vanish. No single homogeneous Casimir term can strictly
stabilize both the Wilson angle and radius.

Even the favorable oriented shape

\[
F(\theta)=1-\cos(\theta-\phi)
\]

selects \(\theta=\phi\) with positive angular curvature while leaving every
positive radius degenerate. Doubling the radius preserves the selected angle
and energy but halves every absolute threshold.

A second scaling,

\[
\frac{Af}{R^4}+\frac{Bg}{R^6},
\]

can stabilize only at

\[
R_*^2=-\frac{3Bg}{2Af}.
\]

The clock is therefore transferred to a signed coefficient ratio.

## Classification

Hosotani dynamics can be a dimensionless angle selector and rigidifier. The
full source still requires two independently derived radius homogeneities
whose relative sign and coefficient are fixed by the same chiral operation.
