# Homology Descent Is Strictly Weaker Than a Physical Chain Map

Epistemic-graph event: 1331.

For a pairing-forced graded map with boundary defect `Omega`, an induced map
on degree-`n` homology exists exactly when

`Omega_n(Z_n(G))=0`

and

`Omega_(n+1)(C_(n+1)(G))` is contained in `B_n(H)`.

These conditions are weaker than `Omega=0`.  With
`D_G=D_H=diag(1,0)`, `S_1=diag(0,1)`, and `S_0=diag(1,1)`, the defect is
`diag(-1,0)`: nonzero but confined to a contractible summand.  The map still
induces the identity on the surviving homology summand.

Therefore a class-level physical readout might survive even when the strict
relative-chain Mackey object fails.  Future five-site boundary data should
report both verdicts separately.  Homology descent does not supply the
missing trace/Gysin map or justify chain-level composition.
