# 2767 — Theta Cross Zeros Are Incidence-Divisor Crossings, Not Operator Failures

For an invertible full transfer \(T(z)\), source vector \(v\), and endpoint
covector \(\ell\), the theta scalar is

\[
F(z)=\ell(T(z)v).
\]

Its zero means that the transported source ray enters the
endpoint-orthogonal hyperplane. The full relation can remain unitary,
invertible, and orientation preserving. A \(90^\circ\) planar rotation is the
minimal hostile: its selected diagonal coefficient vanishes while its
determinant remains one.

Thus a scalar zero is a Schubert-type incidence-divisor crossing—a loss of
transversality in the chosen labelled chart—not destruction of the complete
two-sector object.

The RH-bearing conjecture is now geometric: the source-derived theta/Tate
transfer remains in the corresponding open incidence cell throughout both
open reciprocal sectors and may cross the divisor only on the sewing seam.
Generic multiport faithfulness or unitarity cannot enforce this; the source
must provide total positivity, an acute semigroup, a nonvanishing Gauss pivot,
or another chamber-confinement law.
