# Positive Coherence Alone Does Not Imply Double-Slit Complementarity

The stronger reading of Entry 2026 is false.

The normalized Gram

\[
G=\begin{pmatrix}1&4/5\\4/5&1\end{pmatrix}
\]

is strictly positive and supplies visibility \(V=4/5\).  If
distinguishability is an independently attached readout, positivity does not
forbid \(D=4/5\), giving

\[
V^2+D^2=32/25>1.
\]

Thus a positive coherence pairing does not by itself explain the
visibility--distinguishability bound.

Entry 2026 closes because both probes come from the same positive record
object: visibility is its overlap, while distinguishability is the optimal
positive separator of its two rank-one projectors.  Then

\[
D^2=\det G=1-V^2.
\]

The surviving Deutsch--Popperian statement is therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{complementarity follows when coherence and discrimination are
source-generated probes of one record object, not independent policies.}
}
\]

The general mixed-record frontier is to derive the positive cone,
dagger/effects, fidelity, discrimination norm, forgetful contraction, and
purification from the source-generated interface.  Importing the
Fuchs--van de Graaf inequality establishes standard quantum mechanics but does
not yet explain why Marici generates that readout structure.
