# The First Soft Deformation Mixes the Euler Plane with the Quartic Tail

Benincasa Entry 449 proves symbolically that the frozen exact-form cokernel is
the quartic tail together with the Euler-resonant plane generated by
\([1]\) and \([a^7(b+1)]\) after removing the universal \(a^4\) factor.
Entry 448 shows that the soft comparison is necessarily meromorphic. The
next invariant is the first deformation without choosing either resonant
representative as a splitting.

Work over \(\mathbf F[u]/(u^2)\) with the source-fixed first-order data
\[
K=a^4+u,a^2(1-b^2),
\qquad
L_1=b+1-u,
\qquad
L_2=a-\frac{u}{2}.
\]
All four exact sectors and both exact columns are deformed before taking the
filtered cokernel. At polynomial cutoffs
\(D=12,16,20,24,28\), the special-fibre dimensions reproduce
\[
\dim C_D=4D.
\]
If the complete cokernel lifted flatly over the dual numbers, its underlying
field dimension would be \(8D\). Instead the calculation gives
\[
\dim C^{(1)}_D=7D+6,
\qquad
8D-\dim C^{(1)}_D=D-6.
\]

Thus the first deformation is not a flat direct lift of a rank-four
Cayley--Menger tail plus an independent two-plane. Its flatness defect grows
with the cutoff, so the soft operator mixes the finite Euler resonance with
the infinite quartic tail already at first order.

This is a statement about the frozen polynomial-degree filtration over the
certificate field. A logarithmic blowup may change the correct filtration by
assigning exceptional weights; the linear defect is therefore not a no-go
for a geometric nearby-cycle object. It is a no-go for transporting the two
special-fibre summands independently in the unmodified filtration.

The next gate is to pull the entire dual-number exact complex—not merely the
quartic relation—to the blowup of \((u,a)\), assign its source-derived
exceptional filtration shifts, and recompute flatness there. Only afterward
can one define the logarithmic Gauss--Manin action or a support-forgetting
