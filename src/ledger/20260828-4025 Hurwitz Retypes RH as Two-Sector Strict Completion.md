# Hurwitz Retypes RH as Two-Sector Strict Completion

Every finite completed Euler section is holomorphic and nowhere zero in the
open critical strip. If source-derived unit renormalizations make these
sections converge locally uniformly to \(\xi\) separately on

\[
D_+=\{1/2<\Re s<1\},
\qquad
D_-=\{0<\Re s<1/2\},
\]

then Hurwitz's theorem forces \(\xi\) to be nowhere zero in both sectors.
This proves RH. Conversely, under RH the backward ratios \(\xi/C_X\) are
sectorwise units, so existence of such unit completions is equivalent to RH.

The explanatory target is noncircular: derive the renormalization units and
their normal-family compactness from the graded Euler and theta boundary data.
An isolated zero is exactly where strict invertible descent fails through
nonuniform convergence, loss of a unit, escaping inverses, determinant
discontinuity, or a nonexact boundary operation.

This also gives a precise reason for two sectors. The critical seam may remain
a singular common boundary while completion stays strict in each open
chamber.
