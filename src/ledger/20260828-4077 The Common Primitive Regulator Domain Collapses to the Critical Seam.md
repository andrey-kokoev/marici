# The Common Primitive Regulator Domain Collapses to the Critical Seam

For \(z=s-1/2\), Mellin modulation changes the two regulated primitive prime
layers to

\[
p^{-1-\varepsilon/2+\Re z}
\]

and

\[
p^{-1-\varepsilon/2-\Re z}.
\]

Both all-prime sums converge exactly when

\[
|\Re z|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
\]

As \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), their common relative domain collapses to

\[
\Re z=0,
\qquad
\Re s=\frac12.
\]

Thus the critical line is the unique common renormalization threshold of the
two reciprocal primitive currents, independently of zero locations.

This explains the seam but does not prove RH: a zero must still be shown to
define a state in the common completed object.
