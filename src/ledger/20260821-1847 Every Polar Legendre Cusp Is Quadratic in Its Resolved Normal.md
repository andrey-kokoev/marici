# 1847 — Every Polar Legendre Cusp Is Quadratic in Its Resolved Normal

## Coarse modular invariant

For Entry 1846's Legendre modulus,

\[
j^{-1}(m)
=
\frac{m^2(1-m)^2}{256(1-m+m^2)^3}.
\]

Its three cusp expansions are

\[
j^{-1}=\frac{m^2}{256}+O(m^3),
\]

\[
j^{-1}=\frac{(1-m)^2}{256}+O((1-m)^3),
\]

and

\[
j^{-1}=\frac{m^{-2}}{256}+O(m^{-3}).
\]

## Pullback to labelled normals

Write

\[
c=h_2\delta_1-h_1\delta_2.
\]

Substitution of

\[
m=\frac{h_2\delta_1}{c}
\]

gives, at generic points of the three cusp divisors,

\[
\delta_1=0:qquad
j^{-1}
=
\frac{h_2^2}{256h_1^2\delta_2^2}\delta_1^2
+O(\delta_1^3),
\]

\[
\delta_2=0:qquad
j^{-1}
=
\frac{h_1^2}{256h_2^2\delta_1^2}\delta_2^2
+O(\delta_2^3),
\]

and

\[
c=0:qquad
j^{-1}
=
\frac{c^2}{256h_2^2\delta_1^2}
+O(c^3).
\]

Thus every labelled linear normal is a square-root coordinate over its coarse
modular cusp.  The ordinary first normal grade vanishes, while the second
normal grade is generically nonzero.

## Consequence

The five-site polar sector independently reproduces the three-site pattern

\[
\boxed{
\text{linear resolved normal}
\longmapsto
\text{quadratic coarse modular invariant}.
}
\]

This is not merely a property of total energy: it also holds for the two
labelled wall normals and for their Hessian-weighted coefficient collision.
Accordingly, first-jet transport cannot detect the first ordinary deformation
of the coarse elliptic modulus at any of these cusps.  Nearby-cycle monodromy
and second Rees grade must remain distinct pieces of the comparison calculus.

No new carrier datum is introduced.
