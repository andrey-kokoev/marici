## Exact residue matrix

The checker computes every residue of every logarithmic coefficient at these
three poles. The first moving logarithm has zero residue at all three poles.
After grouping equal zero-minus-pole differences, only three nonzero classes
remain:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{difference letter}&\text{grouped coefficient}\\
\hline
2y&-\dfrac{1}{(x_2-y)(x_2+y)(x_1+y)}\\[6pt]
x_2+y&\dfrac{1}{2y(x_2-y)(x_1+y)}\\[6pt]
x_2-y&-\dfrac{1}{2y(x_2+y)(x_1+y)}
\end{array}
\]

In particular, the collision of \(w_1+x_1+x_2\) with \(w_1+2y\) might have
generated

\[
x_1+x_2-2y.
\]

Its residue coefficient is exactly zero:

\[
\boxed{
[x_1+x_2-2y]_{\rm collision}=0.
}
\]
