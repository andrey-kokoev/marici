# An Ultraviolet Fixed Point Can Fix a Portal but Not Its Clock

An isolated interacting ultraviolet fixed point can make a dimensionless
portal coefficient non-adjustable when that direction is ultraviolet
repulsive. In the exact normal form

\[
\frac{d\,\delta p}{ds}=2\delta p,
\]

ultraviolet completion forces \(\delta p=0\). This is a conditional selector
for dimensionless portal magnitude, not merely a presentation rigidifier.

The same source does not fix a relevant mass deformation:

\[
\frac{dm}{ds}=-m.
\]

The exact trajectories with coefficients \(m=1\) and \(m=4\) reach the same
ultraviolet endpoint but have thresholds in ratio \(1:2\). An even fixed-point
equation \(p^2=1\) also leaves two sign branches, and detector calibration is
an independent coordinate. A complete explanation therefore still requires
an orientation-odd source invariant, a unique massive vacuum or
source-generated relational clock, threshold matching, and a calibrated
physical16 instrument.
