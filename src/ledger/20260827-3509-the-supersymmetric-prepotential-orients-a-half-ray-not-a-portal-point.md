# 3509 — The Supersymmetric Prepotential Orients a Half-Ray, Not a Portal Point

## Claim

On the \(SU(4)\) Coulomb ray, the five-dimensional cubic prepotential has

\[
\mathcal F(a)=6ra^2-4ka^3,
\qquad
G(a)=\mathcal F''(a)=12r-24ka.
\]

At the formal fixed-point face \(r=0\), positivity fixes the sign of \(a\)
relative to the level \(k\). Extended gauge structure also fixes the
fundamental signed masses to

\[
(a,a,a,-3a),
\]

removing WP787's independent matter vertex.

However, the prepotential is kinetic geometry rather than the WP787 scalar
potential. Unbroken supersymmetry leaves \(a\) as a flat Coulomb modulus, and
the scalar masses are \((a^2,a^2,a^2,9a^2)\). The points \(a=-s\) and
\(a=-2s\) share the same level, positive cone, and relative threshold ratio
three while their scalar contrasts differ by four.

## Classification

The supersymmetric prepotential parallelizes topological level, kinetic
geometry, and matter charge. It selects a relative orientation half-ray but
not a vacuum point, absolute threshold, signed scalar portal, or calibrated
readout.
