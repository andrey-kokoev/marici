# Endpoint Faces Leave a Torsion-Free Road Ideal

## Result

The Sommerfeld-style minimal calculation can be completed at the exact
coefficient-shadow level, but it also exposes why the physical hidden-extension
group cannot yet be assigned a rank.

There are two distinct statements.

First, the unrestricted one-road mixed block

\[
R\langle m\rangle
\xrightarrow{(1,-x_3)}
R\langle q,\xi\rangle
\xrightarrow{(x_3,1)}
R\langle b\rangle
\]

has the integral contraction

\[
h(b)=\xi,
\qquad h(q)=m.
\]

Thus its ordinary mapping complex is acyclic, in agreement with Entry 133.
The desired realization cannot be an ordinary coefficientwise extension.

Second, use the literal one-road target and its two endpoint quotients from
Entries 130 and 380.  Their simultaneous degreewise kernel is

\[
K_{\partial,2}=0,
\qquad
K_{\partial,1}=R\langle e_2\rangle,
\qquad
K_{\partial,0}=R\langle e_3,e_4\rangle,
\]

with differential

\[
d e_2=-x_0e_3+x_1e_4.
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
H_1(K_\partial)=0,
\qquad
H_0(K_\partial)\simeq (x_0,x_1)
}
\]

up to the evident grading shift.  The isomorphism is induced by

\[
(a,b)\longmapsto x_1a+x_0b,
\]

whose syzygy is generated by \((-x_0,x_1)\).  This module is rank one and
torsion-free, but it is not a free line at the joint endpoint ideal.  Hence
the two endpoint restrictions leave a genuine mixed road-interior receptacle;
they do not leave a new free top cell.

## Strong versus weak generic restriction

The endpoint-relative module \((x_0,x_1)\) has no \(x_3\)-torsion.  Therefore
the localization map

\[
(x_0,x_1)\longrightarrow (x_0,x_1)[x_3^{-1}]
\]

is injective.  If “the generic face is fixed” means that the entire
deformation becomes zero after generic \(x_3\)-localization, then

\[
\boxed{
\ker(\operatorname{res}_{v_{00}},
      \operatorname{res}_{v_{10}},
      \operatorname{res}_{x_3\ne0})=0
}
\]

in this coefficient shadow.

That strong statement is not what Entries 378--381 currently construct.
They fix the component

\[
q_J\longmapsto x_3q_{03}^{Q}
\]

and its first Cartier symbol.  A first-symbol condition alone does not control
higher filtration.  In particular

\[
x_3^2(x_0,x_1)\ne0
\]

but has zero first \(x_3\)-Rees symbol.  Thus componentwise agreement on the
listed faces is weaker than a restriction map on the entire deformation
complex.

This is the precise well-posedness gate revealed by the hidden-extension
audit: before computing

\[
H^\bullet\mathcal D_{03}^{\rm rel},
\]

one must define the generic, Cartier, lower-Cech, and endpoint restrictions
as morphisms out of one common admissible mapping complex.  Otherwise the
answer changes depending on whether “generic face” means one checked
coefficient or full localization of every deformation.

## Reconciliation with the earlier lift spaces

The three previously observed phenomena concern different quotients and do
not conflict:

1. Entry 136's affine rank-nine family consists of strict full-cone
   representatives.  The canonical AW roof is common to every member, so
   those nine parameters are not nine intrinsic extension classes.
2. Entry 133's ordinary Hom complex is acyclic because its contraction is
   allowed to cross the based (Q)-filtration and erase endpoint support.
3. Entries 141 and 143 isolate a possible endpoint-fixed
   \(\mathbb Z/2\) reflection defect only after admissible connector data are
   supplied.  That mapping fibre is still uninstantiated, so its parity is
   not presently a computed class.

Benincasa's Entry 313 remains the correct warning: compatible slices do not
by themselves determine an interior.  The present calculation sharpens that
warning for (D03): there is no hidden coefficient class under the strong
full-generic interpretation, while the weaker face list does not define a
unique restriction kernel at all.
