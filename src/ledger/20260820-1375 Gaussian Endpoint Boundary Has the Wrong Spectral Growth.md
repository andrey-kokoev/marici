# 1375 — Gaussian Endpoint Boundary Has the Wrong Spectral Growth

Epistemic-graph event: 1407.

The normalized Gaussian norm-two correspondence

\[
U=\frac{1+J}{\sqrt2}=e^{\pi J/4}
\]

defines a self-adjoint endpoint law for \(D=-i\,d/dy\) on a compact
logarithmic interval:

\[
f(L)=Uf(0).
\]

If the eigenphases of \(U\) are \(\theta_j\), its spectrum is exactly

\[
\lambda_{j,n}=\frac{2\pi n+\theta_j}{L},
\qquad n\in\mathbf Z.
\]

Consequently every finite-rank, energy-independent endpoint unitary has

\[
N_D(T)=\frac{rL}{2\pi}T+O(1).
\]

Its characteristic determinant is, up to a nowhere-zero exponential factor,

\[
\det(e^{izL}I-U),
\]

a finite product of shifted sine factors with linear zero density. This
cannot equal completed Riemann \(\xi\), whose nontrivial-zero count has leading
term \(T\log T/(2\pi)\).

The Gaussian boundary therefore survives as the source-derived eighth-phase
corner factor, but it cannot supply the full zero density or prime
fluctuations. A successful boundary must be infinite-dimensional or
energy-dependent and must couple to the intrinsic-prime sector.

Scope: this falsifies only the direct finite-dimensional endpoint
realization. It does not falsify the integral correspondence \(K=1+J\),
construct the unavailable physical relative-chain pushforward, or exclude an
infinite-rank prime-coupled boundary.
