# 2587 — Theta Relative Euler Finite Part Is Regulator-Universal

For every smooth rapidly decreasing regulator \(\rho\) normalized by
\(\rho(0)=1\), Mellin inversion gives

\[
Z_{\rho,\varepsilon}(s)
=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(c)}
\widehat\rho(z)\varepsilon^{-z}\zeta(s+2z)\,dz.
\]

The pole at \(z=(1-s)/2\) produces a regulator-dependent boundary current,
whereas the unit residue of \(\widehat\rho\) at \(z=0\) produces the universal
constant term

\[
\operatorname{FP}_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
\left[Z_{\rho,\varepsilon}(s)-B_{\rho,\varepsilon}(s)\right]
=\zeta(s).
\]

Thus an admissible regulator change transports the boundary presentation but
does not alter the relative Euler section. A hostile constant counterterm or
divisor-bearing multiplier is not a regulator gauge transformation unless it
changes the source incidence.

## Scope

This proves regulator covariance and canonical-section rigidity within the
admissible smoothing class. It does not orient the universal finite part or
prove its nonvanishing.
