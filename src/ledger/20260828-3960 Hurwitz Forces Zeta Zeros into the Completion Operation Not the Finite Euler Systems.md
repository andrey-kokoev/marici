# Hurwitz Forces Zeta Zeros into the Completion Operation, Not the Finite Euler Systems

Every finite Euler transfer is zero-free away from its local poles. A locally
uniform limit of such transfers multiplied by nowhere-zero holomorphic units
is, by Hurwitz's theorem, either zero-free or identically zero.

Hence isolated completed zeta zeros cannot be produced by benign locally
uniform descent of finite Euler products. Completion must lose local
uniformity, introduce divisor-bearing boundary data, enlarge the system before
scalar compression, or use a distinct source sewing operation.

Aspect's two-prime hostile closes the opposite shortcut: positive scalar
addition of locally confined cells already creates off-seam zeros. The missing
completion must therefore retain cross-prime incidence before scalarization;
it is neither naive product limit nor naive scalar sum.
