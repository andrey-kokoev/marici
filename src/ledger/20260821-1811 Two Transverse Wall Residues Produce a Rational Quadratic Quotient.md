# 1811 — Two Transverse Wall Residues Produce a Rational Quadratic Quotient

## Question

Do Entry 1810's active transverse pairs carry the tensor product of their two
one-wall Kummer coefficient lines?

## Local model

Normalize the threshold Morse germ to

\[
\tau+u^2+v^2.
\]

For two transverse wall normals, write

\[
g_A=h_1+au+bv,
\qquad
g_B=h_2+cu+dv,
\]

and

\[
L=
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
D=\det L\neq0.
\]

Entry 1804 certifies \(D\neq0\) for every pair considered here, and Entry
1809 certifies its source coefficient as nonzero.

## Double residue

The two wall equations fix both Morse variables:

\[
\binom uv=-L^{-1}\binom{h_1}{h_2}.
\]

Consequently, up to the ordered residue orientation,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Res}_{g_A,g_B}
\frac{du\,dv}
{(\tau+u^2+v^2)g_Ag_B}
=
\frac{(2\pi i)^2}
{D\left(\tau+h^TL^{-T}L^{-1}h\right)}.
}
\]

The quadratic numerator before division by \(D^2\) is

\[
(c^2+d^2)h_1^2
-2(ac+bd)h_1h_2
+(a^2+b^2)h_2^2.
\]

## Result

The coefficient object is a rank-one meromorphic quadratic-pole quotient:

\[
T_s=+1,
\qquad
N=0.
\]

It is not the tensor product of two one-wall Kummer lines. The reason is
geometric: each one-wall residue leaves one Morse integration variable and
therefore a square root, while two independent wall residues fix both Morse
variables and leave no integration variable from which a second square root
could arise.

All 22 local active types have this form. Their labelled cyclic assembly has
rank 110 and character

\[
(110,0,0,0,0).
\]

The quadratic pole is coefficient support compiled from the existing
threshold and wall normals. It is not a new carrier generator.

## Architectural consequence

Coefficient formation is not multiplicative under transverse carrier
intersection:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Kummer wall quotient}\otimes\text{Kummer wall quotient}
\not\simeq
\text{two-wall residue quotient}.
}
\]

The correct object is obtained by applying the iterated residue functor to the
shared Morse coefficient germ, not by tensoring already-integrated one-wall
answers.
