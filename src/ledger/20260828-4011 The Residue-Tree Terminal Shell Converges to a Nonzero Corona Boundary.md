# The Residue-Tree Terminal Shell Converges to a Nonzero Corona Boundary

The local residue state group is

\[
A_p=\mathbb Q_p/\mathbb Z_p,
\qquad
\widehat A_p=\mathbb Z_p.
\]

Finite residue states and finite-conductor observables have the canonical
Pontryagin pairing. For \(r_k=p^{-k}\), the coset states
\(e_{r_k}=1_{r_k+\mathbb Z_p}\) have norm one and converge weakly, but not
strongly, to zero.

The terminal difference shell therefore satisfies

\[
\|\Delta_{r_k}e_0\|_2^2=2,
\qquad
\Delta_{r_k}e_0=e_{r_k}-e_0\rightharpoonup-e_0.
\]

Thus the inductive residue tree and its Fourier-dual observables pair
nondegenerately at every finite level, but dilation is not uniformly
observable. Finite-cutoff telescoping leaves a nonzero unramified corona
boundary. A vanishing-flux Green theorem requires an independently sourced
reciprocal or archimedean cancellation of this common mode.
