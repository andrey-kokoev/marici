\[

## Accessibility calculation

In the ordered basis \((e_2,e_3,e_4,e_5)\), consider

\[
I_y,
\quad
\nabla I_y,
\quad
I_q,
\quad
\nabla I_q.
\]

Their coordinate rows are

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0&0\\
0&1/\lambda&0&0\\
0&1&-2\lambda&0\\
0&-1/\lambda&-2&-2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The determinant is

\[
\boxed{4}.
\]

Equivalently, after multiplying the two derivative rows by \(\lambda\),
the determinant is \(4\lambda^2\), nonzero on the declared slice locus.
Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\langle I_y,I_q\rangle_{\nabla}
=
\langle e_2,e_3,e_4,e_5\rangle,
\qquad
\operatorname{rank}=4.
}
\]

## Sector classification

The minimal Gauss–Manin-stable subsystem generated by the exceptional
moment pair is exactly the sum of the two algebraic rank-two blocks. It has
no component in the final block

\[
\langle e_6,e_7,e_8,e_9\rangle,
\]

whose infinity-Gysin quotient is the elliptic system.

Thus, on this certified slice, the candidate cosmological fault-tolerant
readout is transported by algebraic/Tate–Kummer coefficient data rather
than by the pure elliptic quotient. In particular, this mechanism does not
provide a new home for \(\mathcal Q\).

## Narrow conclusion

The exceptional physical circuit cannot be a constant algebraic relation
between \(I_y\) and \(I_q\), and it cannot be a rank-two flat coefficient
subsystem. Any scalar circuit selected by a physical current must be a
period relation inside a rank-four algebraic Gauss–Manin system.

This is a theorem only on Entry 169's one-parameter slice. It does not prove
that the full multivariate closure has rank four, nor that a Bunch–Davies
relative chain selects a scalar quotient.
