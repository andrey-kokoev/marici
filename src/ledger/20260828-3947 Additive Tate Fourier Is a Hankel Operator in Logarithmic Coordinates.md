# Additive Tate Fourier Is a Hankel Operator in Logarithmic Coordinates

Under the unitary half-density pullback

\[
(Uf)(q)=e^{q/2}f(e^q),
\]

the additive Tate Fourier transform becomes a Hankel operator. For even and
odd additive lifts its kernels are respectively

\[
(K_+g)(r)=2\int e^{(r+q)/2}\cos(2\pi e^{r+q})g(q)\,dq,
\]

\[
(K_-g)(r)=-2i\int e^{(r+q)/2}\sin(2\pi e^{r+q})g(q)\,dq.
\]

They depend on (r+q\), unlike ordinary Fourier transform in the logarithmic
coordinate. For (h_1(q)=q e^{-\pi q^2}\),

\[
(K_+h_1)(r)
=\frac{1}{2\pi}e^{1/(16\pi)}e^{r/2}+O(e^{5r/2})
\]

as (r\to-\infty\), whereas (h_1(r)\) decays super-exponentially. Thus the
Hermite endpoint witness is not a Tate Fourier eigenvector. Endpoint pointing
and sheet transport are separate rungs; any orientation theorem must use the
actual Hankel operator.
