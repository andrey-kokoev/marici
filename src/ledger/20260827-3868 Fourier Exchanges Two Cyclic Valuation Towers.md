# Fourier Exchanges Two Cyclic Valuation Towers

Normalized additive dilation satisfies

\[
\mathcal F U_q=U_{-q}\mathcal F.
\]

Therefore Fourier sends every positive prime valuation to its negative and
cannot act internally on the positive Euler cyclic tower. This remains true at
every cyclic arity: word length is preserved while valuation orientation is
reversed.

The smallest Fourier-stable label object is the Grothendieck completion

\[
\bigoplus_p\mathbb N
\longrightarrow
\bigoplus_p\mathbb Z,
\]

whose positive and negative cones are exchanged by Fourier. The primitive,
square, and connected-tail observers must therefore occur in two polarized
cyclic towers joined by a relative sewing correspondence. A one-tower
Fourier endomorphism is ill-typed unless it has erased valuation orientation.

The one-prime falsifier is already complete: `e_1` must map to `e_-1`, and at
square grade `e_1 odot e_1` must map to `e_-1 odot e_-1`. Neither target lies
in the positive Euler cone.
