# 1757 — Singular Calibration Curvature Leaves a Parabolic Residue

## Singular frame

Use Entry 1744's source-derived singular frame

\[
F_\varepsilon=\operatorname{diag}(1,\varepsilon)
\]

and transport one calibration:

\[
A_\varepsilon
=F_\varepsilon^{-1}AF_\varepsilon
=
\begin{pmatrix}
a_{11}&\varepsilon a_{12}\\
\varepsilon^{-1}a_{21}&a_{22}
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Let \(B\) remain regular. Entry 1756's mixed curvature becomes

\[
K_\varepsilon=[[A_\varepsilon,B],H].
\]

It has at most a simple pole, entirely generated by the negative parabolic
grade \(a_{21}/\varepsilon\).

## Forced regularization

Multiplication by the source-derived Hom/Rees factor \(\varepsilon\) gives a
regular polynomial family. Its specialization is

\[
\boxed{
R_{\rm par}
=
\left[
\left[
\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\a_{21}&0\end{pmatrix},
B\right],H\right].
}
\]

This is not a fitted residue: the matrix

\[
\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\a_{21}&0\end{pmatrix}
\]

is exactly the negative-grade component selected by the reference-frame
valuation.

## Narrow result

Parabolic regularization removes the Laurent divergence but need not kill
its supported coefficient:

\[
\boxed{
\varepsilon K_\varepsilon
\longrightarrow R_{\rm par},
\qquad R_{\rm par}\ \text{generically nonzero}.
}
\]

Thus singular noncommuting calibrations can leave a genuine class supported
at the rank-drop locus. Its provenance is coefficient curvature in the
existing Hom/Rees packet. No new Cut carrier stratum is required.
