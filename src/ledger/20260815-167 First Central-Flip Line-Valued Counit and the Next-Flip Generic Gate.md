## The first marked central flip

Let \(e\) be the oriented first edge of the positive marked \(D03\)
corridor, from the normalization endpoint \(v_+\) to the first marked road
state \(m_+\). Over the polynomial occurrence ring its boundary is

\[
\boxed{
de=X_{D03}m_+-x_5v_+.
}
\]

Keep the occurrence ideals \((X_{D03})\) and \((x_5)\) as separately
labelled principal lines. Neither coefficient is inverted.

An endpoint functional is a pair

\[
(f_m,f_v):
R\langle m_+,v_+\rangle\longrightarrow L
\]

into the common shifted occurrence-value line \(L\). Closedness is exactly

\[
\boxed{
X_{D03}f_m=x_5f_v.
}
\]

This is the first central-flip occurrence syzygy.

## Primitive saturated solution

Because \(x_5\) and \(X_{D03}\) are independent primitive polynomial
variables, every solution is a common multiple of

\[
(f_m,f_v)=(x_5,X_{D03}).
\]

Thus

\[
\ker\bigl[X_{D03},-x_5\bigr]
=R\,(x_5,X_{D03}).
\]

The oriented generator is primitive and saturated. Equivalently, the row
\((x_5,X_{D03})\) admits a unit completion, so its quotient has no integer
torsion and no hidden common polynomial factor. The marked orientation fixes
the positive generator.

This yields the canonical shifted line-valued counit

\[
\epsilon^{\rm line}_{e,+}:
R\langle m_+,v_+\rangle
\longrightarrow
L,
\qquad
(m_+,v_+)\longmapsto(x_5,X_{D03}).
\]

It is line valued: the two coordinates remain in their named occurrence
ideals until evaluated against their principal dual lines.

## Principal-dual normalization without inversion

Let

\[
x_5^\vee\in(x_5)^\vee,
\qquad
X_{D03}^\vee\in(X_{D03})^\vee
\]

be the principal occurrence-ideal evaluations. Then

\[
x_5^\vee(x_5)=1,
\qquad
X_{D03}^\vee(X_{D03})=1.
\]

These evaluations normalize the primitive line without adjoining
\(x_5^{-1}\) or \(X_{D03}^{-1}\) to the base ring. They are bivariant
principal-line evaluations, not ordinary scalar division.

The tempting scalar endpoint pair is not closed:

\[
(f_m,f_v)=(1,1)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
X_{D03}-x_5\ne0.
\]

Therefore the ordinary same-scalar counit is falsified over the independent
occurrence ring. Replacing the line-valued pair by \((1,1)\) would identify
two unrelated occurrence variables and erase the syzygy.

## Normal-line separation and abstract excess compatibility

The reciprocal source normal \(u_5^\vee\) is a third, independent line. It
evaluates the exclusive plus-sheet normal \(u_5\); it evaluates neither
\(x_5\) nor \(X_{D03}\). Hence

\[
u_5^\vee\ne x_5^\vee,
\qquad
u_5^\vee\ne X_{D03}^\vee.
\]

After removing this exclusive normal bivariantly, the normals \(u_1,u_3\)
are shared between the plus packet and the first road chart. The repeated
\(u_3\) factor retains the two-support excess grades

\[
\operatorname{Tor}_0=R/J,
\qquad
\operatorname{Tor}_1=R/J.
\]

Thus the first-flip occurrence line, reciprocal \(u_5^\vee\) evaluation,
shared \(u_1/u_3\) packet, and repeated-\(u_3\) excess are compatible as an
abstract bivariant coefficient tensor product. No naive contraction of the
two Tor grades is permitted.

This compatibility does not identify the coefficient tensor product with a
spatial entry-143 costalk or edge.

## Legal Cech arrival of the long normal

The target-side BM--Cech denominator rule permits \(u_a^{-1}\) only on a
state \([S,H]\) with \(a\in S\setminus H\). On arrival at the uncircled
long-divisor state

\[
[D03,H_D],
\qquad
H_D=\varnothing,
\]

the long normal is present in the face and absent from the circle set.
Consequently

\[
\frac1{u_{D03}}[D03,\varnothing]
\]

is a legal target Cech term. This legality is local to the arrival state. It
does not put \(u_{D03}^{-1}\) on the source, does not legalize inverse short
normals on circled states, and does not construct the spatial map carrying
the first-flip counit into that summand.

The checker proves the finite occurrence syzygy and its line typing. It does

\[

- the endpoint comparison with the fixed entry-143 filtration.

Without that spatial comparison, the line-valued counit is a canonical
coefficient operation, not an arrow in the global endpoint/\(Q\) mapping

## The next-flip and generic gate

The second middle flip must join the first road chart to the chart carrying
the \(D03\) long normal and then to the \(x_0\) branch. Its analogue of the
first syzygy must be constructed with the actual marked labels, not obtained
by setting the first counit equal to a scalar.

More importantly, gluing the two flips must retain the pre-quotient generic
generator in

\[
dH=q_J-x_3\widetilde\xi_{03}.
\]

The existing gallery terms lie in \(F_1\) and have zero image in
\(Q=F_K/F_B\). Therefore the first-flip counit cannot by itself produce the
generic component. The next construction must supply a ringed
extraordinary-support comparison

\[
\operatorname{BC}^{\log}_{+;03}
\]

that glues both central flips, maps the retained \(q_J\) nontrivially to the
fixed \(Q03\) leg, and restricts to the repeated-\(u_3\) overlap residue in
both Tor grades.

The legal \(1/u_{D03}\) arrival is a necessary local target check for this
map; it is not evidence that the gluing or generic leg already exists.

## Falsifiers and scope

The line-valued theorem would be falsified by another independent syzygy,
failure of \((x_5,X_{D03})\) to be saturated, integer torsion in its quotient,
or a closed scalar pair \((1,1)\) over the independent occurrence ring.

The geometric boundary would be crossed by an independently constructed
ringed support identification of the first flip with the literal entry-143
edge, followed by a second-flip comparison and \(U_{03}/Q\) gluing that
retains \(q_J\), both Tor grades, and the local overlap residue.

No no-go is claimed for that enlarged spatial correspondence. The scoped
falsifier concerns only the ordinary scalar counit.
