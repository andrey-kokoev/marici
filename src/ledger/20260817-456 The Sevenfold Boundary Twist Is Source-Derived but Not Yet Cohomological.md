# The Sevenfold Boundary Twist Is Source-Derived but Not Yet Cohomological

Entry 455 found the divisor

\[
3[b=1]+4[b=-1]
\]

between the graded Euler representative and the geometric Cartier generator.
It is not an arbitrary correction.  On the Cartier section,

\[
t=\frac12(b-1)(b+1),
\qquad
L_1|_{u=0}=b+1.
\]

Therefore

\[
\operatorname{div}(t)=[1]+[-1],
\qquad
\operatorname{div}(L_1)=[-1],
\]

and the boundary discrepancy has the unique nonnegative decomposition

\[
3([1]+[-1])+[-1]
=3\operatorname{div}(t)+\operatorname{div}(L_1).
\]

Both factors are forced by the source.  The three powers of (t) arise from
the three conversions (a^2=ut) in

\[
a^7(b+1)=a\,u^3t^3(b+1),
\]

while the remaining (b+1) is precisely one legal difference of incidence
sector exponent (e_a=2-s_a).  Thus the boundary lattice modification
requested by Entry 455 exists and is derived rather than inserted post hoc.

This does not identify the Euler plane with
(pi_*\mathcal O_D).  Entry 449 on our line proved that naive multiplication
by (a) or (b) does not descend to the Euler quotient.  The Euler plane is a
two-dimensional cohomological quotient, whereas
(pi_*\mathcal O_D) is a rank-two (mathbb Q[b])-module.  Equal generic
bases and the correct lattice transition are necessary, not sufficient.

The corrected candidate is a **twisted Cartier complex**: its odd generator
carries three tautological Rees shifts and one incidence shift.  The next gate
is to derive the residual exact/Euler differential on this twisted layer and
compute its cohomology.  Only if that cohomology is generated by the two
resonant degrees may the identification be promoted.
