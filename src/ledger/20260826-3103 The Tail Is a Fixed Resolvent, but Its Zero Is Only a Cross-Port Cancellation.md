# 3103 — The Tail Is a Fixed Resolvent, but Its Zero Is Only a Cross-Port Cancellation

The theta tail is a resolvent vector of the fixed translation generator, and
carrier reflection turns its future-tail operator into the adjoint past-head
operator. Thus fixed-carrier recovery succeeds without the singular
augmentation.

But the scalar transform is a cross-port coefficient:

\[
G_s(0)=\langle\delta_0,T_s f\rangle.
\]

Self-adjointness does not confine zeros of cross-resolvent coefficients. An
exact three-point self-adjoint model with distinct real source and observation
ports has zeros at \(z=\pm i\).

The remaining law must identify or positively couple the endpoint observer
and theta source port. Reverse carrier evolution exists; reverse port
incidence does not.

This sharpens the existing two-port cross-transfer theorem by deriving the
tail–head adjoint pair and adding a minimal explicit nonreal-zero witness; it
does not count the two-port typing as a separate new mechanism.

## Scope

The resolvent and hostile finite theorems are exact. The required
source-derived port colligation remains open.
