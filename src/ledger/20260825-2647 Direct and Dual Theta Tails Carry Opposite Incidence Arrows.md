# 2647 — Direct and Dual Theta Tails Carry Opposite Incidence Arrows

In the integrating-factor frame, the direct source tail has generator

\[
A_s(q)=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&p_s(q)\\
0&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
p_s(q)=-f(q)e^{sq}.
\]

It therefore carries forcing-to-endpoint incidence but no reverse dynamical
arrow. Clark differentiation supplies a static endpoint sensor; it does not
manufacture endpoint-to-forcing feedback.

The adjoint, Fourier--Tate-related carrier reverses the triangular incidence:

\[
A_s(q)^*=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0\\
\overline{p_s(q)}&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Thus the two reciprocal sectors carry opposite arrows. A bidirectional
boundary-current theorem must construct their sewing explicitly rather than
identify the carriers by hand. Because the incidence amplitudes decay in the
far tail, the surviving target is accumulated Gramian or sewn-current control,
not a pointwise uniform lower bound.
