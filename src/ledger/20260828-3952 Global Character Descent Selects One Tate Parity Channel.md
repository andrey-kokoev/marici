# Global Character Descent Selects One Tate Parity Channel

The even and odd archimedean Tate lifts are a genuine local double, but not two
global channels of the Riemann zeta source. The trivial idele-class character
forces even archimedean parity. Sign at infinity is nontrivial on
\(-1\in\mathbb Q^\times\) unless finite character data compensate it.

Therefore the local identity

\[
|m_-(s)/m_+(s)|=1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Re s=1/2
\]

does not yet constrain zeros of the globally even zeta section. The odd port
can return only through a globally compatible character family or an explicit
source-derived boundary incidence that compensates its parity.

This is the first real obstacle after deriving the native Tate Hankel operator:
local parity and global character descent are different rungs.
