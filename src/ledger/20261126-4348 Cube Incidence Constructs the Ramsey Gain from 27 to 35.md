# 4348 — Cube Incidence Constructs the Ramsey Gain from 27 to 35

**Question:** [Entry 4347](20261126-4347%20Oriented%20Squares%20and%20Cyclic%20Offsets%20Realize%20Different%20Ramsey%20Gains.md) realized the 27 → 35 gain by cyclic offsets. Could we find the eight neighborhoods together without supplying those offsets, and explain why they fit a 26-vertex core?

**Answer:** Yes. Label eight newcomers by the corners of a cube. Take its **8 corners, 12 edges, and 6 faces** as the 26 core vertices. Join each newcomer in red to the seven cells containing its corner: one corner, three edges, three faces. Add one root red to the eight newcomers and blue to the core. There is an explicit red coloring inside the core that makes the result triangle-free with independence number eight. The incidence search found the cube without reading the endpoint or its distances; comparison *after* verification showed the graph is isomorphic to the stored witness. This reconstructs a known lower-bound witness, not a new Ramsey number. [Witness and discovery path](../../research/voevodsky/results/ramsey_incidence35_endpoint.json) · [Standalone constructor](../../research/voevodsky/checkers/ramsey_cube_construct35.py).

## What the predecessor forces

Every red degree on 35 vertices is 7 or 8: a red neighborhood is blue, and the blue neighbors form a legal (3,8) core of order at most 27. If there are \(t>0\) degree-seven vertices, take one with \(s\) degree-seven neighbors. Deleting its closed red neighborhood leaves a 27-core with \(84-t/2+s\) edges. The minimum in the complete stored 27-vertex catalogue is 85. Thus each of the \(t\) vertices has more than \(t/2\) neighbors among them, impossible in a triangle-free graph. **The endpoint must be 8-regular.** At any root its 26-core therefore has exactly \(140-8\cdot8=76\) red edges. The cube cell incidences then require internal degrees 7, 6, and 4 on corners, edges, and faces. The previously observed edge count is a necessary condition, conditional on catalogue completeness. [Edge-floor audit](../../research/voevodsky/results/ramsey_gain35_regular_gate.json).

Searching core-first showed why the distinction between *individual* and *joint* coverage matters. Of 372 independently generated core types, 126 passed a five-neighborhood test on blue sevens; only 32 admitted five once smaller cliques were checked. The highest-ranked core had 4,887 five-panels passing the seven test and none passing the six test. Two red edits elsewhere raised capacity from five to six, each removing a different five of ten forbidden blue nines. But **none of the 305 tested deletion-minimal cores** admitted a route to eight by red additions alone. This is a family exclusion, not a bound on all 26-cores. [Panel census](../../research/voevodsky/results/ramsey_gain26_verify_five.json) · [Two-edit witness](../../research/voevodsky/results/ramsey_gain26_six_edit_effect.json) · [Final sparse layer](../../research/voevodsky/results/ramsey_gain26_regular_triples.json).

## How incidence selected the cube

Give each core vertex the set of newcomers red-adjacent to it. Two cells sharing a label must be blue. A blue core clique of size \(q\) must collectively carry at least \(q\) labels; otherwise it joins the unused newcomers in a blue nine. This is a constructive test: if five cells carry only four labels between them, at least one internal edge must be red. In one search state each of the 56 five-label regions passed separately, but a single degree-correct core could satisfy at most 40. Reassigning labels on pairs of cells and rebuilding the core raised the **joint** score from 40 to 56; the winning completion also passed the full nine-clique check. [Joint obstruction](../../research/voevodsky/results/ramsey_incidence_five_frontier.json).

Two classification results distinguish selection from recognition:

- If relabelings preserving the incidence pattern act **transitively on the eight labels**, only the cube extends. Of 234 incidence types, 65 passed the three-label demands, five passed four-label demands, and one passed five-label demands. A separate census found the same unique survivor if the incidence pattern admits **one permutation with a cycle of length at least five**, without requiring transitivity. Neither test imposed symmetry on the red core. [Transitive selection argument](../../research/voevodsky/ramsey-transitive-cube-selection.md) · [Long-cycle census](../../research/voevodsky/results/ramsey_long_cycle35_levels.json).
- Once cube incidence is fixed, there are exactly **eight labeled core completions**, all equivalent by cube symmetries. Twenty-seven red edges are common to all. Choosing a body diagonal and a handedness determines the rest. The standalone constructor generates the last 48 edges from eight representative pairs by rotating coordinates and complementing bits. [Completion classification](../../research/voevodsky/results/ramsey_cube_edge_rule.json).

## How much of the cube may be erased?

**Any five whole cube neighborhoods force cube incidence back**, up to relabeling, even if all core edges may change. The three possible geometries for the erased corners required separate checks; smaller Ramsey bounds ruled out the last body-diagonal branches. This is rigidity of the incidence, not uniqueness of the labeled core coloring. [Coverage audit](../../research/voevodsky/results/ramsey_cube_five_anchor_audit.json).

Four retained neighborhoods remain open. If they form one face, the four erased newcomers and nine untouched core cells form the unique 13-vertex (3,5) graph: all thirteen are blue to the four retained newcomers. The all-four-label cell acts as a **second root**. It and the original root share exactly the four retained red neighbors: a fifth would require a 22-vertex (3,7) common blue core with at most 58 edges, below the catalogue minimum of 60. Their common blue core has order 21. Its possible 51-edge layer failed all 900 marked embeddings; the 52-, 53-, and 54-edge layers remain, with the known cube at 53. [Double-root derivation](../../research/voevodsky/ramsey-face-double-root.md) · [51-edge exclusion](../../research/voevodsky/results/ramsey_face_bridge_triples51.json).

## What the smaller controls taught us

- On two matched 17-vertex cores, edge expansion and conductance were identical despite different capacities. A quadratic form on **support conflicts**, rather than on the original adjacency matrix, gave an exact bound below four on the failing core. [Cut control](../../research/voevodsky/results/ramsey_expansion17.json) · [Integer certificate verifier](../../research/voevodsky/checkers/ramsey_verify_rayleigh17.py).
- On 22 vertices, sparse balanced cuts helped *find* square diagonals. On 27 vertices they did not certify extension. The 73 failed unmodified square lifts all admitted a one-red-edge patch: an exterior vertex hitting every offending blue five could join a newcomer without making a triangle. The same repair is not universal one order lower. [Cut transfer](../../research/voevodsky/results/ramsey_cut_match_exterior27.json) · [All 73 patches](../../research/voevodsky/results/ramsey_patch_edge27.json).
- Regrowth at the **old** threshold is a different problem. Some blocked 26-cores had a unique route back to 27, always destroying the square. On 62 sparse nonextendible controls, an adaptive red-pair argument certified that every possible neighborhood missed a blue seven; the same argument failed on all 61 edited nonextendible cases. Neither obstruction alone selects high capacity at the relaxed threshold. [Regrowth counterexamples](../../research/voevodsky/results/ramsey_regrow_counterexamples.json) · [Adaptive certificates](../../research/voevodsky/results/ramsey_regrowth_fork_witness.json).

**Unanswered:** Does any legal noncube incidence exist without either symmetry hypothesis? Can four retained cube neighborhoods force the other four? The face case now has a concrete test: glue two compatible sets of four attachments over the *same marked* 21-vertex predecessor. Passing the tests separately is not enough. More broadly, cube incidence explains how the gain is attained; we do not yet have a parameter-only rule predicting when to choose the cube over the square.
