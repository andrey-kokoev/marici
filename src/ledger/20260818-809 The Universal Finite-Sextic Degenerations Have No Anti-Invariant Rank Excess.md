# 809 — The Universal Finite-Sextic Degenerations Have No Anti-Invariant Rank Excess

## Purpose

Entry 807 confines the generic higher critical locus of every finite marked
sextic to

\[
E=0
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
\Lambda(P_1,P_2,P_3)=0.
\]

The remaining question is whether degeneration of Entry 804's generic
rank-one Kato line creates a vanishing-cycle rank not generated by the
existing total-energy nearby-cycle and momentum-triangle Gysin maps.

## Completed local model

The Cayley--Menger determinant is exactly quadratic in

\[
A=a^2,
\qquad
B=b^2.
\]

At its universal critical point, its critical value is (E^2\Lambda) and
the determinant of its ((A,B))-Hessian is (-\Lambda).  After completing at
the source-labelled critical marked point, localizing one nonzero Hessian
minor, and absorbing units, the parameterized Morse lemma gives

\[
\boxed{
w^2-u^2=\Lambda\,(v^2+E^2).
}
\]

The marked coordinates and the ordered residue orientation of Entry 804 are
retained.  This is a formal/étale normal form; it is not an identification of
expanded sextic polynomials.

## Total-energy degeneration

Set (E=0) with generic (Lambda\ne0).  The fiber equation has a
nondegenerate quadratic critical point in ((w,u,v)).  Its Jacobian algebra is

\[
\frac{k[[w,u,v]]}{(w,u,v)}\simeq k.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\mu_E=1.}
\]

The vanishing class is anti-invariant under (w\mapsto-w), and Entry 804's
generic (1/w) Kummer line specializes isomorphically onto it.  Its
provenance is the existing total-energy nearby-cycle map.

Because the smoothing parameter contains (E^2), the coarse A1 monodromy is
squared to the identity.  The resolved level-two/occurrence character still
retains the square-root sheet.  This is the same linear-resolved versus
quadratic-coarse distinction already present in the cosmology program.

## Momentum-triangle degeneration

Set (Lambda=0) with generic (E\ne0).  The equation becomes

\[
w^2-u^2=0
\]

with the coordinate (v) free.  Thus the singularity is Morse--Bott, not
isolated.  Its ordinary Milnor algebra is

\[
k[[v]],
\]

so an ordinary finite Milnor number does not exist.  The correctly typed
finite invariant is the transverse Milnor rank:

\[
\boxed{\mu_{\Lambda}^{\rm tr}=1.}
\]

The transverse class is again anti-invariant and is the specialization of
the generic (1/w) line.  Its provenance is the existing
momentum-triangle Gysin map.

## Intersection

At (E=\Lambda=0), the function controlling smoothing along the
Morse--Bott critical line is

\[
g_E(v)=v^2+E^2.
\]

At (E=0), its one-variable Jacobian quotient is

\[
\frac{k[[v]]}{(2v)}\simeq k.
\]

Hence the iterated vanishing rank is one:

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}\phi_E\phi_\Lambda=1.}
\]

The reduced divisors ((E)) and ((\Lambda)) form a regular sequence, so
their reduced derived intersection has

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Tor}^{\rm excess}_1=0.}
\]

There is nevertheless a filtered correction: the source discriminant is

\[
E^2\Lambda.
\]

Thus the total-energy normal direction carries a length-two Cartier/Rees
layer.  This is the already required second normal grade, not an additional
reduced vanishing class.

The two existing maps meet in the same anti-invariant generator.  The
intersection class is their iterated specialization, not a new direct-sum
summand.

## Decisive comparison

On reduced vanishing cohomology,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Sp}(\mathcal K_S)
=
\operatorname{im}
\left(
\psi_E\oplus\operatorname{Gys}_{\Lambda}
\right).
}
\]

The ranks are one on the generic (E)-branch, one transversely on the
generic (Lambda)-branch, and one at their intersection.  No anti-invariant
rank excess occurs.

## Classification

- total-energy degeneration: existing nearby-cycle calculus;
- momentum-triangle degeneration: existing Cayley--Menger/Kato Gysin
  calculus;
- intersection: regular reduced crossing plus the existing second
  Cartier/Rees grade in (E);
- new carrier datum: none.

This is a positive higher-vanishing-cycle test of H2 on the universal
interior critical locus.

## Scope

The coordinate-boundary branches (a=0) and (b=0) from Entry 807 remain
separate.  The momentum-triangle branch is nonisolated; claims about an
ordinary finite Milnor number there are prohibited.
