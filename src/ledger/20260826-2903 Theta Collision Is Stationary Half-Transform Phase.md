Let

\[
H_L(x)=\int_0^L\Phi(u)e^{ixu}\,du
=R_L(x)e^{i\theta_L(x)}.
\]

At a real zero of the even truncation \(X_L=2\operatorname{Re}H_L\), provided
\(H_L\ne0\), the phase velocity is

\[
\theta_L'(x)
=
\frac{\int_0^L u\Phi(u)\sin(xu)\,du}
     {\int_0^L \Phi(u)\sin(xu)\,du}.
\]

Thus a finite double zero is exactly a stationary imaginary-axis crossing of
the half-transform.  The sufficient theta-specific transversality statement
is

\[
\left(\int_0^L\Phi(u)\sin(xu)\,du\right)
\left(\int_0^L u\Phi(u)\sin(xu)\,du\right)\ne0
\]

on the cosine-zero locus. No common sign is asserted: successive simple real
zeros normally alternate crossing direction. Half-transform origin crossings
remain separately typed because phase division is then unavailable.

This is the minimal conditional form of the earlier signed adjacent-band
transport problem.  Generic source positivity is insufficient: the positive
two-atom source \(\delta_0+\delta_L\) has exact double zeros.
