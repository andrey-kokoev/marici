# 4338 — Two pair views and three closure tests

**Question:** Since [entry 4337](./20261122-4337%20One%20Eight-State%20Action%20and%20Two%20Witness%20Rules.md), what has become computable from a native contact, and what still requires an assembly or a history?

**Answer:** A proton–neutron entry or neutron–electron exit gives two readings of the same contact: **which coefficient pair is exchanged, and which pair stays active in place**. Ten such records can reconstruct a specific 22-vertex Ramsey witness. But three tests once called “closure” are different: completing the pair record, giving every retained packet a route to an exit, and witnessing every occupied-cell contact. The dynamics permits them to come apart.

**The earlier crossing ambiguity is exact.** In the eight-wiring comparison, slots 0 and 6 label a cell; slot 4 distinguishes its two readings. Swapping slots 0 and 6 preserves stay and exchanges the two candidate quarter-turns. Unlabelled return paths cannot select one. Reopening this question needs an independently identified directed Ramsey event, not another static wiring. [Direction test](../../research/voevodsky/ramsey-pne-crossing-direction-gate.md). In the separate probe-equality witness search, two adaptive bit observations suffice in four of the 22 unresolved replacement cases; the other eighteen require at least three. [Decision trees and countermodels](../../research/voevodsky/results/ramsey_two_replacement.json).

**Where ten and `3|7` come from.** Count one tick as a proton reread, P→N transfer, neutron advance, and return to P. On aligned seeds this cycles five coefficient labels. Their ten unordered pairs are *relations*, not particles. Relative to any pair, three pairs are disjoint and seven meet it (including itself). The two poles of the Ramsey witness give different such partitions. Two distinct middle positions are red-linked exactly when disjoint in both views. The earlier interpretation as future and past populations is not needed: opposite endpoint readings can give opposite signs on the same directed step. Moreover the full signed overlap match depends on sampling exits at arrivals; intermediate exits can be valid and certified. [Anchored split](../../research/voevodsky/results/ramsey_37_anchored_pair_split.json) · [sampling control](../../research/voevodsky/results/pne_all_exit_opportunities.json).

**From contacts to the whole witness.** In slot order, entry exchanges `0,3` and holds `1,2`; exit exchanges `1,4` and holds `2,3`. Reading the coefficients occupying those slots gives the two pole addresses *at the contact*, without reconstructing a predecessor. Require all ten pairs once in each view. Add two copies of the five labels and two poles to the ten contacts. Incidence to labels, matching label copies, pole links, and disjointness in both views reproduce all 66 red edges. The finite coefficient projection permits four complete named covers, all isomorphic; each is realized by a valid assembly from the same marked start. Of 252 ways to select five offers from one period, precisely all-early and all-late completed both views; every other selection admitted a blue seven. **A closure can recur without triggering the next period.** In the five-tick route census, only five of 216 certified selections per aligned seed were complete in both pair views. Complete and incomplete records can admit exactly the same next transfers. Even identical *named* complete records can allow different continuations because of occupancy. For the specified period family, a current reader and an updatable boundary of 56 cells and 17 sites sufficed indefinitely for **geometric validity** from the tested starts. Certification additionally requires witnessed contacts and recent packet information. Of 72 starts, 48 admit a continuation certified at every subsequent period boundary. In the other 24, one original P–N face remains unwitnessed; adding the root transfer’s target packet repairs it. The only direct repair is not the only legal move. No operation was found that promotes a completed graph to a new level. **The price of a certificate.** Through nine packet additions from the reference seed, all 556 two-pending-face cases admitted contact repair: 105 directly, 444 after one locally selected reader advance, seven after two independent preparations. Witnessing both faces need not connect every packet to an exit. Conversely, opening an exit can connect everyone but create a new unwitnessed face. Among 4,782 resolved starts initially free of pending faces but lacking exit reachability, certification cost more than reachability in 191. In all 2,622 resolved cases with a single N→E addition sufficient for reachability, inspecting the proposed electron cell priced the full certificate exactly: one addition if no further face needed a witness (2,481); two if another reader was ready (110); three if it first had to advance (31). These are packet additions, not a universal number of crossings. **How to choose a repair.** Counting stranded packets misranked three choices in a 49-case test: different packets can share an exit but require different setups. On 20 further assemblies, pricing one setup followed by jointly legal exits gave 19 strictly cheaper choices and one correct tie. Lower bounds come from each stranded packet’s necessary first addition, then checking whether the proposed additions actually give a path onward. Those bounds certified all 40 individual costs on the new cohort. On the earlier cohort, recursive first-addition tests resolved 35 deeper costs (26 of four; nine of five). This is evidence for a planning rule and a way of proving a plan shortest, not a claim that the assembly optimizes automatically. [New cohort and certificates](../../research/voevodsky/results/pne_one_pending_new_cohort.json) · [deeper plans](../../research/voevodsky/results/pne_deep_exit_frontiers.json).

**Scope.** Here 22 is the size of a witness avoiding a red triangle and a blue seven; `R(3,7)=23`. The twelve non-contact vertices are label copies and poles, not an identification of neon-22’s neutrons. The construction supplies neither an atomic stabilization law nor a transfer-volume count comparable to the 1,836-arrow convention.

**Unanswered / restart:** When two electron exits are needed, can their joint certification cost be predicted before opening either? Start from states with no pending contacts, separate the packets each proposed exit would rescue, then test whether preparations or face witnesses are shared, incompatible, or newly required by the pair. Compare the prediction with exact shortest completions; do not add two single-exit prices by assumption.
