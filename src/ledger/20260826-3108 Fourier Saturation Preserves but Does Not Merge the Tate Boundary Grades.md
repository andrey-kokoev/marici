# 3108 — Fourier Saturation Preserves but Does Not Merge the Tate Boundary Grades

On a finite prime cutoff (X), the Mellin-analytic primitive form and the
square-current form have exact Fourier saturations

\[
Q_{\delta,X}^{\mathrm{sat}}
=2\operatorname{diag}(D_{\delta,X},D_{\delta,X}),
\qquad
Q_{0,X}^{\mathrm{sat}}=2I,
\]

where

\[
D_{\delta,X}=\operatorname{diag}_{p\in X}(p^{2\delta}).
\]

Both forms become nondegenerate on the direct and reciprocal sheets. But
their smallest generalized eigenvalue is

\[
\min_{p\in X}p^{-2\delta},
\]

which tends to zero as the cutoff grows. Fourier saturation preserves the
source-derived distinction between the distributional primitive grade and
the Hilbert square grade; it does not merge them into one uniformly
equivalent Hilbert norm.

The connected tail supplies an absolute-summability seminorm rather than a
single Gramian. Seam and archimedean incidence must add their own graph
seminorms after they are derived. The canonical candidate is therefore a
Fourier-invariant pro-Gram topology, not one five-channel Euclidean matrix.

## Scope

The finite saturation and collapsing generalized reserve are exact. The seam
and archimedean graph maps, common-kernel theorem, and completed topology are
still open.
