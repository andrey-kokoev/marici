# The Topological Mobius Inverse Needs a Cross-Label Differential to Survive Completion

Nima's divisor-chamber theorem constructs every Möbius coefficient
source-natively. For squarefree `n` with `r` primes, the proper-divisor order
complex has reduced Euler characteristic

\[
(-1)^r=\mu(n).
\]

Thus the coefficients of the Euler-chamber inverse `1/zeta` are topological
indices of factorization coherence, not fitted signs.

But these complexes are separate fibers indexed by `n`. Their internal
differentials provide no cancellation between distinct integer labels, and
ordinary Hilbert norms forget Euler-characteristic signs. Fiberwise topology
therefore does not extend the inverse toward the critical seam.

The missing constructor is a source-derived cross-label differential whose
completion-stable supertrace produces the Möbius inverse. This is sharper than
scalar Mertens estimation: cancellation must exist before aggregation. The
known three-prime hexagon systems are flat and do not supply such an arrow.
