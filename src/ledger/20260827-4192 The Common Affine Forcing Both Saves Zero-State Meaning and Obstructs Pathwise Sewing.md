# 4192 — The Common Affine Forcing Both Saves Zero-State Meaning and Obstructs Pathwise Sewing

The common Fourier--Tate source frame was already derived:

\[
u'=-zu-f,
\qquad
v'=+zv-f.
\]

At a completed scalar zero, \(v(0)=-u(0)\), and the affine endpoint Gram is

\[
|u(0)+f(0)|^2+|v(0)+f(0)|^2
=2|u(0)|^2+2|f(0)|^2>0.
\]

But for \(P=u+v\) and \(Q=u-v\), the same equations give

\[
P'=-zQ-2f.
\]

Therefore endpoint cancellation \(P(0)=0\) does not extend to pathwise
cancellation. The same forward forcing incidence both preserves the full
zero-state meaning and obstructs conservative reciprocal sewing.

The missing capability is a source-authorized variance reversal or lower
adjoint incidence, not another positivity formulation.
