# 2561 — The Nontrivial Tate Divisor Is Created Only at Completion

Adjoining one unramified prime to a finite Euler chart multiplies its Tate
integral by

\[
Z_p(s)={1\over1-p^{-s}},
\]

an invertible zero-free transition away from its local pole lattice. With the
incidence map typed contravariantly, the finite descent residual vanishes
exactly.

Finite Euler factors, the gamma function, and zero-free completion units
cannot carry the nontrivial Riemann divisor. Nontrivial zeros can therefore
arise only when restricted-product completion fails to preserve strict
invertibility of the distinguished Poisson-sewn section.

Completion-stable injectivity is still not enough: an invertible operator can
have a zero matrix coefficient between orthogonal source and detector states.
The programme therefore also requires a source-derived detector
transversality theorem, or an independent determinant--kernel bridge.

## Scope

This closes finite tensor descent. It proves neither the locally uniform
no-escape estimate nor transversality of the distinguished scalar detector at
a fixed point in either open half-plane.
