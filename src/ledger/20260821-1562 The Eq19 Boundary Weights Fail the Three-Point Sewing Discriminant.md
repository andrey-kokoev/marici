# 1562 — The Eq. (19) Boundary Weights Fail the Three-Point Sewing Discriminant

## Question

Can the relative bulk, mixed, and boundary-boundary weights printed in Eq. (19) of Collins–Holman–Vardanyan be obtained by sewing two independently normalized copies of the source three-point kernel?

## Frozen data

The direct expansion of the source exponent gives the location multiplicities

\[
(c_{BB},c_{BS},c_{SS})=(1,2,1),
\]

before the common momentum kernel is inserted.  Eq. (19) instead requires

\[
(c_{BB},c_{BS},c_{SS})=(1,4,2).
\]

Here `BB` denotes two bulk vertices, `BS` the sum of the two labelled mixed placements, and `SS` two initial-surface vertices.

## Hard-to-vary sewing invariant

Let the independently normalized bulk and surface three-point vertices carry weights \(V\) and \(W\). Symmetric sewing necessarily produces

\[
(c_{BB},c_{BS},c_{SS})=(V^2,2VW,W^2).
\]

Therefore every such sewing obeys the rank-one discriminant identity

\[
\Delta_{\rm sew}
=c_{BS}^2-4c_{BB}c_{SS}=0.
\]

This identity is unchanged by arbitrary independent rescalings of the two vertex types.

For the direct source expansion,

\[
\Delta_{\rm direct}=2^2-4(1)(1)=0.
\]

For the weights required by Eq. (19),

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_{\rm Eq.(19)}=4^2-4(1)(2)=8.
}
\]

## Result

The printed boundary enhancement cannot arise from sewing two copies of the source-normalized three-point kernel, even after allowing independent bulk and boundary vertex normalizations.

Together with Entries 1558–1561, this closes the last elementary source-internal normalization repair:

- a scalar endpoint weight fails;
- a smooth separated-endpoint convention is multiplicative and gives no enhancement;
- the complete labelled Wick census gives identical fish multiplicity in all three location sectors;
- independent three-point vertex rescaling cannot reproduce the printed weights.

The surviving narrow diagnosis is therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{within the frozen cubic toy truncation, Eq. (19)'s boundary-containing weights are not generated by the printed source construction.}
}
\]

This is evidence for a normalization defect in the displayed one-loop formula, not evidence for a new cosmological carrier cell or coefficient object. It does not identify the corrected complete formula beyond the already audited leading finite-time sectors.
