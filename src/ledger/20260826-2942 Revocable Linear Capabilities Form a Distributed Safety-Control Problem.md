Buzzard's execution leases and fencing epochs, combined with Strominger's
distributed single-use theorem, form a finite distributed safety supervisor.
Revocation is an uncontrollable environment event; the target's fencing epoch
is authority-state observation at the actuator locus; and atomic
check-and-consume is the guarded transition that closes the time-of-check to
time-of-use gap.

Disconnected sites with identical valid lease and nonce views cannot realize
exactly one execution by symmetric local decisions. In supervisory-control
language, global single use is not coobservable under those local observation
maps. A shared linearizer or prior locus partition repairs safety; immediate
bilateral execution during partition does not.

The reconstruction separates safety from nonblocking. Failing closed can
preserve at-most-one execution during partition, but no unconditional liveness
follows. The exact checker passes 14 of 14 interleaving and hostile tests.
