# Graded Self-Adjoint Dilation Does Not Confine Transform Zeros

For \(0<a<1\), the function

\[
A(u)=e^{-u^2}(1+a\cos(bu))
\]

is positive, even, Schwartz, and positive-definite. It is therefore a
reflection-fixed unitary matrix coefficient of a self-adjoint generator
anticommuting with spectral reflection.

Nevertheless,

\[
\widehat A(z)
=
\sqrt\pi e^{-z^2/4}
\left[
1+a e^{-b^2/4}\cosh\left(\frac{bz}{2}\right)
\right]
\]

has explicit zeros with nonzero real part.

Thus self-adjoint centered dilation, Fourier grading, positive unitary
transport overlap, decay, and a positive spectral measure still do not imply
zero confinement.

Correction: the initial finite cosh model used the wrong transport coordinate.
This Gaussian-cosine witness is the faithful unitary replacement.

# Graded Self-Adjoint Dilation Does Not Confine Overlap Zeros

Take

\[
H=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,2,-2)
\]

with an involution \(R\) swapping each spectral pair, so \(RHR=-H\). A
reflection-fixed vector has overlap

\[
A(z)=\cosh z+\frac1{10}\cosh(2z).
\]

This overlap is entire, even, and strictly positive for real \(z\). Nevertheless
it has an off-axis zero because the equation in \(y=\cosh z\) has the root

\[
y=-\frac{5+3\sqrt3}{2}<-1.
\]

Therefore self-adjoint centered dilation, Fourier grading, reflection-fixed
states, and positive real-axis overlap do not imply zero confinement.

The half-offset explanation survives, but the RH bridge requires a stronger
arithmetic or canonical-system ordering of the comb–current interaction.
