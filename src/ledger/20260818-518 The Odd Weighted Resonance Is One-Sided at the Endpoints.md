# The Odd Weighted Resonance Is One-Sided at the Endpoints

Restrict the resonant `q` source of Entry 517 to `s_b=1`, `i=7`.

At `b=-1`, `L_1=-u`.  If `e_a=2`, all terms vanish modulo `u^2`.  If
`e_a=1`, direct reduction gives

\[
m=-(-1)^jua^8,
\qquad
C_q=(-1)^ju(1-i)a^i,
\]

and hence

\[
(\widehat H_q)_a=
(-1)^j\frac{7-i}{4}ua^{i+1},
\qquad
(\widehat H_q)_u=0.
\]

Thus the `i=7` resonant lift vanishes at the negative endpoint in every
`s_a` sector.

At `b=1`, write the two orbit lattices as
`L_2=a+epsilon*u/2`, with `epsilon=+-1`.  Since `L_1=2-u` remains a unit,
the same resonant source gives

\[
(\widehat H_q)_a
=\epsilon\,2^{e_a-3}ua^7,
\qquad
(\widehat H_q)_u
=3\,2^{e_a}ua^7,
\]

modulo `u^2`.  It therefore survives nontrivially in the Cartier layer at
the positive endpoint.

The odd weighted resonance is consequently one-sided:

\[
\operatorname{res}_{b=-1}(r_7)=0,
\qquad
\operatorname{res}_{b=1}(r_7)\ne0.
\]

It cannot be selected by the ordinary kernel of the two endpoint
evaluations.  A relative-support realization must use the homotopy fiber
of the endpoint map and supply a boundary nullhomotopy for the positive
endpoint value.  This is the weighted analogue of the missing
Beck--Chevalley/Gysin cell in the conductor problem.
