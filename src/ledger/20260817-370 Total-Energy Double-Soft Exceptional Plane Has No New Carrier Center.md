# Total-Energy Double-Soft Exceptional Plane Has No New Carrier Center

## Question

Entry 355 closed the physical Cut/Rees comparison at (x=y=0), while
Entry 369 resolved the ((E,y)) corner only with (x\ne0). The remaining
carrier-provenance question is the origin

\[
(E,x,y)=(0,0,0).
\]

The hard-to-vary claim tested here is

\[
\boxed{
\text{the first resolved exceptional plane contains a center not generated
by the frozen energy arrangement or coefficient divisors.}}
\]

This is a center census. It does not compute the full rank-twelve
Gauss--Manin connection on a log resolution.

## Frozen homogeneous data

The signed-energy elliptic coefficients and conductor discriminants are

\[
A=(2x-E)(E-2y),\qquad B=E(2x+2y-E),\qquad A-B=-4xy,
\]

\[
\Delta _1=4x[xy^2+2Exy-E^2(x+2y-E)],
\]

\[
\Delta _2=4y[x^2y+2Exy-E^2(2x+y-E)].
\]

Thus (A,B) are exactly homogeneous of degree two and
\(\Delta _1,\Delta _2\) are exactly homogeneous of degree four.

## Radial blowup

On the (E)-chart

\[
E=u,\qquad x=ur,\qquad y=us,
\]

the exceptional elliptic modulus is

\[
\boxed{
m_{\rm exc}(r,s)=\frac{(2r-1)(1-2s)}{2r+2s-1}.}
\]

The conductor pullbacks are

\[
\Delta _1=4u^4rF_1,\qquad
F_1=rs^2+2rs-r-2s+1,
\]

\[
\Delta _2=4u^4sF_2,\qquad
F_2=r^2s+2rs-2r-s+1.
\]

Both Kummer lines have radial exceptional residue (-2), hence trivial
semisimple monodromy. The executable also verifies the exact (x)- and
(y)-chart transforms.

## Elliptic base points

The rational map (m=A/B) has four base points on
\(\mathbf P^2_{[E:x:y]}\):

\[
[0:1:0],\quad[0:0:1],\quad[2:1:0],\quad[2:0:1].
\]

They are exactly the four intersections obtained by choosing one linear
factor of (A) and one linear factor of (B). Every indeterminacy center is
therefore an incidence of predeclared signed-energy hyperplanes.

The first two axis points reproduce Entry 369 and its cyclic companion. At
each of the other two points, the elliptic lines, relevant soft line, and
one conductor branch have distinct tangent directions. One point blowup
separates those directions; no fitted center is required.

## Conductor center census

The exact identity

\[
F_1-F_2=(r-s)(1-rs)
\]

splits the common conductor locus into

\[
r=s,\qquad r^3+2r^2-3r+1=0,
\]

and

\[
rs=1,\qquad r^2-3r+1=0.
\]

These five derived intersections are crossings of the two frozen conductor
collision curves. They are coefficient-support loci, not carrier generators.

Four finite conductor--energy contacts are tangent:

\[
(r,s)=\left(\tfrac12,1\right),\quad
\left(1,\tfrac12\right),\quad
\left(\tfrac32,-1\right),\quad
\left(-1,\tfrac32\right).
\]

For example,

\[
2F_1\left(\tfrac12,s\right)=(s-1)^2,
\]

while on (2r+2s-1=0), with (r_2=2r),

\[
8F_1=r_2(r_2-3)^2.
\]

Their blowup centers are forced tangencies between an existing energy line
and an existing conductor coefficient divisor. They add support-resolution
flags but no divisor to the original base.

## Physical Cut/Rees layer

Entry 355 already supplies the exact two-axis presentation over

\[
I_1=(1),\qquad I_2=(2x,2y),\qquad I_3=(4xy).

The origin has ordinary rank one and two separate shifted Rees directions.

## Verdict

The tested carrier-center claim is falsified:

\[
\boxed{
\text{every required center is generated by signed-energy incidence,
conductor coefficient support, or the existing }(x,y)\text{ Rees normals}.}
\]

The generic radial exceptional plane carries a two-parameter pullback of the
same Legendre variation. Its four modulus base points are existing energy
incidences. The remaining finite centers are conductor crossings or
conductor--energy tangencies. No new carrier datum is found.

## Epistemic scope

This proves exact homogeneity, all three ordinary blowup-chart formulas,
the modulus base-point list, the conductor common-locus split, and the finite
tangency list. It does not prove that the complete rank-twelve connection
extends logarithmically across the resulting resolution, nor that the
\(e_6\) bridge survives at every exceptional intersection.

Accordingly this is evidence for resolved H2 at the carrier/coefficient-
support level, not a global H2 theorem.
