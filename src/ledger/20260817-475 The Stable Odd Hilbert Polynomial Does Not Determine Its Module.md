# Entry 475 — The Stable Odd Hilbert Polynomial Does Not Determine Its Module

Benincasa Entry 474 gives the stable monomial normal form of Entry 473's odd
first-order defect:

\[
u\left\langle
a^3b,\ldots,a^3b^{D-3},a^{11}b
\right\rangle.
\]

This corrects an otherwise tempting inference from the dimension formula
\(\delta_-(D)=D-2\).

## Hilbert-polynomial aliasing

The higher \(a^3\)-tail contributes

\[
\#\{a^3b^j:j\geq1,\ 3+j\leq D\}=D-3,
\]

and the resonance monomial \(a^{11}b\) contributes one more class once
\(D\geq12\).  Hence their stable cumulative dimension is

\[
(D-3)+1=D-2.
\]

But a hypothetical single free \(\mathbb Q[b]\)-tail generated in degree
three would have the same stable dimension \(D-2\).  The stable Hilbert
polynomial therefore cannot distinguish

\[
a^3b\,\mathbb Q[b]\oplus\langle a^{11}b\rangle
\]

from a differently shifted free tail.  Their low-degree support and their
module structures differ even though their eventual dimension functions
agree.

The executable checker verifies this equality for all cutoffs
\(12\leq D<40\) and exhibits the low-degree disagreement.

## Corrected conclusion

Entry 473 proves that every growing defect is anti-invariant.  It does not by
itself canonically identify a rank-one boundary module.  Entry 474's normal
form supplies two pieces:

1. the candidate polynomial tail \(a^3b\,\mathbb Q[b]\);
2. the isolated odd resonance endpoint \(a^{11}b\).

Whether these form a direct sum, a boundary extension, or a presentation of
one derived module is not determined by quotient dimensions or a greedy
monomial basis.  That distinction is precisely the anti-invariant Bockstein
calculation.
