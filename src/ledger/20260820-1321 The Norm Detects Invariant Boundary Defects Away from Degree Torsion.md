# The Norm Detects Invariant Boundary Defects Away from Degree Torsion

Epistemic-graph event: 1336.

For a quotient pushforward `S` and fiber trace `T` with fiber size `d`, the
formal Mackey norm gives

`S T=d id`.

If multiplication by `d` is injective on the target chain module, `S` is
injective on the trace submodule `im(T)`.  Hence a boundary defect whose image
is fiber-invariant cannot disappear under the next quotient.  Together with
separated transported supports and the preceding map's surjectivity, this
closes Ledger 1319's terminal-detection gap.

At degree primes the conclusion fails sharply.  For `C2->1` over `F_2`, the
nonzero trace vector `(1,1)` is killed by the sum map because `S T=2=0`.
Thus the same primes that destroy norm splitting can hide invariant physical
boundary defects.

This theorem is conditional on supplied chain-level trace and pushforward
maps.  It does not construct the missing five-site physical maps.
