# 1376 — Intrinsic-Prime Scattering Stops at the Continuation Gate

Epistemic-graph event: 1408.

On the Hilbert space generated by source-derived intrinsic primes, let
\(Ne_p=pe_p\). For \(\Re s>1\),

\[
D(s)=\det(I-N^{-s})
=\prod_p(1-p^{-s})
=\frac1{\zeta(s)}
\]

is an honest Fredholm determinant. For fixed \(\sigma>1\),

\[
S_\sigma(t)
=\frac{D(\sigma+it)}{D(\sigma-it)}
\]

is unitary, and its phase derivative is exactly

\[
\frac1i\frac d{dt}\log S_\sigma(t)
=2\operatorname{Re}
\sum_{p,\ k\ge1}(\log p)p^{-k(\sigma+it)}.
\]

This supplies the prime-power fluctuations missing from the finite Gaussian
endpoint without inserting any zeros.

The construction stops before the critical line. Trace class fails for
\(\sigma\le1\), and at \(\sigma=1/2\) the operator is not Hilbert--Schmidt
because \(\sum_p1/p\) diverges. Higher regularized determinants delete
divergent prime terms; restoring precisely the terms that recover
\(1/\zeta(s)\) needs a new source-derived renormalization law. Importing the
analytic continuation of \(\zeta\) for that purpose would be circular.

Thus the surviving architecture has three separate exact pieces: Gaussian
corner phase, dual-cutoff smooth \(T\log T\) growth, and intrinsic-prime
fluctuations in \(\Re s>1\). Their critical-line gluing remains the theorem to
be found.

Scope: this is a trace-class theorem and continuation gate, not a denial of
classical meromorphic continuation and not a construction of a
Hilbert--Polya operator.
