# Entry 501 — The Even Conormal Block Has No Soft Bockstein

Entry 500 retypes the \(a^3\) conormal socle and its \(u\)-Bockstein as
anti-invariant.  The invariant block of

\[
I/I^2\cong R_0=\mathbb Q(b)[a]/(a^4)
\]

is instead

\[
(I/I^2)_+=\mathbb Q(b)\langle1,a^2\rangle.
\]

Under ordinary \(u=0\) specialization, the Euler comparison is
multiplication by \(a/4\) into the \(e_a\)-component.  Hence

\[
1\longmapsto {a\over4}e_a,
\qquad
a^2\longmapsto {a^3\over4}e_a.
\]

These two images are independent in \(R_0e_a\).  Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{the even conormal block specializes injectively and has no }
u\text{-Bockstein.}
}
\]

The soft Bockstein mechanism of Entries 494--496 is exclusively the odd
top-socle phenomenon.

## Incidence lift

For the smallest \((1,1)\) \(q\)-sector, Entry 487 gives at \(u=0\)

\[
m=f(b+1)a,
\qquad
H_q=-{3\over2}me_a.
\]

Lifting the two even Euler images requires

\[
f_0=-{1\over6(b+1)},
\qquad
f_2=-{a^2\over6(b+1)}.
\]

Both are regular away from the incidence endpoint \(b=-1\), and both have
the same simple incidence denominator.  Orbit conjugation does not remove
it because \(L_1=b+1-u\) is deck invariant.

## Consequence

The plus defect is not created by soft \(u\)-specialization.  Its only
remaining location in the conormal comparison is incidence descent across
\(L_1=0\).  Although the even block has two vector-space generators, it is
one cyclic module over the even Cartier algebra, generated by \(1\) with
\(a^2\) as its second layer.  A single module-level descent obstruction can
therefore be compatible with Entry 473's constant defect one.

This is not yet the identification: a filtered calculation must determine
whether the two apparent poles define one cyclic obstruction or two
independent endpoint classes.
