# 2557 — Off-Seam Tate Anomaly Lines Require Two Inequivalent Metrics

For a real displacement from the critical seam,

\[
s={1\over2}+\delta,
\qquad 0<\delta<{1\over2},
\]

the local Tate transition satisfies

\[
\gamma_p(s)
={1-p^{-1/2-\delta}\over1-p^{-1/2+\delta}}>1.
\]

Its finite-prime product diverges to infinity. Reciprocal reflection sends
\(\delta\mapsto-\delta\) and inverts the product, which then tends to zero.
Consequently no cutoff family of Hilbert metrics uniformly equivalent to the
seam metric can control both orientations.

Each half-sector nevertheless admits its own transported isometric metric.
The two half-planes are therefore reciprocal metric polarizations of one
anomaly line, and their metrics coincide exactly on the unitary seam.

## Scope

The theorem is proved on the nonoscillatory displacement \(t=0\). It
falsifies a universal fixed-metric completion but does not construct the
relative two-metric pairing, close its Green current, or prove RH.
