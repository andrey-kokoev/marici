# Reciprocal Zero Germs Have Equal Boundary Grade Only on the Seam

## Direct relative germ

For \(0<\Re s<1\), Euler--Maclaurin continuation gives

\[
\zeta(s)
=
\lim_{N\to\infty}
\left(
\sum_{n=1}^{N}n^{-s}
-
\frac{N^{1-s}}{1-s}
\right).
\]

At a zero \(\zeta(s)=0\), the finite label sum is therefore asymptotic to
the explicit boundary current

\[
C_s(N)=\frac{N^{1-s}}{1-s}.
\]

The zero is not disappearance of the finite packet. It is equality of its
renormalized germ with the boundary current.

The magnitude of this current has power-law grade

\[
|C_s(N)|\asymp N^{1-\Re s}.
\]

## Reciprocal relative germ

The functional equation pairs \(s\) with \(1-s\). The dual
Euler--Maclaurin germ is

\[
\zeta(1-s)
=
\lim_{N\to\infty}
\left(
\sum_{n=1}^{N}n^{s-1}
-
\frac{N^s}{s}
\right).
\]

At the reciprocal zero, its boundary current is

\[
C_{1-s}(N)=\frac{N^s}{s},
\]

with grade

\[
|C_{1-s}(N)|\asymp N^{\Re s}.
\]

## Exact seam equation

The two reciprocal boundary grades agree precisely when

\[
1-\Re s=\Re s.
\]

Therefore

\[
\Re s=\frac12.
\]

The critical line is the unique locus where the direct and reciprocal
zero-germs have the same asymptotic filtration degree.

## What is proved

The following statements are exact:

- every critical-strip zero is a relative cancellation against a universal
  Euler boundary current;
- reciprocal zeros carry dual boundary currents;
- their power-law grades are \(1-\Re s\) and \(\Re s\);
- equal grade is equivalent to the critical line.

This gives a source-native meaning to the half-offset that is specific to zero
germs rather than merely to the functional equation.

## What remains conjectural

Functional symmetry alone does not require equal grade. A hypothetical
off-line quartet may consist of two valid relative germs with complementary
but unequal grades.

The RH-bearing statement must therefore be a comparison theorem:

> The completed theta/Tate normalization identifies reciprocal zero-germs by
> a filtration-preserving morphism.

If that morphism is source-derived and preserves nonzero leading boundary
classes, unequal grades are impossible. Every reciprocal zero event must then
lie on the seam.

This is strictly stronger than the scalar functional equation. It says the
two presentations agree not only after taking their finite part, but also in
the grade at which their divergent boundary meaning is carried.

## Hostile multiplier test

A symmetric multiplier inserting an off-line zero quartet preserves the scalar
functional equation. It creates paired zero-germs with grades

\[
1-\Re s
\qquad
\Re s.
\]

Such a multiplier passes scalar reciprocity but must fail the proposed
filtration-preserving comparison unless \(\Re s=1/2\).

A successful construction must reject it by source provenance before using
known zero locations.

## Relation to loss of meaning

Ordinary label summation loses meaning throughout the critical strip. Each
sector repairs that loss by adjoining its own boundary current. On the
critical line the two repairs occur at the same grade, so they can be two
presentations of one relative event. Off the line their grades differ, so
declaring them to be one event erases filtration data.

This is the current sharp form of the “one thing that is actually two things”
intuition.
