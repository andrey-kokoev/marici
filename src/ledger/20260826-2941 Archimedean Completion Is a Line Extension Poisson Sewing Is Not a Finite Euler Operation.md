The archimedean factor supplies an independent one-dimensional determinant
line.  It tensors with the arithmetic determinant-three packet and does not
act by similarity, additive perturbation, compression, or a graph relation on
the finite valuation transition.

A finite Euler truncation is not a finite modification of the global Tate
source.  Suppressing every omitted Euler factor replaces the distinguished
unramified vector at infinitely many primes, leaving the original restricted
tensor product.  Therefore global Fourier--Poisson sewing has no
source-authorized finite-Euler action.

The correct order is to apply Poisson sewing to the full adelic test object,
then take the Mellin readout and extract the determinant packet.  Euler
truncations are diagnostic projections afterward, not constructors of the
global functional equation.
