# 700 — The Second-Normal Localization Square Commutes at Chain Level

## Hard-to-vary claim

For the generic three-site five-pole twisted de Rham family obtained by
adjoining \(q_{\mathcal G_{12}}\) before homogeneous specialization, the
base-change/localization square commutes on each labelled generator of

\[
N_2=\langle\nu_1\nu_2,\nu_1\nu_3,\nu_2\nu_3\rangle
\subset I^2/I^3.
\]

This is a chain-level statement. It does not assume or construct a generic
master basis.

## Frozen generic five-pole form

Use

\[
c=y_{12},\qquad a=y_{23},\qquad b=y_{31}
\]

and the generic Cayley--Menger twist \(\mu_d(K(X,P;c,a,b))\) of Entry 185.
The lower marked planes are

\[
L_1=c+b+X_1,quad L_2=c+a+X_2,quad
L_3=a+b+X_3,quad L_{23}=c+b+X_2+X_3.
\]

The primary source defines

\[
q_{\mathcal G_{12}}=X_1+X_2+X_3+c.
\]

The generic five-pole chain family is therefore frozen as

\[
\boxed{
\Phi^{\rm gen}_{\boldsymbol\tau,\tau_G}
=
\mu_d(K)
\frac{dc\wedge da\wedge db}
{L_1^{\tau_1}L_2^{\tau_2}L_3^{\tau_3}
 L_{23}^{\tau_{23}}q_{\mathcal G_{12}}^{\tau_G}}.
}
\]

The labelled basis used here is the pole-exponent/de Rham basis generated by
these five frozen equations, tensored separately with

\[
\nu_1\nu_2,\qquad\nu_1\nu_3,\qquad\nu_2\nu_3.
\]

No claim is made that this is a reduced master basis. The paper does not
print a generic five-pole master basis or connection matrix.

## Deletion--restriction triangle

Let \(U_{\rm low}\) be the complement of the four lower planes in the
Cayley--Menger cover and let

\[
D_G=V(q_{\mathcal G_{12}})\cap U_{\rm low}.
\]

Since \(q_{\mathcal G_{12}}\) is a nonzero linear fiber equation, it is a
relative Cartier divisor on the generic locus. The chain-level localization
triangle is

\[
R\Gamma_{D_G}(U_{\rm low},\mathcal L_K)
\longrightarrow
R\Gamma(U_{\rm low},\mathcal L_K)
\longrightarrow
R\Gamma(U_{\rm low}\setminus D_G,\mathcal L_K)
\xrightarrow{+1}.
\]

Equivalently, on the algebraic de Rham model, adjoining the fifth pole is
scalar extension

\[
\operatorname{Loc}_{q_G}(C)=C\otimes_A A[q_G^{-1}].
\]

This supplies the generic map that Entry 699 correctly refused to infer from
the homogeneous rank identity \(35=15+20\).

## Filtration and labelled directions

Set

\[
I=(\nu_1,\nu_2,\nu_3),\qquad \nu_i=P_i^2-X_i^2.
\]

The key source-derived identity is

\[
\boxed{
\partial_{\nu_i}q_{\mathcal G_{12}}=0
\quad(i=1,2,3).
}
\]

Thus multiplication by \(q_G^{-1}\) and the relative de Rham differential
preserve the three labelled square-free directions in \(I^2/I^3\). Normal
differentiation itself has filtration degree \(-1\), so it does **not**
preserve \(I^2/I^3\); the typed operation is the labelled second-coefficient
map \(\partial_{\nu_i}\partial_{\nu_j}|_{I=0}\). Because every normal
derivative of \(q_G\) vanishes, that coefficient map does not mix the three
labels during localization.

Algebraically,

\[
(A[q_G^{-1}])\otimes_A A/I^3
\cong
(A/I^3)[\bar q_G^{-1}],
\]

and this isomorphism respects each labelled summand of \(I^2/I^3\).

## Beck--Chevalley commutator

Write \([\nu_i\nu_j]\) for the conormal label in \(I^2/I^3\), not for its
ordinary pullback (which is zero). For each \(i<j\), both routes send the
labelled chain \(\nu_i\nu_j\omega\) to the same associated-grade class:

\[
\operatorname{gr}^2_I\operatorname{Loc}_{q_G}
(\nu_i\nu_j\omega)
=
[\nu_i\nu_j]\frac{i^*\omega}{i^*q_G}
=
\operatorname{Loc}_{i^*q_G}\operatorname{gr}^2_I
(\nu_i\nu_j\omega).
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
[\mathrm{BC}]_{12}=[\mathrm{BC}]_{13}=[\mathrm{BC}]_{23}=0.
}
\]

The commutator matrix on the ordered labelled basis

\[
(\nu_1\nu_2,\nu_1\nu_3,\nu_2\nu_3)
\]

is the zero \(3\times3\) matrix and has rank zero.

## Consequence for \(\mathcal Q\)

Because the typed commutator is identically zero, determinant, minor, and
rank-one \(\mathcal Q\)-valuation tests are vacuous. In particular, the
mixed base-change/localization coherence does not generate \(\mathcal Q\):

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal Q\text{ is absent from the second-normal Beck--Chevalley
commutator.}
}
\]

Combined with Entry 698, this closes the leading generic-lower route:

- the three second-normal coefficients are individually \(\mathcal Q\)-coprime;
- localization preserves them label-by-label;
- the specialization/localization commutator is zero.

## Classification and scope

- carrier: unchanged Cayley--Menger and five marked linear divisors;
- coefficient data: labelled \(I^2/I^3\) normal grades;
- comparison: source-derived commuting localization/base-change square;

This does not prove that a reduced generic master connection specializes
flatly, nor that higher direct images satisfy base change across every
discriminant component. It proves the comparison on the frozen twisted de
