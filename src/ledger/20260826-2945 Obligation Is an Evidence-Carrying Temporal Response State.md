Permission changes which actions are admissible. Prohibition imposes a safety
condition. Obligation instead creates a trigger-response state that remains
pending until its primary action or declared fallback occurs.

Marici's direct-instruction and record-execution rules expose two coupled
monitors. State-changing execution may discharge the action obligation while
activating an evidence obligation; completion without the required checkpoint
is therefore not global discharge. A blocking report discharges only when the
declared safety or authority exception enables that exact fallback.

The finite monitor has inactive, pending, discharged, fallback-discharged, and
violated states. A deadline or fairness condition must be declared rather than
invented: without one, only unbounded liveness is expressible and finite
violation cannot be certified.

The exact trace checker passes 13 of 13 tests. Universal policy realizability,
multi-agent transfer of obligations, and concrete implementation refinement
remain open.
