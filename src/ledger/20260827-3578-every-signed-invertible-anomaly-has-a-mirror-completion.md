# Every Signed Invertible Anomaly Has a Mirror Completion

Let \(a\) be a finite invertible anomaly class. Boundary--bulk cancellation
pairs \(a\) with \(-a\), while orientation reversal exchanges

\[
(a,-a)\longleftrightarrow(-a,a).
\]

If \(a\ne-a\), the class is signed but the two consistent source packets are
distinct mirror partners. If \(a=-a\), then \(2a=0\), and the self-inverse
class carries no opposite anomaly label. No invertible anomaly class is both
signed and a singleton under the full orientation groupoid.

Anomaly matching preserves the class across RG transport, and gapped
vectorlike thresholds shift it by zero. These mechanisms protect an admitted
relative class; they do not select its absolute orientation or fix continuous
portal magnitude, the RG clock, detector calibration, or a `physical16`
response.

## Claim boundary

The theorem applies to invertible anomaly classes with orientation reversal by
the group inverse. It leaves open a non-invertible or microscopic chiral
source whose mirror is provably absent from the admitted physical category.
Such a source must still co-generate the portal magnitude and calibrated
instrument rather than merely preserving a chosen class.
