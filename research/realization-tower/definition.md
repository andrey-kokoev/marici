# Realization Tower

Canonical glossary name: **Realization Tower**. Version **1**.
Research home: [research/realization-tower/](README.md).

## Definition

The Realization Tower is the named research structure organizing generation of
a decorated twelve-sector source, its labelled/source-indexed/target-indexed
presentations, coherent transports toward a specified rung4 reference/readout,
and retained return or further realization. Its established diagrammatic core is

```text
12 --9--> 6
 |        |
11 --8--> 5
 |        |
10 --7--> 4.
```

In this core, 12/11/10 and 6/5/4 are object presentations; 9/8/7 are horizontal
transport roles. The numerical labels are not sector counts, coordinate
dimensions, or a proved sequence of Postnikov degrees.

## Proposed origin and circulation

The operator proposes that generation begins at the empty simplex in the
augmented simplicial convention and proceeds through joins/cones to the
underlying decorated twelve-sector space. Forward and reverse realization then
circulate through the rung structure. Quotienting is proposed at comparison
boundaries where realizations meet, with the witnesses needed for reconstruction
or continued construction retained in the complete package.

This is the broader interpretation to investigate, not a claim that the
existing diagram already constructs that full cycle. In particular:

- Elementary join/cone identities and specific retained-return constructions
  exist; their identification with the complete intended source cycle is open.
- Source indexing retains all four source groups. Holding one source fixed
  selects a three-sector fibre. A decorated vertex link is a candidate geometric
  representation of that selection, not an already-proved identity of APIs.
- The proposed meeting of 4D representations and the location of its quotient
  remain unresolved. No assignment to rung8, rung4 or the 4-to-12 return seam
  is made by this definition.
- Reversal of a retained construction is not inversion of a bare information-
  losing quotient. Observational equivalence must respect the intended future
  continuation law.

## Existing twelve-sector field candidate

For four endpoint labels, the field-slot candidate has

    V = direct-sum_(i != j) C^3_(i -> j).

It has twelve directed arrow sectors and 36 complex field components. Its
rung11 source-indexed presentation retains four groups of three sectors;
rung10 is target-indexed. The candidate's horizontal maps generate 137
structured comparison leaves. The numerical incoming-promotion layer is a
separate typed construction connected only through declared adapters.

The twelve sectors do not assert twelve spatial dimensions. A simplex with
four vertices has dimension three; the four-dimensional cone/lift construction
in Ledger 4329 is separate data and must not be silently substituted for it.

## Non-equivalences

The Realization Tower is not automatically identical to:

- either older counter-directed tower of the Coherence Pyramid;
- the fixed-carrier Gram restriction tower;
- a classical Postnikov tower;
- nine serial object stages inferred from the integers 12 through 4;
- a bare simplex, a toroidal encoding or an unmarked geometric volume;
- a physical Hamiltonian/clock or GR derivation.

No claim that related constructions cannot be connected is intended. These
non-equivalences require an explicit adapter rather than an identification by
name, count or dimension.

## Quotient vocabulary

Existing prior work supplies distinct notions:

1. Endpoint quotient: forget member arrows and retain their source or target key.
2. Retained indexing: keep the key AND its member data; not that quotient alone.
3. Phase quotient: unanchored pair comparisons identify common-phase-related
   fields; the normalized alignment example has base CP2.
4. Terminal source quotient: S/I for a specified multiplicative recorder,
   retaining relation/attachment witnesses separately where required.

The term Realization Tower does not collapse these operations into one unnamed
quotient. See [the prior-material index](README.md) for their owning sources.
