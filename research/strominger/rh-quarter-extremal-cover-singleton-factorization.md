# Extremal degree-two cover inequality falsifies small-coefficient reduction

## Problem

Explain the smallest strict singleton Hall inequality on the Gale Hasse graph.

## Bold conjecture

After removing the common integer scale, its two supplies and one demand form a primitive small-coefficient determinant identity.

## Strongest falsification

The exact primitive coefficients are

\[
p_1=63434452123368102909028864984185398248601273,
\]
\[
p_2=249307448332288746034546147399808240200002486,
\]
\[
d=274410248355782311804265813266051994896099884.
\]

They satisfy \(p_1+p_2-d>0\), with primitive slack

\[
38331652099874537139309199117941643552503875,
\]

but the largest coefficient exceeds the preregistered smallness threshold \(100\) by many orders of magnitude. The conjecture is rejected.

## Surviving conjecture

The inequality is structural at the complementary-minor level rather than a small integer relation. The narrowest next rival is a multiplicative two-neighbor bound: test whether \(4p_1p_2\geq d^2\), which by AM–GM would imply \(p_1+p_2\geq d\). An exact failure would show that even this extremal singleton needs a genuinely additive or determinant-specific argument.

## Disposition

The factorization and strict slack are verified at fixed \(k=8\). No all-order promotion follows.
