# Two directed closures are the kernel and cokernel of one residual

## Additive compression

In an additive source category, two parallel routes

\[
f,g:X\to Y
\]

have a canonical residual

\[
d=f-g.
\]

The two directed closure operations are then

\[
\operatorname{Eq}(f,g)=\ker d,
\qquad
\operatorname{Coeq}(f,g)=\operatorname{coker}d.
\]

Thus contravariant compatibility and covariant aggregation remain distinct,
but they are generated by one typed comparison map.

The comparison cell is not another peer quotient.  It is the differential
whose two homological shadows are the equalizer and coequalizer.

## Exact examples

For the two faithful routes on \(\mathbb F_7\),

\[
f=1,
\qquad
g=-1,
\qquad
d=2.
\]

Since \(2\) is invertible, both kernel and cokernel vanish.

For the magnetic local Smith model,

\[
d=[7]:\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z,
\]

one obtains

\[
\ker d=0,
\qquad
\operatorname{coker}d=\mathbb Z/7.
\]

This explains why the magnetic seven is a covariant observation residue with
no corresponding source kernel in the one-generator model.

For \(d=0\), both sides survive:

\[
\ker d=\mathbb Z,
\qquad
\operatorname{coker}d=\mathbb Z.
\]

The distinction between a kernel tower and a cokernel residue is therefore
encoded by the same residual at different variances.

## Higher coherence

If several comparison residuals are composable, the first higher requirement
is

\[
d_{n+1}d_n=0.
\]

When this holds, the comparisons form a chain complex and their remaining
distinctions are homology classes.  When it fails, the composite

\[
\kappa_n=d_{n+1}d_n
\]

is a curvature or coherence residual.  A higher filler must trivialize this
residual with source authority; it cannot be added merely to force the chain
condition.

This supplies a more precise interpretation than a numeric tower count:

- the two directed towers are kernel-side and cokernel-side views;
- the mixed cell is the residual differential;
- the next coherence level tests whether consecutive residuals compose to
  zero.

## Attempted falsification and boundary

The compression is a theorem in additive categories.  It is not universal.
For sets, spaces, or nonlinear constructors there may be no canonical
subtraction \(f-g\).  Equalizers and coequalizers still exist, but need not be
the kernel and cokernel of one source-derived arrow.

Therefore the Deutschian claim must remain scoped:

> Whenever the source supplies an additive enrichment, parallel-route closure
> is controlled by the residual differential and its kernel, cokernel, and
> curvature composites.  Without additive enrichment, the two variances must
> remain separately constructed.

This is directly applicable to the finite magnetic integer matrices.  It is
not automatically transferable to every nonlinear instrument network.
