# Why the first exceptional coefficients are (1,-1)

The `q=1` component at depth zero contains the reflected sources

\[
(a,m)=(0,-2),(0,0)
\]

at grade two.  Away from grade two, their observation matrix has two rows.
The second row is carried by the plus source with coefficient

\[
(g-2)4^{\overline g}.
\]

It vanishes uniquely at `g=2`.  At that grade both source columns collapse to
the single surviving observation

\[
R_1(0,-2)=R_1(0,0)=-40.
\]

The unique primitive difference invisible to this observation is therefore

\[
(1,-1),
\]

which lifts to

\[
1-\bar z^{-2}\in\ker M_2.
\]

The two exceptional families now have the same information-theoretic form:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
\text{component}&\text{lost carrier}&\text{states/observations}&\text{circuit}\\
\hline
q=1&(g-2)4^{\overline g}&2/1&(1,-1)\\
q=7&-6(d-1)(d-5)&3/2&(1,-3,2)
\end{array}
\]

They are nevertheless different local mechanisms.  The first is a singular
initial grade step.  The second is a simultaneous fold-endpoint and
derivative-endpoint failure at penetration depth `d=5`.
