# The first Jacobi fluctuation is controlled by the hard-edge density exponent

## Question

Is the vanishing first shift specific to one finite sequence, or does it follow the hard-edge density exponent?

Consider the exact moment family

\[
w_\alpha(x)=x^\alpha e^{-2x^{1/4}},
\qquad
m_k^{(\alpha)}
=4\frac{\Gamma(4k+4\alpha+4)}{2^{4k+4\alpha+4}}.
\]

For \(\alpha=0,1/4,1/2,1\), fraction-free Hankel determinants through degree eighteen give fitted first shifts

\[
-1.05\times10^{-5},
\quad0.499987,
\quad0.999985,
\quad2.000046.
\]

These agree with

\[
a_1=\frac{\alpha}{2\beta}=2\alpha
\]

to absolute error below \(5\times10^{-5}\), while the leading constant \(A\) remains common. The nonzero-\(\alpha\) cases are deliberate controls: they show that the fitting procedure detects a first shift when the source density contains one.

## Disposition

Promote the source-specific finite conclusion: the \(\alpha=0\) quarter-Weibull measure has no detected first hard-edge fluctuation. Adopt

\[
a_1=\frac{\alpha}{2\beta}
\]

as the bounded theorem target, not as a proved universality law.

The next leaf is `hard-edge-alpha-shift-theorem`: derive this formula from a valid hard-edge recurrence or determinant asymptotic. Its \(\alpha=0\) specialization would prove the required vanishing.

## Claim boundary

Exact finite determinants eliminate quadrature error but do not prove the family law. The fixed-start transfer and recessive-branch selection remain separate gates.
