# Degree-thirty-six grid rejects the thirteen-sixtieths candidate

## Problem

The proposed first correction \(u=6/5\) presupposed the finite-recognition limit \(\theta_2=13/60\).

## Bold conjecture

The cross ratio has

\[
n^2\Theta_n=rac{13}{60}
\left(1+rac{6}{5n}+O(n^{-2})\right).
\]

## Named rivals

The rivals are a nearby nonrational limit and correction, higher-order terms outside the cubic inverse-degree model, and logarithmic corrections that bias polynomial extrapolation.

## Risky consequences

Joint nested fits of the limit and three inverse-degree corrections must remain compatible with \(13/60\) and \(6/5\) as the lower cutoff changes.

## Strongest falsification attempt

Exact determinants through degree thirty-six were fitted on four nested tails. The fits stabilize at

\[
\theta_2\approx0.2167044,
\qquad
u\approx1.20935.
\]

Across the nested windows, \(\theta_2\) varies by less than \(10^{-6}\) and \(u\) by less than \(3\times10^{-4}\), while remaining separated from \(13/60\) by more than \(3\times10^{-5}\) and from \(6/5\) by more than \(0.009\). All six refutation gates passed.

## Disposition

Reject \(13/60\), \(6/5\), \(47/10\), and \(-17/10\) under the declared cubic correction model. Withdraw every downstream numerical interpretation that used them, including \(104/1575\) and the rational double-scaling crossover; retain only exact conditional equivalences. The next leaf is `quarter-cross-limit-model-reassessment`, testing inverse powers against logarithmic correction models before recognizing another constant.

## Claim boundary

Finite nested fits do not prove different constants. A logarithmic or slower correction could reverse the disposition, so no replacement exact value is promoted.
