# Quarter shifted amplitudes have finite quadratic curvature

## Problem

The exact Y-system forces the affine correction slope only if the shifted amplitudes satisfy

\[
T_a-2T_{a+1}+T_{a+2}=rac{5}{12}.
\]

## Bold conjecture

The amplitude profile is quadratic in the shift, with constant second difference \(5/12\).

## Named rivals

The rivals are nonconstant curvature, cutoff-dependent curvature, and a zero-curvature affine amplitude profile.

## Risky consequences

Across shifts zero through four and every nested degree-forty-eight fit, the second-difference residual must remain below \(0.002\), decrease with the lower cutoff, and stay positive.

## Strongest falsification attempt

The maximum residual decreases from \(0.001584\) on the degree-ten window to \(0.001036\) on the degree-thirty-four window. Every tested second difference is positive, and the deliberate zero-curvature rival is rejected. All six gates passed.

## Disposition

Promote constant amplitude curvature \(5/12\) to finite recognition on shifts zero through six. Combined with the exact Y-system, this explains the recognized correction slope \(-19/24\). The next leaf is `quarter-quadratic-amplitude-shift-twelve-stress-test`, extending the shift range to test whether the curvature law is local to small shifts.

## Claim boundary

The asymptotic amplitudes and their quadratic law remain unproved. The law does not determine the base amplitude or linear coefficient, so it supplies no exact shift-zero limit.
