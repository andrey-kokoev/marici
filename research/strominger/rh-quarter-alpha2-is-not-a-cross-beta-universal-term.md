# Quarter alpha2 is not a cross-beta universal term

## Question

Does the quarter-Weibull value \(\alpha_2=-3/16\) follow from a general law

\[
\alpha_2(\beta)=-\frac{1-\beta}{4}?
\]

Exact factorial Hankel determinants through degree twenty test \(\beta=1,1/2,1/3,1/4\). The fitted values are

\[
0,
\quad-0.07531,
\quad-0.13455,
\quad-0.18750094.
\]

The proposed law predicts

\[
0,
\quad-0.125,
\quad-0.16667,
\quad-0.1875.
\]

It fails the intermediate controls by \(0.0497\) and \(0.0321\), while the quarter value agrees with \(-3/16\) within \(10^{-6}\). Therefore the quarter coefficient cannot be imported from that guessed cross-\(\beta\) law.

## Disposition

Reject the universal explanation and retain \(\alpha_2=-3/16\) as a quarter-specific finite candidate. The next leaf is `quarter-mellin-alpha2`: extract the \(n^{-2}\) coefficient directly from the asymptotic of

\[
\frac{D_{n+1}D_{n-1}}{D_n^2},
\qquad
D_n=\det[(4(i+j)+3)!].
\]

## Claim boundary

The control fits may contain different subleading scales, so they do not establish the true function \(\alpha_2(\beta)\). They are sufficient only to reject the proposed finite universal fit as a source derivation.
