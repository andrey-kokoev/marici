# The Quadratic Endpoint Algebra Cannot Internally Control Its Metaplectic Lift

## Internal-control attempt

The endpoint has two oscillator modes. One might try to use the \(v\)-mode as
an internal selector for the metaplectic rotation of the \(u\)-mode.

Inside the even module, choose selector code states \(n_v=2\) and \(n_v=4\).
The corresponding polynomial selector is

\[
P_v=\frac{N_v-2}{2},
\]

which takes values zero and one on the two code grades. The desired controlled
generator is

\[
H_{\mathrm{ctrl}}
=
P_v\left(N_u+\frac12\right).
\]

It acts trivially on the inactive code grade and as the forced oscillator
generator on the active grade.

## Mixed-difference obstruction

A number-preserving quadratic generator has diagonal eigenvalue of the form

\[
q(n_u,n_v)=a n_u+b n_v+c.
\]

Every such affine function has zero mixed difference:

\[
q(4,4)-q(4,2)-q(2,4)+q(2,2)=0.
\]

For \(H_{\mathrm{ctrl}}\), the same mixed difference is \(2\). Hence no
quadratic diagonal generator realizes the controlled action even on the
smallest four-state even code.

The remaining quadratic monomials change number grades. If a quadratic
operator is required to agree with the diagonal controlled generator on the
code without leakage, their coefficients must vanish. Thus allowing the full
quadratic \(\mathfrak{sp}_4\) algebra does not evade the obstruction.

## Degree theorem

The product \(N_vN_u\) has Bernstein degree four. Commutators of quadratic Weyl
operators remain quadratic, so no Lie word in the existing metaplectic
generators can produce it. The minimum new endpoint constructor is a quartic
cross-mode interaction, analogous to a cross-Kerr coupling:

\[
N_vN_u
\quad\hbox{together with the required lower-degree corrections}.
\]

## Consequence for executable closure

The source algebra forces the correct linear lift but cannot condition that
lift using its own quadratic degrees of freedom. Therefore controlled
metaplectic observability needs one of two genuine extensions:

- an external coherent control with a primitive controlled implementation; or
- an internal non-Gaussian quartic interaction.

Neither is generated by the established endpoint \(Mp(4,\mathbb R)\) action.
This gives a concrete algebraic meaning to the executable-closure barrier:
Gaussian symmetry control cannot control its own global Gaussian phase.
