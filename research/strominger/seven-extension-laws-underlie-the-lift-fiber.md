# Seven extension laws underlie the lift fiber

## The relational observable

The added residue layer at \(g=14\) is not determined merely by listing seven
lifts.  One must say how the new \(\mathbb Z/7\) layer is attached to the old
one.  Such attachments are classified by

\[
\operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathbb Z}(\mathbb Z/7,\mathbb Z/7)
\cong\mathbb Z/7.
\]

Thus there are seven endpoint-labelled extension laws.

Represent the class \(t\in\mathbb F_7\) by generators \(x,y\) and relations

\[
7x=0,
\qquad
7y=tx.
\]

For \(t=0\), the layers do not bind:

\[
E_0\cong\mathbb Z/7\oplus\mathbb Z/7.
\]

For every \(t\neq0\), they bind into a cyclic lift:

\[
E_t\cong\mathbb Z/49.
\]

The magnetic lift sequence

\[
0\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/7
\xrightarrow{\times7}\mathbb Z/49
\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/7
\longrightarrow0
\]

is therefore a nonzero extension class.

## What can be observed

There are three levels of information.

1. Cardinality sees only \(49\) elements and cannot distinguish
   \(\mathbb Z/49\) from \(\mathbb Z/7\oplus\mathbb Z/7\).
2. Exponent distinguishes split from nonsplit: the former has exponent \(7\),
   the latter exponent \(49\).
3. A labelled endpoint comparison distinguishes all seven classes
   \(t\in\mathbb F_7\).

Without fixed generators at the two endpoint residue ports, automorphisms act
on \(t\), and the six nonzero classes collapse to one isomorphism type.  The
current cokernel canonically says “nonsplit cyclic extension.”  It does not by
itself canonically name a particular nonzero scalar \(t\).

## Consequence for the tower picture

The new datum is neither another state nor another independent observation
port.  It is a relation between two residue layers.  This is exactly the kind
of object that a higher coherence level should carry: not more values, but the
law by which lower-level values are attached.

The magnetic seven therefore gives a finite model of a relational-residue
level:

\[
\text{old residue layer}
+\text{new residue layer}
+\text{extension class}.
\]

Only the first two layers can be counted pointwise.  The third is observable
through lifting behavior, exponent, or a source-labelled comparison of
endpoint generators.
