# Sulfur-32: a global reversible 2-by-2 wire fibration

## Constructed result

The edge-three carrier admits a candidate global periodic wiring with:

- **16 P source fibers**;
- **16 N channel fibers**, including the three retained opposite-tip pairs;
- **16 E target fibers**;
- **64 distinct wires**, four through every N channel and four at every P/E endpoint;
- reversible **P|E / E|P** presentations with unchanged payloads.

All 51 source cells remain recorded. The fibration has 48 cell fibers because precisely three pairs of N cells share a channel identity. It does not remove three physical cells or turn 19 independent amplitudes into 16 scalars.

This implements the [operator's channel specification](sulfur-32-reversible-channel-specification.md). It follows the [global-construction directive](global-coherence-standing-assumption.md): form one globally closed incidence object first, then verify its local port constraints. No native reader-growth algorithm selects the construction.

Here the endpoint layers are the tip-cell centers. For a cell center q, subtract the sector center c and take half its dot products with `(1,1,-1,-1)`, `(1,-1,1,-1)` and `(1,-1,-1,1)` to obtain orthogonal coordinates `(x,y,z)`.

The N centers are exactly the finite set of integer triples with even coordinate sum and `|x|+|y|+|z| <= 2`. Thus they form a **finite FCC patch**, with 19 sites: a center, twelve nearest neighbors and six axial sites. The five N planes have populations 1,4,9,4,1.

The P and E centers occupy the two complementary tetrahedral-interstitial sublattices of this FCC organization. In these coordinates the ambient E sublattice is the FCC lattice shifted by `(1/2,1/2,1/2)`, and P is shifted by its negative. Restriction to the carrier retains 16 sites of each kind. This is an exact description of the existing center geometry, not a newly imposed placement rule.

## Global closure, not an inversion-only boundary patch

Work in the source barycentric integer lattice with coordinate sum one. Declare the translation lattice generated by

\[
t_i=(1,1,1,1)-4e_i,\qquad i=0,1,2,3.
\]

Each vector has coordinate sum zero. Its distinguished coordinate changes by minus three and the other three by plus one. These translations compare complementary boundary triangle occurrences at edge units three.

A displacement belongs to this lattice exactly when its integer coordinates sum to zero and all four coordinates have the same residue modulo four. The determinant in an integer basis of the sum-zero lattice has absolute value **16**.

This is an explicit candidate periodic closure, not a claim that geometry uniquely selects it. The initial inversion-only boundary-sewing idea was not used: inversion is retained as a dual comparison, while translations perform the actual face sewing without exchanging cell orientation.

Group whole cells and complete faces under this single translation action. For every source member retain its vertices, faces and exact translation to the representative. The quotient identifies only the three prescribed opposite-tip N pairs. Their center differences are sums of two period generators.

The complete quotient has:

| Incidence | Count |

Its Euler characteristic is zero. Every face has exactly two owners, one N and one P or E. Each N channel has four P-facing and four E-facing ports. The construction adds **24 periodic boundary connections** to the source's 104 native shared-face connections. The three tip-pair identifications are retained separately from those 128 face connections.

## Where the 2-by-2 ports come from

Every triangular face has one constant barycentric coordinate, giving a global axis label from 0 through 3. An N octahedron has two opposite faces for each axis. A P or E tetrahedron has one face for each axis.

Restrict the complete quotient incidence to one axis at a time. Its connected components—not a locally grown traversal—determine the wires. Every component contains precisely one P endpoint, one N channel and one E endpoint. There are no discarded closed components or branching leftovers.

For each of the four axes, the result is a perfect matching between all 16 P fibers and all 16 E fibers, passing through all 16 N channels. The four axes are displayed in the declared array

```
0  1
2  3
```

Thus every N channel exposes four incoming and four outgoing wire records in a 2-by-2 presentation. Source and target groupings also each contain 16 arrays of four records. These are groupings of the same 64 wires, not three independent collections of wire contents.

## Reversal and retained information

Selecting E|P reverses each wire's endpoint and port order. It preserves the wire and channel identities, reverses the seam-comparison sequence, and negates the accumulated period displacement. No wire payload is transformed.

Tests use structured payloads containing coefficients, context and occurrence histories. Either endpoint grouping reconstructs every input record exactly. The record is stored in the grouped fibers themselves, not recovered from a hidden input copy. Geometric inversion descends to an involution of the complete wiring after exchanging the endpoint presentation.

Local validation currently covers actual face geometry, P–N/N–E incidence, four-port completeness, axis continuity and retained-record reconstruction. A binding to native phase-reader witnesses and a physical sulfur readout are **not** supplied by this geometric construction. The periodic closure and the 2-by-2 display convention are explicit inputs, not derived dynamics.
