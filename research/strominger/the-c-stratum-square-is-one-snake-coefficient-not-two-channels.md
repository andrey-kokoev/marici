# The C-Stratum Square Is One Snake Coefficient, Not Two Channels

## Result

The paired snake-channel conjecture is refuted as stated.

For each oriented C packet, the relational response has a primitive integral
kernel generator.  Embedding that generator into the full four-port lattice and
applying the full response difference produces a diagonal vector.  This is the
canonical snake connecting map.  Its source and target both have rank one.

The exact scalars are

\[
\partial_C(+1)=-1204224=-2^{13}3(8-1)^2,
\]

and

\[
\partial_C(-1)=1990656=2^{13}3(8+1)^2.
\]

Thus the previously observed extension modulus is not the determinant of two
independent canonical channels.  The entire square already occurs as the
coefficient of one primitive connecting arrow.

## What survives

The affine conductor identity remains exact, but its square must be explained
inside the rank-one coefficient.  A factorization into two source-dual terms is
still possible only if a new typed correspondence refines this connecting
arrow.  The existing full and relational lattices do not supply that refinement.

## Hostile control

A square determinant alone cannot authorize a two-channel interpretation.  For
example,

\[
\begin{pmatrix}c&1\\0&c\end{pmatrix}
\]

has determinant \(c^2\), while for primitive off-diagonal entry its Smith packet
is \((1,c^2)\), not \((c,c)\).  Determinant factorization, invariant-factor
repetition, and source-level splitting are distinct claims.

## New frontier

The informative question is now narrower: why does the single primitive snake
coefficient factor as

\[
2^{13}3(8-\omega)^2?
\]

Any explanation must act before numerical evaluation and must construct two
typed factors of this one arrow, rather than infer them from its value.
