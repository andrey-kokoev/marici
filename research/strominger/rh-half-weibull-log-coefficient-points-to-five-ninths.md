# The half-Weibull log coefficient points to five ninths

## Question

Where does the drifting \(\beta=1/2\) hard-edge logarithmic coefficient converge?

Exact factorial Hankel determinants through degree thirty-two give a monotonically decreasing coefficient estimate. It crosses the earlier candidate \(9/16\) and reaches \(0.561853\) at degree thirty-two. In contrast, relative to

\[
c(1/2)=\frac59,
\]

the scaled correction

\[
n\left(c_n(1/2)-\frac59\right)
\]

stays between \(0.2015\) and \(0.2037\) for degrees twenty-five through thirty-two. This supports

\[
c_n(1/2)=\frac59+\frac{d}{n}+o(n^{-1}),
\qquad d\approx0.202.
\]

## Disposition

Reject \(9/16\) and select \(5/9\) as the half-Weibull finite candidate.

The next leaf is `half-weibull-five-ninths-proof`: derive the relative Hankel free-energy logarithmic coefficient from the moment matrix \(\Gamma(2(i+j)+2\alpha+2)\).

## Claim boundary

A stable rational extrapolation is not an asymptotic proof. No general formula in \(\beta\) follows from the value \(5/9\).
