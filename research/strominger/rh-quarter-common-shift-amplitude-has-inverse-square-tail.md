# Quarter common-shift amplitude has an inverse-square tail

## Problem

The shift-dependent curvature amplitude needs a large-shift model that can constrain a future Barnes divisor.

## Bold conjecture

The common-shift amplitude satisfies

\[
C(t)\sim\frac{1}{4(t+2)^2}
\]

as the common parameter shift grows.

## Named rivals

The named rivals are inverse-first-power decay, inverse-third-power decay, and a finite-window crossover without a stable power law.

## Risky consequences

On an enlarged shift grid, \(4(t+2)^2C(t)\) should approach one and have smaller tail spread than the corresponding first- and third-power normalizations.

## Strongest falsification attempt

Exact determinants at degrees fourteen through twenty-four were fitted independently for shifts zero through twelve. On shifts five through twelve, \(4(t+2)^2C(t)\) increases toward one, reaching \(0.9855\) at shift twelve. Its relative spread is smaller than those of the inverse-first- and inverse-third-power normalizations. All five checker gates passed.

## Disposition

The inverse-square conjecture survives this finite discrimination; the first- and third-power rivals are rejected on the declared tail window. The next leaf is `quarter-common-shift-inverse-square-source`: derive the offset two and coefficient one quarter from the shifted source parameters or a Barnes divisor.

## Claim boundary

The input amplitudes are finite inverse-degree extrapolations. The test does not prove a large-shift limit, exclude another crossover, or identify the Barnes arguments.
