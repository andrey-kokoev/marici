# Quarter seven-halves transport is forced by absence of a logarithmic pivot remainder

## Problem

The boundary value \(-7/2\) must be transported to finite simultaneous-scaling ratios.

## Bold conjecture

If

\[
\log d_n(\kappa n)
=4n\log n+n f(\kappa)+\gamma(\kappa)\log n+O(1),
\]

then the normalized pivot quotient has no \((\log n)/n\) term.

## Named rivals

The rivals are a ratio-dependent \(\gamma\), a hidden logarithmic pivot remainder, and a nonuniform expansion invalidating next-order transport.

## Risky consequences

The shifted quotient must contain

\[
-(\kappa+2)\gamma'(\kappa)\frac{\log n}{n}.
\]

Its absence forces \(\gamma'=0\), after which the boundary gives \(\gamma=-7/2\).

## Strongest falsification attempt

Constant, linear, and quadratic trial functions were inserted into the exact shifted arguments. After removing the ordinary \(\gamma/n\) term, the scaled residual converges to \(-(\kappa+2)\gamma'\). Variable trials produce nonzero obstructions, while the constant trial produces zero. All five repaired gates passed.

## Disposition

Prove conditional transport: an \(O(1/n)\) normalized-pivot remainder without \((\log n)/n\) forces the coefficient \(-7/2\) at every positive ratio. The next leaf is `quarter-normalized-pivot-no-log-remainder`: test and then derive the required remainder condition.

## Claim boundary

The absence of a logarithmic remainder is not yet proved. Without it, finite-ratio constancy remains supported by exact fits rather than theorem-level transport.
