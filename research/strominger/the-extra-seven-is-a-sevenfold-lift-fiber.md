# The extra seven is visible only as a sevenfold lift fiber

## Why reduction modulo seven is insufficient

The source-authorized preferred cokernel is

\[
C_g=\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/h_g.
\]

Write

\[
e_g=v_7(h_g)=b_g+\mathbf 1_{\{14\mid g\}},
\]

where \(b_g\) is the factorial-overlap baseline.  A readout modulo seven sees

\[
(\mathbb Z/7^{e_g})/7(\mathbb Z/7^{e_g})\cong\mathbb Z/7
\]

for every \(e_g\geq1\).  It cannot distinguish one factor of seven from two.

At the first admissible cocircuit grade, \(g=14\), the baseline has
\(b_{14}=1\), while the actual exponent is \(e_{14}=2\).  Thus:

\[
C_{14}^{\mathrm{baseline}}[7^\infty]\cong\mathbb Z/7,
\qquad
C_{14}^{\mathrm{actual}}[7^\infty]\cong\mathbb Z/49.
\]

The smallest finite readout that separates them is therefore modulo \(49\),
not modulo \(7\).

## The object having seven members

The reduction map

\[
\mathbb Z/49\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/7
\]

has a seven-element fiber over every residue.  The cocircuit contribution is
the availability of this additional lift coordinate.  It is a Bockstein-like
extension datum:

\[
0\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/7
\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/49
\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/7
\longrightarrow0.
\]

Hence the domain phenomenon is not seven parallel modes.  It is that a
previously visible residue acquires seven inequivalent integral lifts.

## Authority boundary

The cokernel exponent is an invariant of the current integral matrix, so the
modulo-\(49\) distinction is algebraically source-derived.  An executable
physical port resolving those lifts is additional instrument data and has not
been constructed.

There is also no canonical attribution of one factor inside
\(\mathbb Z/49\) to “factorial” and the other to “cocircuit” from a single
unlabelled cyclic group.  That attribution uses the independently derived
factorization law for \(h_g\).  Forgetting source labels retains the order
\(49\) group but forgets which constructor supplied its second layer.
