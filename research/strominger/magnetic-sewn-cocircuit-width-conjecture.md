# Sewn cocircuit support has distinct wedge laws

For native \(\beta=4\), search for the smallest contiguous target-row interval
whose restricted ordinary cokernel detects the full current quotient generated
by \(K_0,\ldots,K_3\).

The bounded atlas over \(2\le g\le8\), \(1\le q\le14\) gives the following
sharp patterns away from insufficient-cutoff points.

On the low width-two wedge,

\[
w_{\mathrm{low}}=q+7=q+\beta+3.
\]

On the high width-two wedge,

\[
w_{\mathrm{high}}=q-g+4=q-g+\beta.
\]

At the high boundary \(q=2g+1\), this becomes \(w=g+5\); each further unit
of branch separation adds one required observation row.

This separates two finiteness notions:

- the invariant current quotient is generated by the fixed four-current source
  packet;
- a contiguous row cocircuit witnessing all of it need not have fixed width.

Thus finite source generation does not imply a uniformly local presentation.
A proof of the high wedge must transport a cocircuit across the growing gap,
not replace the problem by one fixed contiguous minor.

The flat-connection constant modes do not supply this cocircuit: they are
branch-local chart coordinates and are contaminated by the opposite ordinary
branch. The sewn cocircuit is a comparison object whose support law records
how much of the gap must be crossed.

The next target is to derive these two support formulas from interval endpoints
and construct the cocircuit recursively, allowing a fixed-size boundary state
even though its expanded row support grows with \(q\).
