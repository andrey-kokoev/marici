# Quarter cross limit stabilizes near 0.21670457

## Problem

The inverse-power model won degree-thirty-six holdouts, but its limit could remain sensitive to degree range or correction order.

## Bold conjecture

Cubic and quartic inverse-degree expansions extrapolate to the same positive cross-limit constant.

## Named rivals

The rivals are order-sensitive extrapolation, tail-window drift, and the previously proposed value \(13/60\).

## Risky consequences

After extending exact determinants through degree forty-eight, cubic and quartic fits on four nested tails must stabilize separately and agree at the latest cutoff.

## Strongest falsification attempt

The cubic fits range from \(0.2167039599\) to \(0.2167045208\). The quartic fits range from \(0.2167045701\) to \(0.2167045881\); the latest cubic and quartic values differ by less than \(5\times10^{-8}\). Every fit remains more than \(3\times10^{-5}\) from \(13/60\). All seven gates passed.

## Disposition

Promote \(0.21670457\) only as a stabilized finite extrapolation, not an exact constant or limit theorem. The degree-forty-eight result strengthens withdrawal of \(13/60\). The next leaf is `quarter-cross-limit-small-denominator-falsification`, testing whether any stable low-denominator rational is compatible with the extrapolation uncertainty.

## Claim boundary

Agreement between two truncated inverse-power models does not prove existence of the limit or exclude still slower terms. No downstream physical or exact algebraic interpretation is restored.
