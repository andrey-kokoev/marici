# The Square Stratum Is Classified by Anti-Invariant Fox Units

For a nonzero free-group power, the Fox derivative is

\[
\frac{\partial x^n}{\partial x}
=
\begin{cases}
1+x+\cdots+x^{n-1},&n>0,\\
-(x^{-1}+\cdots+x^n),&n<0.
\end{cases}
\]

It is a unit in the integral group ring exactly when \(|n|=1\).

Across the complete sixteen-case tail-power census, the cyclic target index is
twice a square exactly when:

1. both tail Fox derivatives are units; and
2. their augmentations have opposite signs.

Neither condition alone suffices.  Nonprimitive opposite tail powers fail, and
primitive equal-polarity tails fail.

This gives a source-level classification of the exceptional square stratum:

\[
\text{square stratum}
=
\text{Fox-unit tails}
\cap
\text{anti-invariant polarity}.
\]

The classification explains why completed Moore deletion data was insufficient:
deletion proves that a cycle exists, while Fox differentiation remembers whether
its constructors were atomic units or geometric sums.

One theorem is still missing.  We have not derived a comparison map carrying the
anti-invariant tensor of the two Fox derivatives into the cyclic diagonal target
intersection.  Without that map, the classifier is exact bounded evidence but
does not yet derive the numerical conductors \(28\) and \(36\).
