# Intrinsic cut and domain determination

## Strong conjecture: the cut determines the law-domain

For a physical-law domain carried by a WG-generated system, the formed triangle exposed by a tower cut determines, and permits reconstruction of, the domain's admissible operations, constraints and observable distinctions, up to the declared structure-preserving equivalence. The triangle and its comparison data are induced by the actual tower operations, not supplied by an independent wrapper containing an opaque copy of the domain.

This is stronger than faithful decorated encoding. Its quantification is over the specified WG-carried domains; extending it to every known law-domain additionally requires the separate source-coverage claim.

## Weaker intrinsic-cut conjecture

The tower's serialization, promotion and recovery operations themselves supply the formed triangle exposing the domain: its three presentations, actual comparisons and compatibility witness. No independently added cone is needed to obtain that triangle.

The strong claim entails this weaker claim on the same scope. The converse is not asserted: an intrinsic triangle could still forget a domain distinction.

## Frozen test and falsifiers

First freeze the actual cut object, its retained fields and accessible membership records, its comparison maps and witnesses, and the allowed notion of triangle equivalence. Independently freeze the physical-domain equivalence and the observations/operations used to distinguish domains. This is part of executing the test, not another open research gate.

Start with the promoted oscillator. Remove `ThreeWitnessRetainedCone` as the source of the triangle, and inspect what the tower itself exposes. The bath may remain nested if that nesting belongs to the actual cut interface; it need not become a separate outer port. Do not erase genuine retained data merely to manufacture a collision.

Then vary or remove the bath contribution and compare the exposed objects:

1. **Collision falsifier:** equivalent cut triangles correspond to law-domains distinguished by an admitted operation, constraint or observation, such as the required output commutator.
2. **Reconstruction falsifier:** a required operation, constraint or distinction cannot be recovered without data outside the frozen cut interface.
3. **Intrinsic-source falsifier:** a required triangle vertex/comparison/witness comes only from the added wrapper, not from the declared tower construction.

A failed proposed decoder is not by itself a proof that no decoder exists. A certified collision or a source invariant excluding the required comparison supplies the stronger obstruction. Success on one oscillator variant is a scoped test, not universal closure.

Rival explanation: the tower retains the domain, and a generic cone can faithfully wrap it, but the exposed triangle is neither intrinsic nor sufficient to determine that domain.

## Implications, with their scope

- **Cut determines domain => intrinsic cut**, on the same domain class, by the intrinsic-source clause of the stronger claim.
- **Cut determines domain => cut-faithful operations/observations**, on the same fixed equivalences, by reconstruction. In particular, its negation is witnessed by the collision test above.
- **Cut determines domain AND physical-domain source coverage => governing law-domain tower-cut claim**, provided the intrinsic cut has the compatible FCC/equivalent tetrahedral representation. That last representation condition is explicit; existing wrapper-based charts do not silently discharge it for a different intrinsic object.

These are conditional mathematical entailments, not proof-status promotions. Their antecedents remain conjectures.

No reverse implications are asserted. In particular:

- A recoverable cone carrying an entire supplied packet does not imply intrinsic determination.
- Native compilation of promotion alone does not imply that its exposed cut determines every physical domain.
- Convergence of a particular Berry readout does not imply all-domain cut completeness.
- Failure of the stronger claim does not refute the weaker existence of a faithful decorated tetrahedral encoding.

The two previous bounded compiler/continuum conjectures remain useful probes. They are not prerequisites for testing a cut collision and do not replace this sharper test.
