# Spin-memory rank-16 quotient theorem

## Extraction and presentation classification

Reduce the 45-by-21 contour matrix at the good prime \(991\). The field
contains the required latitude radicals and primitive ninth roots, while all
harmonic denominators remain invertible.

For two complete dark latitude rings, the 18 zero rows span rank 16. The
three apparent boundary states are the same row space recorded at support
costs zero, one, and two. There are respectively 1, 18, and 144 presentations
that retain rank 16. All 163 presentations define one invariant object.

Longitude rotation and reflection permute the presentations and fix their
common row space. Thus the distinction is

\[
\text{one boundary object}\quad+\quad\text{three cost-labelled charts},
\]

not three kernel mechanisms.

## Five-dimensional quotient

The annihilator of any two-dark-ring row space has dimension

\[
21-16=5.
\]

Restrict the remaining 27 contour rows to this annihilator. This produces a
27-by-5 quotient code for each of the ten latitude pairs.

Every zero set of a nonzero quotient word is contained in a proper row
subspace of dimension at most four. Enumerating all row-generated subspaces
of dimensions one through four is therefore exhaustive. The largest number
of quotient rows contained in such a subspace is at most 11 for every pair.
Hence every nonzero continuation spends at least

\[
27-11=16
\]

support positions over the modular certificate field. Six pair types also
attain 16 over characteristic zero through explicit three-dark-ring packets.
The remaining four receive modularly certified characteristic-zero lower
bound 22. Matching characteristic-zero words are supplied by the cyclotomic
five-zero construction below.

The six distance-16 pairs have a direct geometric classification. They are
exactly the two-element subsets of one of the only admissible three-dark-ring
triples,

\[
\{0,2,4\},\qquad \{1,2,3\}.
\]

For these and only these pairs, a quotient word can make a third latitude
ring identically dark. The remaining two active rings each contain one
azimuthal zero, giving \(9+1+1=11\) quotient zeros and support 16.

For either remaining reflection orbit, impose three consecutive ninth-root
zeros on one active ring and the middle root on each of the other two. The
resulting five-by-five determinant is divisible by
\(\Phi_9(t)=t^6+t^3+1\), so a nonzero characteristic-zero word with five
zeros exists. Its support is 22. The \(\mathbf F_{991}\) certificate excludes
six zeros, proving that 22 is exact.

## Continuation theorem

Starting at the rank-16 two-ring boundary, every admissible continuation has
exactly two alternatives:

- its added zero constraints raise the total row rank to 21, eliminating the
  kernel;
- a kernel survives, but every surviving measurement word occupies at least
  16 ports.

This proves the complete lower-bound continuation claim from the extracted boundary
object. It does not yet prove global cospark 16 for packets having zero or one
completely dark latitude ring; those do not factor through this boundary
quotient and remain a separate theorem.

## Why one modular certificate suffices

The reduction is chosen away from all denominators and realizes every needed
radical and ninth root. A characteristic-zero dependence would reduce to a
dependence over this field. Therefore modular full-rank certificates prove
the corresponding characteristic-zero nondependence claims. The explicit
16-support harmonic hostile also survives reduction, showing that the lower
bound is attained within the quotient family.
