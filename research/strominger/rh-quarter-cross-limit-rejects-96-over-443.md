# Quarter cross limit rejects 96/443

## Problem

The first rational surviving the deliberately conservative extrapolation interval was \(96/443\).

## Bold conjecture

The fraction \(96/443\) remains compatible when immature low-cutoff cubic fits are removed.

## Named rivals

The rivals are rejection by the mature interval, sensitivity to correction order, and later re-entry caused by unmodelled asymptotic terms.

## Risky consequences

The candidate must lie within the interval formed by the latest cubic estimate and all quartic estimates, not merely within the envelope containing every early cubic fit.

## Strongest falsification attempt

The candidate value is \(0.216704288939\ldots\). The mature interval is

\[
[0.216704520832,0.216704588098].
\]

The fraction lies \(2.32\times10^{-7}\) below this interval, while the interval width is \(6.73\times10^{-8}\). It also lies below every quartic fit. All six gates passed.

## Disposition

Reject \(96/443\) as the active rational candidate, conditional on the inverse-power model. The next leaf is `quarter-cross-limit-mature-rational-bound`, enumerating the first rational compatible with the mature interval rather than expanding the search around a preferred fraction.

## Claim boundary

The rejection is model-conditional. The mature interval is not a rigorous error enclosure, so the result neither proves irrationality nor permanently excludes \(96/443\) under a different asymptotic model.
