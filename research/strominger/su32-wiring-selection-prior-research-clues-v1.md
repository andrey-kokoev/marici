# Prior-research clues for the two surviving wiring-shape families

## Search result

The strongest clue is **simultaneous compatibility on the four middle-overlapping comparison cones**, not minimization of the newly found distance invariant. Prior work also contains direct counterexamples to selection by symmetry, retained history or generic coherence alone.

This was a source/document review. Historical checker results below are reported from their existing documents; they were not freshly replayed in this search. No new selector or wiring was installed.

## 1. Most directly applicable: six overlapping Sulfur cones

Source: `sulfur-operation-reconstruction-v1.md`, especially sections 3–6.

The entry-to-central comparison is only one of six residual cones. In a fixed reading their starting-slice counts are:

```
slice 1: one cone
slice 3: four cones
slice 5: one cone.
```

The four middle cones overlap the routing region where the current candidates differ. Prior work explicitly says that the entrance cone and reflected exit cone do not exhaust the global reconciliation obligation.

**Implication for the current result:** the global transparent-reference checker covers actual paths and retains entry-side central histories, but this does not by itself establish a simultaneous comparison/filling law on all six cones. The two distance classes may be distinguished by an additional overlap obligation rather than an external optimization preference.

**Concrete next test:** freeze the complete six-cone restriction problem, then compare the already constructed sixteen assignments against it. Retain operand identities, shared references, outer branches and alternative composite histories. First distinguish absent required attachment/data from disagreement of supplied operations. Where the comparison map or coherence condition is still unbound, report that obligation as unformulated—not automatically passed or failed.

Do not silently require every potential geometric path to be an executed wire, or manufacture local operations by extending paths. The standing global-construction directive still applies.

## 2. A secondary gap: record-reference flow versus retained wire occurrences

Sources:

- `five-side-oct-routing-obligations-v1.md`, section 4;
- `sulfur-32-sixteen-strand-surface-candidate.md`;
- `carrier-particle-circuits.md`, instance/interface boundary.

The obligation document distinguishes a simultaneous routing assignment with disjoint retained-instance partitions and inverse regroupings from mere support coverage. The sixteen-strand candidate retains a named endpoint permutation and checks that actual strands neither branch nor disappear. It also explicitly marks its sorting and middle matching conventions as assumptions.

Our current relay repeatedly carries the same record identity and checks equality at merges. That is valid as reference propagation, but it has not established a bijective matching of named physical wire occurrences. A record appearing twice is not automatically two independent wires, nor may the two appearances simply be identified as one physical passage.

**Useful audit:** specify the occurrence/port type and multiplicity budget for the complete assignment, then test whether the relay can be refined to that retained occurrence matching. Do not impose a new no-fanout law on record references merely because a different strand model uses one. The missing binding between record references and wire instances must be explicit.

This is a task-admission question before choosing between geometric orbits.

## 3. Selection can come from an independently specified task

Sources:

- `intrinsic-retained-selector-conjecture.md`;
- `task-relative-retained-transport-conjecture.md`.

The intrinsic-selector test reports 96 identical retained-source forks with two certified continuations each. Retaining ordered histories, coefficients, contact witnesses and eventual exits did not select one next operation.

The follow-up fixes tasks independently of operation names. At eight N markings, a broad realization-advance task still has two solutions. Requiring either **the same carrier** or **crossing an actual shared face** selects a unique continuation where the respective task is available.

**Transferable lesson:** specify what the six-pair placement must accomplish, rather than choosing a preferred family and naming its distance feature a law. For example, a source-supported simultaneous comparison-access or boundary-communication requirement might discriminate the families. Nothing in these papers supplies that requirement for Su-32 automatically.

## 4. Symmetry alone has already failed to select routing

Sources:

- `pe-coupled-n-routing.md`, including its falsification notice;
- `n-routing-conjecture-falsification.md`.

The complete twelve-port enumeration has 27 reversible C3-symmetric matchings. Restricting the companion strands to stay straight leaves three, two nontrivial. Equally reversible and geometrically P/E-dual alternatives produce different complete trajectory inventories and loop counts.

The restricted result survives only with the added face-supported-interaction assumption. It was not derived from the original symmetries.

**Relevance:** neither duality, reversal nor the existence of two geometric orbits chooses our `{2,2}` family over `{2,10}`. A native support restriction could be useful, but it must be supplied independently and tested on the full assignment.

## 5. An intriguing two-orbit precedent—but not an identification

Source: `carrier-particle-circuits.md`, “classify the whole sign-only coupling class.”

That work exhausts sixteen diagonal sign maps on a fixed binary tensor interface. Independent output sign actions give two eight-member orbits, distinguished by

```
chi = d00*d01*d10*d11.
```

Here the two classes also distinguish product versus joint operator behavior. Full operators remain retained despite the declared local-operation quotient.

Our sixteen wirings likewise give two eight-member coarse geometric orbits. **The equal counts do not identify the carriers, group actions, or meanings.** The useful method is to seek an intrinsic combinatorial invariant of the placements and prove its transformation law. A relation to `chi` would require an actual action-compatible map, not an assigned sign for each distance class. No phase, entanglement or coupling interpretation follows from the analogy.

## 6. Why not choose the shorter-distance family now?

Sources:

- `../nima/graph-action-selection-audit.md`;
- its successor `../nima/dsc-action-selection-countermodels.md`.

Locality, additive assembly, symmetry and shift covariance admit both quadratic and quartic actions. The actual dependent-substitution core also admits nonstationary executions; even stationary segment composition does not force quadraticity.

These are counterexamples to deriving an action or its selection principle from generic retention/composition alone. Squared geometric distance is a valid orbit invariant here, but it is not thereby an energy, cost or objective to minimize.

Similarly, the earlier surface-strand candidate explicitly uses shortest paths and centre-preferring ties as embedding conventions, not source-selected laws.

## Recommended order

1. Audit **all six overlapping comparison contexts** against the fixed complete candidates; make the missing laws explicit before counting survivors.
2. Bind reference appearances to retained wire occurrences if the intended task demands a bijective physical wiring rather than reference propagation.
3. If both geometric families still satisfy the independently specified task, retain both. Introduce a further task/support condition only with its source and a falsifier.
4. Use distance or parity-like invariants to describe surviving distinctions, not to smuggle in a preferred answer.

The search found useful tests and warnings, but **no prior source-selected rule choosing between the two current Su-32 distance classes**.
