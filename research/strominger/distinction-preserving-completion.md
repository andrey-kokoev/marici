# Distinction-preserving completion theorem

Let \(Q:V\to W\) be a quotient or presentation map and let \(R:V\to D\)
be the complete packet of distinctions required by a later repair. If an
induced observation \(\bar R:W\to D\) satisfies \(R=\bar RQ\), then

\[
\ker Q\subseteq\ker R.
\]

The converse holds when \(Q\) is surjective, including the canonical quotient
\(V\to V/\ker Q\). For an arbitrary map it holds only after replacing \(W\)
by \(\operatorname{im}Q\), unless a separate extension from the image to the
declared codomain is available. Over the integers, \(Q(x)=2x\) and \(R(x)=x\)
have equal trivial kernels, but a factor on the declared codomain would have
to send \(2\) to \(1\), which no integer-linear map can do.

This is the precise algebraic form of the repair-precedence law. The tempting
expression “\(\ker Q\cap\mathrm{Req}(R)=0\)” is ill-typed unless the required
distinctions have first been represented as a subspace of states. The kernel
inclusion supplies a counterexample vector when descent fails; sufficiency
also requires the presentation scope just stated.

For completion, kernel inclusion is only the finite algebraic gate. Continuous
descent additionally requires

\[
Qv_n\to0\Longrightarrow Rv_n\to0.
\]

Across cutoffs, the operational certificate is one fixed source-authorized
constructor family and one uniform constant satisfying

\[
R_N^*R_N\le C^2Q_N.
\]

Full rank at every cutoff is not enough.

The invariant object is not an isolated domain \(L\), but the pair

### Kernel transport requires an operator square

Global injectivity of \(T\) is sufficient but unnecessary. One fixture uses a

### Equal-volume robustness is forbidden by a local Plücker relation

The Cauchy--Binet average could be attained only if all 2024 squared maximal

constructs the optimal frame. The large design problem has now reduced to a

f=1,qquad c=1,qquad ad-bc=1,qquad be-af=1.
\]

Their Jacobian has rank four; a convenient nonzero LICQ minor is
$\varphi^2$. The KKT equations have the common strictly positive multiplier

\[
\lambda=5+\sqrt5.
\]

On the two-dimensional critical tangent space, the Lagrangian Hessian is

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
18+6\sqrt5 & 12+6\sqrt5\\
12+6\sqrt5 & 18+6\sqrt5
\end{pmatrix},
\]

with eigenvalues

\[
6,qquad 30+12\sqrt5.
\]

Both are positive. LICQ, strict complementarity, and the second-order
sufficient condition therefore prove that the golden packet is a strict
constrained local minimum in this sign chamber.

This does not prove global minimality. Other sign chambers and other active
constraint patterns remain to be excluded or compared. The local certificate
turns the next step into a finite chamber-classification problem rather than
another unconstrained numerical search.

### All realizable rank-two sign chambers are equivalent

The minor floor makes the five-column real rank-two configuration uniform. A
linear functional can be chosen outside the finite union of the five column
annihilators. Reorient each column by a sign so that the functional is positive
on it. All five vectors then lie in one open half-plane. Ordering them by slope
makes every ordered two-minor positive.

Only sign reorientations and a permutation were used. No positive column
rescaling or general linear renormalization is needed. Consequently the
absolute-minor multiset, its lower bound, and its squared energy are preserved.
Every realizable uniform sign chamber therefore reduces to the same positive
cyclic chamber for this optimization problem.

This removes sign chambers from the global obstruction. It does not classify
which subsets of the ten inequalities are active at a minimizer. Global
minimality of the golden packet is now an active-set and boundary-coercivity
problem inside one chamber.

### Minimum-minor normalization makes the global problem coercive

The homogeneous quantity relevant to the local theorem is

\[
\frac{\sum_I p_I^2}{\min_I p_I^2}.
\]

Both numerator and denominator have degree two. The minor floor makes the
denominator nonzero. Relabel a minimum minor as $p_{12}$ and scale the whole
Plücker packet so that $p_{12}=1$. This is a proof gauge for the homogeneous
optimization, not an operation on the physical observation ports.

In the resulting affine chart, the ten coordinates include

\[
1,b,d,f,-a,-c,-e
\]

as well as three quadratic coordinates. Hence the energy obeys

\[
E\ge1+a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2+e^2+f^2.
\]

All minor-floor inequalities are closed. Every bounded-energy feasible
sublevel is therefore closed and bounded in six-dimensional chart space, hence
compact. The normalized problem attains a global minimum and no minimizing
sequence can escape through infinity or the $p_{12}=0$ chart boundary.

Attainment does not identify the minimizer. With sign chambers and escape now
removed, the remaining task is finite in type: classify feasible active
constraint patterns at an attained minimum and compare their exact energies.

### The numerical scout isolates one active pattern

The positive chart has nine nonconstant floor inequalities,

\[
a,b,c,d,e,f,ad-bc,be-af,de-cf\ge1,
\]

and therefore 512 formal active subsets. At the golden packet the active set is

\[
\{c,f,ad-bc,be-af\},
\]

while the other five coordinates all have value $\varphi$.

A deterministic-seed SLSQP scout launched 120 perturbed starts. Ninety-seven
runs returned feasible convergences and all 97 had the golden active set and
energy $10+5\varphi$. Twenty-three runs failed or returned infeasible output;
they are retained in the accounting and are not treated as evidence for other
minima.

This scout does not exhaust 512 active patterns and proves no global theorem.
It identifies the exact symbolic branch to prioritize and supplies a hostile
falsifier: any certified feasible KKT branch below the golden energy disproves
the conjecture immediately.

### The golden packet minimizes the entire five-cycle face

View the ten positive Plücker coordinates as the edges of $K_5$. On the golden
face, five unit coordinates form a cycle and the complementary diagonals are
$x,y,z,u,v$. The five Plücker equations can be solved with $y,z$ free:

\[
v=yz-1,qquad
u=\frac{y+1}{yz-1},qquad
x=\frac{z+1}{yz-1}.
\]

Set $p=yz$ and $s=y+z$. The minor floors imply $2\le p\le4$. At fixed $p$,
the diagonal energy is strictly increasing in $s$, so $s\ge2\sqrt p$ forces
the minimum to occur at $y=z=\sqrt p$. Writing $t=\sqrt p$ reduces the
diagonal energy to

\[
H(t)=2t^2+(t^2-1)^2+\frac{2}{(t-1)^2},
\qquad \sqrt2\le t\le2.
\]

Its derivative factors exactly as

\[
H'(t)=
\frac{4(t^2-t-1)
\left[t^2(t-1)^2+t^2-t+1\right]}{(t-1)^3}.
\]

The bracketed factor is positive and the denominator is positive on the
interval. Hence the only critical point is $t=\varphi$, with the derivative
changing from negative to positive. The golden packet is the unique global
minimum on the entire five-cycle face, with total energy $10+5\varphi$.

This face theorem does not yet exclude minima whose unit-minor graph is not a
five-cycle. That graph classification is now the sole missing global step.

### Positive Plücker geometry leaves 24 unit-graph types

Represent the ten minor-floor constraints as the edges of a cyclically ordered
$K_5$. Two crossing diagonals cannot both be unit: their product is, by the
positive Plücker relation, a sum of two products each at least one. Therefore
the unit-minor graph is noncrossing.

It also has no isolated vertex at a minimum. If vertex $i$ met no unit edge,
rescaling column $i$ slightly downward would preserve every floor constraint
and strictly decrease every incident squared minor, contradicting minimality.
Thus the unit graph must be a noncrossing vertex cover.

Exact enumeration now replaces the 512 chart patterns. Among the 1024 labeled
subgraphs of $K_5$, 176 are noncrossing vertex covers. Their counts by edge
number are

\[
15, 60, 66, 30, 5
\]

for three through seven edges. Quotienting by the dihedral group $D_5$ leaves
24 types, distributed as

\[
2, 8, 9, 4, 1.
\]

The golden five-cycle is one of the nine five-edge types. Hence 23 non-golden
graph types remain to exclude or bound. Graph pruning is exact, but it is not
yet the global minimum theorem.

### The 24 graph types are generated by three base faces

Order admitted unit graphs by edge inclusion. An inclusion-minimal graph must
lose vertex coverage whenever any edge is removed. Exact enumeration finds 20
labeled minimal graphs: 15 with three edges and five with four edges. Modulo
$D_5$, only three types remain:

- an adjacent wedge plus a disjoint edge;
- a boundary wedge plus a separated edge;
- the four-edge star.

Every one of the 176 admitted labeled graphs contains one of these minimal
graphs. If $G\subseteq H$, imposing all unit equalities of $H$ gives a subset
of the feasible face for $G$. Therefore face minima are monotone under adding
unit edges.

It follows that a golden-energy lower bound on these three base faces implies
the same bound for all 24 dihedral graph types. This is an exact combinatorial
reduction, not yet the three required analytic inequalities.

### Two base faces have elementary exact minima

For the four-edge star, set $p_{12}=p_{13}=p_{14}=p_{15}=1$. The remaining
positive Plücker coordinates can be ordered as

\[
D\ge1,qquad F-D\ge1,qquad G-F\ge1.
\]

Every energy term increases with these positive variables, so the minimum is
$D=1,F=2,G=3$. Direct substitution gives

\[
E_{\mathrm{star}}=24.
\]

For the adjacent-wedge plus disjoint-edge face, write two remaining minors as
$x,y\ge1$. The Plücker equations take the form

\[
F=BD+x,qquad G=CD+y,qquad By-Cx=1.
\]

Monotonicity gives $D=C=x=1$, hence $By=2$ at the minimum. The energy obeys

\[
E\ge8+2(B^2+y^2+B+y).
\]

Since $By\ge2$, AM--GM gives $B^2+y^2\ge4$ and
$B+y\ge2\sqrt2$, with simultaneous equality at $B=y=\sqrt2$. Therefore

\[
E_{\mathrm{wedge}}=16+4\sqrt2
\approx21.65685425.
\]

Both exact values exceed $10+5\varphi$. The separated-wedge base face is now
the only remaining analytic branch.

### The separated wedge descends to the solved faces

On the final base face, $p_{12}=p_{15}=p_{34}=1$. Introduce positive minors
$y,z$ so that the Plücker equations give

\[
F=\frac{BD+1}{A},qquad
G=\frac{D+y}{A},qquad
By=1+Az.
\]

For fixed $A,B,y,z$, the energy is strictly increasing in $D$. Decreasing $D$
therefore reaches one of $D=1$, $F=1$, or $G=1$. The latter two boundary
graphs contain an adjacent-wedge minimal face and hence have energy at least
$16+4\sqrt2$.

On the $D=1$ branch, decrease $z$ while preserving $By=1+Az$. Then $y$ and
$G$ decrease with $z$, while the remaining coordinates stay fixed. The first
boundary reached is $z=1$, $y=1$, or $G=1$. The $z=1$ graph is the solved
five-cycle face. The other two contain an adjacent-wedge minimal face.
Consequently the separated-wedge minimum is exactly

\[
10+5\varphi.
\]

All three base faces are now controlled. Therefore every positive real
five-row Plücker packet whose squared minors are at least $m$ satisfies

\[
\sum_{|I|=3}p_I^2\ge(10+5\varphi)m.
\]

Equality is the golden packet: five cycle minors at the floor and five
complementary minors larger by the factor $\varphi$.

### Global propagation through all five-row subsets

A 24-by-3 Parseval complement has ${24\choose5}=42504$ five-row subsets. Each
three-minor occurs in ${21\choose2}=210$ of them. Summing the golden five-row
inequality and using Cauchy--Binet gives

\[
210\ge42504(10+5\varphi)m.
\]

Since $42504/210=1012/5$, this simplifies to

\[
m\le\frac{1}{1012(2+\varphi)}.
\]

Naimark deletion duality now yields

\[
\max_{|S|=3}\kappa(A_{S^c})
\ge\left[1012(2+\varphi)\right]^{1/6}
\approx3.92592675.
\]

This is a proved universal lower bound. It is not asserted optimal for the
24-row design problem, and no frame attaining it has been constructed.

### Global equality is combinatorially impossible

Equality in the summed bound would force equality on every five-row subset.
Each five-set would therefore contain exactly five triples whose squared minor
equals the global floor. Let $x$ be the resulting zero-one function on the
2024 triples. The equality condition is

\[
W_{3,5}x=5\mathbf1,
\]

where $W_{3,5}$ sums a triple function over each five-set.

The Gram matrix $W_{3,5}^*W_{3,5}$ has four eigenvalues

\[
2100,qquad1140,qquad513,qquad153
\]

with multiplicities

\[
1,qquad23,qquad252,qquad1748.
\]

All are positive and their multiplicities sum to 2024, so $W_{3,5}$ has full
column rank. The constant equation has the unique real solution

\[
x\equiv\frac{5}{{5\choose3}}=\frac12.
\]

That is not a zero-one indicator. Hence no 24-row complement can make every
five-subset golden simultaneously, and the universal condition obstruction is
strict:

\[
\max_{|S|=3}\kappa(A_{S^c})
>\left[1012(2+\varphi)\right]^{1/6}.
\]

Full rank proves strictness but supplies no numerical gap above the displayed
constant. The optimal 24-row frame remains unconstructed.
