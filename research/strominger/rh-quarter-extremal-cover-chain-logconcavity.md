# The extremal chain is log-concave but lacks one-neighbor dominance

## Bold conjecture

The two-cover chain has a log-concave magnitude peak,

\[
d^2\geq p_1p_2,
\]

and one adjacent positive term alone dominates the demand.

## Falsification

Log-concavity survives with exact positive residual

\[
d^2-p_1p_2=
59486303007448491506711069312441927942369098598889975658496982585612823971222573482048778.
\]

The supplementary boundary conjecture fails:

\[
\max(p_1,p_2)-d=
-25102800023493565769719665866243754696097398.
\]

Thus curvature is correctly typed as log-concave, but neither neighbor individually covers the demand. Log-concavity alone cannot imply the additive Hall inequality.

## Surviving conjecture

The positivity is irreducibly collective at this node. Test whether the exact surplus \(p_1+p_2-d\) is a two-branch determinant-condensation identity involving the adjacent complementary minors. This differs from the rejected scalar curvature criteria: it permits cross terms and additive branching.

## Disposition

Retain chain log-concavity as finite structure; reject one-neighbor boundary dominance and every claimed scalar-curvature proof of Hall positivity.
