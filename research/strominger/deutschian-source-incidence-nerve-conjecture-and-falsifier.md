# Deutschian source-incidence nerve conjecture and falsifier

## Conjecture

The strongest natural claim suggested by the incidence classifier is:

> A source-authorized atomic theory, together with the arity, variance,
> independence, and local incidence of its constructors, uniquely determines
> the minimal higher nerve in which their coherence must be tested.

This would be explanatory because it predicts the next boundary before its
residual is computed.  It forbids choosing globular, simplicial, or cubical
geometry merely because one presentation makes the desired filler easier.

The conjecture predicts that two systems with the same atomic constructors and
the same local incidence must generate equivalent next horns.

## Minimal falsifier

Take four states and four authorized boundary arrows forming a directed
square.  This fixed one-skeleton admits at least four global attachments:

1. no two-cell;
2. one cubical square filler;
3. a diagonal from vertex zero to vertex two and two triangular fillers;
4. the opposite diagonal and two triangular fillers.

Every attachment preserves the same boundary states, arrows, arities, and
local source/target incidence.  Nevertheless:

- the unfilled square retains a one-dimensional cycle;
- the filled square kills that cycle with one cubical cell;
- either triangulation kills it using two simplicial cells;
- the two triangulations authorize different diagonal comparisons.

Thus local constructor incidence does not determine whether a global filler
exists, which filler shape is admitted, or which internal comparison becomes
executable.

## Disposition

The strong uniqueness conjecture is false.

The missing datum is an independently typed global attachment grammar.  It
must declare which boundaries may be filled, the shape of the filler, and the
authority for any new internal face.  A compiler cannot infer this from local
arrows or endpoint commutativity.

This reproduces the source-theory separation:

- atomic theory supplies local constructors;
- attachment data supplies global cells;
- completion data controls admitted limiting objects;
- presentation invariance compares authorized realizations without creating
  an attachment.

## Surviving Deutschian conjecture

> A source-authorized atomic theory together with a separately authorized
> global attachment grammar determines a typed pasting scheme up to declared
> changes of presentation.  The next explanatory obligation is the first
> boundary in that scheme whose filler is neither constructed nor excluded by
> a source-derived obstruction theorem.

This surviving version remains falsifiable.  It fails if two inequivalent
pasting schemes satisfy the same complete atomic and attachment contracts but
predict different admitted probes, or if the purported presentation
equivalence fails to preserve those probes.

## Consequence for the RH packet

Three independent directions make a cube a candidate, not an automatic fact.
The source must separately authorize all six square attachments and their
common cube boundary.  Pairwise commuting endpoint bytes do not construct
those faces.  A two-route comparison can detect a residual but cannot supply
the missing attachment authority.
