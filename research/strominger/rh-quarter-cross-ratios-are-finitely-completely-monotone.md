# Quarter cross ratios are finitely completely monotone

## Question

Do higher degree differences exhibit a structure stronger than ordinary monotonicity?

## Claim boundary

The test covers difference orders zero through six, degrees two through thirty-two, and shifts zero through twelve. It does not prove complete monotonicity at all orders or construct a representing measure.

## Disposition

Every exact signed difference satisfies

\[
(-1)^k\Delta^k\Theta_{n,a}>0
\]

for all tested orders, degrees, and shifts. All five gates pass. This finite complete-monotonicity pattern is consistent with a Hausdorff-moment representation, which would imply monotonicity and furnish integral barriers, but finite differences alone do not construct that representation. The next leaf is `quarter-cross-ratio-hankel-moment-test`, testing exact Hankel and shifted-Hankel minors required by a positive moment sequence.
