# Quarter amplitude curvature survives through shift twelve

## Problem

The recognized curvature \(5/12\) had only been tested on amplitudes through shift six.

## Bold conjecture

The same quadratic curvature persists through shift twelve without retuning the residual gate.

## Named rivals

The rivals are a small-shift coincidence, residual growth with shift, and loss of cutoff improvement at larger shifts.

## Risky consequences

Using exact recurrence fits through degree forty-eight, all second differences available from shifts zero through twelve must remain positive, and the latest maximum residual must stay below \(0.002\) while improving from the early window.

## Strongest falsification attempt

The shift range was doubled without changing the fit order or gate. The maximum residual is \(0.007781\) on the early degree-ten window but falls to \(0.001181\) on the degree-thirty-four window. Every tested curvature is positive, including those based on shifts ten through twelve. All six gates passed.

## Disposition

Retain \(5/12\) as the finite quadratic-curvature candidate through shift twelve. The larger-shift data strengthen the Y-system explanation of the affine slope but still do not prove uniformity. The next leaf is `quarter-affine-law-shift-twelve-stress-test`, applying the unchanged normalized-correction formula and residual gate to the enlarged shift range.

## Claim boundary

The stress test is finite in degree and shift. No exact base amplitude, linear amplitude coefficient, or uniform remainder is established.
