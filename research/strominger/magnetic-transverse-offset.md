# The offset deformation has one transverse exceptional point

Replace the rigid source coefficient `(4-a) rising j` by
`(h-a) rising j`, while retaining the reflected lattice construction.  The
preferred-chart divisor moves coherently to

\[
q=2g+2h,
\qquad
a_{\max}=g+2h.
\]

At this locus the two endpoint observations on rows `0,1` have the common
ratio

\[
\frac{R_1}{R_0}
=\frac{3g+2h-1}{2g+2h-1}.
\]

Thus chart failure persists throughout the deformation family; it is not
special to offset four.

The repair mechanism persists as well.  Row `3` sees uniquely the
penultimate plus column

\[
(a,m)=(g+2h-2,3)_+,
\]

whose diagonal pivot is `-3(g+2h-2) rising g`.  The same one-pivot Schur
calculation gives

\[
\boxed{
\tau_{g,h}=
-\frac{g\,Q_h(g)(2g+2h)!}
{6(2g+2h-3)(2g+2h-1)(g+2h-1)!},
}
\]

where

\[
Q_h(g)=g^2+(2h-9)g-10h+14.
\]

Solving `Q_h(g)=0` for the offset gives

\[
h=\frac{4-g}{2}+\frac{3}{g-5}.
\]

For positive integral `h` and even `g>=2`, this has the unique solution

\[
\boxed{(h,g)=(2,6).}
\]

Hence locality and the one-pivot architecture do not uniquely select `h=4`.
They survive throughout the offset family.  What distinguishes the rigid
value is twofold:

1. weighted-diamond confluence together with the fixed weight origin forces
   `h=4` independently;
2. that forced value avoids the unique positive-integral transverse
   degeneration, whereas the neighboring value `h=2` hits it at grade six.

The counterfactual therefore strengthens the explanation without circularly
selecting the physical value from successful output alone.  Confluence fixes
the engine; transverse classification shows the consequence of varying it.
