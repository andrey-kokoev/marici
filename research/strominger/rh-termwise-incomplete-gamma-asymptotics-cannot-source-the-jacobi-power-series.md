# Termwise incomplete-gamma asymptotics cannot source the Jacobi power series

## Question

Can the observed inverse-degree recurrence expansion be derived by inserting the truncated moment formula term by term?

For \(m=4r+3\) and \(z=2X^{1/4}\), the truncated/full moment ratio is

\[
R_r(X)=e^{-z}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{z^k}{k!}.
\]

Its deficit is the Poisson tail

\[
1-R_r(X)=e^{-z}\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}\frac{z^k}{k!}.
\]

For fixed \(z\) and sufficiently large \(m\), the ratio test gives

\[
1-R_r(X)
\leq
\frac{e^{-z}z^{m+1}}{(m+1)!}
\frac{1}{1-z/(m+2)}.
\]

This is smaller than \(r^{-M}\) for every fixed \(M\). Consequently the termwise moment ratio has the zero Poincare series:

\[
R_r(X)\sim1+0/r+0/r^2+\cdots.
\]

## Obstruction

The nonzero inverse-degree behavior of Jacobi coefficients therefore cannot arise by applying a regular termwise expansion to individual moments. It is generated by the singular large-size operation of forming and inverting Hankel matrices whose condition deteriorates with degree. The limits “moment order large” and “Hankel size large” do not commute.

## Disposition

Reject termwise incomplete-gamma expansion as the source of the recurrence coefficients. Retain `hankel-determinant-truncation-asymptotic`: derive the correction at the determinant or Riemann--Hilbert level, where the growing matrix size is present before asymptotic expansion.

## Claim boundary

This does not reject an integer inverse-degree Jacobi expansion. It identifies the level at which such an expansion must be derived.
