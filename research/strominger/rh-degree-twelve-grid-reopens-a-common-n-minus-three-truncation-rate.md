# The degree-twelve grid reopens a common n-minus-three truncation rate

## Question

Does the diagonal relative perturbation settle near the short-grid exponent \(2.72\)?

A 220-versus-340-digit computation at \(q=12\) extends the exact incomplete-gamma Gram--Schmidt grid to degree twelve. Fits on successively later windows give diagonal slopes

\[
-2.7703\quad(4\!:\!12),\qquad
-2.8131\quad(6\!:\!12),\qquad
-2.8385\quad(8\!:\!12).
\]

They drift toward \(-3\), rather than stabilizing near \(-8/3\). The corresponding off-diagonal slopes are \(-3.0094,-3.0038,-3.0015\). At degree twelve the relative errors are \(5.16\times10^{-5}\) and \(4.53\times10^{-5}\).

## Disposition

Reject the short-window suggestion that the diagonal exponent has already settled near \(2.72\). Reopen a common \(n^{-3}\) limit with slower diagonal corrections as the leading conjecture, without promoting it to theorem.

The next leaf is `diagonal-correction-to-scaling`: test whether the diagonal data fit \(C n^{-3}(1+c n^{-\delta})\) and identify a falsifiable correction exponent.

## Claim boundary

Window drift is finite numerical evidence. It neither proves the limiting exponent nor supplies the uniform remainder needed for Jacobi cocycle comparison.
