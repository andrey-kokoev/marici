# Reflected prime-index lattice transversality theorem

## Setup

Let

\[
\Lambda=\mathbb Z^2
\]

and let \(L\subset\Lambda\) be a sublattice of prime index \(p\). Let

\[
X=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}
\]

act on \(\Lambda\). Write

\[
\overline L=L/p\Lambda
\subset
\Lambda/p\Lambda\cong\mathbb F_p^2.
\]

Since \(L\) has index \(p\), \(\overline L\) is a line.

## Dichotomy

Exactly one of two cases occurs.

### Invariant chart

If

\[
X\overline L=\overline L,
\]

then \(XL=L\). Reflection descends directly to the one-chart discriminant

\[
\Lambda/L\cong\mathbb Z/p.
\]

No \(p^2\)-class refinement is forced.

### Transverse chart pair

If

\[
X\overline L\ne\overline L,
\]

then the two distinct lines span (mathbb F_p^2) and intersect only at zero.
Consequently

\[
L+XL=\Lambda,
\qquad
L\cap XL=p\Lambda.
\]

The canonical reflection-stable refinement is therefore

\[
\widehat D_L
=\Lambda/(L\cap XL)
\cong(\mathbb Z/p)^2.
\]

It has \(p^2\) raw classes. For odd \(p\), exchange splits it into symmetric
and antisymmetric lines, each containing \(p\) classes.

## Exact categorical square

Intersection and sum form the lattice pullback and pushout:

```text
L intersect XL  --->  XL
      |                 |
      v                 v
      L       --->    L + XL
```

The associated Mayer--Vietoris sequence is

\[
0\longrightarrow
\Lambda/(L\cap XL)
\xrightarrow{\delta}
\Lambda/L\oplus\Lambda/XL
\longrightarrow
\Lambda/(L+XL)
\longrightarrow0,
\]

where

\[
\delta([v])=([v]_L,[v]_{XL}).
\]

In the transverse case \(L+XL=\Lambda\), so the last term vanishes and

\[
\widehat D_L
\cong
\Lambda/L\oplus\Lambda/XL.
\]

Reflection exchanges these two chart discriminants.  The stable packet is
therefore not an externally doubled coefficient group; it is the exact descent
object of the reflected chart cover.

## Application to the magnetic frame

For

\[
F=\begin{pmatrix}2&7\\3&7\end{pmatrix},
\qquad L=\operatorname{im}F,
\]

the mod-seven line is generated by ((2,3)), while its reflection is generated
by ((3,2)).  They are distinct.  The transverse case applies:

\[
L\cap XL=7\mathbb Z^2,
\qquad
L+XL=\mathbb Z^2,
\]

and

\[
\widehat D_L\cong(\mathbb Z/7)^2.
\]

The 49 raw classes and seven antisymmetric classes follow from one theorem.

## Corrected prediction after hostile testing

Whenever a source-derived index-\(p\) presentation is moved by an involution to
a distinct mod-\(p\) chart, the minimal jointly faithful common refinement has
\(p^2\) classes and its two parity sectors have \(p\) classes each. Conversely,
if the mod-\(p\) chart is invariant, observing \(p^2\) jointly faithful classes
requires additional structure and cannot be attributed to reflected-chart
refinement alone.

The qualifier "jointly faithful" is essential. A selected relative target can
factor directly through the order-\(p\) quotient defined by

\[
q_-=\ell-\ell\circ X.
\]

Thus inequality of the chart lines proves forced squaring only for a contract
that requires reconstruction of both chart observations. It does not prove
that every adequate single-parity carrier has \(p^2\) states. The direct
relative quotient is documented in
`direct-relative-quotient-falsifies-forced-p-squared-carrier.md`.

## Boundary at \(p=2\)

For \(p=2\), the transverse lattice statement remains valid, but the symmetric
and antisymmetric characters coincide because \(1=-1\). A two-class relative
sector is not selected by eigenspace splitting.  The odd-prime hypothesis is
therefore essential only for the final parity decomposition, not for the
pullback--pushout theorem.

## Remaining authority boundary

The theorem constructs an equivariant finite coefficient packet.  It does not
construct an executable observation of that packet in the characteristic-zero
magnetic carrier.  The canonical linking pairing supplies a dual phase-valued
candidate, but physical coupling authority remains separate.
