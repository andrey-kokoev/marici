# Atomicity is the unit stratum of an additive charge

## Shift in viewpoint

The previous result said that primitive atomicity is invariant under presentation but fails under composition. The missing composition law is now explicit.

For the nilpotent response family,

\[
E(a)=I+aN,
\qquad
N^2=0,
\]

composition is

\[
E(a)E(b)=E(a+b).
\]

Thus every local response carries an integral charge

\[
q(E(a))=a,
\qquad
q(fg)=q(f)+q(g).
\]

Atomicity is not itself the compositional datum. It is the predicate

\[
|q(f)|=1.
\]

The same law appears in Fox calculus. For one generator,

\[
\partial_x(uv)=\partial_xu+u\partial_xv.
\]

For powers, the effective exponent is additive, and the Fox derivative is a group-ring unit exactly on charges \(\pm1\).

## Categorical consequence

Atomic morphisms do not form a subcategory.

Two atomic morphisms may compose to:

- charge \(2\), producing a nonprimitive repetition;
- charge \(0\), producing cancellation to the identity.

Conversely, non-atomic factors may compose to an atomic effective morphism, for example

\[
-3+2=-1.
\]

Therefore neither rule is valid:

- atomic factors imply an atomic composite;
- a non-atomic factor prevents an atomic composite.

The correct functorial object is the charge. Atomicity is a non-compositional stratum obtained by pulling back the unit locus \(\{-1,1\}\subset\mathbb Z\).

## Compiler law

A constructor compiler must carry the effective charge through composition and evaluate atomicity only afterward:

\[
(f,g)
\longmapsto
q(f)+q(g)
\longmapsto
\bigl|q(f)+q(g)\bigr|=1.
\]

A Boolean atomic certificate may be retained as evidence about an individual node, but it has no authority at the composite node.

This is stricter than the earlier anti-laundering rule. It also detects the reverse mistake: rejecting a composite merely because one or both factors were non-atomic.

## Relation to the square law

The response-side primitive-transvection condition is now understood as a unit-charge condition. The word-side Fox-unit condition is its cocycle analogue. The remaining anti-invariant endpoint condition selects the allowed polarity sector.

So the square stratum is not generated by a subcategory of atomic maps. It is the intersection of:

1. a unit locus of an additive response charge;
2. a unit locus of a Fox cocycle;
3. an anti-invariant endpoint sector.

This predicts that any extension to longer factorizations should compute effective charges and cocycles before applying the square criterion.

## Evidence

The checker exhausts all 49 ordered charge pairs in \([-3,3]^2\). All six gates pass:

- exact response additivity;
- exact Fox cocycle composition;
- atomic-to-nonatomic composition;
- non-atomic-to-atomic composition;
- cancellation of opposite atomic charges;
- exact identification of atomicity with the effective unit locus.
