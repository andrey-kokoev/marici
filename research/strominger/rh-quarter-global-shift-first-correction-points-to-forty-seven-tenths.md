# The quarter global-shift first correction points to forty-seven tenths

## Question

How is the first correction to \(R_m\) related to the cross-ratio correction?

Write

\[
\Theta_n=rac{13}{60n^2}
\left(1+\frac{u}{n}+O(n^{-2})\right).
\]

Since

\[
R_{n-1}=\Theta_n\frac{q_{n-1}}{q_0},
\qquad
q_{n-1}=n^4\left(1+\frac{3}{2n}+O(n^{-2})\right),
\]

conversion from \(n\) to \(m=n-1\) gives

\[
R_m=rac{104}{1575}m^2
\left(1+\frac{a}{m}+O(m^{-2})\right),
\qquad a=u+\frac72.
\]

A fixed-limit fit over degrees ten through twenty-four gives \(u=1.2114112\), hence

\[
a=4.7114112,
\qquad
\beta=3-a=-1.7114112.
\]

These values point respectively to the rational candidates

\[
u=\frac65,
\qquad a=\frac{47}{10},
\qquad \beta=-\frac{17}{10}.
\]

## Disposition

Select \(a=47/10\) as the finite first-correction candidate. The next leaf is `quarter-cross-first-correction-six-fifths-proof`: derive \(u=6/5\) from the next-order condensation balance.

## Claim boundary

All three rational values are finite recognitions conditional on \(\theta_2=13/60\). The exact relations \(a=u+7/2\) and \(\beta=3-a\) do not prove those candidate values.
