# Quarter common-shift amplitudes are nonconstant

## Problem

A Barnes divisor must be inferred from the parameter dependence of the common-shift curvature amplitude, not from the single value at shift zero.

## Bold conjecture

The staircase determinant has a nontrivial common-shift constant whose second finite difference produces a shift-dependent amplitude \(C(t)\).

## Named rivals

The rivals are a shift-independent amplitude absorbed into bulk normalization, a finite-grid artifact caused by uncontrolled inverse-degree corrections, and a non-Barnes parameter dependence.

## Risky consequences

Independent fits at integer shifts must remain positive, recover \(C(0)\) near \(104/1575\), and give mutually distinct amplitudes rather than one constant within the fitted model.

## Strongest falsification attempt

Exact determinants for degrees fourteen through twenty-four were fitted to

\[
\frac{R_m(t)}{m^2}=C(t)+\frac{A(t)}m+\frac{B(t)}{m^2}
\]

at shifts zero through five. All fits were positive and distinct. They decreased from \(0.0660754\) to \(0.00468727\); the shift-zero discrepancy from \(104/1575\) was \(4.37\times10^{-5}\). The exact residual is that no parameter-uniform remainder bound is available, so these fitted constants cannot define a Barnes divisor.

## Disposition

The shift-independent rival is rejected within this finite model. The nontrivial-shift conjecture survives but is not verified. Defer divisor extraction at the first missing object: a parameter-uniform determinant asymptotic with an explicit remainder sufficient to identify \(C(t)\). Its acceptance test is stable recovery of the same \(C(t)\) under an enlarged degree window. Reallocate to the executable rival `quarter-common-shift-amplitude-large-t-model`.

## Claim boundary

Finite extrapolations do not establish exact amplitudes, rationality, a Barnes representation, or the shift equation.
