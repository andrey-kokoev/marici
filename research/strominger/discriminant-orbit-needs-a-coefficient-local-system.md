# The discriminant-orbit constructor needs a coefficient local system

## Shift in perspective

The underlying abelian group

\[
\mathcal A_F(O)=\operatorname{coker}(F)\otimes\widetilde{\mathbb Z}[O]
\]

does combine the affine discriminant and the relative two-route coordinate.
It does not by itself specify how reflection acts on the coefficient group.
Consequently it is an associated object, but not yet a complete equivariant
source constructor.

## The missing datum

Let

\[
D_F=\operatorname{coker}(F),
\qquad O=\{+,-\},
\]

and let reflection exchange the two points of (O).  The reduced orbit module
is generated by (e=(1,-1)), with

\[
\tau e=-e.
\]

An equivariant coefficient system additionally requires an involution

\[
\alpha:D_F\longrightarrow D_F,
\qquad \alpha^2=1.
\]

The diagonal reflection law on the combined object is then

\[
\tau(x\otimes e)=-\alpha(x)\otimes e.
\]

This action is not determined by the determinant of (F), the Smith normal
form of (F), or the free orbit incidence.

## Modulus seven

For the magnetic frame

\[
F=\begin{pmatrix}2&7\\3&7\end{pmatrix},
\qquad D_F\cong\mathbb Z/7,
\]

there are at least two canonical-looking coefficient involutions:

\[
\alpha_+(x)=x,
\qquad
\alpha_-(x)=-x.
\]

They give opposite descent outcomes.

For trivial coefficient parity, 

\[
\tau(x\otimes e)=-x\otimes e.
\]

Because two is invertible modulo seven, both the invariant and coinvariant
descents vanish:

\[
\mathcal A_F(O)^{C_2}=0,
\qquad
\mathcal A_F(O)_{C_2}=0.
\]

For sign coefficient parity,

\[
\tau(x\otimes e)=x\otimes e,
\]

so both descents retain the full discriminant group:

\[
\mathcal A_F(O)^{C_2}\cong\mathbb Z/7,
\qquad
\mathcal A_F(O)_{C_2}\cong\mathbb Z/7.
\]

Thus the anti-diagonal route geometry and the modulus-seven arithmetic do not
alone decide whether a seven-valued observable survives reflection descent.
Their relative parity does.

## General odd cyclic theorem

Let (D\cong\mathbb Z/n) with (n) odd.  On the reduced free (C_2)-orbit,
the two coefficient parities satisfy

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
\alpha & \text{combined action} & \text{invariants} & \text{coinvariants}\\
\hline
+1 & -1 & 0 & 0\\
-1 & +1 & D & D
\end{array}
\]

For even (n), two-torsion survives in the first row, so the clean
all-or-nothing statement is specifically odd-primary.

## Hostile falsifier

Hold fixed all previously used data:

- the same affine frame (F);
- the same discriminant group (D_F\cong\mathbb Z/7);
- the same free reflection orbit;
- the same reduced anti-diagonal generator.

Change only the coefficient involution from (alpha_+) to (alpha_-).  The
descended object changes from zero to (mathbb Z/7).  Therefore no theorem
using only frame determinant and orbit incidence can predict the physical
seven-valued descent.

## Corrected source constructor

The minimal constructor is not the bare tensor product but the oriented local
system packet

```text
EquivariantAffineDiscriminantOrbit
  affine_frame
  discriminant_group
  reflection_orbit
  reduced_orbit_orientation
  coefficient_involution
  diagonal_action
  selected_descent_variance
  source_authority
```

Its decisive invariant is the relative character

\[
\chi_{\mathrm{rel}}=\chi_{D_F}\chi_O.
\]

The modulus-seven relational channel survives exactly when this relative
character is trivial.  The missing magnetic theorem is therefore sharper
than the earlier phase-port question: first derive the reflection action on
the affine discriminant coefficient.  Only then is there a well-typed object
for a phase port to observe.
