# Primitive Transvection Is the Local Law Behind the Fox Square

The Fox-cancellation theorem is not forced by the previously named global
principles alone.

Consider the integral local response family

\[
E_i(a)=I+aN_i,
\qquad N_i^2=0.
\]

For every nonzero integral \(a\), these blocks are unimodular, fix the
translation vector, possess exact inverse \(E_i(-a)\), and respect endpoint
reflection at the elementary-block level.  Changing \(a\) does not alter the
abstract Moore deletion word or its primitive anti-invariant Fox tails.

Nevertheless, the cyclic twice-square law holds exactly for

\[
|a|=1.
\]

These are precisely the primitive transvections:

\[
\gcd(E_i(a)-I)=1.
\]

For \(|a|=2,3\), every previously named global condition survives while the
square fails.

Thus the local constructor theorem has two independent atomicity requirements:

1. each tail word has a unit Fox derivative;
2. each elementary response is a primitive transvection.

The source-coherent diagonal cancellation is forced only on their intersection.
This supplies the Deutschean failure condition: replace a primitive local
response by a nonprimitive multiple without changing the abstract word.  The
cycle remains legal, but the conductor-square explanation ceases to apply.
