# Beta-dependent two-wedge conjecture

Retain every consecutive pole depth and replace the native source coefficient
\(4-a\) by \(\beta-a\). For \(\beta\ge2\), exact bounded evidence predicts

\[
W_{g,q}^{(\beta)}=2
\]

exactly when either

\[
q\ge2g+\beta-3
\]

or

\[
g\ge4,\qquad \min(\beta-1,g-3)\le q\le g-3.
\]

Otherwise \(W_{g,q}^{(\beta)}=1\). The law passed all 945 exact rational-rank
cases with \(2\le\beta\le8\), \(2\le g\le10\), \(1\le q\le15\), and
consecutive cutoff \(N=20\).

The high boundary is

\[
2g+\beta-3=(\beta-1)+2(g-1).
\]

This decomposes it into source-atom displacement \(\beta-1\) plus reflected
local sewing reach \(2(g-1)\). The low wedge is a collision face. Its
small-grade endpoint saturates at \(q=g-3\), so it cannot yet be identified
with source displacement alone.

The degenerate tests separate the mechanisms. At \(\beta=1\), the high face
survives at \(q=2g-2\) but the low wedge disappears. At \(\beta=0\), the
zero-depth source atom vanishes. Hence overlap degeneracy requires two distinct
source atoms, whereas high support separation does not.

The type is essential: imposing the old even-depth lattice falsifies the same
formula in all 945 audited cases, often with quotient width greater than two.
Thus the candidate invariant belongs to

\[
(\text{source polynomial},\ \text{admitted depth lattice}),
\]

not to either datum separately. Counterfactual \(\beta\ne4\) remains a
diagnostic deformation, not an asserted magnetic sector.

The proof target is a two-endpoint presentation whose Fitting ideal separates
an overlap factor from a support-separation factor and proves that no third
factor occurs.
