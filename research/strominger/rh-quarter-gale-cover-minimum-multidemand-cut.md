# The minimum Hasse Hall cut is genuinely multi-demand

## Bold conjecture

After all scalar and three-term explanations fail, the minimum nonempty Hasse Hall cut is attained by a branching demand set rather than a singleton.

## Falsification attempt

An exact forced-mincut census evaluates 54,261 forced cuts across all 3,584 terminal cases. The conjecture survives. The global minimum has demand-set size two:

\[
\{0,2,3,4,5,7\},\qquad \{0,2,3,5,6,7\},
\]

for base \(\{1,2,3,5,6\}\) and \((i,j)=(7,0)\). Its exact positive slack is

This agrees with the earlier all-comparability minimum, so long jumps do not improve the extremal cut.

## Surviving conjecture

The irreducible finite mechanism is a two-demand branching Hasse neighborhood. Extract its complete positive-neighbor graph and test whether it is the minimal \(K_{2,3}\)-type branching pattern suggested by the earlier three-supply/two-demand identity. Failure would require a larger topology; survival would isolate the precise local network cell for an all-order proof.

## Disposition

The multi-demand network conjecture survives at fixed \(k=8\); no all-order promotion follows.
