# Quarter-source lexicographic monotone transport

## Question

Does the source-derived lexicographic order turn the exact weighted Hall transport into a canonical noncrossing monotone coupling?

## Claim boundary

The exact northwest-corner coupling uses source-minor masses in lexicographic source-index order and tests each used pair against interlacing. Positive capacity exceeds negative demand in all 769 bounded cases, but the canonical monotone coupling is admissible in only 217. The first forbidden transport occurs for base `(1,2)`, endpoints `i=3`, `j=4`, from negative index `(0,4,5)` to positive index `(1,2,3)`. This rejects the lexicographic monotone common-network rival, not arbitrary planar orders or source-level networks.

The earlier direct-coupling, sign-gauge, and rectangular-factorization packets are excluded from the governing DPC evidence chain: they elaborate one endpoint residual and do not test source-derived common-network structure.

## Disposition

Reject lexicographic monotone transport as the all-order mechanism. Preserve exact unrestricted interlacing max-flow feasibility as bounded evidence. The surviving scope requires a different source-derived order or a genuinely branching common network whose paths are not forced by monotone coupling.
