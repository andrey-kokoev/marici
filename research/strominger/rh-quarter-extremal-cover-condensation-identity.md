# Canonical two-branch condensation does not equal the singleton surplus

## Bold conjecture

The collective surplus is a unit-coefficient combination of the three adjacent \(2\times2\) cross-condensation determinants formed from the corresponding \(A\)- and \(B\)-minor factors.

## Falsification

The checker evaluates all 26 nonzero coefficient choices in \(\{-1,0,1\}^3\). None equals the exact Hall surplus. All three canonical cross determinants are nonzero, but no direct unit-coefficient condensation identity exists.

This rejects the narrow canonical condensation route. It does not exclude an identity with additional minors or source-derived nonunit coefficients.

## Surviving conjecture

The finite positivity is fundamentally a weighted network-cut property rather than a scalar or three-term determinant identity. The next falsifier must move from singleton structure to the minimum multi-demand Hasse cut: compute the exact nonempty cut slack and its demand/neighbor shape. If the extremal cut is genuinely multi-demand, it identifies the branching configuration an all-order MTP2/Hall theorem must control.

## Disposition

Small coefficients, AM–GM, one-neighbor dominance, and canonical two-branch condensation are all rejected. Preserve only the exact singleton surplus, chain log-concavity, and network interpretation.
