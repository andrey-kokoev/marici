# Reflection does not presently descend to the affine discriminant frame

## Question

Can the physical two-sheet reflection determine the coefficient involution on

\[
D_F=\operatorname{coker}F,
\qquad
F=\begin{pmatrix}2&7\\3&7\end{pmatrix}?
\]

## Descent criterion

An automorphism (U) of the target lattice induces an automorphism of
(D_F) only when it preserves the image lattice:

\[
U(\operatorname{im}F)=\operatorname{im}F.
\]

Equivalently, there must be an integral unimodular matrix (V) satisfying

\[
UF=FV.
\]

Over the rationals the only possible comparison is

\[
V=F^{-1}UF.
\]

Thus integrality of this matrix is the exact lift gate.

## The tempting exchange fails

The completed parity packet has a free two-point orbit on which physical
reflection has exchange matrix

\[
X=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

If one identifies the two affine-frame target coordinates with that physical
orbit, then

\[
F^{-1}XF
=
\begin{pmatrix}
-1&0\\
5/7&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

This is not integral.  Equivalently, modulo seven the image of (F) is the
line generated by ((2,3)), while exchange sends it to the different line
generated by ((3,2)).  Therefore (X) does not preserve
(operatorname{im}F) and induces no action on (D_F).

The same failure holds for (-X).

## The surviving signed symmetries do not select a parity

Among the four signed coordinate permutations

\[
I,-I,X,-X,
\]

only (I) and (-I) preserve the affine image lattice.  They induce the two
opposite automorphisms

\[
[x]\longmapsto[x],
\qquad
[x]\longmapsto-[x]
\]

on (D_F\cong\mathbb Z/7).

Hence the affine frame itself permits both coefficient parities and rejects
the physical exchange candidate.  It does not select the local system needed
by reflection descent.

## Application of Aspect's theorem

Aspect's marked-germ theorem said that coefficient parity must be retained and
mated with orbit orientation before descent.  The lift calculation now shows
that the required marked germ has not yet been constructed: the known
physical reflection does not act on the affine discriminant presentation.

The first unavailable arrow is therefore earlier than previously stated:

```text
physical reflection orbit
        -/-> affine discriminant coefficient system
```

There is no legitimate relative-parity mate until a source-derived crossing
supplies this arrow.

## Corrected status of the proposed single source

The object

\[
D_F\otimes\widetilde{\mathbb Z}[O]
\]

remains an exact associated abelian group.  It is not presently an
equivariant source object.  Its two factors come from different typed
presentations:

- (D_F) comes from the adjacent-row affine cocircuit frame;
- (widetilde{\mathbb Z}[O]) comes from the physical label/helicity orbit.

Their tensor product does not authorize identifying their involutions.

## Smallest possible repairs

Any repair must add one of the following source-derived structures:

1. an integral reflection lift ((U,V)) on a larger affine lattice whose
   discriminant quotient contains (D_F);
2. a reflection-stable saturation or overlattice, together with proof that its
   quotient still retains the seven-primary class;
3. a correspondence, rather than an action, from the physical orbit to the
   discriminant duality pairing;
4. a direct calculation showing that the relevant physical involution on the
   cocircuit coordinates is (I) or (-I), not exchange.

The first hostile test for every repair is reduction modulo seven: the chosen
target action must preserve the image line generated by ((2,3)).

## Consequence

The current data do not answer whether the seven-valued channel survives
reflection descent.  More strongly, they show that this question is not yet
well typed.  Before choosing trivial or sign coefficient parity, one must
construct a reflection action on the coefficient object itself.
