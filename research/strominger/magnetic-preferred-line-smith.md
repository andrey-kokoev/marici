# Integral residue of the preferred observation line

On the even chart divisor, only two source columns meet the preferred
observation rows \(R_0,R_1\). Their row pairs are scalar multiples of

\[
v_g=(2g+7,3g+7).
\]

To separate this observation geometry from the large factorial contents of
the two source columns, independently saturate each contributing source ray.
Up to column signs, the resulting boundary matrix is

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
2g+7&2g+7\\
3g+7&3g+7
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its Smith form is

\[
\operatorname{diag}(\gcd(g,7),0).
\]

The zero invariant records the already proved rational cocircuit. The nonzero
invariant records the index of the generated observation line inside its
saturation:

\[
\operatorname{Sat}(\mathbb Z v_g)/\mathbb Z v_g
\cong \mathbb Z/\gcd(g,7).
\]

For even grade this quotient is \(\mathbb Z/7\) exactly when \(14\mid g\),
and is trivial otherwise. Thus the divisor has two layers:

- a rational rank-one presentation failure at every admissible even grade;
- an additional integral presentation residue at grades divisible by \(14\).

This statement is deliberately scoped to the ray-saturated boundary packet.
It does not classify the Smith form of the unsaturated full operator and does
not create a new physical kernel state. Promoting it further requires a
source-derived reason that independent primitive-ray saturation is the
admissible integral constructor.

The authority obstruction is concrete. The maps with column packets
\([v,v]\) and \([7v,14v]\) determine the same pair of saturated rational rays,
but their integral images have different indices. Ray saturation identifies
them and therefore forgets source multiplicity. It cannot be inferred merely
from the desire to expose the small Smith factor. Until the magnetic source
authorizes that quotient, the \(\mathbb Z/7\) class is a projectivized
presentation invariant rather than an executable or physical residue.

## Authority verdict

The current source does not authorize the quotient. In the integral Laurent
engine, the monomial labels are a fixed free basis. A one-generator integral
basis change is invertible only when its multiplier is \(1\) or \(-1\);
division by nonunit column content is not an automorphism. The completed
physical source category is stricter still: it explicitly excludes independent
Laurent monomials and admits only labelled puncture kernels and their
source-derived distributional jets.

The smallest hostile example is

\[
\mathbb Z\longrightarrow\mathbb Z,\qquad 1\longmapsto7.
\]

Its cokernel is \(\mathbb Z/7\). Saturating the image replaces \(7\mathbb Z\)
by \(\mathbb Z\) and kills precisely that cokernel. Thus saturation cannot be
called a harmless normalization while simultaneously using its Smith residue
as information. The projectivized \(\mathbb Z/7\) pattern remains a legitimate
associated presentation grade, but it is not an ordinary magnetic source
class under the current constructor grammar.

## Source-authorized unsaturated Smith form

Keeping the fixed integral source normalization, the two nonzero boundary
columns are

\[
-(4)^{\overline g}v_g,\qquad
-(g+8)^{\overline{g-1}}v_g.
\]

The actual boundary matrix therefore has Smith form

\[
\operatorname{diag}(h_g,0),
\]

where

\[
h_g=
\gcd(g,7)\,
\gcd\!\left((4)^{\overline g},(g+8)^{\overline{g-1}}\right).
\]

Its cokernel is

\[
\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/h_g.
\]

The free summand records the rational observation loss. The torsion summand
records the complete integral coefficient content supplied by the operator.
Unlike the isolated projectivized factor \(\gcd(g,7)\), this full \(h_g\) is
an invariant of the current unsaturated boundary map. It is the arithmetic
statement authorized by the existing source grammar.

Prime by prime, this becomes

\[
v_p(h_g)=
\min\!\left(
v_p\!\left(\frac{(g+3)!}{3!}\right),
v_p\!\left(\frac{(2g+6)!}{(g+7)!}\right)
\right)
+\mathbf 1_{\{p=7,\;7\mid g\}}.
\]

The minimum is the overlap of the two source coefficient intervals. The final
indicator is the cocircuit contribution. Thus grades divisible by \(14\) do
carry one additional \(7\)-adic factor, but it sits on top of—not in place
of—the source-derived factorial torsion.
