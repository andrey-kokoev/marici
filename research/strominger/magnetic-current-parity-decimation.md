# The magnetic current tower is created by parity decimation

The native pole-depth set is the even lattice

\[
A_k=\{0,2,\ldots,2k\}.
\]

As a hostile comparison, enlarge it to the consecutive lattice

\[
\widetilde A_N=\{0,1,ldots,N\},
\]

while leaving the path formula and source coefficient \(4-a\) unchanged.
This is not asserted to be an authorized magnetic sector.

The quotient generated by inter-component currents changes dramatically.
Across the tested native parameters, the consecutive-depth quotient has
dimension only one or two. The even-depth quotient can be much larger and
grows one class per admitted depth before reaching its known finite
saturation.

Thus the many current classes are not independent in the undeleted
integer-depth complex. Odd depths supply relations sewing currents at
neighboring even depths. Restriction to the even sublattice removes the
mediating objects and exposes their unresolved differences.

## Structural interpretation

This is a decimation phenomenon:

\[
\text{neighboring-depth current relations}
\longrightarrow
\text{delete one parity}
\longrightarrow
\text{alias-current tower}.
\]

The tower is still genuine in the native magnetic source because odd pole
depths are not admitted constructors. But its multiplicity is explained by
the source type restriction, rather than by unrelated new physics at every
even depth.

The one-dimensional zero-depth cokernel class survives the enlargement. A
second residual class appears on collision loci. Hence two structures should
be separated:

- the intrinsic inter-component current already detected by the two-row
  functional;
- additional alias classes caused by deleting the opposite parity.
