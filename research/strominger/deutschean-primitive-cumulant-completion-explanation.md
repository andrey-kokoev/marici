## Status and problem

Status: source-typed explanatory conjecture with an exact finite-jet
variation audit.  All-orders completion remains open.

The background expectation is that positivity observed in finitely many
far-wall coefficients may be accidental and need not survive completion.
The recalcitrant observation is that the primitive fourth-cumulant margin:

- vanishes through normal order two at the memoryless divisor;
- begins with \(4q^3t^3\);

normal-jet relations to persist in the completed section.

## Candidate explanatory mechanism

The resolvent atom is completely monotone in \(t\).  Integer grade \(q\)
forms a \(q\)-fold product of this same atom rather than introducing
independent coefficient data.  The boundary current fixes the characteristic
wall \(c_{\rm wall}=3/2\), while the quarter-density factor supplies the
source normalization needed by the cumulant readout.

\[

\[

> Reuse of one completely monotone resolvent constructor across all grade

> quarter density orient the cumulant quotient.  Consequently the
> \(q-4\) positive cone is preserved contractively by normal-jet transport,

This resolves the problem only if the cumulant readout is proved to preserve
the relevant oriented cone.  Without that bridge, it is a source-motivated

## A rejected all-orders shortcut

After the geometric retyping, the most economical candidate proof was to
represent

\[
f_q(u)=(1+u)^{1/4}
\left(\frac{\log(1+u)}u\right)^q
\]

as a logarithmically completely monotone carrier.  Such a representation
would turn completion into positive-measure transport before the gamma-fiber
integration.  It fails at all orders for every finite \(q\).

Indeed, logarithmic complete monotonicity would require

\[
g_q'(u)=qA(u)-\frac1{4(1+u)}
\]

to be completely monotone, where

\[
A(u)=\frac1u-\frac1{(1+u)\log(1+u)}.
\]

The function \(A\) has the Stieltjes representation

\[
A(u)=\int_1^\infty
\frac{1}{u+s}
\frac{ds}{(s-1)(\log^2(s-1)+\pi^2)}.
\]

Its representing measure is continuous on \((1,\infty)\) and has no atom at
\(s=1\).  In contrast, \((1+u)^{-1}\) is the Stieltjes transform of the unit
atom at \(s=1\).  Consequently no finite multiple \(qA\) can dominate
\((4(1+u))^{-1}\) as a positive representing measure.  Equivalently, for
every finite \(q\), some sufficiently high derivative violates complete
monotonicity.

This is not a counterexample to the requested cone theorem: logarithmic
complete monotonicity of the unreduced carrier was only a proposed sufficient
mechanism.  It does show why a finite census of normal jets cannot be promoted
through that shortcut.  The quarter-density is a boundary atom, whereas the
resolvent curvature approaches the boundary through a continuous spectrum.
The cumulant quotient and the gamma-fiber integration must therefore be used
essentially; proving positivity before those operations asks for a stronger
property that the source does not possess.

Geometrically, the obstruction says that the completed carrier cone is not
generated solely by the closure of its positive interior normal spectrum.
There is an additional boundary-supported generator.  The remaining theorem
must show that the physical readout pairs that boundary generator with the
continuous normal spectrum inside the reserve, not that the generator is
absent.

Ordinary complete monotonicity remains a weaker possible mechanism.  An exact

This makes the repeated grade incidence potentially structural: combining at

## Completion topology: the normal germ is Gevrey, not analytic

The preceding \(I_\Sigma\)-adic description correctly types finite jets but
does not yet type reconstruction of the physical integral.  The fixed-measure
coordinate contains singularities at

\[
t=-\frac1y,
\qquad 0<y<\infty.
\]

They accumulate at \(t=0\) as \(y\to\infty\).  Hence there is no uniform
analytic disc around the memoryless divisor obtained by expanding the
integrand before the gamma-fiber integral.  Treating the inverse limit of
finite jets as an ordinary convergent Taylor germ would therefore smuggle in
a reconstruction map that the source has not supplied.

The derivative estimate already present in the source has the correct
strength:

\[
\left|
\partial_t^n\frac{h_t(y)}y
\right|
\le \frac{n!}{n+1}y^n.
\]

After integration against \(y^q e^{-y}dy\), the extra \(y^n\) produces
\(\Gamma(q+n+1)\).  Thus the natural completed object is a Gevrey normal germ,
not an analytic normal germ.  Logarithms and the finite adjacent-grade
operations preserve this Gevrey class because the zeroth partition function
is nonzero.

Let \(J_{\mathrm{fin}}\) denote the finite normal jets, \(\widehat G\) the
formal Gevrey germ, and \(m_q\) the physical margin.  Accordingly, the
completion arrow required by the explanation must factor as

\[
J_{\mathrm{fin}}
\longrightarrow
\widehat G
\xrightarrow{\mathcal B}
\mathcal B\widehat G
\xrightarrow{\mathcal L_t}
m_q(t).
\]

The earlier fixed ratio target

\[
B_{n+1}\le\Lambda B_n
\]

for every \(n\) is therefore too strong unless a nontrivial weighting is
included.  The topology-compatible ratio is factorially normalized:

\[
\frac{B_{n+1}}{(n+1)B_n}\le\Lambda_{\mathcal B}.
\]

Contractivity at wall parameter \(t\) then means

\[
t\Lambda_{\mathcal B}<1
\]

in the Borel-weighted cone.  The finite bound \(B_{n+1}<37B_n\) through grade
12 remains valid evidence, but it cannot by itself identify the completed
norm.

This also changes the invariant meaning of the reconstruction defect.  The
formal kernel \(K_N=I_\Sigma^{N+1}\widehat{\mathcal O}_\Sigma\) records which
jets were omitted, while the physical defect is

\[

\mathcal L_t\mathcal B(\widehat m)-
\sum_{n=0}^{N}a_nt^n.
\]

Only the latter can be compared with the positivity reserve.  A Watson or
Borel--Laplace remainder theorem is therefore not optional analytic polish;

## Exact nonlinear Gevrey descent through grade 40

The carrier used by the exact checker has the closed source form

\[

\[
\deg_x v_n\leq n+1.
\]

If \(X_q\) is Gamma with shape \(q\), the coefficient of normal grade
\(n\) in the logarithm of the expected exponential is a sum of connected
joint cumulants

\[
\kappa_r\bigl(v_{k_1}(X_q),\ldots,v_{k_r}(X_q)\bigr),
\qquad k_1+\cdots+k_r=n.
\]

For positive integral \(q\), write the Gamma variable as a sum of \(q\)
independent exponential atoms.  Expanding a joint cumulant assigns every
polynomial leg to atom labels.  A term survives connectedization only when
the overlap hypergraph on the \(r\) legs is connected, which requires at
least \(r-1\) identifications among their labels.  It therefore uses at most
the sum of the polynomial degrees minus \(r-1\) independent labels.  Since
Gamma moments are polynomial in \(q\), the resulting bound extends from
positive integers to the polynomial identity in \(q\).  Hence

\[
\deg_q\kappa_r
\leq
\sum_{i=1}^r(k_i+1)-(r-1)
=n+1.
\]

This proves the source degree filtration at every normal grade; it is no
longer an empirical premise of the continuous-\(q\) reconstruction.  The
remaining descent contains two genuinely additional claims.  The adjacent
third-cumulant response must first acquire the universal factor \(q^3\), and
the quotient after removing that factor must lie in the positive
\((q-4)\)-coefficient cone.  Degree counting alone proves neither statement.

A separate exact propagation carries a cubic jet in the grade variable

## The completed cone is sign-regular, not scalar Stieltjes-positive

A stronger hostile asks whether either the factorially normalized oriented
jets or the raw oriented jets are moments of one positive scalar measure.
Neither sequence is.  Their ordinary and shifted Hankel matrices already
have negative minors.  The first test rejects a scalar Stieltjes
representation of the Borel transform; the second rejects complete
monotonicity of that transform via a scalar Laplace measure.  Coefficientwise
positive scalar-measure transport therefore cannot be the missing completion
theorem.

The failures again organize rather than scatter.  For shifted Hankel leading
minors through size eight, every integer \(4\le q\le10\) obeys

\[
\operatorname{sgn}\det H_r
=(-1)^{r(r-1)/2}.
\]

The signature is

\[
+,-,-,+,+,-,-,+.
\]

Thus one preferred leading-minor chart carries a coherent period-four
orientation.  This initially suggested a strictly sign-regular Hankel cone.
A stronger all-chart hostile rejects that promotion.

The unshifted matrix, which retains the universal cubic seed, has exactly
three finite-cutoff mismatches in the tested range:

\[
(q,r)=(4,6),(4,8),(5,8).
\]

The shifted leading blocks have no mismatch.  However, testing every shifted
contiguous Hankel block through size six produces 19 orientation failures
among 1,344 exact minors.  Writing \(s\) for the starting index, the first
occurs at

\[
(q,r,s)=(4,5,18).
\]

These are not finite-precision defects: every determinant is rational.  The
period-four law is therefore a leading-chart regularity, not a global
total-positivity or sign-regularity theorem.  Reversing the observation flag
explains the leading signature because its parity is
\((-1)^{r(r-1)/2}\), but it does not orient every translated chart.

Two tempting stronger completions are now exactly falsified:

- a scalar Stieltjes-positive moment cone;
- a globally sign-regular Hankel cone obtained by reversing one flag.

What survives is the diagonal oriented Gevrey cone: every individual normal
coefficient has the predicted sign through grade 40, its factorially
normalized transport is contractive at the far wall, and oriented summation
keeps the known reconstruction block inside reserve.  An all-orders theorem
must preserve precisely that weaker cone unless the source supplies a richer
non-Hankel atlas.  It may not infer total positivity from the preferred
leading chart.
