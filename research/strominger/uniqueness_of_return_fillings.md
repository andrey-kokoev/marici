# Uniqueness of generated return fillings

Date: 2026-10-28.

## Scope and result

This note continues `period_relation_selection.md`. Its question is uniqueness
of the filling comparison `w => 1` derived from the declared, invertible local
comparisons `rho2: w^2 => 1` and `rho3: w^3 => 1`. Neither of those comparisons
was established as a native Su-32 producer. The uploaded native specification
provides period translations and invertible wire regroupings, explicitly not a
comparison-packet constructor.

Three levels of uniqueness must be kept separate:

- The smallest comparison relation generated by specified rules is unique.
- A signed-use certificate for that relation need not be unique.
- A contractible space of full filling witnesses is stronger than either a
  chosen certificate or one equivalence class of fillings.

For the declared two-rule model, the existence relation is uniquely fixed, but
free filling witnesses are not unique. There is exactly one primitive
second-homotopy ambiguity, up to sign. Adjoining a single higher comparison
with that primitive boundary makes the isolated model contractible. This gives
an explicit sufficient uniqueness theorem and an explicit proof that the two
original rules alone do not imply it.

No arbitrary raw histories are equated in this statement. Dimensions below
are dimensions of paths, fillings and comparisons between fillings, not a new
assignment of the user's dual packet ranks.

## 1. Exact arithmetic of the generated relation

For signed rule counts `(a,b)`, define

    B(a,b) = 2a + 3b.

The generated subgroup is the image of B. Since B(-1,1)=1, it is all of Z.
This is the uniquely smallest subgroup containing 2 and 3. It is a result
relative to the specified rules, not selection of those rules by the native
period formula.

For a fixed return difference n, the certificate set is

    W_n = {(a,b) in Z^2 : 2a+3b=n}.

Every solution is uniquely

    (a,b) = (-n+3k, n-2k),    k in Z.

Proof: let k=a+b. Substituting n=2a+3b gives the displayed formula. Conversely,
the displayed formula has boundary n and a+b=k.

In particular, two certificates for one return are

    A=(-1,1),       B=(2,-1),
    B-A=(3,-2).

They have actual pasting expressions using the declared invertible rules:

    A: w = w*1 --(id_w tensor rho2^-1)--> w^3 --rho3--> 1;
    B: w = w*1 --(id_w tensor rho3^-1)--> w^4
           --(rho2 tensor rho2)--> 1.

Associators and unit comparisons are suppressed in this notation, not
assumed to identify unrelated paths. A strict topological path model can
implement these composites with chosen standard parameterizations.

The kernel is

    ker B = Z z,       z=(3,-2).

The integer coefficient of z is the entire ambiguity at certificate level.
This calculation alone would NOT show that all raw derivations are classified
by coefficients, or that different coefficients give inequivalent native
fillings. The next section supplies a separate geometric test.

## 2. A free witness-preserving geometric test

Declare the following model rather than attribute it to Su-32. Begin with a
circle representing the return w. Attach one 2-cell along w^2 and a second
2-cell along w^3, retaining them as independent witnesses. Attach no new
3-cells. Call the resulting CW complex K:

    K = S^1 union_(degree 2) D^2 union_(degree 3) D^2.

Path composition, inversion, and the usual coherence of homotopies are
available in its fundamental infinity-groupoid. The original return witnesses
remain specified maps of disks, not proof-irrelevant equalities.

The fundamental group is

    pi_1(K) = <w | w^2=1, w^3=1> = 1.

The integral cellular chain complex in positive degrees is

    0 -> Z^2 --[2 3]--> Z -> 0.

Hence

    H_1(K;Z)=0,
    H_2(K;Z)=Z(3,-2).

Since K is simply connected, the Hurewicz theorem identifies pi_2(K) with
H_2(K;Z). In fact K is homotopy equivalent to S^2: choose a sphere map
representing the primitive H_2 class; it induces all homology isomorphisms,
and the simply-connected homology version of Whitehead's theorem makes it
a homotopy equivalence. See Hatcher [H], Theorem 4.32 and Corollary 4.33.

Fix the exact based boundary loop w and one filling A. Homotopy classes of
other disk fillings relative to that boundary form a torsor for pi_2(K).
Gluing a filling to the reversed A gives the sphere difference. Equivalently,
the space of based nullhomotopies of w, once A is selected, has the homotopy
type of Omega^2 K. Its components are therefore indexed by Z.

The sphere made from B and reversed A has cellular coefficient z=(3,-2),
so these two fillings cannot be connected by a homotopy relative to their
boundary. Repeating this sphere difference gives all integer classes.

Thus the lack of uniqueness is not merely a redundant way of writing one
proof. It survives homotopy in a model satisfying the stated two-rule
assumptions. Any theorem deriving witness uniqueness from those assumptions
alone would have to hold in this model, and does not.

The earlier thin comparison-groupoid model removes this distinction by
construction. Its contractibility does not prove contractibility of the free
witness-preserving model.

## 3. The primitive higher comparison

There are two parallel fillings of six returns:

    A6 = rho2 tensor rho2 tensor rho2 : w^6 => 1,
    B6 = rho3 tensor rho3             : w^6 => 1.

Here tensor denotes horizontal pasting of fillings on consecutive return
subpaths. Gluing A6 to the reversed B6 along their common boundary gives a
sphere s:S^2->K with cellular class

    3 e2 - 2 e3 = z.

By the computation above, s represents a generator of pi_2(K).

The required additional comparison is

    Gamma : A6 ==> B6.

A geometric realization of one freely adjoined Gamma is a 3-cell attached
along s. Set

    Y = K union_s D^3.

This does not erase either original disk, either route, or their provenance.
It adds a higher comparison. It does change their homotopy relation.

The positive-dimensional cellular chain complex becomes

    0 -> Z --(3,-2)--> Z^2 --[2 3]--> Z -> 0.

It is exact over the integers. In particular the primitive boundary matters,
not merely a nonzero boundary. An explicit chain contraction is

    s1(n)=(-n,n),
    s2(a,b)=a+b,

with identities

    B s1 = id_Z,
    s1 B + z s2 = id_(Z^2),
    s2 z = id_Z.

These are all-integer identities, not inferences from bounded testing.
Attaching a 3-cell leaves pi_1 trivial. Therefore Y is a simply-connected,
acyclic CW complex, hence contractible by [H], Corollary 4.33 applied to
Y->point.

Consequently the homotopy type of the space of fillings of the fixed w in Y
is contractible. This is uniqueness up to coherent comparison: a chosen
filling, comparisons to every other filling, and their compatible higher
comparisons. It is not literal equality of retained syntax. Contractibility
is the corresponding homotopy-theoretic notion of unique existence [T].

This all-level conclusion concerns the isolated declared CW model with one
primitive higher attachment. For arbitrary restricted native proof syntax,
one must also justify that its interpretation captures the required mapping
spaces and that the higher comparisons are source-admitted. Topological
existence does not automatically compile to a native proof term.

## 4. An explicit uniqueness theorem for certificate comparisons

At the arithmetic level, declare Gamma to act by

    T(a,b) = (a+3,b-2).

It preserves B and increases k=a+b by one. On W_n this Z-action is free and
transitive. Between certificates c,d with the same n there is exactly one
integer comparison

    k = (d_a+d_b) - (c_a+c_b),
    T^k(c)=d.

The action groupoid consequently has exactly one arrow between any two
objects. Its nerve is contractible. Composition coherence is the identity

    (k_b-k_a)+(k_c-k_b)=k_c-k_a.

Normalization to `(-n,n)` uses `-(a+b)` copies of the declared primitive
coherence. Different raw orders of applying Gamma and its inverse can remain
in a provenance record. The certificate theorem classifies their interpreted
net action, not their raw syntax.

The geometric argument of Sections 2-3 is needed to make a stronger statement
about the free filling model. Arithmetic alone is not substituted for topology.

## 5. Negative controls and the next cases

Attaching one 3-cell along m times the primitive sphere, for m nonzero, gives

    pi_2 = H_2 = Z/|m|.

For |m|>1, filling classes remain nonunique. A nonprimitive coherence is not
enough. If m=0, H_2 remains Z and the added null-boundary cell also gives
H_3=Z.

Attaching two independent primitive 3-cells instead of one kills H_2 but
creates H_3=Z, since their boundaries coincide. The resulting simply-connected
space is homotopy equivalent to S^3. Filling classes of w then agree, but
comparisons between them have a remaining higher ambiguity. Thus unique
components are weaker than a contractible filling space. This negative
control prevents extrapolating a claim about pi_2 to full coherent uniqueness.

For other coprime positive integers r,s the free two-rule model has

    pi_1=<w | w^r,w^s>=1,
    ker[r s]=Z(s,-r).

The same Hurewicz and Whitehead argument applies: the two-rule complex is
homotopy equivalent to S^2, and one 3-cell on the primitive class (s,-r)
makes it contractible. The checker tests (2,5),(3,4),(3,5),(4,7) in addition
to (2,3); the displayed argument proves the coprime case generally.

When gcd(r,s)>1 the fundamental group is nontrivial. The ordinary cellular
kernel is then not sufficient to identify pi_2; universal-cover or other
nonabelian data are required. No such generalization is claimed here.

## 6. What has and has not been established

The generated relation is unique relative to the specified elementary rules.
Those rules alone do not force unique filling witnesses. The precise primitive
higher comparison needed in this test is now identified, and its sufficiency
for coherent uniqueness is proved in the free geometric model.

The boundary of Gamma is computed from rho2 and rho3. Its admissibility is not
a consequence of merely computing that boundary. For C1, a native extension
rule must produce Gamma, with the same full boundary and retained witnesses.
Adjoining Gamma just because uniqueness is desired would select a new rule,
not derive it.

The native Su-32 input establishes neither rho2 nor rho3 nor Gamma. No native
uniqueness conclusion follows. Nor does the uniqueness theorem for this
one-return comparison sector require unrelated loops or geometric sectors to
be filled. In the free model K, the nonuniqueness is itself genuine S^2
homotopy; whether to retain or fill it is a source-admissibility question.

## 7. Reproduction and evidence

    python check_uniqueness.py --output results.json

The standard-library checker validates integer normal forms, uniqueness of
the declared certificate comparison, inverse and composition equations,
explicit chain-contraction identities, nonprimitive attachment controls, and
other coprime pairs. It rejects comparisons with different boundaries.

It does not compute homotopy groups or native proof objects. The claims about
pi_2, homotopy equivalences, and mapping spaces are mathematical proofs above,
using [H] and [T], not claims about what the program established.

Native return-comparison records evaluated: zero. No Agda or Lean compilation,
source repository modification, or execution of native Node checkers occurred.
