# Quarter shifted curvature has a uniform finite exponent and nonuniform amplitudes

## Problem

The shift-zero exponent-two balance could be accidental rather than stable across the half-lattice required by the recurrence.

## Bold conjecture

For each tested fixed integer shift, the shifted cross ratio has inverse-square rather than inverse-first-power scaling.

## Named rivals

The rivals are a shift-dependent exponent, universal shifted amplitude, and a common monotone approach shared by all shifts.

## Risky consequences

Across shifts zero through six, late-window relative spread must favor \(n^2\Theta_{n,a}\) over \(n\Theta_{n,a}\). A universal-amplitude or universal-monotonicity claim must fail if the shift profile carries additional structure.

## Strongest falsification attempt

Exact recurrence values through degree twenty-two favor inverse-square scaling at every tested shift. The amplitudes differ across all seven shifts. Moreover, \(n^2\Theta_{n,a}\) decreases for shifts zero and one but increases for shifts two through six; the convergence-direction split occurs between shifts one and two. All five gates passed.

## Disposition

Retain exponent two as the finite uniform candidate on shifts zero through six. Reject universal amplitudes and universal monotonic approach. The next executable leaf is `quarter-shifted-first-correction-sign-profile`, testing whether a shift-dependent first correction explains the monotonicity flip.

## Claim boundary

The finite profile does not prove uniformity in degree or unbounded shift. Shift-dependent amplitudes are numerical recurrence evidence, not recognized exact constants.
