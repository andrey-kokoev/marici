# Quarter inverse-square source spacing numerator is refuted

## Problem

The observed law \(C(t)\sim1/[4(t+2)^2]\) requires separating the roles of staircase base and spacing.

## Bold conjecture

For an arithmetic source \((a,a+h,a+2h,a+3h)\),

\[
C(t)\sim\frac{h}{(t+a+4h)^2}.
\]

## Named rivals

The rivals are a universal numerator \(1/4\), a spacing-dependent non-linear numerator, and finite-degree bias in the extracted amplitudes.

## Risky consequences

Changing \(a\) must translate the shift grid, while changing \(h\) must preserve convergence of \(C(t)(t+a+4h)^2/h\) to one.

## Strongest falsification attempt

Four exact determinant families were evaluated. Changing \(a\) by one translated the fitted grid exactly on the overlapping shifts. The \(h=1/2\) family refuted the numerator \(h\): at the last tested shift, \(C(t)(t+a+4h)^2/h=0.5539\), not one. Under the rival numerator \(1/4\), the normalized value is \(1.1078\), which is closer but moves nonmonotonically on the finite window.

## Disposition

Reject the numerator \(h\). Preserve the completed endpoint \(a+4h\) as the translation-compatible offset candidate. Type the \(h=1/2\) behavior as an unresolved observation rather than promoting the universal \(1/4\) rival. The next leaf is `quarter-inverse-square-numerator-spacing-discriminator`: enlarge the degree and shift windows for \(h=1/2\) to distinguish finite bias from a different numerator.

## Claim boundary

The family grid does not prove the endpoint formula or any numerator. Three tested \(h=1/4\) families differ only by shift translation and are not independent spacing tests.
