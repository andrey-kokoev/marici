# Functional-completion extension correction audit

## Claims retained

- The completed finite-point theorem remains valid on finite point-supported
  packets and fixed finite jets.
- No finite point-supported distribution equals a nonzero smooth finite
  spherical-harmonic sum.
- The local scalar factor `p^4-q^4` correctly describes a projected chart
  equation and its four ridge families.
- The finite collision vector `(1,-3,2)` remains a genuine primitive
  second-jet stencil in its original point-collision sector.

## Claims narrowed or corrected

1. `p^4-q^4` is not the determinant of the global paired spin-bundle symbol.
   The paired helicity operator is elliptic. The scalar characteristic set is
   a projection artifact unless additional global gluing data authorizes it.
2. "The kernel is exactly the electric sector" is valid only for the earlier
   finite point-supported source category and its specified parity readout.
   Smooth weak-* hard-flux completion adds magnetic `l=2,3,4` aliases.
3. The absence of localized characteristic modes does not imply an empty
   global kernel. The global kernel is the smooth finite spectral block, born
   from zeros of `lambda_l`, not from characteristic ridge propagation.
4. Atomic total-variation closure must not be called Radon completion. It is
   the countably atomic `l1` closure. All finite Radon measures appear only in
   weak-* closure.
5. Finite energy alone does not remove the low modes. Endpoint restrictions
   remove permanent endpoint records only when separately imposed and still
   miss returning pulses.

## Authority boundary

Linear Bondi source equations authorize the smooth electric and coexact
magnetic channels used here. They do not by themselves prove a nonlinear
dominant-energy matter model for every prescribed history. That stronger
constructor claim remains open and is not needed for the linear kernel
classification.

The audit therefore records a source-space refinement, one symbol-type
correction, and one topology correction. It does not overwrite the finite
point theorem or convert presentation ridges into physical states.
