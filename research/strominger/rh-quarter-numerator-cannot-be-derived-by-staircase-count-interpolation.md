# Quarter numerator cannot be derived by staircase-count interpolation

## Problem

The coefficient \(1/4\) might be sourced by the count of four staircase factors.

## Bold conjecture

For a complete unit-interval source with \(p\) staircase factors,

\[
C_p(t)\sim\frac{1}{p(t+2)^2}.
\]

## Named rivals

The rivals are a coefficient specific to the four-column determinant, a coefficient depending nonlinearly on \(p\), and failure of a common \(m^2\) scaling law across staircase counts.

## Risky consequences

The \(p=3,4,5\) families must share \(m^2\) shift-ratio scaling before their fitted amplitudes can be compared, and \(p(t+2)^2C_p(t)\) must approach one.

## Strongest falsification attempt

Exact determinants on the common degree window 20–28 were tested for \(p=3,4,5\). The compatibility preflight failed: spread minimization selected power one even for the known \(p=4\) control, not power two. The adjacent-count amplitude normalizations were \(0.2050\) and \(2.8089\) at the last shift, but these are not admissible refutations because their scaling types were not established.

## Disposition

Do not promote or refute the \(1/p\) conjecture from incompatible fits. Defer count interpolation at the first missing typed object: a common degree-scaling theorem for \(p=3,4,5\). Its acceptance test must recover the \(p=4\) exponent two and type the adjacent exponents on one controlled asymptotic window. Reallocate to the distinct executable rival `quarter-numerator-from-four-column-curvature`.

## Claim boundary

The failed compatibility preflight says nothing about the exact value \(1/4\). It blocks only derivation by cross-count interpolation.
