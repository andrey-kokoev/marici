# Quarter double scaling has a nontrivial crossover

## Problem

The fixed-shift quarter coefficient and fixed-degree leading-symbol coefficient disagree, so their simultaneous scaling must be determined.

## Bold conjecture

For \(t=\kappa n\),

\[
R_n(\kappa n)\longrightarrow
1+\frac{1}{4\kappa^2}.
\]

## Named rivals

The rivals are a nontrivial crossover function, failure of convergence on the tested degree range, and direct persistence of the fixed-degree coefficient \(3/2\).

## Risky consequences

Quadratic inverse-degree fits at several \(\kappa\) must match the displayed formula, while

\[
\kappa^2(F(\kappa)-1)
\]

must remain fixed at \(1/4\).

## Strongest falsification attempt

Exact determinants for \(n=14,\ldots,28\) were tested at \(\kappa=1/2,1,2,4\). The fitted limits were \(2.8916,1.6543,1.2187,1.0681\), rejecting the naive formula at every point. The scaled curvatures were \(0.4729,0.6543,0.8748,1.0897\): monotone and strictly between \(1/4\) and \(3/2\). All five repaired gates passed.

## Disposition

Reject the additive crossover. A nontrivial function survives, interpolating between the quarter small-\(\kappa\) coefficient and the \(3/2\) large-\(\kappa\) coefficient. The next leaf is `quarter-double-scaling-crossover-function`: fit and falsify source-motivated rational forms for this function.

## Claim boundary

The fitted limits are finite extrapolations. Monotonicity is established only at four sampled ratios and does not prove either endpoint limit.
