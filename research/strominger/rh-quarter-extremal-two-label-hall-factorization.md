# Extremal Gale Hall cut is a three-supply versus two-demand identity

## Question

Does the strict fixed-eight minimum reduce to a small labelled source-minor inequality?

## Claim boundary

Yes at the incidence level, but not to small integer coefficients under the extracted common scale. The two negative labels

\[
023457,\qquad 023567
\]

have exactly three comparable positive neighbors:

\[

\]

After division by the common integer scale, the positive coefficients are

and the demands are

\[

\qquad

\]

Their exact normalized supply-minus-demand is positive:

\[
98228145156170453429479348620499761647718440.
\]

The checker reconstructs the raw slack exactly from the common scale and these five coefficients. This is a finite complementary-minor identity, not a symbolic factorization or all-order inequality.

## Disposition

The extremal cut has minimal incidence complexity—three supplies against two demands—but coefficient positivity is not transparent. Test whether its five-term combination is an instance of a Plücker or Desnanot–Jacobi relation plus a manifestly positive remainder. Failure would leave weighted Hall as a global optimization certificate rather than a local determinant identity.
