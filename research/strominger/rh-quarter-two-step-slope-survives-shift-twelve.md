# Quarter two-step slope survives shift twelve

## Problem

The absolute affine intercept failed the enlarged shift test, but the exact Y-system constrains only

\[
u_{a+2}-u_a=-\frac{19}{12}
\]

when the amplitude curvature is \(5/12\).

## Bold conjecture

This recurrence-supported two-step difference survives through shift twelve even though the absolute intercept does not.

## Named rivals

The rivals are failure of the slope itself, a high-shift nonlinear correction profile, and apparent agreement caused by subtracting correlated fitted quantities.

## Risky consequences

Across shifts zero through ten, the latest maximum residual must remain below \(0.01\), improve from the early window, and keep every two-step difference negative.

## Strongest falsification attempt

The maximum residual falls from \(0.05231\) on the early window to \(0.00770\) on the latest degree-thirty-four window, crossing the fixed \(0.01\) gate. Every two-step difference remains negative, and the zero-slope rival is rejected. All six gates passed.

## Disposition

Retain \(-19/12\) as the finite two-step difference through shift twelve. This isolates the recurrence-supported slope from the unsupported absolute intercept. The next leaf is `quarter-first-correction-parity-mode-bound`, testing the even/odd intercept freedom left by the Y-system.

## Claim boundary

The result is finite and based on correlated extrapolations. It does not prove the asymptotic expansion or eliminate parity-dependent intercepts.
