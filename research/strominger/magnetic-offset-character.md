# Even magnetic transport detects the rigid fold offset

Retain the confluent binomial family but deform its vertical offset:

\[
(4-a)^{\overline j}\longrightarrow(h-a)^{\overline j}.
\]

The stable parity characters respond asymmetrically.

For odd `q`, nothing changes:

\[
R^{\mathrm{odd}}_{g,q}(a)
=-(a^{\overline g})^2
(a+g-q-1)(a+g+q-1).
\]

Odd transport uses two left endpoints.  Those depend on
`a rising g` but not on the opposite endpoint offset `h`, so the independence
is structurally expected.

For even `q`, the endpoint collision forces the adjacent response and gives

\[
\boxed{
R^{\mathrm{even}}_{g,q,h}(a)
=qg(g+h-1)a^{\overline g}a^{\overline{g-1}}(a+g+q-1).
}
\]

Thus the even lane measures the offset through

\[
g(g+h-1).
\]

At the rigid engine value `h=4`, this becomes

\[
\boxed{g(g+3)}.
\]

This explains why the factor appears only in even magnetic transport.  Odd
endpoint localization is blind to the vertical boundary.  Even collision
transport probes one coefficient inward and therefore reads the fold offset.

The factor also preserves positivity for every natural deformation `h>=2`.
Its role is curvature-like: it is the grade character through which the
translated branch detects the opposite endpoint geometry.

The deformation family can move the preferred maximal-minor chart onto a zero.
The audit finds this at `(h,g,q)=(2,2,8)`. Those `0/0` ratios are excluded as
presentation boundaries rather than counted as failures of the invariant
character law. The physical baseline `h=4` has no such zero in the audit.

The checker verifies the deformed formulas on more than eight hundred exact
stable determinant ratios.  Establishing the `g(g+h-1)` law symbolically from
the order-four contiguous system remains the final algebraic extraction.
