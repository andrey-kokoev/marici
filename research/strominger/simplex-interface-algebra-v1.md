# First simplex-operation algebra screen

## Frozen scope

Use the existing P-field L,G and return N=LG, together with the independently
constructed12 proper tetrahedral actions U from the rotation/realization square.
These act on one36-dimensional field. They are available geometric and fiber
operations, not yet inter-instrument comparison maps or native reader paths.
Do not invent an interface product or fit generators to a sporadic group.

1. Form the four joint idempotents LG, L-LG, G-LG, I-L-G+LG. Check their
   products, completeness, metric orthogonality and exact ranks. This determines
   the unital algebra generated by L,G: four nonzero orthogonal sectors imply
   C^4, with multiplication inherited from actual matrix composition.
2. Declare the derived reflections R_L=2L-I and R_G=2G-I. They are mathematical
   operations supplied by the projector algebra, not newly authorized native
   moves. Test involution, metric unitarity and the four-element generated group.
3. Combine these reflections with all12 independently sourced proper geometric
   actions. Check commutation, distinctness of the48 candidate matrices and
   closure using actual factor multiplication. The pairwise action table must
   agree with the independently known tetrahedral composition law. If this
   passes, the generated group is A4 x C2 x C2, not Monster or Baby Monster.
   Its unital associative matrix algebra is the span of these48 matrices,
   hence has dimension at most48; no claim of linear independence is made.
4. Retain the scope distinction: ordinary associative operator composition is
   not the nonassociative Griess product. Projector idempotents are not identified
   with Griess axes or VOA Ising vectors; no Monster2A involution is constructed.

Rigid-copy translation has identity action on amplitudes in the checked
co-moving chart, so adding that fiber action does not enlarge this group.
The spatial translation action itself is a separate infinite group acting on
placements, not a finite sporadic group.

## Interpretation

This is a first exact candidate screen, not a census of all possible interface
algebras. Ordered corner cycles, P/N conversion, changing readers and extra
readout structures may impose different operations or obstructions. The next
step is an independently typed interface family with its actual compositions,
not enlarging this list until a desired group appears.
