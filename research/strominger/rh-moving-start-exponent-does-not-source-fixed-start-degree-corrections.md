# The moving-start exponent does not source fixed-start degree corrections

## Question

Does \(1-\beta=3/4\) provide source authority for an \(n^{-3/4}\) correction to the fixed-start Jacobi defect?

No. The exponent \(1-\beta\) was derived from the moving-start scale

\[
L_X=\frac{X^{1-\beta}}{2a\beta}
\]

in the limit \(X\to\infty\). The hard-edge defect correction is taken in a different limit:

\[
n\to\infty
\quad\text{with fixed }X.
\]

No source-derived relation \(X=X(n)\) is present in this leaf. Therefore an exponent governing growth in \(X\) cannot be transported into a decay exponent in \(n\).

The degree saddle for the unshifted moments is instead

\[
x_n\asymp n^{1/\beta}=n^4.
\]

At fixed \(X\), the direct dimensionless hard-edge ratio is \(X/x_n=O(n^{-4})\). This observation also does not determine the recurrence correction, because large Hankel inversion is singular, but it confirms that \(3/4\) has no direct dimensional derivation in the degree limit.

## Disposition

Reject `hard-edge-three-quarter-source`. The finite fitted exponent \(0.785\) remains a preasymptotic diagnostic, not a source-selected value. The next leaf is `fixed-start-jacobi-expansion-class`: determine from a valid fixed-\(X\), large-\(n\) asymptotic method whether corrections use integer powers, fractional powers, or another scale.

## Claim boundary

This does not prove that a numerical \(3/4\) correction is impossible. It proves that the moving-start derivation supplies no authority for it in the fixed-start degree limit.
