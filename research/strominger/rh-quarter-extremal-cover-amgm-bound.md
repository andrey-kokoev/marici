# AM–GM does not explain the extremal singleton Hall slack

## Bold conjecture

The additive inequality \(p_1+p_2>d\) follows from the multiplicative bound

\[
4p_1p_2\geq d^2
\]

and AM–GM.

## Falsification

Exact arithmetic gives

\[
4p_1p_2-d^2=
-12042258821747579149769661413850141214402259281012729871223651971074067707693111613754744.
\]

The sufficient multiplicative bound fails even though the additive Hall slack is positive. Thus neither a small-coefficient identity nor this direct log-convex/AM–GM argument explains the extremal inequality.

## Surviving conjecture

The three labels form a two-cover Gale chain whose middle magnitude is log-concave rather than log-convex. Test the exact reverse inequality \(d^2\geq p_1p_2\), then determine whether a boundary-ratio condition can combine chain log-concavity with the positive alternating sum. Survival of log-concavity alone will not prove Hall positivity; it only identifies the correct curvature type.

## Disposition

The AM–GM route is rejected at fixed \(k=8\). The additive singleton inequality remains a genuine source-specific constraint.
