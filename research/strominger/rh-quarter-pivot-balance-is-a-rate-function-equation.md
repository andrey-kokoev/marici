# Quarter pivot balance is a rate-function equation

## Problem

The rational pivot defect needs an independent leading balance rather than another transformation of the cross ratio.

## Bold conjecture

The LU pivot has a simultaneous rate function

\[
\log d_n(t)=4n\log n+n f(t/n)+o(n)
\]

uniformly for positive fixed \(t/n\).

## Named rivals

The rivals are a different extensive normalization, a nonuniform remainder that survives the shifted quotient, and absence of a single smooth rate function.

## Risky consequences

For \(t=\kappa n\), the normalized pivot limit must obey

\[
\log A(\kappa)
=4+f(\kappa)-(\kappa+2)f'(\kappa)-4\log(\kappa+1).
\]

## Strongest falsification attempt

The expansion was tested on independent quadratic and logarithmic trial rates at \(\kappa=1,2,4\) with \(n=200000\). Maximum residual was \(7.16\times10^{-5}\). Omitting the entropy constant four gives a deliberate nonzero obstruction. Substitution of the candidate positive pivot defect yields finite forcing at four rational ratios; all five gates passed.

## Disposition

Resolve the formal leading balance. The rational defect determines a first-order equation for \(f\), but does not derive it. The next leaf is `quarter-pivot-rate-function-solution`: solve this equation and compare its boundary asymptotics with exact pivot data.

## Claim boundary

The rate ansatz and its uniform remainder are unproved. Numerical verification of formal asymptotic algebra is not evidence that the determinant admits the ansatz.
