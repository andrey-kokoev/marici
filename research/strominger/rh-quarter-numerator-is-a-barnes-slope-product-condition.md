# Quarter numerator is a Barnes slope-product condition

## Problem

The fixed four-staircase structure must explain both the inverse-square power and its coefficient \(1/4\).

## Bold conjecture

The parameter-dependent constant has a generalized Barnes divisor whose large-shift factors are \((\lambda_r t+a_r)^{-\varepsilon_r}\), and the four-staircase structure forces

\[
\sum_r\varepsilon_r=2,
\qquad
\prod_r\lambda_r^{\varepsilon_r}=4.
\]

## Named rivals

The rivals are an arbitrary external normalization, finite-window bias in the coefficient, and a non-Barnes asymptotic with the same aggregate tail.

## Risky consequences

The first invariant must produce inverse-square decay and the second must produce numerator \(1/4\):

\[
C(t)\sim
\left(\prod_r\lambda_r^{-\varepsilon_r}\right)t^{-\sum_r\varepsilon_r}
=\frac1{4t^2}.
\]

## Strongest falsification attempt

Exact algebra verifies the aggregate condition, but it also exposes nonidentifiability. Slopes \((2,2)\) and \((4,1)\), each with positive multiplicity, have the same degree two and slope product four. Arbitrary canceling signed slope pairs preserve both invariants. All five audit gates passed.

## Disposition

The aggregate Barnes condition explains what must source \(1/4\), but four-column count alone does not derive the divisor. Defer divisor assignment at the first missing object: a source-derived factorization of the fixed determinant's parameter constant. Its acceptance test must return degree two and signed slope product four. Reallocate to `quarter-four-column-leading-symbol`, the distinct executable rival based on direct large-shift determinant degree.

## Claim boundary

The slope-product condition is necessary within the generalized Barnes ansatz, not a proof that the determinant has such an ansatz or that either witness list is source-derived.
