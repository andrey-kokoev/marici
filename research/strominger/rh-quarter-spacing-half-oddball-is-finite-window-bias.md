# Quarter spacing-half oddball is finite-window bias

## Problem

The \(h=1/2\) family gave a normalized amplitude \(1.1078\), leaving the universal numerator \(1/4\) unresolved.

## Bold conjecture

For arithmetic four-staircase sources,

\[
C(t)\sim\frac{1}{4(t+a+4h)^2},
\]

independently of spacing in the numerator.

## Named rivals

The rivals are numerator \(h\), another spacing-dependent numerator, and instability of the finite inverse-degree extrapolation.

## Risky consequences

For \(h=1/2\), enlarging the degree window must move \(4(t+a+4h)^2C(t)\) toward one and away from the spacing-half normalization.

## Strongest falsification attempt

At shifts eight through fourteen, amplitudes were independently fitted on degree windows 12–20, 16–24, and 20–28. At shift ten the universal-quarter normalization moved from \(1.1078\) to \(1.0453\) to \(1.0139\), while the spacing-half normalization moved toward \(0.5069\). On the finest window every tested shift was closer to one under the universal-quarter normalization; all five gates passed.

## Disposition

Classify the earlier \(1.1078\) oddball as explained finite-window bias on the tested nested windows. Reject numerator \(h\) again and retain the universal numerator \(1/4\) as the surviving finite conjecture. The next leaf is `quarter-universal-quarter-numerator-proof`: derive the coefficient from the four-staircase structure.

## Claim boundary

Nested finite windows do not prove the large-degree or large-shift limits. The test covers one non-quarter spacing and does not establish universality over arbitrary arithmetic sources.
