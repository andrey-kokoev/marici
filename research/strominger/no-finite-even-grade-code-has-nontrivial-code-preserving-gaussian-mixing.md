# No Finite Even-Grade Code Has Nontrivial Code-Preserving Gaussian Mixing

## Selector-code preparation problem

The code-relative controlled gate uses selector grades \(n_v=2,4\). To expose
the metaplectic sign, the selector must also be prepared and read in coherent
superpositions of those grades.

The one-mode quadratic mixing generators are the paired raising and lowering
operators

\[
K_+=\frac12(v^\dagger)^2,
\qquad
K_- = K_+^\dagger=\frac12v^2.
\]

They connect the even-grade chain

```text
0 -- 2 -- 4 -- 6 -- 8 -- ...
```

with nonzero weights on every edge.

## Boundary-leakage theorem

A self-adjoint quadratic mixer has the form

\[
\alpha K_+ + \overline\alpha K_- + q(N_v).
\]

If \(\alpha\ne0\), the raising term takes the top grade of every finite even
code to the next grade outside the code. Its adjoint supplies the reverse
edge. Therefore no finite interval of even grades is invariant under a
nontrivial self-adjoint quadratic mixer.

For the proposed code, the same coefficient that enables \(2\leftrightarrow4\)
also enables both boundary leaks

\[
2\leftrightarrow0,
\qquad
4\leftrightarrow6.
\]

Code preservation forces \(\alpha=0\). What remains is diagonal in number
grade and cannot prepare an \(X\)-basis selector state or read its coherence.

## Consequence

The quartic controlled phase is not an internally complete experiment. It
assumes two additional capabilities absent from the quadratic endpoint source:

```text
coherent preparation across selector grades
coherence-sensitive readout across selector grades
```

Both can be supplied by an external finite controller. An internal realization
would require spectrally shaped transitions that turn off at the code boundary,
or a genuinely finite invariant subsystem. Neither is generated by the
Gaussian \(Mp(4,\mathbb R)\) action.

This sharpens the executable-closure packet. A conditional phase gate without
preparation and complementary readout ports does not make its phase observable.
