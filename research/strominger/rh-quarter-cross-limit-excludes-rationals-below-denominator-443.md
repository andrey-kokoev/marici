# Quarter cross limit excludes rationals below denominator 443

## Problem

The stabilized finite interval could still support a simple rational constant other than the rejected \(13/60\).

## Bold conjecture

No reduced rational with small denominator lies in the conservative interval spanned by all degree-forty-eight cubic and quartic extrapolations.

## Named rivals

The rivals are a low-denominator rational inside the interval, a rational just outside it hidden by model error, and a nonrational limit.

## Risky consequences

Exhaustive reduced-rational enumeration must identify the first compatible denominator rather than selecting a visually close fraction.

## Strongest falsification attempt

Enumeration through denominator ten thousand finds that every reduced rational below denominator 443 is excluded. The first compatible rational is

\[
\frac{96}{443}=0.216704288939\ldots,
\]

which lies inside the conservative interval \([0.216703959859,0.216704588098]\). All six gates passed.

## Disposition

Exclude all rational candidates with denominator below 443, including \(13/60\). Do not promote \(96/443\): compatibility follows only because the interval deliberately includes early cubic extrapolations, and the fraction has no source derivation. The next leaf is `quarter-cross-limit-96-over-443-falsification`, testing this first surviving rational against narrowed late-order intervals and new degrees.

## Claim boundary

This is conditional exclusion against a model-derived interval, not a proof that the asymptotic constant is irrational or that a limit exists.
