# The second pivot coefficient equals three minus the global correction

## Question

What does the global shift-ratio expansion determine about the local curvature coefficient?

Assume

\[
R_m=Cm^2\left(1+\frac{a}{m}+O(m^{-2})\right).
\]

Since \(c_m=R_m/R_{m-1}\), direct expansion gives

\[
c_m
=1+\frac{2}{m}+\frac{3-a}{m^2}+O(m^{-3}).
\]

Thus the second local coefficient is not independently determined by the leading \(m^2\) law:

\[
\beta=3-a.
\]

The finite estimate \(\beta=-1.70960002\) corresponds to \(a=4.70960002\). In logarithmic form,

\[
\log c_m
=\frac{2}{m}+\frac{1-a}{m^2}+O(m^{-3}).
\]

This identifies why the pivot recurrence cannot supply \(\beta\) from the leading balance alone: one must match the first relative correction to \(R_m\).

## Disposition

Resolve the second-coefficient leaf to the exact relation \(\beta=3-a\). The next leaf is `quarter-global-shift-first-correction`: derive \(a\) from the next order of vector-shifted condensation. The amplitude \(C=104/1575\) remains a separate product-normalization problem.

## Claim boundary

The displayed relation is conditional on the stated asymptotic expansion. The numerical value of \(a\) is extrapolated and is not an exact constant.
