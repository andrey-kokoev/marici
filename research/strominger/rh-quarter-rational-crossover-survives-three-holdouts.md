# Quarter rational crossover survives three holdouts

## Problem

The selected rational crossover lacked tests at ratios not used in its initial recognition.

## Bold conjecture

The simultaneous curvature satisfies

\[
2\kappa^2(\kappa+2)(F(\kappa)-1)=1+3\kappa.
\]

## Named rivals

The rivals are the quadratic endpoint interpolation, a higher-degree rational function, and systematic finite-degree bias masquerading as the selected form.

## Risky consequences

The formula must predict new values at \(\kappa=3/2,3,6\) without changing parameters, while retaining its accuracy at \(1/2,1,2,4\).

## Strongest falsification attempt

Exact determinant fits were extended to all seven ratios. On the three new holdouts, scaled-curvature residuals were \(-0.00479,0.00443,0.00738\). Across all seven ratios the maximum residual remained \(0.02710\), inherited from \(\kappa=1/2\), and the rational form beat the quadratic rival. All five gates passed.

## Disposition

The rational crossover survives the preregistered holdouts but remains a finite conjecture. Analytic proof stops at the first missing object: a simultaneous leading-order pivot or equilibrium equation. Its acceptance test is derivation of the displayed algebraic identity with a uniform error tending to zero. Reallocate to the executable leaf `quarter-double-scaling-pivot-equation`.

## Claim boundary

Holdout survival does not prove the formula. The fitted limits use degrees at most twenty-eight, and the residuals may include extrapolation bias.
