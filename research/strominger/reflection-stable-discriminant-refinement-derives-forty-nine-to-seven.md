# Reflection-stable discriminant refinement derives forty-nine to seven

## Starting obstruction

Let

\[
L=\operatorname{im}F\subset\mathbb Z^2,
\qquad
F=\begin{pmatrix}2&7\\3&7\end{pmatrix},
\]

and let (X) exchange the two target coordinates.  The previous lift test
proved

\[
XL\ne L.
\]

Thus reflection does not act on the one-chart discriminant
(mathbb Z^2/L\cong\mathbb Z/7).

## Canonical stable refinement

The pair of lattices (L) and (XL) is reflection-stable.  The largest
reflection-stable lattice contained in both charts is their intersection

\[
K=L\cap XL.
\]

Modulo seven, (L) is the line generated by ((2,3)), equivalently

\[
3x-2y=0.
\]

The reflected lattice (XL) is the line generated by ((3,2)), equivalently

\[
2x-3y=0.
\]

The determinant of these two equations is

\[
\det\begin{pmatrix}3&-2\\2&-3\end{pmatrix}=-5\equiv2\pmod7,
\]

which is invertible modulo seven.  Hence satisfying both equations forces

\[
x\equiv y\equiv0\pmod7.
\]

Therefore

\[
K=7\mathbb Z^2.
\]

## The 49-class reflection packet

The canonical stable discriminant packet is

\[
\widehat D_F
=\mathbb Z^2/K
\cong(\mathbb Z/7)^2.
\]

It has 49 classes.  Unlike the one-chart cokernel, it carries the literal
reflection action

\[
X(u,v)=(v,u).
\]

No coefficient involution is chosen after the fact: it is inherited from the
target-lattice exchange on the common refinement.

## Seven contextual classes

Over (mathbb Z/7), two is invertible, so the reflection packet splits into
its symmetric and antisymmetric lines:

\[
\widehat D_F
=\Delta\oplus A,
\]

where

\[
\Delta=\{(t,t)\},
\qquad
A=\{(t,-t)\}.
\]

Each line has seven classes.  Quotienting the common mode gives

\[
\widehat D_F/\Delta\cong A\cong\mathbb Z/7,
\]

with coordinate (u-v).  Thus the previously observed 49 raw ports and seven
contextual anti-diagonal types are forced by the reflection-stable lattice
refinement.

## Relation to Aspect's germ theorem

The one-chart quotient consumed the comparison port too early.  Retaining both
marked chart germs produces

```text
L --------\
           intersection germ K=7 Z^2 --> stable 49-class packet --> parity split
XL -------/
```

The outer relational mate is lattice intersection.  It must be formed before
either chart quotient because reflection does not descend through either
one-chart completion separately.

This is precisely Aspect's relational-first architecture.  The missing
coefficient local system is replaced by a source-derived two-chart carrier on
which reflection acts honestly.

## Dual operation and information loss

The smallest reflection-stable lattice containing both charts is

\[
L+XL=\mathbb Z^2.
\]

Its quotient is zero.  Hence there are two canonical reflection-stable repairs:

- intersection retains both chart distinctions and produces 49 classes;
- sum identifies both chart distinctions and destroys the discriminant.

Aspect's kernel-descent test selects the intersection whenever the later
anti-diagonal relation is required, because that relation is not constant on
the fibers of the sum quotient.

## Interpretation

The number 49 is not an accidental enlargement of seven.  It is the index of
the common reflection-stable sublattice required to place the two incompatible
index-seven chart lattices in one equivariant packet.  The final seven is the
rank-one antisymmetric component of that packet.

The source sequence is therefore

\[
\text{affine frame}
\longrightarrow
\text{reflected chart pair}
\longrightarrow
\text{49-class stable discriminant}
\longrightarrow
\text{seven-class relative sector}.
\]

This supplies the equivariant crossing that the bare tensor product lacked.
Promotion of the final seven classes to an executable physical phase port
remains a separate authority question.
