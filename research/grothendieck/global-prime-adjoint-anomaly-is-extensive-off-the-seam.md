# The global prime adjoint anomaly is extensive off the seam

## Bounded question

Can the global Euler product or the fixed archimedean completion cancel the
local mismatch between reciprocal prime transport and Hilbert adjunction?

## Finite-cutoff anomaly

Put

\[
z=s-\frac12,
\qquad
x=\operatorname{Re}z.
\]

At one prime, the modulus ratio between the reciprocal coefficient and the
adjoint direct coefficient is

\[
r_p(z)=p^{2x}.
\]

For the finite prime set \(p\leq X\), the product anomaly is therefore

\[
R_X(z)=\prod_{p\leq X}r_p(z)
=\exp\left(2x\vartheta(X)\right),
\]

where

\[
\vartheta(X)=\sum_{p\leq X}\log p.
\]

Because \(\vartheta(X)\) is unbounded,

\[
R_X(z)\longrightarrow
\begin{cases}
+\infty,&x>0,\\
1,&x=0,\\
0,&x<0.
\end{cases}
\]

This conclusion needs only the infinitude of primes, not a prime-number
asymptotic.

## Prime-power filtration strengthens the same conclusion

If every prime-power incidence is retained, the corresponding logarithmic
mass is

\[
\psi(X)=\sum_{p^k\leq X}\log p.
\]

The filtered mismatch becomes

\[
R_X^{\rm pp}(z)=\exp\left(2x\psi(X)\right).
\]

It has the same trichotomy. Primitive and square channels do not cancel the
anomaly; they make its typed cutoff growth explicit.

## A fixed archimedean factor cannot cancel it

Let \(A_\infty(z)\) be any nonzero archimedean normalization independent of
the finite-prime cutoff. Then

\[
|A_\infty(z)|R_X(z)
\]

still tends to zero or infinity for \(x\ne0\). Hence the gamma factor or any
other fixed endpoint unit cannot turn the native restricted product into an
adjoint comparison off seam.

## What a cutoff-dependent repair would mean

The unique scalar modulus counterterm is proportional to

\[
\exp\left(-2x\vartheta(X)\right)
\]

or to its prime-power analogue. This counterterm depends simultaneously on the
spectral displacement and the Euler cutoff. It is therefore an additional
boundary current at prime infinity, not a fixed local or archimedean unit.

Using it can normalize the modulus for every \(x\), so its existence has no
zero-confining force. To become explanatory it would need an independently
derived endpoint law that permits the counterterm only for admissible states
and whose vanishing boundary flux forces \(x=0\). No such law has been
constructed.

## Result

Global multiplication does not soften the local star defect. It amplifies it
into an extensive cutoff anomaly. Native restricted-product adjointness is
possible without a cutoff-dependent metric counterterm exactly on the
critical seam.

This closes the shortcut in which a fixed archimedean completion was expected
to repair unequal local adjoint coefficients automatically.

## Remaining global gate

The only remaining version of this route is a genuine conservation theorem at
prime infinity. It must derive the cutoff countercurrent from labelled
primitive, square, higher, and archimedean channels and prove that a scalar
zero-state has zero total boundary flux. Merely subtracting the displayed
divergence is regularization, not a proof of RH.
