# Typed event-cut reconstruction and the joint observation criterion

## Main result

A receiver faithfully records every linear combination of marked source paths at finite event length if:

1. its elementary marked-arrow map is injective on each source/target block;
2. it retains the joint ordered event slots;
3. it retains the corresponding intermediate object labels (or equivalent separating attachment data).

Under these conditions reconstruction is compatible with every lossless regrouping. Regrouping comparisons are generated by restriction and reassembly and satisfy their identity and composition laws.

Keeping all individual cut observations is weaker. The three-prime regression has full-cut rank 48 but joint rank only 46 for its two separate single-cut observations. The missing two directions admit strictly positive mixture witnesses. Thus compatibility of cut marginals does not by itself reconstruct their joint attachment data.

## 1. General typed source

Let Q be an admitted directed graph and let k be a field. Retain the free path source, rather than quotienting distinct routes by their terminal objects. For each ordered vertex pair (x,y), let W_xy be the vector space freely spanned by the elementary marked arrows from x to y.

The length-n path space from x_0 to x_n has the canonical decomposition

H_n(x_0,x_n)=direct_sum_(x_1,...,x_(n-1))
             W_(x_0,x_1) tensor ... tensor W_(x_(n-1),x_n).

The formula is an isomorphism on path bases: a path determines its vertices and elementary arrows, and these data rejoin to that path. It remains true with parallel arrows, provided they remain distinct generators of W_xy.

For a finite vertex set, put B=direct_sum_x k e_x and W=direct_sum_(x,y) W_xy. Then H_n=W tensor_B ... tensor_B W, and the full typed path algebra is T_B(W). The vertex idempotents enforce the actual attachment conditions. The ordinary untyped tensor record is a further realization, not this source algebra itself.

Length-zero paths are typed identities. A forgotten marked event remains a length-one arrow; its retained-record degree may be zero, but its source event length is not zero.

## 2. Reconstruction under regrouping

A lossless regrouping divides the ordered events into blocks and retains each block's internal typed path data. Equivalently, its block record is the direct sum over internal vertices of the corresponding tensor products of elementary records.

Associativity of tensor product and its distribution over direct sums give the canonical identification of every such regrouping with the finest joint event record. On basis elements this is simply splitting and rejoining the same typed word.

If c_T restricts a complete marking to the records of a cut tree T and a_T reassembles those records, then

a_T c_T=id, c_T a_T=id.

Define rho_(T,U)=c_U a_T. It follows that

rho_(T,T)=id,

rho_(U,V) rho_(T,U)=rho_(T,V).

This proves independence of regrouping while preserving attachment data. It does not assert that a coarser observation which has already forgotten internal vertices or merged record slots is invertible.

## 3. Formal marking-level theorem

`agda/TypedCutReconstruction.agda` defines arbitrary finite binary cut trees on a typed route. Each leaf retains its complete block marking. The module constructs restriction and assembly and proves that they are inverse, then proves the identity and composition laws for the generated regrouping maps.

The checked theorems are:

- `reconstruction-iso`;
- `regroup-id`;
- `regroup-compose`.

The source route and every intermediate vertex occur in the dependent types. This formalizes marking reconstruction conditional on those source attachments. It does not claim that an arbitrary analytical observation can recover a route hidden by its projection. That latter issue is addressed by the separate receiver theorem below.

Compilation succeeds with `--safe --cubical --guardedness`. The imported typed-history module still reports its previously documented indexed-pattern transport-computation warnings. No holes or postulates are used.

## 4. Receiver faithfulness theorem

Suppose each W_xy has a linear observation j_xy:W_xy->K_xy. The full cut receiver is

J_n=direct_sum_(vertex chains) tensor_i j_(x_(i-1),x_i).

If every local j_xy is injective, then J_n is injective.

Proof: tensor products over a field preserve injections, and direct sums preserve injections. Equivalently, choose left inverses on the finite-dimensional local images; their tensor products and direct sum give a left inverse of J_n. In the vertex-algebra description B is semisimple, so the same argument applies to the balanced tensor products.

This is a local condition on all marked generators in each endpoint block. Merely checking that each individual feature is nonzero is insufficient in the presence of parallel elementary arrows whose images might coincide.

For the prime cube, there is at most one elementary prime arrow for fixed adjacent endpoints. The local marked space has basis (e^0,e^1). Its receiver is

e^0 -> vacuum, e^1 -> g_e,

with vacuum in degree zero and g_e in degree one. The two vectors are independent exactly when g_e is nonzero. Therefore every finite prime packet has faithful full-cut observation whenever these event features are nonzero and the typed joint slots are retained. No independence between features at different endpoint blocks is required.

This is a criterion applicable to Voevodsky's receiver, not a new construction of that analytical receiver or a proof of physical event access.

## 5. Finite stability estimate

Suppose local Hilbert norms and endpoint-block orthogonal sums are specified, and j_xy has lower bound sigma_xy>0 on the finite local source space. Then J_n has lower bound at least

min_(admitted vertex chains) product_i sigma_(x_(i-1),x_i).

In the two-mark vacuum/letter model with degree weight tau, the local lower bound is min(1,tau ||g_e||) for the standard source basis norm.

This gives quantitative finite-stage reconstruction. It does not supply a uniform bounded inverse at all event lengths: products can tend to zero. An infinite weighted completion needs its own uniform or weight-compensated bound.

## 6. What terminal rejoining forgets

Let R be the faithful full-cut receiver and let J be a specified rejoining/compression map. The terminal observation is J R. Its lost information is exactly

ker(J R)=R^(-1)(ker J intersect image R).

For several coarser cut observations J_alpha R, the undetected space is

intersection_alpha ker(J_alpha R).

Consequently a collection of cut observations reconstructs the source precisely when this family is jointly injective. Compatibility on shared coarsenings is necessary but need not imply that condition.

For finite-dimensional source, rank loss equals the dimension of the indicated kernel. This identifies the premature quotient without postulating an extra scalar probe.

## 7. Three-prime regression: joint cuts versus cut marginals

Use the six complete orders of primes (2,3,5), rooted at 2, with their eight markings per route. The source space has dimension 48. The checker uses the same arithmetic window incidences as the existing implementation, in the fifteen-chamber refinement.

Exact ranks:

| Observation | Rank |
| --- | ---: |
| Terminal composed record | 26 |
| First interior cut alone | 36 |
| Second interior cut alone | 36 |
| Both individual cut observations, stacked | 46 |
| Joint full event-cut record | 48 |

The full rank is certified by an explicit selected-coordinate left inverse: retain the vertex chain, read vacuum versus letter at each event, and choose one nonzero chamber coordinate for each recorded event. In this simple source graph different vertex chains identify different routes, and different vacuum patterns identify different markings.

Let epsilon(w) be the sign of a prime permutation. The joint kernel of the two individual-cut observations is exactly spanned by

k_0=sum_w epsilon(w) [w,000],

k_1=sum_w epsilon(w) ([w,100]+[w,010]+[w,001]).

For the first cut, fixing the first prime leaves two opposite-sign orders of the remaining pair. Forgotten records cancel, and the sum of single-letter records over that pair depends only on its common total interval. The second cut has the analogous cancellation with the last prime fixed. The two vectors are independent because their retained-record degrees differ. The exact rank 46 proves that they exhaust the joint kernel.

For either k_i, the coefficient vectors

c_plus=(1/48)1+k_i/96,

c_minus=(1/48)1-k_i/96

are distinct, strictly positive, and sum to one. They have identical individual-cut observations and hence identical common terminal observations. The full event-cut record separates them.

Thus the failure is not restricted to signed or complex mixtures. It is a genuine loss of joint attachment information. The same single-cut charts are individually faithful on smaller pieces but their marginals cannot recover all route correlations.

## 8. Relation to the Clark diamond

For two primes the sole interior cut is already the full event cut, so its rank is eight; terminal rejoining has rank six. The checker reproduces those ranks.

For three primes there are two interior positions. Keeping them separately is not the same as keeping both simultaneously. The new theorem specifies the needed receiver: retain the full vertex chain and all elementary record factors jointly, or prove a replacement family jointly injective.

The passage from the diamond to arbitrary packets is therefore controlled by a general local-embedding theorem plus a precise joint-observation requirement, rather than an expectation that more pairwise charts automatically preserve all information.
