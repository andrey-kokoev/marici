## Horocycle completion of the metaplectic geodesic

The theta matrix coefficient restricted to diagonal dilation follows one
geodesic in the modular homogeneous space. Adding the stable and unstable
horocycle directions produces the nonholomorphic Eisenstein series on the
modular surface.

The modular surface has one cusp. Its scattering operator is therefore a
scalar coefficient. With the completed zeta factor

\[
\Lambda(w)=\pi^{-w/2}\Gamma(w/2)\zeta(w),
\]

the coefficient is

\[
\varphi(s)=\frac{\Lambda(2s-1)}{\Lambda(2s)}.
\]

This formula occurs as the constant-term coefficient of the Eisenstein
series. A primary published account gives the equivalent expression
(Lambda(2-2s)/\Lambda(2s)), using the functional equation of
(Lambda): [Quantum limits of Eisenstein series and scattering

## Why this still does not prove RH

Self-adjointness of the modular Laplacian makes the scattering coefficient
unitary on

\[
\Re s=\frac12.
\]

It does not force analytically continued scattering poles onto
(Re s=1/4). Resonances of a self-adjoint open system may lie away from its
unitary axis.

Moreover, one cusp means the scattering matrix has rank one. The full
horocycle completion supplies a canonical operator provenance for the scalar
ratio, but it does not retain a multi-channel defect space capable of turning
unitarity into pole confinement.

The result is therefore an Explanation of the offsets and reciprocal
pairing, not an RH proof.
