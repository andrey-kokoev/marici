# The prime-Fock plethystic logarithm splits off a distributional primitive current

## Bounded question

Is the local subtraction in packet 135 generated by the already derived prime
Fock source, or is it merely a convergence-motivated phase choice?

## Local Fock overlap

For `r_p=p^(-1/2)`, let the normalized prime Fock vacuum be

\[
 \Omega_p=\sqrt{1-r_p}\sum_{a\ge0}r_p^{a/2}e_a
\]

and let the number rotation satisfy

\[
 U_\theta e_a=e^{-ia\theta}e_a.
\]

Its vacuum overlap is

\[
 a_p(\theta)
 =\langle\Omega_p,U_\theta\Omega_p\rangle
 =\frac{1-r_p}{1-r_pe^{-i\theta}}.
\]

The local Tate seam transition is exactly the reciprocal ratio

\[
 \boxed{
 \gamma_p(1/2+it)
 =\frac{a_p(-\theta_p)}{a_p(\theta_p)},
 \qquad \theta_p=t\log p.}
\]

Thus the local phase is a ratio of two source-derived Fock vacuum overlaps.

## Plethystic logarithm

The logarithm of the overlap is

\[
 \log a_p(\theta)
 =\log(1-r_p)
 +\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{r_p^ke^{-ik\theta}}{k}.
\]

Taking the reciprocal ratio cancels the real vacuum-normalization term and
gives

\[
 \log\gamma_p
 =2i\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{r_p^k\sin(k\theta_p)}{k}.
\]

The index `k` is the connected occupation/prime-power degree in the
plethystic logarithm.  Therefore the packet-135 subtraction removes exactly
the one-particle connected character

\[
 2ir_p\sin\theta_p,
\]

while retaining every connected multiparticle cluster `k>=2`.

This derives the **identity** of the subtraction entirely from the Fock
source, before global zeta continuation.

## Global Hilbert typing

Across all primes, the primitive coefficient vector has squared norm

\[
 \sum_pr_p^2=\sum_p\frac1p=\infty.
\]

It is therefore not a vector in the canonical prime Hilbert direct sum.  It is
a distributional current in the dual rigging.

For the retained connected tail,

\[
 \sum_p\left|
 \sum_{k\ge2}\frac{r_p^k\sin(k\theta_p)}{k}
 \right|^2
 <\infty,
\]

because each local tail is `O(1/p)`.  The prime-power dressing belongs to the
Hilbert implementation class.

Hence the source itself splits into

\[
 \boxed{
 \text{distributional one-particle prime current}
 +
 \text{Hilbert-implementable connected prime-power tail}.}
\]

## Two-rigging interpretation

This is not permission to delete the primitive current.  It places the two
pieces in their correct types:

- the `k=1` current belongs to the distributional chart;
- the `k>=2` dressing belongs to the Hilbert/Fock chart.

The renormalized local family is the Hilbert component. The removed phase must
remain as a relative determinant-line or boundary current coupling the two
riggings.  This recovers, at the prime-Fock level, the two-chart Clifford
architecture of packets 98--100.

## Remaining authority gate

The plethystic logarithm canonically separates the primitive sector, but it
does not prove that the global completed readout is unchanged when that sector
is represented distributionally. One must construct the relative pairing
between the primitive current and the archimedean/modular seam and recover the
original completed scalar.

The falsifier is a residual scalar phase or divisor introduced by moving the
primitive term between riggings. A valid relative determinant must reproduce
the unrenormalized finite-prime products at every cutoff and have a unique
cutoff-independent completed limit.
