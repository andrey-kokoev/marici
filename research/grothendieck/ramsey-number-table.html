Two-color Ramsey numbers

R(ℓ,k) is the smallest number of vertices forcing a red clique of size ℓ or a blue clique of size k in every red/blue coloring of the complete graph. Swapping colors transposes the table.

A single number is exact; a dash gives inclusive lower and upper bounds, not probabilities. Intervals are conservative, not a claim to the latest records. For k = 12 the interval is from Radziszowski, Small Ramsey Numbers, revision 18 (2026), Table Ia (lower: Kolodyazhny [Kol1]; upper: Lesser [Les]). For the other entries with k = 11–18 the displayed bounds are deliberately loose: propagate the k = 10 lower bound by monotonicity, and the upper bound by R(3,k) ≤ R(3,k−1) + k. For example, R(3,7) = R(7,3) = 23.

Isotope annotations are separate from Ramsey bounds. In row 3, for k = 3 through 18, the label is the heaviest stable isotope of the element with k + 3 protons. A question mark denotes a proposed assignment. From (3,10) to (6,7), three inward steps at fixed sum 13 would remove three neutrons from ²⁷Al under the step rule. The resulting ²⁴Al is radioactive; a stable-isotope-only rule would not select it.

Reference: Stanisław P. Radziszowski, Small Ramsey Numbers (survey; consult the latest revision for improvements).
