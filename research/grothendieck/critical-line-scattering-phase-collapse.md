# Completed critical-line scattering collapses to the identity

Epistemic-graph event: 1414.

## The natural prime and archimedean phases

Let

\`G(s)=pi^(-s/2) Gamma(s/2)\`,
\`Lambda(s)=G(s) zeta(s)\`,

and let \`D(s)=1/zeta(s)\` denote the continued scalar value of the
intrinsic-prime determinant. In its honest Fredholm domain \`Re(s)>1\`,
Ledger 1376 produces the unitary ratio

\`S_p(s)=D(s)/D(conj(s))=zeta(conj(s))/zeta(s)\`.

The matching archimedean boundary ratio is

\`S_infinity(s)=G(conj(s))/G(s)\`.

Both ratios have modulus one wherever they are finite and nonzero. The
Gaussian eighth phase is the first constant term in this archimedean
half-rotation data.

## Functional-equation collapse

Put \`s=1/2+i t\`. Then \`1-s=conj(s)\), and the completed functional equation
gives

\`G(s) zeta(s)=G(conj(s)) zeta(conj(s))\`.

Away from zeros,

\`S_p(s)=zeta(conj(s))/zeta(s)=G(s)/G(conj(s))\`.

Therefore

\`S_infinity(s) S_p(s)=1\`

for every real \`t\` where the ratios are defined. As a meromorphic identity,
the apparent zero-over-zero singularities cancel and the product extends as
the constant one.

The polynomial completion factor \`s(s-1)/2\` does not change the conclusion:
on the critical line it is real and its conjugate ratio is one.

## Failure of phase quantization

The most direct unitary boundary equation,

\`det(I-S_infinity(t)S_p(t))=0\`,

is therefore satisfied identically, not only at Riemann zeros. Reversing one
orientation makes it a smooth nonconstant phase equation, but then it merely
quantizes the archimedean phase twice and still has no amplitude-zero
criterion.

At a zeta zero the individual prime ratio is undefined, but its completed
product has a removable singularity. Thus declaring the undefined points to
be eigenvalues would add an external domain convention whose exceptional set
is exactly the target zero set. That is circular unless a source-derived
operator-domain defect independently forces those points.

The reason is structural: scalar unitary scattering retains phase and discards
amplitude. The functional equation fixes the complete phase balance on the
critical line, while the zeros reside in the vanishing amplitude of the real
completed function \`Xi(t)\).

## Consequence for the surviving architecture

Neither of the two canonical scalar gluings works:

- multiplying the prime and archimedean scattering phases yields the identity
  continuum; and
- compressing the smooth completed theta amplitude yields the overly sparse
  Hankel determinant of Ledger 1378.

A viable boundary must therefore be operator-valued, not merely a scalar
unitary ratio. It must retain a nontrivial defect space at amplitude zeros
before taking determinants. Equivalently, the prime and archimedean factors
must be glued at the level of domains, extensions, or a canonical system—not
multiplied after scalar determinant reduction.

## Scope

This is an exact no-go for scalar phase-only critical-line quantization using
the natural intrinsic-prime and archimedean ratios. It does not exclude a
matrix/operator-valued scattering system whose defect multiplicity survives
completion.
