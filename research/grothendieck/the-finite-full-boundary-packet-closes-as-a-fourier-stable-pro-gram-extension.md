# The Finite Full Boundary Packet Closes as a Fourier-Stable Pro-Gram Extension

## Previously separate components

Three source constructions are now available:

1. Fourier-saturated primitive and square seminorms on the reciprocal sheets;
2. the five-component archimedean cell `(phi,1,delta_0,K,V)`;
3. the complete-label-graph seam coboundary `delta_label`.

They should not be collapsed into one Hilbert Gramian. The primitive and square
grades are not uniformly equivalent, the connected tail is naturally
absolute-summable, and the seam and archimedean ports are graph extensions.

## Finite-cutoff packet

For a finite prime set `X`, define a locally convex packet using:

- every saturated primitive seminorm `q_delta^sat`, for positive `delta`;
- the saturated square seminorm `q_0^sat`;
- the connected-tail absolute-summability seminorm and its Fourier orbit;
- graph seminorms of the seam coboundary on both reciprocal sheets;
- the Euclidean norm on the five-component archimedean cell.

The reciprocal sheet action

\[
J=\begin{pmatrix}0&-I\\I&0\end{pmatrix}
\]

preserves every saturated arithmetic seminorm. The archimedean Fourier action
is the signed permutation

\[
\phi\mapsto\phi,
\quad 1\leftrightarrow\delta_0,
\quad K\mapsto V,
\quad V\mapsto-K,
\]

whose fourth power is the identity. The seam coboundary is natural under the
transported augmentation/control anchor.

Therefore the complete finite boundary packet is Fourier-stable as a
pro-Gram extension.

## Separation

Every saturated primitive or square form is strictly positive at finite
cutoff. Hence the seminorm family has trivial common kernel before adding the
tail and graph ports. Adding those ports cannot create a kernel. The finite
packet is Hausdorff and faithfully retains every declared channel.

## Correction to the orbit question

The prime-square and archimedean channels do not need to be identified as the
Fourier image of the primitive current. The primitive orbit closes through
its reciprocal/control copy inside the saturated packet. The square grade
remains a distinct regularity layer, and the archimedean five-cell closes its
own Fourier orbit.

This is exactly the behavior suggested by `2(3+2+1)+1`: corresponding roles
are transported across typed copies, not merged into one block.

## Remaining obstruction

Finite closure is complete. The unresolved theorem is passage to the
restricted-product limit:

- cutoff maps must be continuous for every seminorm;
- Fourier sewing must extend to the projective/inductive completion;
- the common kernel must remain zero;
- the zero-to-state map and Green forcing pairing must be continuous;
- no normalized state may escape through the weakening primitive grade.

The next hostile sequence should therefore be constructed in the full
pro-Gram topology, not in a single Hilbert norm.
