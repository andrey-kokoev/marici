# Scalar functional-equation completion collapses the faithful odd quadrature

## The completed scalar pair

Let

\[
X(z)=\xi\!\left(\frac12+z\right).
\]

The completed functional equation is

\[
X(-z)=X(z).
\]

If one forms the two-sector pair only after scalar completion, it is

\[
(X(z),X(-z))=(X(z),X(z)).
\]

Its even and odd quadratures are therefore

\[
T_{mathrm{sc}}(z)=2X(z),
\qquad
A_{mathrm{sc}}(z)=0.
\]

The faithful odd comparison channel has been collapsed identically.

## What a zero means after this collapse

At a zero of \(X\), both scalar components vanish together. Away from zeros,
their projective direction is always

\[
[X(z):X(-z)]=[1:1].
\]

Thus the completed projective relationship contains no divisor information.
The divisor resides entirely in the common scalar factor \(X(z)\).

This explains why the earlier ratio or projective approaches could not
constrain RH. By the time the scalar functional equation is imposed, the
relative channel has already been erased.

## Hostile multiplier test

Let \(H(z)\) be any even divisor-bearing factor. Then

\[
X_H(z)=H(z)X(z)
\]

still satisfies

\[
X_H(-z)=X_H(z).
\]

Its completed pair has the same odd quadrature zero and the same projective
direction \([1:1]\), while its divisor has changed. Therefore neither scalar
evenness nor completed projective coherence can reject hostile off-seam
zeros.

## Location of the lossy operation

Finite labelled Euler amplitudes retain two faithful quadratures and never
vanish together. Scalar functional-equation completion identifies their
readouts and kills the odd channel. This is the first explicit lossy arrow in
the construction chain:

The finite pair \((F_X^+,F_X^-)\) passes through scalar completion and is
then read out as the diagonal pair \((X,X)\). The completion arrow cannot be
treated as a harmless presentation change. It is a quotient that forgets
relative amplitude data.

## Required lift

The functional equation must be lifted before scalarization to a
correspondence containing:

- the two sectoral amplitude objects;
- the Fourier–Tate sewing morphism;
- its comparison homotopy or Green current;
- the even and odd quadratures before identification;
- the scalar completed section as a final projection.

Only such a lift can ask whether a scalar zero leaves a nonzero complementary
state and whether that state is admissible off the seam.

## Scope

This is a no-go theorem for scalar and projective functional-equation data.
It does not show that a suitable nonscalar lift exists or that it confines
zeros.

## Result

Scalar completion turns the two-sector relationship into the diagonal pair
\((X,X)\), annihilating the odd quadrature. The divisor becomes a common
scale factor invisible to projective comparison and freely alterable by even
hostile multipliers. RH-bearing information must therefore live in a
pre-scalar lift of Fourier–Tate sewing.
