# The half-offset is the first authorized decay threshold

## Failure of literal almost-periodic preservation

The Euler chamber is generated by discrete Mellin characters, but completed
continuation is not a literal endomorphism of the Bohr almost-periodic
category. The canonical endpoint cancellation already contains

\[
\frac1{s-1}-\frac1s.
\]

On a vertical line, the Fourier transforms of the model terms are one-sided
exponentials. For `a>0`,

\[
\mathcal F_t\!\left(\frac1{a+it}\right)(x)
=
2\pi e^{ax}\mathbf 1_{x<0}
\]

under the chosen Fourier convention. Reciprocal orientation supplies the
opposite half-line term.

Thus legal endpoint completion itself introduces decaying boundary modes.
The previous target, strict preservation of almost periodicity before typing
these currents, was too strong.

## The source-fixed decay threshold

On the critical seam, the endpoint singularities `s=0` and `s=1` are each at
horizontal distance

\[
\frac12.
\]

Their Fourier-resolution modes therefore decay at rate `1/2`. The poles of
the gamma completion lie at equal or greater distance from the seam, so their
authorized rates are also at least `1/2`.

By contrast, every nontrivial off-seam zero lies in the critical strip. A
right-sector zero has

\[
0<a_\rho=\Re\rho-\frac12<\frac12
\]

and contributes the slower mode

\[
e^{-a_\rho|x|}e^{-i\gamma_\rho x}.
\]

Therefore the half-offset of the critical line has an exact dynamical
meaning: it is the first decay threshold already authorized by the endpoint
and archimedean completion. A nontrivial off-line zero creates a sub-threshold
decay channel.

## Correct completion category

Let `C_decl` be the preregistered closed span of the regularized endpoint and
archimedean decay modes, derived before inspecting the nontrivial divisor.
Its exponential rates begin at `1/2`. The source completion target is not

```text
almost periodic -> almost periodic.
```

It is

```text
Euler discrete grammar
-> almost-periodic channel + declared completion channel,
```

with no residual decay exponent in the open interval `(0,1/2)`.

After the regularizing weight `(1+t^2)^(-k)`, convolution with its
exponentially decaying Fourier transform preserves the slowest exponent.
Hence a sub-threshold mode cannot be reclassified as an endpoint mode by the
regularization used in the almost-periodicity theorem.

## Refined RH criterion

Project the regularized resolution signal onto its `C_0` component using the
unique almost-periodic plus vanishing-at-infinity decomposition. Remove the
independently constructed declared completion channel. RH is equivalent to
absence of residual exponential rates in `(0,1/2)`.

This is a stricter hostile test than merely asking whether some `C_0` term is
present. Authorized endpoint and gamma currents are allowed; a slower decay
mode is not.

## Why cancellation cannot fit away the defect

The diffuse divisor measure is positive. Distinct right-sector zeros add
positive Poisson densities before Fourier transformation. Therefore the
sub-threshold sector cannot be removed by a signed cancellation at the
measure level. Any source compiler that cancels it only after scalar Fourier
summation has erased the positive type and fails the audit.
