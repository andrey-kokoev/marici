## Bounded question

Does the additive Poisson continuation provide the higher-dimensional
pre-scalar lift required after packet 216, and does that lift independently
exclude off-seam cancellation?

## The lift exists

For a Schwartz--Bruhat source \(f\), retain the four continuation channels

\[
V_f(s)=
\begin{pmatrix}
A_f(s)\\
A_{\widehat f}(1-s)\\
\widehat f(0)/(s-1)\\
-f(0)/s
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The Tate--Poisson readout is the fixed aggregation row

\[
\Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
Z(f,s)=\Sigma V_f(s).
\]

Thus the four-channel object is a genuine pre-scalar lift. It retains the two
reciprocal bulk terms and both polar boundary currents separately.

## Kernel theorem

The aggregation row has rank one and hence

\[
\dim\ker\Sigma=3.
\]

Poisson sewing determines how the four components are constructed and how
they transform under reciprocal reflection. It does not make \(\Sigma\)
faithful. A nonzero boundary-bearing state can therefore have zero scalar
readout while every one of its channels remains well-defined.

The reciprocal involution permutes the two bulk channels and exchanges the
oriented boundary channels. Its fixed and anti-fixed subspaces both meet
\(\ker\Sigma\). Consequently reflection covariance alone cannot prevent a
four-channel state from entering the aggregation kernel.
