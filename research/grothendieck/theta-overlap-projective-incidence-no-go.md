# Overlap gives canonical projective incidence, but not spectral incidence

## 1. The strongest automatic construction

Let \(v\) and \(w_z\) be nonzero vectors in a Hilbert space, or compatible
vectors and covectors in a rigged Hilbert pairing. Define their lines

\[
  \ell_v=\mathbb Cv,
  \qquad
  \ell_z=\mathbb Cw_z.
\]

Orthogonal projection gives a canonical one-dimensional map

\[
  P_v|_{\ell_z}:\ell_z\longrightarrow\ell_v.
\]

After choosing the source normalizations of the two lines, its determinant is
exactly their overlap:

\[
  \det(P_v|_{\ell_z})
  =
  \frac{\langle v,w_z\rangle}{\|v\|\,\|w_z\|}.
\]

In the distributional theta setting the same statement is a determinant-line
pairing between the comb covector and the transported vacuum line. Thus the
completed theta scalar already has a canonical projective determinant
interpretation:

\[
  X(z)=0
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \ell_z\subseteq\ker v
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  P_v|_{\ell_z}\text{ loses rank}.
\]

## 2. Why this is not Hilbert--Pólya

This loss of rank is orthogonality, not spectral incidence. It does not say
that \(z\) is an eigenvalue of a self-adjoint operator. For an arbitrary
analytic line curve \(z\mapsto\ell_z\), intersections with the fixed
hyperplane \(\ker v\) may occur anywhere in the complex parameter plane.

The self-adjointness of the carrier generating \(w_z\) does not change this.
Analytic continuation of a unitary orbit produces a holomorphic curve in
projective space, and a fixed hyperplane section of that curve has no generic
real-zero theorem.

\[
\boxed{
\text{overlap zero}
=\text{projective hyperplane incidence}
\not=\text{self-adjoint spectral incidence}.}
\]

This is the exact category gap that remained hidden when the matrix
coefficient was called a determinant.

## 3. Symplectic graph lift

Any operator \(A_z:E\to F\) can be encoded by its graph in
\(E\oplus F\):

\[
  \operatorname{graph}(A_z)\cap(E\oplus0)
  \cong\ker A_z.
\]

Applying this to the one-dimensional projection above turns every overlap
zero into an intersection of subspaces. But this graph is Lagrangian only
after supplying a symplectic form and an adjoint relation compatible with
\(A_z\). Choosing those structures from \(X\) merely repackages the scalar
zero condition.

The automatic graph lift therefore has no RH force. Its construction works
for every hostile analytic matrix coefficient.

## 4. Required extra law

For Green's identity to exclude nonreal intersections, the moving line must
be the trace of a deficiency solution:

\[
  \ell_z=\Gamma\ker(S^*-z),
\]

and the fixed relation must be a maximal self-adjoint Lagrangian
\(L_{\mathrm{arith}}\). Then

\[
  (z-\bar z)\|u\|^2
  =
  \Omega(\Gamma u,\Gamma u)
\]

supplies the missing orientation. Neither the projective overlap nor its
rank-one graph manufactures this identity.

Hence an admissible overlap-to-incidence construction must derive all three
objects independently:

1. a symmetric bulk operator \(S\);
2. its Green boundary trace \(\Gamma\); and
3. the arithmetic maximal Lagrangian \(L_{\mathrm{arith}}\).

Only afterward may the determinant of the resulting Fredholm pair be
compared with \(X\).

## 5. Naturality no-go

From only a pair \((v,w_z)\), the unitary-covariant finite-rank operators are
generated by their rank-one tensors and scalar contractions. Their
determinants are universal functions of Gram data such as
\(\langle v,w_z\rangle\). Consequently any construction using only this pair
and Hilbert operations applies unchanged to hostile Fourier-stable vacua.

Arithmetic discrimination must therefore enter through additional
source-labelled structure:

\[
  \text{local Weyl actions}
  +\text{rational descent}
  +\text{Green-compatible dilation trace},
\]

not through the aggregated overlap.

## 6. Revised next target

Do not seek another determinant representation of \(X\). Construct the
minimal symmetric operator whose deficiency trace carries the two reciprocal
Weyl polarizations. Then ask whether the rational comb fixed line defines a
boundary relation in that trace space.

The first finite falsifier is a variance mismatch: if rational translation
or modulation fails to preserve the Green form of the proposed trace, the
comb cannot define a self-adjoint boundary condition even though its scalar
overlap and Poisson identity remain valid.

## 7. Scope

The one-dimensional projection determinant and graph-kernel equivalence are
exact. They show that a canonical incidence description already exists but
is too weak. No symmetric bulk operator, Green trace, maximal arithmetic
relation, Fredholm comparison, or RH result is supplied.
