# Reciprocal Euler ratio is the odd arithmetic boundary channel

## Question

The archimedean continuation packet has antisymmetric coordinate
\(z=s-1/2\).  What source-local arithmetic object transforms in the same odd
channel before scalar completion?

## Reciprocal local determinant ratio

The bordered prime block derives the local Euler denominator on the direct
valuation sector.  Comparing it with the reciprocal sector gives the local
transition

\[
\gamma_p(s)
=
\frac{1-p^{-s}}{1-p^{s-1}}.
\]

Put \(s=1/2+z\).  Then

\[
\gamma_p(z)
=
\frac{
1-p^{-1/2}e^{-z\log p}
}{
1-p^{-1/2}e^{z\log p}
}.
\]

It obeys

\[
\gamma_p(-z)=\gamma_p(z)^{-1}.
\]

On the critical seam \(z=it\), numerator and denominator are complex
conjugates, so

\[
|\gamma_p(it)|=1.
\]

Thus local seam unitarity is exactly the arithmetic realization of boundary
polarization reversal.

## Odd determinant cumulants

In the initial local logarithmic chamber,

\[
\log\gamma_p(z)
=
2\sum_{k\ge1}
\frac{p^{-k/2}}{k}
\sinh(kz\log p).
\]

Every cumulant is odd in \(z\).  The three current levels become

\[
A_{1,p}(z)
=
2p^{-1/2}\sinh(z\log p),
\]

\[
A_{2,p}(z)
=
p^{-1}\sinh(2z\log p),
\]

and

\[
A_{\ge3,p}(z)
=
2\sum_{k\ge3}
\frac{p^{-k/2}}{k}
\sinh(kz\log p).
\]

Therefore the primitive, square, and connected currents have not only
different regularity types; each carries a canonical odd reciprocal
polarization.

## Central convergence of the connected odd tail

After retaining the first two odd currents explicitly, define

\[
\gamma_p^{[3]}(z)
=
\gamma_p(z)
\exp\left(
-A_{1,p}(z)-A_{2,p}(z)
\right).
\]

Its logarithm is \(A_{\ge3,p}(z)\).  The product over primes converges
absolutely whenever

\[
|\Re z|<\frac16,
\]

because the worst connected term has \(k=3\).  Hence the ordinary odd
determinant tail already exists on a full strip around the critical seam.
Only the primitive and square polarizations require relative boundary
typing there.

## Exact match to the archimedean boundary channel

The completed continuation boundary transforms by

\[
z\longmapsto-z.
\]

The arithmetic transition transforms by inversion under the same operation.
At logarithmic level, both are odd.  Thus the two axes found previously are
not merely analogous:

- \(z\) is the archimedean antisymmetric boundary coordinate;
- \(\log\gamma_p(z)\) is its local arithmetic odd response;
- the three Tate currents are the regularity-graded components of that
  response.

This is the first exact pre-compression coupling between boundary orientation
and arithmetic determinant data.

## Hostile even multipliers remain invisible

Let \(H(z)=H(-z)\) be a reciprocal-even hostile factor.  Its transition ratio
is

\[
\frac{H(z)}{H(-z)}=1.
\]

Therefore multiplying the determinant section by \(H\) leaves every
\(\gamma_p\), every odd cumulant, and the complete reciprocal transition
unchanged.

This is a decisive scope boundary.  The odd arithmetic channel explains the
two-sector sewing and the critical seam, but transition data alone cannot
protect the divisor of the section.  An even hostile divisor lives in the
kernel of the transition functor.

## Remaining theorem

The source must select not only a reciprocal transition but a distinguished
section of its determinant line.  The missing rigidity statement is:

Every source-authorized change of the bordered prime and archimedean blocks
changes the completed section only by a nowhere-zero unit.

That statement must follow from the allowed local blocks, augmentation ports,
and cutoff-compatible completion.  It cannot be inferred from the functional
equation, since every reciprocal-even hostile factor preserves that equation.

The next finite test is to classify determinant-line sections generated by a
fixed collection of bordered prime blocks and the rank-two archimedean
boundary packet.  If an extra even polynomial factor can be produced without
adding a labelled block or changing a boundary port, canonical-section
rigidity fails.

## Result

The reciprocal Euler determinant ratio is the canonical odd arithmetic
boundary channel paired with \(z=s-1/2\).  Its cumulants recover the three
Tate current levels and its connected tail converges around the seam.  But all
reciprocal-even hostile divisors are invisible to this transition, so section
rigidity remains the decisive missing theorem.
