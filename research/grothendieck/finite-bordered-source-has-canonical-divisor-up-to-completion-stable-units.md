# Finite bordered source has canonical divisor up to completion-stable units

## Question

Reciprocal transition data cannot detect an even hostile divisor.  Can the
fixed bordered source system nevertheless determine its determinant section
uniquely up to a nowhere-zero factor?

## Finite source presentation

Let \(M_X(s)\) be a finite bordered matrix assembled from a fixed collection
of source-authorized blocks:

- prime valuation shifts;
- primitive vacuum columns;
- valuation augmentation rows;
- retained square-current ports;
- the two archimedean continuation boundary components.

The entries are holomorphic on a domain \(U\).  Its determinant section is

\[
\sigma_X(s)=\det M_X(s).
\]

No claim is made here that the final completed \(M_X\) has already been
constructed.  The theorem classifies the divisor once such a finite source
packet is fixed.

## Authorized finite presentation changes

Consider three operations.

### Invertible source-coordinate change

Let \(P_X(s)\) and \(Q_X(s)\) be holomorphic and invertible throughout \(U\).
Replace

\[
M_X
\longmapsto
P_XM_XQ_X.
\]

Then

\[
\det(P_XM_XQ_X)
=
\det P_X\det Q_X\sigma_X.
\]

Both additional determinants are nowhere zero on \(U\).

### Contractible stabilization

Adjoin a holomorphically invertible block \(C_X(s)\):

\[
M_X
\longmapsto
M_X\oplus C_X.
\]

Then

\[
\det(M_X\oplus C_X)
=
\det C_X\sigma_X,
\]

again changing the section only by a unit.

### Source-preserving relabelling

Permutation and signed orientation changes are special cases of invertible
coordinate changes.  Their determinants are constant units and cannot alter
the divisor.

## Finite canonical-divisor theorem

Any finite sequence of the preceding authorized operations changes
\(\sigma_X\) by a holomorphic nowhere-zero factor.  Consequently its zero
divisor is invariant.

A factor with a zero can enter only if at least one of the following occurs:

1. a coordinate change ceases to be invertible;
2. a noncontractible block is added;
3. a boundary port or source incidence is changed;
4. the determinant is regularized by deleting reconstruction data.

This classification does not define authorization by divisor preservation.
The allowed operations are independently specified source-coordinate and
contractible-complex moves; divisor preservation is their consequence.

## Hostile multiplier test

Let \(H\) be reciprocal-even and carry an off-seam zero quartet.  If

\[
\sigma_{H,X}=H\sigma_X,
\]

then \(H\) is not a unit on any domain containing that quartet.  Therefore
\(\sigma_{H,X}\) cannot arise from the authorized finite presentation changes.
It requires a new noncontractible block or a singular transformation.

The hostile multiplier is thus rejected at finite source level without using
the locations of zeros of \(\sigma_X\).

## Passage to completion

Let two cutoff presentations differ by units \(u_X(s)\):

\[
\widetilde\sigma_X(s)=u_X(s)\sigma_X(s).
\]

Choose a source basepoint \(s_*\) and normalize

\[
u_X(s_*)=1.
\]

Assume \(u_X\) converges locally uniformly on \(U\) to a holomorphic limit
\(u\).  Hurwitz's theorem implies that \(u\) is either nowhere zero or
identically zero.  The basepoint normalization gives \(u(s_*)=1\), so the
second case is impossible.  Hence \(u\) remains a unit.

Therefore completion-stable, normalized presentation equivalence cannot
manufacture a divisor.

## Exact remaining completion gate

Canonical-section rigidity can fail in the limit only through a concrete
completion defect:

- failure of local uniform convergence of the comparison units;
- loss of the basepoint normalization;
- singular limiting coordinate maps;
- a new completed kernel not generated by the finite core;
- collapse of the reduced minimum modulus.

These are precisely the completion failures already isolated independently.
The hostile divisor problem and the no-escape problem are therefore the same
gate viewed on determinant sections and operator graphs.

## Scope boundary

This theorem proves finite rigidity and its conditional persistence under
stable completion.  It does not yet prove:

- that the full bordered prime--archimedean system exists;
- that its completed relative determinant is \(\xi\);
- that its comparison units converge locally uniformly off the seam;
- that the completed operator has no off-seam kernel.

Those are now the only remaining places where a divisor could enter without
an explicit source block.

## Next construction

Assemble the finite-Euler direct sum of bordered prime blocks and the
rank-two archimedean boundary packet.  Normalize its determinant section at a
point in the Euler chamber.  Then prove cutoff comparison maps are invertible
and their determinant ratios form a locally normal family on each off-seam
compact set.

The sharp falsifier is a cutoff sequence whose normalized comparison units
lose local boundedness or whose operator graphs exhibit either a new kernel
or reduced-minimum-modulus collapse.

## Result

A fixed finite bordered source system has a canonical determinant divisor.
Authorized coordinate changes and contractible stabilizations alter its
section only by units, and locally uniform normalized completion preserves
that fact.  Hostile divisor insertion is therefore confined to explicit new
blocks or a measurable failure of completion stability.
