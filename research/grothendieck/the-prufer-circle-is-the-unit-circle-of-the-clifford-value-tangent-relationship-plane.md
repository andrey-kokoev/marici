# The Prüfer Circle Is the Unit Circle of the Clifford Value--Tangent Relationship Plane

## The actual two-plane

The geometrically relevant plane is not the complex spectral plane by itself.
It is the source relationship plane

\[
V(\lambda,x)
=H_\lambda(x)e_1+H_x(\lambda,x)e_2.
\]

Its two axes are the scalar readout and its tangent response. They are the two
channels that must vanish simultaneously at a Newman collision.

In the Euclidean Clifford algebra of this plane,

\[
V^{-1}dV
=
\frac{V\mathbin{\cdot}dV}{|V|^2}
+
\frac{V\mathbin{\wedge}dV}{|V|^2}.
\]

The scalar part is the logarithmic change of relationship magnitude. The
bivector part is its oriented angular change.

## Spatial and heat bivectors

For spatial transport,

\[
d_xV=H_xe_1+H_{xx}e_2,
\]

so

\[
V\mathbin{\wedge}d_xV
=(HH_{xx}-H_x^2)e_{12}.
\]

For Newman heat transport,

\[
d_\lambda V=-H_{xx}e_1-H_{xxx}e_2,
\]

and therefore

\[
V\mathbin{\wedge}d_\lambda V
=(H_xH_{xx}-HH_{xxx})e_{12}.
\]

These are exactly the two Prüfer-connection numerators from the preceding
packet. The connection is the normalized bivector component of one Clifford
logarithmic derivative.

## What the circle is

Whenever \(V\neq0\), normalize it:

\[
n=\frac{V}{|V|}\in S^1.
\]

This is the circle that appears after calibrating the value and tangent axes
to the same metric. An ellipse in an uncalibrated plot is merely the image of
this circle under unequal channel gains.

It is a circle of relationship directions:

- one point represents value-dominated comparison;
- a quarter-turn represents tangent-dominated comparison;
- winding records how the completed source rotates between those meanings.

It is not a circle on which the Riemann zeros themselves live.

## Meaning loss

At a simple zero of Xi,

\[
H=0,
\qquad
H_x\neq0.
\]

The relationship vector points along the tangent axis. Its scalar projection
vanishes, but the full comparison retains a perfectly defined direction.

At a double zero,

\[
H=H_x=0.
\]

Now \(V=0\), so neither normalization nor angular meaning exists. This is the
precise “loss of meaning” event the operator anticipated. It is not every
Riemann zero; it is the collision state through which a pair of zeros can
leave the seam under Newman heat.

The cubic split occurs because the relationship direction becomes undefined
at the origin and re-emerges on the other side with a different support type.

## The ninety-degree rotation

The conceptual quarter-turn is now literal:

\[
H
\longmapsto
H+iH_x.
\]

Instead of asking only where the scalar channel vanishes, retain its orthogonal
tangent channel and study the direction of their pair. The previously hidden
rigid structure is the Clifford rotor of this value--tangent plane.

This explains why scalar-zero reasoning repeatedly lost information and why
the first universal coupled theorem appeared only after both channels were
kept.

## Relation to the two reflected sectors

Even theta symmetry sends

\[
(H,H_x)(x)
\longmapsto
(H,-H_x)(-x).
\]

This is reflection of the relationship circle across the value axis. The two
spectral half-cells are therefore reflected Clifford charts. Their shared
center has \(H>0,H_x=0\), fixing the gluing direction to \(+e_1\).

A mirrored collision pair simultaneously loses direction in both charts and
then emits the complete off-seam quartet.

## What this does and does not prove

The Clifford formulation explains the carrier, the circle, the quarter-turn,
the meaning-loss event, and the quantized winding. It does not prevent the
relationship vector from reaching zero. The remaining theorem is still
source-specific nonreachability of the Clifford origin on the nonnegative
Newman strip, including its all-scale infinity boundary.
