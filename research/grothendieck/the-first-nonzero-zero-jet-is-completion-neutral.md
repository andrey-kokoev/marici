# The First Nonzero Zero Jet Is Completion-Neutral

## Product curvature

Let the completed section factor on the critical strip as

\[
X(s)=A(s)Z(s),
\]

where \(A\) is holomorphic and nowhere zero there. For the Riemann section,
\(A\) contains the polynomial and archimedean gamma factors, while \(Z\) is
the analytically continued arithmetic section.

For

\[
\mathscr C_f=ff''-(f')^2,
\]

the product rule gives

\[
\mathscr C_X
=
A^2\mathscr C_Z
+
Z^2\mathscr C_A.
\]

There is no mixed term.

## Evaluation at a zero

If \(Z(s_0)=0\), then

\[
\mathscr C_X(s_0)
=
A(s_0)^2\mathscr C_Z(s_0).
\]

For a simple zero,

\[
\mathscr C_X(s_0)
=
-A(s_0)^2Z'(s_0)^2.
\]

The primitive archimedean curvature has disappeared because its coefficient
is \(Z(s_0)^2\).

Thus the completed aggregation does not couple arithmetic and archimedean
curvature pointwise at a zero. It becomes purely arithmetic, multiplied by a
nonzero change of frame.

## Higher multiplicity

Suppose \(Z\) has multiplicity \(m\) at \(s_0\). Then

\[
X^{(j)}(s_0)=0
\qquad
(j<m),
\]

and

\[
X^{(m)}(s_0)=A(s_0)Z^{(m)}(s_0).
\]

Therefore the first nonzero jet invariant detecting the zero is also changed
only by the nowhere-zero scalar \(A(s_0)\). Derivatives of the completion
factor enter only after the divisor and its multiplicity have already been
detected.

## Consequence

No pointwise curvature or first-detecting jet calculation at a hypothetical
zero can explain why the zero lies on the critical seam. Archimedean
completion preserves the divisor and supplies a frame; it does not exert a
local force at the zero.

Any RH-bearing use of completion must be nonlocal. It must constrain:

- the transport of the section before it reaches zero;
- the phase or winding of a neighborhood;
- the motion of zeros under source completion;
- or a global boundary-value problem whose admissible state exists only on
  the seam.

This formally separates two statements:

1. completion determines the reciprocal geometry and the critical coordinate;
2. zero confinement requires a global trajectory or boundary law.

The first cannot be promoted to the second by evaluating more local
derivatives at the zero.

## Falsifier

The statement would fail if the completion factor vanished or had a pole at
the candidate nontrivial zero. It is explicitly restricted to a domain where
\(A\) is holomorphic and nowhere zero.
