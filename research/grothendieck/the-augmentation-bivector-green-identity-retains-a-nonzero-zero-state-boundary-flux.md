# The augmentation-bivector Green identity retains a nonzero zero-state boundary flux

## Exterior tail dynamics

Let the labelwise tail satisfy

\[
G'(q)=-sG(q)-f(q),
\]

and let `Omega` be the augmentation vector. Define

\[
W=\Omega\wedge G,
\qquad
h=\Omega\wedge f.
\]

Because `Omega` is constant,

\[
W'=-sW-h.
\]

Taking the derivative of the bivector norm gives

\[
\frac{d}{dq}\lVert W\rVert^2
=-2\operatorname{Re}(s)\lVert W\rVert^2
-2\operatorname{Re}\langle h,W\rangle.
\]

For a decaying tail, integration yields

\[
2\operatorname{Re}(s)
\int_0^\infty\lVert W\rVert^2\,dq
=\lVert W(0)\rVert^2
-2\operatorname{Re}
\int_0^\infty\langle h,W\rangle\,dq.
\]

## Why scalar nullity does not close the flux

The scalar zero-state condition is

\[
\langle\Omega,G(0)\rangle=0.
\]

On that subspace,

\[
\lVert W(0)\rVert^2
=\lVert\Omega\rVert^2\lVert G(0)\rVert^2.
\]

Thus the exterior observer's endpoint flux is nonzero precisely when it
successfully detects a nontrivial scalar cancellation. Faithfulness and
vanishing boundary flux pull in opposite directions unless another
source-derived current is present.

## Canonical-tail off-seam witness

Take two label fibers,

\[
\Omega=(1,1),
\qquad
v=(1,-1),
\qquad
s=\frac12,
\]

and set

\[
G(q)=e^{-3q/2}v,
\qquad
f(q)=e^{-3q/2}v.
\]

Then `G'=-sG-f`, and `G` is the canonical tail generated by `f`:

\[
G(q)=\int_q^\infty f(t)e^{s(t-q)}\,dt.
\]

The scalar augmentation vanishes for every `q`, while

\[
\lVert W(0)\rVert^2=4,
\qquad
\int_0^\infty\lVert W\rVert^2\,dq=\frac43.
\]

Because `h=W`, the Green identity reads

\[
\frac43=4-2\left(\frac43\right).
\]

It holds exactly with positive off-seam real part.

## Consequence

The augmentation bivector solves the cross-fiber observability problem but
does not solve zero confinement. Its native Green law admits off-seam
scalar-null tails because the surviving endpoint relationship energy is
balanced by the forcing overlap.

The next valid theorem must construct an additional modular or arithmetic
current that changes this balance in the actual theta system. It must do one
of the following without being fitted from the zero:

1. identify the forcing overlap as a derivative of retained primitive,
   square, connected, seam, and archimedean currents;
2. cancel the endpoint bivector flux through reciprocal-sheet sewing; or
3. prove a source boundary law tying the two terms so their difference
   vanishes only on the seam.

Simply declaring the exterior energy positive cannot work.
