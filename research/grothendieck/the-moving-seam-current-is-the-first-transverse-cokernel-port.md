# The moving-seam current is the first transverse cokernel port

## From a principal cube to a relative class

Every finite prime-exclusion value port belongs to the principal spectral
module generated by `X`. To obtain information at a zero, one must pass to a
class not divisible by `X`.

The already source-derived two-sector split provides exactly such a class.
Let `U` and `V` be the two reciprocal sector amplitudes. Define

\[
X=U+V,
\qquad
R=U-V.
\]

The matrix

\[
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}
\]

has determinant `-2`, so the common and relative channels together retain the
full pair. The scalar readout keeps only the codiagonal channel `X`. At a
scalar zero,

\[
V=-U,
\qquad
R=2U,
\]

and the relative channel remains visible unless both sectors vanish.

## Exact two-atom quotient

For positive source weights `a,b` at ordered positions `0,1`, write

\[
X(w)=a+bw.
\]

The oriented moving-seam autocorrelation current is

\[
J(w)=ab(w-w^{-1}).
\]

At the scalar zero `w=-a/b`,

\[
J(-a/b)=b^2-a^2.
\]

Therefore `J` is not divisible by `X` in the Laurent polynomial ring unless
the source is balanced. Its residue class

\[
[J]\in k[w,w^{-1}]/(X)
\]

is the first explicit spectral channel surviving the collapse of the
prime-exclusion cube.

For positive `a,b`, this class vanishes exactly when `a=b`. In that balanced
case the zero satisfies `|w|=1`; in the imbalanced case the zero lies off the
unitary seam and the relative class is nonzero.

## Categorical meaning

The value-only prime ports form the subobject `(X)`. The relative current lives
in the quotient by that subobject:

\[
0\longrightarrow (X)
\longrightarrow \mathcal M
\longrightarrow \mathcal Q
\longrightarrow0.
\]

Prime exclusions act inside `(X)` and therefore disappear on the zero fiber.
The moving-seam current maps nontrivially to `Q`. This is the precise version
of “one thing was actually two”: scalar aggregation identifies the common
channel and discards its relative mate.

## What this does and does not prove

This gives the required transverse observation channel. It does not exclude
off-seam zeros: the hostile two-atom source has precisely such a zero together
with a nonzero relative class.

The remaining RH-bearing theorem is now narrower. The completed theta
zero-state must satisfy an independently derived boundary law forcing the
total relative class to vanish. If that law holds, the two-atom calculation
shows why imbalance—and hence off-seam cancellation—is impossible. Without
that law, the cokernel port merely diagnoses the forbidden state after it has
been constructed.

The next attack is therefore not another observation port. It is the
zero-state boundary map

\[
X(z)=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
[J_{\rm total}(z)]=0,
\]

derived from completed modular sewing rather than asserted as a positivity
condition.
