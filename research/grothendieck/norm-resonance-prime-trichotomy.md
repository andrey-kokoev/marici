# Norm and resonance primes obey a strict trichotomy

## Prime-local theorem

For a finite surjection with bidegree `(d,rho)`, one always has

`rad(d) | rho`.

At a prime `p`, exactly one of three regimes occurs:

1. `p` divides neither `d` nor `rho`: the coefficient norm scalar is a
   `p`-local unit and there is no `p`-resonance obstruction;
2. `p` divides both: the norm is nonunit and the power--Mackey spectrum is
   obstructed;
3. `p` divides `rho` but not `d`: the norm is a unit, but conjugation still
   obstructs the power--Mackey spectrum.

The fourth logical cell, norm-bad but resonance-good, is impossible.

## Localization does not repair resonance-only primes

Inverting `d` repairs the scalar pull--push norm, but it need not repair the
basis-level power correspondence. For `A4->C3`, `(d,rho)=(4,6)`. At prime
three, four is a unit while the order-three conjugation action still makes
the third-power fiber word nonbijective.

Thus normalized transfer after degree localization and arithmetic Mackey
compatibility are distinct repairs.

## Scope

This is a coefficient-local statement. No localization manufactures the
missing physical relative-chain pushforward.
