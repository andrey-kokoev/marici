# The Log Gram Radius Has a Lorentzian Radial--Angular Balance, Not a Positive Entropy Law

## Gram radius and Clifford rates

For

\[
G=H+iH_x,
\qquad
R=|G|^2=H^2+H_x^2,
\]

define the spatial radial and angular rates

\[
\kappa_x=\partial_x\log|G|
=\frac{HH_x+H_xH_{xx}}{H^2+H_x^2},
\]

\[
\omega_x=\partial_x\arg G
=\frac{HH_{xx}-H_x^2}{H^2+H_x^2}.
\]

They are the scalar and bivector parts of the Clifford logarithmic derivative
\(V^{-1}\partial_xV\).

## Exact balance and scope correction

Because \(G\) obeys the backward heat equation, direct differentiation gives

\[
(\partial_\lambda+\partial_x^2)\log R
=2(\omega_x^2-\kappa_x^2).
\]

The right side is not nonnegative. It is the split-signature difference
between angular relationship motion and radial relationship compression.

This corrects the tempting but false identity with only \(2\omega_x^2\).
The missing term is not an error estimate; it is the scalar grade of the same
Clifford connection.

## Why the sign matters

The universal collision has infinite local angular action. On the wedge

\[
0<\tau\leq\frac14,
\qquad
0\leq x\leq\tau,
\]

\[
|\omega_x|\geq\frac1{2\tau},
\]

so the angular square diverges logarithmically over dyadic shells.

But the log-radius balance controls only

\[
\omega_x^2-\kappa_x^2.
\]

Near the Clifford origin the radial rate is singular as well. The two
infinities can cancel in the boundary balance. Generic polynomial Newman
collisions demonstrate that they actually can; finite outer boundary data do
not exclude the interior origin.

Therefore a finite bound on the signed log-radius balance is not a collision
barrier.
