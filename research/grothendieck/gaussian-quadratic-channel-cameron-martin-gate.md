# Gaussian exponentiation forces the half but fails at the critical prime norm

For finitely many independent standard real Gaussian variables `g_p` and a
source vector `x=(x_p)`, the moment-generating identity is

```
E exp(sum_p x_p g_p) = exp[(1/2)sum_p x_p^2].           (1)
```

Thus Gaussian/Fock exponentiation derives, rather than chooses, the
coefficient `1/2` in the quadratic Euler channel. With `x_p=p^(-s)`, its
bilinear second cumulant is

```
(1/2)sum_p p^(-2s)=C_2(s).                             (2)
```

This is the first positive structural match for the exact local exponential.
It also explains why no finite algebraic torsion could produce it: the
exponential is generated by summing arbitrarily many Gaussian contractions.

## Cameron--Martin obstruction

A genuine Gaussian linear observable on the positive one-particle Hilbert
space requires `x` to be square summable. On the critical line,

```
||x||^2=sum_p |p^(-1/2-iT)|^2=sum_p 1/p=infinity.      (3)
```

The oscillatory height phase disappears from the Hermitian norm. Therefore
the critical prime source is not a Cameron--Martin vector at any height, and
the unsmoothed Gaussian random variable in (1) does not exist in the ordinary
Fock representation.

This distinguishes two quadratic expressions that agree only in the real
convergent region:

```
bilinear cumulant:  sum_p x_p^2 = sum_p p^(-2s),
positive norm:      sum_p |x_p|^2 = sum_p p^(-2 Re s). (4)
```

The first contains arithmetic phase; the second controls existence and
diverges on the critical line.

## Wick ordering does not restore the missing channel

At finite cutoff, the Wick exponential is

```
:exp(<x,g>): = exp(<x,g>-(1/2)||x||^2),                (5)
```

and has expectation one. Wick ordering makes the Fock observable finite by
subtracting the quadratic self-contraction. But that self-contraction is
exactly the channel `C_2` we are trying to reconstruct. Ordinary Wick
normalization therefore deletes rather than explains the desired anomaly.

Heat smoothing repairs the Hilbert norm. For `tau>0`,

```
x_(p,tau)=p^(-1/2-iT) exp[-tau(log p)^2/2]              (6)
```

is square summable and defines a Gaussian observable. Yet its positive
variance is independent of `T` and diverges as `tau` tends to zero. A source
law must identify a gamma--endpoint counterterm that cancels this divergence
while retaining the bilinear arithmetic phase. Choosing the finite part from
analytic continuation of zeta would be circular.

## Relation to the coefficient--Betti symplectic double

The perfect coefficient--Betti pairing canonically supplies an algebraic
symplectic double and hence the precursor of canonical commutation relations.
It does not select the positive covariance, Fock vacuum, or Cameron--Martin
completion. Those previously open choices are now seen to carry the entire
quadratic renormalization problem.

## Falsifiers

A Gaussian proposal fails if it:

1. treats the critical prime sequence as an ordinary Fock vector;
2. uses the bilinear sum `sum x_p^2` as though it were the positive norm;
3. Wick-orders away the quadratic contraction and later reintroduces it by
   analytic continuation;
4. uses heat smoothing without a source-derived, cutoff-compatible removal
   of the `tau->0` variance divergence;
5. claims that the algebraic symplectic double canonically selects a vacuum.

## Revised target

The surviving Gaussian route is a relative Fock construction: prime and
archimedean Gaussian sectors must share a covariance so that their divergent
positive self-contractions cancel in the completed relative partition
function, while the signed bilinear cumulant (2) remains. The cancellation
must be proved before taking the critical boundary value.

This gate derives the half coefficient and rejects the naïve positive Fock
model. It does not construct the relative covariance, Xi, or an RH proof.
