# Character-orbit desmoothing is exact but endpoint-zero blind

## Exact restriction to the authorized orbit

For the half-line character

\[
e_z(a)=e^{za},
\]

the Hankel history state is

\[
G_z=\mathsf H_fe_z.
\]

Whenever (\operatorname{Re}z<0), the character belongs to
(L^2(0,\infty)), with

\[
\|e_z\|_2^2=\frac{-1}{2\operatorname{Re}z}.
\]

Thus on this source-authorized orbit,

\[
\mathsf H_f^{-1}G_z=e_z
\]

is exact and pointwise finite throughout the open sector. Its norm diverges
only as the unitary seam is approached.

The reciprocal chart supplies the corresponding statement in the opposite
sector.

## Why this still does not confine zeros

The physical scalar is

\[
F(z)=E_0G_z.
\]

An endpoint zero imposes one boundary condition

\[
G_z(0)=0.
\]

It does not imply (G_z=0), (e_z=0), or failure of the inverse relation.
Therefore exact graph control of the unbounded desmoothing on each character
state is compatible with an off-seam endpoint cancellation.

The finite positive Hankel hostile already realizes this pattern: its
character state and full history remain nonzero while the endpoint matrix
coefficient vanishes away from the seam.

## Correction to the desmoothing DPC

Unbounded Hankel inversion explains why positivity of the source-adjoint
curvature face cannot be transferred by a bounded Hilbert operator to the
physical endpoint face. But merely constructing or controlling the inverse on
the character orbit does not prove zero confinement.

The RH-bearing theorem must additionally couple the direct and reciprocal
desmoothed characters through a boundary law. It must show that simultaneous
membership in the completed two-ended endpoint domain is possible only when

\[
\operatorname{Re}z=0.
\]

This law must use information absent from a single Hankel chart, such as
Fourier–Tate sewing, endpoint-current incidence, or a global conserved
boundary form.

## Falsifier

Any proposed desmoothing theorem that is satisfied independently by each
one-sided character chart, without a reciprocal cross-chart condition, is
zero-blind and cannot imply RH.
