# The endpoint–bulk projective orbit is an affine repackaging of xi

## Exact bordered decomposition

The source-derived bordered matrix has determinant

\[
\det\mathcal M_s=1+s(s-1)H(s)=2\xi(s).
\]

With (z=s-1/2) and (X(z)=\xi(1/2+z)), define

\[
B(z)=\frac12\left(z^2-\frac14\right)H(1/2+z).
\]

Then

\[
X(z)=\frac12+B(z).
\]

The endpoint–bulk packet is therefore

\[
v(z)=\left(\frac12,B(z)\right),
\qquad
\ell(e,b)=e+b.
\]

Its projective coordinate in the endpoint chart is

\[
r(z)=\frac{B(z)}{1/2}=2X(z)-1.
\]

## Projective no-go

The scalar zero condition is

\[
X(z)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
r(z)=-1.
\]

Because (X\mapsto r=2X-1) is an affine bijection, the two-coordinate
projective orbit contains exactly the same information as the scalar xi
function. Any theorem asserting that this orbit avoids the incidence
hyperplane off the seam is precisely the desired zero-free theorem in renamed
coordinates.

Its projective velocity adds no independent structure:

\[
r'(z)=2X'(z).
\]

Likewise, its endpoint–bulk blade is

\[
v(z)\wedge v'(z)=\frac12B'(z),e\wedge b,
\]

which is just a fixed multiple of (X'(z)). A signed-angle or cone condition
derived only after this aggregation is therefore circular unless an
independent source law proves it.

## Consequence

The endpoint border is essential for deriving the completed scalar and for
showing that a scalar zero is not packet annihilation. But it is too compressed
to explain the zero location.

The first nontrivial projective object must retain at least the labelled heat
channels before their sum. There the scalar codiagonal has a genuine kernel,
and source operations can constrain motion inside that kernel without being
recoverable from xi alone.

The next attack should use

\[
V(z)=\left(e,(B_n(z))_{n\geq1}\right),
\qquad
X(z)=\ell(V(z)),
\]

with integer labels, Poisson sewing, and the heat generator retained. The
question is whether the labelled Plücker coordinates or variation cone obey a
source-derived evolution law that the scalar affine chart forgets.

## Durable boundary

Do not seek RH force from the projective geometry of
((1/2,X-1/2)). It is a faithful visualization of xi, not an explanation.
Only a pre-codiagonal labelled geometry can add information.
