# Reciprocal prime pairing cancels the seam but windows converge to the unit

## Finite-label reciprocal sewing

For the centered Gaussian tail \(K=H-1/2\), reflection satisfies

\[
RK=-K
\]

and conjugates translation by \(L\) to translation by \(-L\). Therefore

\[
W_L
=
\tau_LK+R\tau_LK
=g_L-g_{-L}
=H(q+L)-H(q-L).
\]

The two boundary quotient classes cancel exactly. The remainder is the even
bulk window

\[
W_L(q)
=-
\int_{q-L}^{q+L}e^{-\pi v^2}\,dv.
\]

For every finite \(L\), this function has Gaussian decay at both ends and
belongs to the archimedean bulk rigging. Its Fourier transform is the smooth
even function

\[
\widehat W_L(\xi)
=-
\frac{\sin(2\pi L\xi)}{\pi\xi}
e^{-\pi\xi^2}.
\]

The reciprocal two-sector object therefore performs a real cancellation:
it converts two affine seam carriers into one ordinary bulk comparison
window before scalar aggregation.

## The completion obstruction

The cancellation is not uniform in the label. For fixed \(q\),

\[
\lim_{L\to\infty}W_L(q)=-1.
\]

At a prime label \(L=\log p\), large primes produce broader windows that are
nearly the negative unit on every fixed compact interval. Each individual
window is bulk-decaying, but the family escapes every fixed Gaussian step as
\(p\to\infty\).

Thus restricted-product completion can recreate a constant boundary mode
from a sequence of finite-label bulk vectors. This is the same structural
danger as finite exact complexes whose contracting partners escape to
infinite norm.

## Arithmetic consequence

Pairing reciprocal sectors before the prime sum is necessary but not
sufficient. It removes the quotient coefficient at each finite label, yet
the resulting window family has no uniform bulk bound. Primitive and square
weights must control this widening explicitly.

For a finite cutoff \(X\), the paired arithmetic bulk is

\[
\sum_{p\in X}a_pW_{\log p}.
\]

On compact \(q\)-sets its large-prime behavior resembles

\[
-\sum_{p\in X}a_p.
\]

Hence the original arithmetic boundary divergence reappears as failure of
uniform bulk convergence. The seam has not disappeared; it has migrated from
an explicit quotient coordinate into the completion behavior of widening
windows.

## Meaning

This explains why finite-cutoff coherence can coexist with a global defect.
At every finite prime, reciprocal sewing produces a legitimate Gaussian
bulk element. Only the directed family remembers that the supports widen
without bound.

The correct global object must therefore retain the window scale as a label
or equip the bulk extension with a topology that detects compact convergence
to the unit. Declaring every \(W_L\) to be merely another Gaussian bulk state
forgets the completion-sensitive capability.

## Falsifier and next gate

Any proposed restricted-product topology fails if

\[
W_{\log p}\longrightarrow-1
\]

locally while it declares the same family to vanish or remain uniformly
Gaussian. The next gate is to test the primitive and square coefficient
grades against this exact widening-window family and determine whether their
reciprocal combination defines a boundary-bearing limit rather than an
ordinary bulk sum.

## Result

Reciprocal prime pairing cancels the five-cell seam class at every finite
label and produces an even Gaussian bulk window. As prime labels go to
infinity, those windows converge locally to the negative unit and escape
every fixed Gaussian step. The global obstruction is therefore a
nonuniform-completion anomaly, not a failure of finite reciprocal sewing.
