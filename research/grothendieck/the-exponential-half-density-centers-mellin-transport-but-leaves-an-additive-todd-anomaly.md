# The exponential half-density centers Mellin transport but leaves an additive Todd anomaly

## The unitary exponential pullback

Pass from the positive coordinate \(x\) to logarithmic scale

\[
x=e^q.
\]

The unitary comparison from \(L^2(\mathbb R_+,dx)\) to
\(L^2(\mathbb R,dq)\) is

\[
(Uf)(q)=e^{q/2}f(e^q).
\]

The exponent \(1/2\) is forced by the Jacobian \(dx=e^q\,dq\):

\[
\int_0^\infty|f(x)|^2\,dx
=
\int_{\mathbb R}|Uf(q)|^2\,dq.
\]

## Exact Mellin centering

For

\[
\mathcal Mf(s)=\int_0^\infty f(x)x^{s-1}\,dx,
\]

substitution gives

\[
\mathcal Mf(s)
=
\int_{\mathbb R}(Uf)(q)e^{(s-1/2)q}\,dq.
\]

Thus the centered spectral coordinate

\[
z=s-\frac12
\]

is not inferred from zeros. It is the character coordinate induced by the
unitary exponential pullback. The critical line \(\Re s=1/2\) is exactly the
Fourier axis \(\Re z=0\).

## The commuting multiplicative generator

Let

\[
E=x\frac{d}{dx}
\]

be the dilation generator. Direct conjugation gives

\[
UEU^{-1}=\frac{d}{dq}-\frac12,
\]

and therefore

\[
U\left(E+\frac12\right)U^{-1}=\frac{d}{dq}.
\]

The same Jacobian half-density that centers the Mellin character also
centers multiplicative dilation into ordinary translation.

## The additive Todd anomaly

The additive Euler–Maclaurin generator does not obey that square. Instead,

\[
U\frac{d}{dx}U^{-1}
=
e^{-q}\left(\frac{d}{dq}-\frac12\right).
\]

The factor \(e^{-q}\) is unavoidable. It records that an additive unit step
in \(x\) has scale-dependent size in \(q=\log x\).

Consequently, the Todd half-cell normalization and the Mellin half-density
are related but not identical:

- the Mellin half is the Jacobian correction for multiplicative scale;
- the Todd half centers an additive lattice cell;
- their comparison carries the nonconstant anomaly \(e^{-q}\).

## The two-axis cell

The correct next object has both axes:

- additive lattice transport generated by \(d/dx\);
- multiplicative scale transport generated by \(E=x,d/dx\);
- exponential-coordinate pullback \(U\);
- the comparison anomaly \(e^{-q}\);
- the endpoint current created where this anomaly is not invertible or
  uniformly controlled.

This is the operator-level version of the previously derived scale–valuation
Beck–Chevalley cell. The new calculation identifies its archimedean
infinitesimal coefficient exactly.

## What is now explained

The offset \(1/2\) of the critical line has a direct source meaning: it is
the half-density required to turn multiplicative Haar transport into
unitary translation on logarithmic scale. This explains why the two
half-planes become symmetric around \(1/2\).

It does not explain why zeros are confined to that line. The remaining RH
content is whether the additive arithmetic boundary currents and the
multiplicative unitary transport form a completed two-axis conservation law
whose only scalar-null states lie on the Fourier axis.

## Sharp next gate

Insert the centered Todd germ as a functional calculus of the two generators
and compute the comparison residual:

\[
U\,\widetilde\tau(D_x)\,U^{-1}
-
\widetilde\tau(D_q).
\]

Because \(UD_xU^{-1}=e^{-q}(D_q-1/2)\), this residual is generally nonzero
and ordering-sensitive. The source-derived ordering must reproduce the
primitive, square, and archimedean boundary currents already found. A
residual with no such typed destination falsifies this pullback route.

## Result

The exponential half-density gives an exact naturality theorem for centered
multiplicative dilation and Mellin transport. It does not make additive Todd
translation commute with Mellin transport; the exact residual is the
scale-dependent multiplier \(e^{-q}\). The next level is therefore a
two-axis operator correspondence, not another scalar normalization.
