# Audit of the representation-theoretic Weyl-to-Cayley boundary lift

## What is canonical

With the global additive character and self-dual Haar normalization fixed, the
regular irreducible adelic Heisenberg representation is unique up to unitary
equivalence. The distributions fixed by every rational translation and
modulation form exactly the line `C Delta_Q`; Fourier fixes its normalized
comb generator. Thus both the ambient Weyl carrier and rational boundary
distribution are source-rigid.

## Cyclicity correction

The rational Weyl stabilizer fixes `Delta_Q`. Its algebraic orbit is therefore
one-dimensional, so the comb is not cyclic for that stabilizer. Cyclic or
separating generation would require a genuinely noncommuting transverse Weyl
polarization. No source theorem currently proves the needed completed joint
orbit is dense in the relevant rigged boundary carrier.

## Cayley-phase nonuniqueness

Even granting a maximal abelian rational boundary algebra `M`, the Cayley
boundary phase is not selected. In `M=L^infinity(Y,mu)`, every measurable unit
phase defines a different Cayley self-adjoint generator. An exact two-atom
fixture supplies two unequal self-adjoint generators in the same algebra with
the same cyclic measure class and different scalar spectral polynomials.

Hence integrality determines commuting observables, not the phase gluing the
reciprocal sectors.

## Conditional rigidity and missing implication

For a complete irreducible pair of noncommuting Weyl polarizations, the joint
commutant is scalar; theta normalization would then remove that scalar. This is
an exact finite Schur-rigidity theorem. Its adelic use requires a source theorem
that every admissible boundary selector intertwines both full Weyl actions.
That implication is not constructed and cannot be imposed as the definition
of admissibility.

## Decision

The Weyl route canonically selects the representation and comb but not a
closed Cayley boundary relation or logarithmic Dirac extension. The first
remaining object is a source-derived boundary selector with proved
full-Weyl covariance (or an equivalent transverse comparison law). After that,
closedness/maximal self-adjointness and determinant identification remain
separate gates.
