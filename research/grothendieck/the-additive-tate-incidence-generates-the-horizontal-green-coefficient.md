# The Additive Tate Incidence Generates the Horizontal Green Coefficient

## Frozen source domain

Let

\[
(R_af)(q)
=
e^{-aq}\int_q^\infty f(v)e^{av}\,dv
\]

on a common test domain where the tail integral, additive differentiation,
and endpoint traces are defined. Introduce the half-density pullback of
additive differentiation

\[
P=e^{-q}\left(\partial_q-\frac12\right).
\]

This is the source-derived incidence from the even additive Tate port to the
odd port. It is fixed before endpoint compression.

## The Mellin-grade-shifted mate square

Direct integration by parts gives

\[
R_sPf
=
-e^{-q}f
-
\left(s-\frac12\right)e^{-q}R_{s-1}f.
\]

Applying \(P\) after the shifted tail propagation gives the same expression.
Therefore

\[
R_sP=PR_{s-1}.
\]

Writing \(z=s-\tfrac12\) and

\[
G_z=R_{z-1/2}f,
\qquad
H_z=PG_z=R_{z+1/2}Pf,
\]

the odd response is

\[
H_z=-e^{-q}(f+zG_z).
\]

The even--odd incidence is consequently not an endomorphism at one Mellin
grade. It connects adjacent grades centered symmetrically around the critical
half-density.

## Native Green current

On the frozen real source domain, \(P\) is formally skew-adjoint. More
precisely,

\[
(Pu)\overline v+u\overline{Pv}
=
\partial_q\left(e^{-q}u\overline v\right).
\]

Setting \(u=v=G_z\) and integrating over the positive ray yields

\[
2\operatorname{Re}\int_0^\infty H_z\overline{G_z}\,dq
=
-|G_z(0)|^2,
\]

assuming the infinity trace vanishes. Substitution of the exact odd response
then gives

\[
2\operatorname{Re}z
\int_0^\infty e^{-q}|G_z|^2\,dq
+
2\operatorname{Re}
\int_0^\infty e^{-q}f\overline{G_z}\,dq
=
|G_z(0)|^2.
\]

Thus the horizontal coefficient \(2\operatorname{Re}z\) is generated by the
source incidence and its Green formula. It is not inserted through an
endpoint form.

## Zero-state reduction

At a scalar zero \(G_z(0)=0\), the identity becomes

\[
2\operatorname{Re}z
\int_0^\infty e^{-q}|G_z|^2\,dq
=
-2\operatorname{Re}
\int_0^\infty e^{-q}f\overline{G_z}\,dq.
\]

The positive relationship energy is now explicit, but the forcing pairing
need not vanish. The archimedean incidence therefore generates the correct
dynamic coefficient without proving zero confinement.

## Scope and falsifier

This theorem is archimedean and one-sided. It does not yet construct the
finite-prime incidence or prove completion stability.

The smallest falsifier is the prime-two replay. Construct the source-authorized
finite incidence \(P_{\mathrm{fin}}\) and compare both orders of the
tail/seam square. A nonzero typed residual which is not the derivative of a
declared primitive, square, seam, or archimedean boundary current closes this
conservation route. The next replay is the two-prime packet \(\{2,3\}\),
where mixed-seam terms first become visible.
