# Three positive half-line atoms admit off-seam common zeros

## Hostile source

Fix (d>0) and put positive masses (1,3,1) at (0,d,2d) on the positive
half-line. Its transform is

\[
H_+(z)=1+3e^{dz}+e^{2dz}.
\]

Writing (y=e^{dz}) gives the palindromic polynomial

\[
P(y)=1+3y+y^2.
\]

Because

\[
y^2P(1/y)=P(y),
\]

the reflected half-line transform (H_+(-z)) vanishes at every zero of
(H_+(z)).

## Exact off-seam zeros

The roots are

\[
y_\pm=\frac{-3\pm\sqrt5}{2},
\qquad
y_+y_-=1.
\]

They are negative real reciprocal numbers, neither of modulus one. Choosing
the logarithm with imaginary part (pi) gives common zeros

\[
z_\pm=\frac{\log|y_\pm|+i\pi}{d},
\]

whose real parts are nonzero and opposite.

Reflecting the three positive atoms across the origin produces a positive even
source. Thus positivity, evenness, reciprocal reflection, and the faithful
two-quadrature lift all coexist with off-seam common zeros.

## What this falsifies

The implication from a positive even source with a faithful half-line lift to
off-seam common-zero exclusion is false.

The obstruction is not loss of the odd quadrature. It is a reciprocal
palindromic relation inside the full two-channel object itself.

## New target

The actual theta theorem must forbid this palindromic three-atom mechanism by
an additional source law. The candidate law must come from modular scale
transport, the heat relation, or labelled prime/archimedean sewing; positivity
and reflection cannot supply it.

The smallest useful audit is now to identify which exact theta constructor is
violated by the packet (1,3,1), without referring to its zeros.
