# No finite theta-label packet is closed under modular sewing

Author: `marici.Grothendieck`

## Question

Can the reciprocal-sector conservation conjecture be attacked first on a
finite set of theta labels while preserving modular completion exactly?

The answer is no for every literal finite label cutoff.  This is an exact
source-level obstruction, not a numerical warning.

## 1. Completed theta source

Write

\[
 \Theta(t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}e^{-\pi n^2t}
 =1+2\sum_{n\ge1}e^{-\pi n^2t},
 \qquad t>0.
\]

Poisson summation gives the modular sewing law

\[
 \Theta(t)=t^{-1/2}\Theta(1/t).
\]

For a finite set (S\subset\mathbb N), the literal labelled truncation is

\[
 \Theta_S(t)=1+2\sum_{n\in S}e^{-\pi n^2t}.
\]

## 2. Finite-label no-go theorem

**Theorem.**  No finite (S\subset\mathbb N), including the empty set,
satisfies

\[
 \Theta_S(t)=t^{-1/2}\Theta_S(1/t)
\]

for every (t>0).

**Proof.**  As (t\to\infty),

\[
 \Theta_S(t)\longrightarrow1.
\]

Because (S) is finite,

\[
 t^{-1/2}\Theta_S(1/t)
 =t^{-1/2}\left(1+2|S|+O(t^{-1})\right)
 \longrightarrow0.
\]

The two limits disagree. \(\square\)

The same argument rules out sewing two different finite packets:

\[
 \Theta_S(t)=t^{-1/2}\Theta_T(1/t),
 \qquad |S|,|T|<\infty.
\]

The obstruction is not failure to choose a sufficiently clever finite set.
Poisson reflection is globally label-mixing: the infinitely many labels are
what turn the small-(t) side into a term of order (t^{-1/2}).

## 3. Tail formulation gives the same obstruction

For

\[
 \psi(t)=\sum_{n\ge1}e^{-\pi n^2t},
\]

the completed relation is

\[
 \psi(t)=t^{-1/2}\psi(1/t)+\frac{t^{-1/2}-1}{2}.
\]

If (psi) is replaced by a finite (psi_S), the left side tends to zero
as (t\to\infty), whereas the right side tends to (-1/2).  Hence retaining
the familiar endpoint correction does not repair a finite label cutoff.

This identifies the endpoint unit as an infinite-source boundary current.  It
cannot be attached consistently to a finite theta packet as though it were an
independent label.

## 4. Consequence for the proposed finite Fredholm attack

A determinant (T_s^{(N)}) built by first imposing (n\le N) cannot be an
exact finite model of completed modular sewing.  Any boundary form obtained
from it contains a regulator defect.  Its smallest generalized eigenvalue may
measure that defect rather than off-seam coercivity of the completed source.

Therefore the proposed order

\[
 \text{finite labels}\to\text{completion}\to\text{energy identity}
\]

is inadmissible.  Completion and truncation do not commute.

## 5. The surviving finite category: completion first, compression second

Let the still-to-be-constructed completed reciprocal Carrier be

\[
 (\mathcal H,J,T_s,\Omega),
\]

where (J) is modular reflection, (T_s) is the Fredholm comparison, and
(Omega) carries the endpoint/vacuum datum.  A finite compression may be
called admissible only after verifying

\[
 E_N^2=E_N=E_N^*,\qquad E_NJ=JE_N,\qquad E_N\Omega=\Omega,
\]

together with

\[
 E_N\longrightarrow I\quad\text{strongly}.
\]

One then compresses the already completed comparison:

\[
 T_s^{(N)}=E_NT_sE_N\big|_{E_N\mathcal H}.
\]

These conditions preserve reciprocal typing and the endpoint channel.  They
do **not** yet prove determinant convergence or coercivity.  For those one
must additionally establish, in the actual operator ideal,

1. convergence of the compressed perturbations;
2. convergence of their regularized determinants;
3. convergence of every Green-identity boundary term; and
4. a coercivity reserve that does not disappear in the limit.

Arbitrary (J)-invariant projections are still presentation choices.  A
canonical regulator must be generated by a source-derived positive operator
commuting with (J), not chosen after inspecting the desired determinant.

## 6. Revised first construction target

The first construction problem is no longer a finite theta packet.  It is:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{l}
\text{construct the completed reciprocal Fredholm Carrier first, then find}\
\text{a source-derived exhausting family of finite-rank projections commuting}\
\text{with modular reflection and fixing the endpoint channel.}
\end{array}}
\]

The cheapest falsifier is also exact: if no such source-derived compact
exhaustion exists, finite-dimensional coercivity is the wrong probe and the
energy theorem must be proved directly on the completed infinite Carrier.

## 7. Deutschian interpretation

The no-go explains why completion cannot be reconstructed by collecting more
finite arithmetic labels.  Modular sewing is a global coherence operation;
its endpoint term is created by the infinite source as a whole.

Thus the relevant distinction is not “finite evidence versus more finite
evidence,” but

\[
 \text{local label presentation}
 \neq
 \text{completed relative source object}.
\]

The hostile lesson is that any proposed finite proof must state whether it
approximates the labels or compresses the completed object.  Only the second
route can preserve the structure whose conservation is supposed to explain
RH.

## 8. Scope

The finite-label no-go is an exact theorem.  The completion-first compression
conditions are an admissibility proposal, not a constructed Fredholm model.
No off-seam coercivity, determinant representation of \(\xi\), limit theorem,
or RH result is claimed.
