## Pairing null versus puncture

For nonzero vectors `v,w`,

\[
 \langle v,w\rangle=0
\]

does not imply that either vector vanishes, that a transport is singular, or
that a complex has cohomology. It records orthogonality or destructive
interference in the chosen pairing.

Consequently the implication

\[
 \Xi(s)=0
 \Longrightarrow H^\bullet(\mathcal C_s)\ne0
\]

is not currently established. Packets 139--141 correctly state it as a gate,
but the two-by-three rigging shows that this gate is prior to the completion-
stable contraction problem.

The operator's earlier intuition should therefore be preserved in its weaker,
better-typed form:

\[
 \boxed{
 \text{an RH zero is first a null relation between the two completed
 perspectives, not necessarily a hole in either perspective}.}
\]

## Geometric-algebra reading

In geometric algebra, a vanishing scalar product can coexist with a nonzero
exterior product. Thus the complete two-state comparison is

\[
 vw=v\mathbin{\cdot}w+v\wedge w.
\]

At a scalar zero,

\[
 v\mathbin{\cdot}w=0,
\]

while `v wedge w` may remain nonzero and retain the oriented relational data
discarded by the scalar readout. This gives a direct formal version of
“destructive interference between two planes”: the scalar channel cancels,
but the bivector channel need not disappear.

The critical-line theorem might therefore be an orientation theorem for the
full Clifford product: off the reciprocal seam, the source-derived bivector
orientation forbids the scalar component from vanishing; on the seam, the two
orientations can become exactly orthogonal while their joint bivector remains
meaningful.

## Revised fork

There are now two distinct admissible routes:

1. **Pairing route.** Derive a full Clifford-valued comparison of the two
   valuation orientations and prove that its scalar component cannot vanish
   off-seam because of a source-derived orientation/integrality law.
2. **Cohomology route.** Construct independently a source Fredholm/Koszul
   complex and prove that its determinant section equals the matrix
   coefficient up to a nowhere-zero unit. Only then may pairing nullity be
   retyped as a puncture.

The pairing route is logically earlier and closer to the presently derived
theta object.

## Hostile test

A symmetric multiplier with an off-line zero can preserve the scalar
functional equation while changing the scalar pairing. The full Clifford
comparison must reject it through a changed bivector, orientation, label
transport, or boundary current before inspecting its divisor.

If the hostile multiplier preserves the entire two-by-three Clifford packet,
then that packet has no more RH force than the scalar readout.

## Present boundary

No off-seam orientation theorem is claimed. The durable correction is that
completion-stable acyclicity is conditional on an unproved determinant
bridge. The primary source question is now whether the full geometric product
contains a conserved relational component that prevents scalar orthogonality
