# The theta half-line lift never vanishes but its scalar projection can

## Scope correction

The faithful half-line lift is

\[
R(z)=(H(z),H(-z)),
\qquad
H(z)=\int_0^\infty\Phi(u)e^{zu}\,du.
\]

The completed scalar section is (X(z)=H(z)+H(-z)). The earlier reduction
from scalar zero exclusion to common-zero exclusion was too strong. A scalar
zero requires antipodal nonzero amplitudes, not a common zero.

## Half-plane nonvanishing

The completed theta source satisfies (Phi(u)>0) and (Phi'(u)<0) for
(u>0), and decays super-exponentially. Define

\[
d\mu(t)=-\Phi'(t)\,dt.
\]

Its total mass is (Phi(0)), and layer-cake integration gives

\[
zH(z)=\int_0^\infty(e^{zt}-1)\,d\mu(t).
\]

If (Re z<0), then

\[
\left|\int_0^\infty e^{zt}\,d\mu(t)\right|<\mu((0,\infty)),
\]

so (H(z)\ne0). Replacing (z) by (-z) proves (H(-z)\ne0) in the
open right half-plane.

## Seam nonvanishing

For (z=it), (t>0), the same representation gives (Im H(it)>0). At
(t=0), (H(0)>0), and reality handles negative (t). Therefore

\[
R(z)\ne(0,0)
\]

for every complex (z).

## Correct meaning of a completed zero

If (X(z)=0), then

\[
H(-z)=-H(z)\ne0.
\]

Every completed zero is a loss in the scalar sum projection of a nonvanishing
two-sector relationship. It is never disappearance of the full source state.

The RH-bearing question is why the route ratio

\[
\rho(z)=\frac{H(-z)}{H(z)}
\]

can reach (-1) only when (Re z=0). This is an incidence-location theorem,
not a common-null theorem.

([H(z):H(-z)]) so that its intersection with ([1:-1]) is confined to the
reciprocal seam. Existing hostile minimum-phase sources show that monotonicity
