# Cauchy Observation Commutes with Finite Euler Exponentiation

## Finite Euler chart

Fix a finite set of primes \(P\) and put

\[
Z_P(s)=\prod_{p\in P}(1-p^{-s})^{-1}.
\]

On the critical boundary write

\[
L_P(T)=\log Z_P\!\left(\frac12+iT\right)
=\sum_{p\in P}\sum_{k\ge1}
\frac{p^{-k/2}p^{-ikT}}{k}.
\]

Let \(T\) have the centered Cauchy law of scale \(1/2\).  Since

\[
\mathbb E(n^{-iT})=n^{-1/2},
\]

termwise expectation gives

\[
\mathbb E(L_P)
=\sum_{p\in P}\sum_{k\ge1}\frac1{k p^k}
=\log Z_P(1).
\]

Taking real parts yields the exact finite BSY identity

\[
\mathbb E\log
\left|Z_P\!\left(\frac12+iT\right)\right|
=\log Z_P(1).
\]

There is no finite-cutoff residual.

## Exponentiation also commutes

The finite Euler product has the absolutely convergent expansion

\[
Z_P\!\left(\frac12+iT\right)
=\sum_{n\in\langle P\rangle}n^{-1/2}n^{-iT},
\]

where \(\langle P\rangle\) is the multiplicative monoid generated by \(P\).
Therefore

\[
\mathbb E
Z_P\!\left(\frac12+iT\right)
=\sum_{n\in\langle P\rangle}\frac1n
=Z_P(1).
\]

Combining the two equations,

\[
\mathbb E(e^{L_P})=e^{\mathbb E(L_P)}.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\mathbb E
\exp\!\left(L_P-\mathbb E(L_P)\right)=1.
\]

Although such an identity is false for a generic random variable, it is exact
here because the Cauchy observer is an algebra character on the entire
positive-frequency Hardy algebra.  Finite Euler exponentiation never leaves
that one-sided algebra.
