# Natural theta operators do not isolate the zeta-zero spectrum

Epistemic-graph event: 1389.

## Operators supplied by the archimedean realization

Work on

`H=L^2(R_+,dx/x)`.

The logarithmic coordinate `y=log x` identifies `H` with `L^2(R,dy)`.  The
Mellin/dilation generator is

`D=-i d/dy`.

On its standard self-adjoint domain, Fourier transformation in `y`
diagonalizes `D` as multiplication by a real variable.  Hence

`Spec(D)=R`

and the spectrum is continuous, not the discrete set of imaginary parts of
zeta zeros.

Inversion defines the unitary involution

`(Jf)(x)=f(1/x)`.

It satisfies `J^2=1` and `JDJ=-D`, so its spectrum is contained in `{+1,-1}`.
This encodes the reflection behind `s <-> 1-s`, but likewise carries no
discrete zero ordinates.

## Hostile verdict

The natural operator content of theta completion supplies:

- a continuous dilation spectrum;
- a two-valued duality involution; and
- the functional-equation symmetry between them.

It does not supply a compact resolvent, boundary condition, quotient, or
trace-class perturbation that would discretize the spectrum at zeta zeros.

Defining a diagonal operator from the already known zeros, or defining a
scattering phase directly from `xi`, would reproduce the desired spectrum
tautologically.  Such a construction fails the explanatory gate because the
target function or its zeros enter as input.

## Sharp next falsifier

The negative result is overturned by a source-derived domain, boundary, or
quotient for the dilation system which:

1. is defined without `xi` or its zeros as input;
2. produces a self-adjoint or otherwise spectrally controlled operator with
   discrete spectrum;
3. admits a trace or determinant theorem; and
4. proves that determinant equals the completed `xi` function.

Until then, the completed function exists analytically but has no derived
spectral home in the Carrier program.

## Scope

This audits the two canonical archimedean operators.  It does not disprove
Hilbert--Polya constructions using additional geometry or dynamics.
