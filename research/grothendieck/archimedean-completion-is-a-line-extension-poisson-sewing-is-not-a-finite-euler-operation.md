# Archimedean completion is a line extension; Poisson sewing is not a finite Euler operation

## Question

What kind of source operation does archimedean completion induce on the
determinant-three packet, and can the Poisson sewing law be replayed at every
finite Euler cutoff?

## The archimedean factor is a tensor extension

Let

\[
\mathfrak D_{3,X}
=
\left(
\tau_{1,X},
\tau_{2,X},
\delta_{3,X}
\right)
\]

be the finite arithmetic determinant packet.  The factor

\[
B(s)
=
\frac12s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)
\]

does not act on the finite valuation transition by similarity, additive
perturbation, compression, or an unbounded graph relation.  It supplies an
independent one-dimensional determinant line

\[
\mathcal L_\infty(s).
\]

The completed finite line is

\[
\mathcal L_{X}^{\mathrm{comp}}(s)
=
\mathcal L_\infty(s)
\otimes
\mathcal L_X(s),
\]

and its scalar reconstruction is multiplication by (B(s)).  Thus the
archimedean operation is a block or tensor extension at determinant-line
level.  It creates no primitive--square mixed trace.

## Finite Euler truncation is not a theta-source truncation

The global Tate source is a restricted tensor product of local test spaces.
At almost every finite prime, its distinguished unramified vector is

\[
\mathbf 1_{\mathbb Z_p},
\]

whose local zeta integral supplies

\[
(1-p^{-s})^{-1}.
\]

To replace an omitted Euler factor by (1), one may use a unit-supported local
vector such as

\[
\mathbf 1_{\mathbb Z_p^\times}.
\]

But a finite Euler truncation omits infinitely many primes.  It would therefore
replace the distinguished unramified vector at infinitely many places.  Such
a tensor is not an element of the original restricted product.

Hence

\[
E_X(s)=\prod_{p\in X}(1-p^{-s})^{-1}
\]

is a legitimate finite analytic determinant packet, but it is not the Tate
integral of a finite modification of the completed theta source.

## Consequence for Poisson sewing

Poisson/Fourier sewing acts on the full adelic test object before Euler
factorization.  Since the finite Euler truncation is not an admitted
restricted tensor, there is no source-authorized operation

\[
\mathcal P_X:
\mathfrak D_{3,X}
\longrightarrow
\mathfrak D_{3,X}
\]

whose scalar shadow is the global modular functional equation.

The finite reciprocal unit (u_X) remains exact and useful, but it is an
analytic comparison of two truncated determinant presentations.  It is not a
finite Poisson theorem.

This corrects the earlier proposed finite compiler diagram.  Finite
reconstruction must replay exactly, but global Poisson sewing need not and
cannot be demanded at each Euler cutoff.

## Correct derivational order

The source-authorized chain is:

1. form the full restricted adelic test object using the unramified vectors;
2. apply global Fourier--Poisson sewing there;
3. take the Tate/Mellin readout;
4. extract the determinant-three arithmetic packet and the archimedean line;
5. reconstruct the completed determinant section.

Euler truncation is permitted afterward as a diagnostic projection.  It is
not a constructor from which the global sewing law may be rebuilt by ordinary
limit.

## Minimal global carrier

The connected reciprocal transition can still be scalarized canonically.
After removing the first two logarithmic cumulants, the product over primes
converges absolutely for

\[
|\Re z|<\frac16.
\]

The completed carrier therefore consists of:

- the ordinary connected transition unit;
- the primitive distributional connection coordinate;
- the square Hilbert connection coordinate;
- the independent archimedean determinant line;
- and the global Poisson correspondence on the full restricted source.

Only the final item can trivialize the completed reciprocal transition.

## Falsifiers

The construction fails if:

- a claimed finite Poisson action requires changing infinitely many local
  unramified vectors;
- the archimedean factor is represented as an additive perturbation that
  invents mixed arithmetic traces;
- the global sewing law is recovered only by scalar continuation of the
  primitive current;
- or the connected transition depends on the exhaustion of the primes.

## Result

Archimedean completion is a determinant-line tensor extension.  Global
Poisson sewing is an operation on the full restricted adelic source and has
no finite-Euler analogue inside the same source category.  The global
transition problem must therefore be solved before, not by, Euler truncation.
