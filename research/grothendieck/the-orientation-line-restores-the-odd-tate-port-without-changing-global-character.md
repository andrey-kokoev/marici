# The Orientation Line Restores the Odd Tate Port Without Changing Global Character

## The parity obstruction revisited

The trivial global zeta character admits the even archimedean scalar channel.
An odd scalar channel fails descent because it changes sign under the rational
unit (-1\).

But the source has a canonical graded enlargement. If (f\) is even, then

\[
f'(-x)=-f'(x).
\]

The orientation form also changes sign:

\[
d(-x)=-dx.
\]

Therefore

\[
f'(-x)\,d(-x)=f'(x)\,dx.
\]

The derivative coefficient is odd, but the degree-one state (f'(x)dx\) is
even as a complete typed object. It can coexist with the scalar even channel
without replacing the trivial global character.

## The minimal graded carrier

The archimedean source should therefore retain the two-term object

\[
\Omega^0_{\mathrm{even}}
\xrightarrow{d}
\Omega^1_{\mathrm{odd\ coefficient}},
\]

where the target carries the orientation line. The words “odd coefficient” do
not mean an odd global character: coefficient parity and form parity cancel.

For the Gaussian vacuum

\[
f_0(x)=e^{-\pi x^2},
\]

the canonical companion is

\[
df_0=-2\pi x e^{-\pi x^2}dx.
\]

Its coefficient is the authentic additive odd Hermite port with Fourier
quarter-phase, while the complete one-form remains compatible with even global
descent.

## Why scalarization lost the channel

The scalar Tate integral retains degree zero and discards the orientation line.
After this projection, the derivative channel either vanishes by parity or
looks like a forbidden odd character. Before projection it is an authorized
source derivative with compensating form degree.

This separates three parities that had been conflated:

1. reciprocal parity in the logarithmic coordinate;
2. additive coefficient parity on the real line;
3. total parity after tensoring with form degree.

Only the third governs global descent of the graded source object.

## Consequence for the RH programme

The local even--odd Tate double can legitimately occur inside one globally
even source, but only as a graded complex, not as two scalar characters. The
candidate comparison is now between the degree-zero vacuum and its degree-one
derivative companion.

The next required identity is a source-derived Green or Clifford current that
pairs these two degrees and retains the orientation line through prime sewing
and completion. A scalar identity obtained after erasing form degree cannot
test this mechanism.

## Scope boundary

This establishes parity-compatible admission of the odd derivative port. It
does not prove that the completed adelic restricted product carries the full
two-term complex, that finite-place boundary currents supply its analogues, or
that a scalar zero creates a nontrivial graded state. Those are the next gates.
