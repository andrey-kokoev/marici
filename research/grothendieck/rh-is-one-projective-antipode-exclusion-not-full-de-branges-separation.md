# RH is one projective antipode exclusion, not full de Branges separation

## Faithful projective route

The globally nonnull half-line pair defines an entire projective map

\[
\mathcal R(z)=[H(z):H(-z)]\in\mathbb{CP}^1.
\]

The completed scalar section is the sum covector applied to this pair:

\[
X(z)=H(z)+H(-z).
\]

Therefore

\[
X(z)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathcal R(z)=[1:-1].
\]

RH asks that the inverse image of this one projective point lie on the seam.

## Stronger de Branges demand

In a chart where (H(z)\ne0), put

\[
\rho(z)=\frac{H(-z)}{H(z)}.
\]

A Hermite–Biehler or de Branges separation would prove a strict magnitude
ordering off the seam, equivalently

\[
|\rho(z)|\ne1
\]

there. This excludes the entire unit circle in the ratio chart.

RH needs only

\[
\rho(z)\ne-1.
\]

For example, (ho=i) lies on the unit circle and violates strict magnitude
separation, but it does not null the sum projection. Thus full de Branges
separation is sufficient but strictly stronger at the level of projective
incidence.

## Why previous attacks felt overconstrained

Norm difference, coercivity, acute-cone, and hyperbolic-rotor programmes all
try to confine the route to one side of the unit circle or a positive cone.
They control a continuum of forbidden directions. The scalar divisor detects
only one direction.

This does not make RH easy. It changes the kind of invariant sought. A useful
source law may allow the route to cross the unit circle off-seam while
protecting only the antipode through a labelled winding, parity, or incidence
constraint.

## Minimal Deutsch–Popperian conjecture

The completed labelled theta constructor produces a nonnull projective route
(mathcal R) whose antipodal incidence is authorized only at the reciprocal
seam. Any hostile source with an off-seam scalar zero must violate the
labelled transport law before scalar aggregation.

The falsifier is now exact and minimal: a source-admissible labelled packet
whose projective route reaches ([1:-1]) off the seam. Failure of global norm
separation at any other projective direction does not falsify this conjecture.
