# The Two Weighted Quadratures Are One Harmonic Gradient and RH Forbids Its Interior Critical Points

## Source-defined primitive

Let `f` be the completed positive half-source and define

\[
Y(z)=\int_0^\infty
f(q)\frac{\sinh(zq)}{q}\,dq.
\]

The apparent singularity at `q=0` is removable. Superexponential decay makes
`Y` entire, and

\[
Y'(z)=\int_0^\infty f(q)\cosh(zq)\,dq
=\frac12X(z).
\]

This primitive is derived from the source before inspecting any zero.

## Quadratures as a gradient

Write

\[
z=a+it,
\qquad
Y(z)=U(a,t)+iV(a,t).
\]

The real potential is

\[
U(a,t)=\int_0^\infty
f(q)\frac{\sinh(aq)}{q}\cos(tq)\,dq.
\]

Its derivatives are

\[
\partial_aU=C_a(t),
\qquad
\partial_tU=-S_a(t),
\]

where `C_a` and `S_a` are the two weighted quadratures selected by the
source-faithful sheet completion. Therefore

\[
X(a+it)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\nabla U(a,t)=0.
\]

The two comparison channels are not independent scalar tests. They are the
two components of one harmonic-gradient field.

## Why the seam is special

Because `sinh(0)=0`,

\[
U(0,t)=0
\]

for every `t`. The critical line is therefore a constant-potential boundary.
Its tangential derivative vanishes identically:

\[
\partial_tU(0,t)=0.
\]

Zeros on the line occur exactly when the remaining normal derivative also
vanishes:

\[
\partial_aU(0,t)=0.
\]

Off the seam, both gradient components are active. An off-line zero would be
an interior critical point of the harmonic potential. Since a nonconstant
harmonic function has no interior maximum or minimum, every simple off-seam
zero would be a saddle of the relationship field.

This gives a literal form to the loss-of-meaning intuition: the scalar
readout vanishes where the completed comparison field loses its direction.
The seam authorizes such loss because one direction is already null by
symmetry. RH says no interior loss of direction occurs after the two sectors
become distinct.

## Orientation of a puncture

The quadratures obey the Cauchy--Riemann relations

\[
\partial_aC=\partial_tS,
\qquad
\partial_tC=-\partial_aS.
\]

Hence the Jacobian of their map is

\[
\det D(C,S)
=
(\partial_aC)^2+(\partial_aS)^2
=
\frac14|X'(z)|^2.
\]

At every simple zero this determinant is strictly positive. An off-seam
puncture is therefore an orientation-preserving critical point of the
two-channel map, even though it is a saddle of the scalar harmonic potential.
Multiple zeros are precisely the points where this local orientation also
degenerates.

## Exact conjecture

The minimal source theorem is now:

> The harmonic potential generated by the completed labelled theta source has
> no critical point in either open reciprocal half-plane.

This is equivalent to RH under the completed theta representation. It is not
a proof. Generic positive sources can create interior harmonic saddles, so the
missing explanation must still use theta's labelled modular construction.

The next attack should track the gradient direction, rather than its norm.
A source-derived winding or foliation theorem may exclude interior saddles
without proving full de Branges modulus dominance.

## Result

The projective antipode condition is exactly the vanishing of one
source-derived harmonic gradient. The critical line is its constant-potential
boundary, and RH is the statement that every critical point of this potential
lies on that boundary.
