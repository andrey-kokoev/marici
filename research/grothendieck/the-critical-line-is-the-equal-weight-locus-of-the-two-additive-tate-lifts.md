# The Critical Line Is the Equal-Weight Locus of the Two Additive Tate Lifts

Scope correction: the equal-weight theorem is an exact local archimedean
statement. The odd lift is not a second global channel of the trivial Riemann
zeta character. Entry 3952 records the global parity obstruction.

## The parity comparator

The even and odd archimedean Tate multipliers are

\[
m_+(s)=2(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)\cos\left(\frac{\pi s}{2}\right),
\]

\[
m_-(s)=-2i(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)\sin\left(\frac{\pi s}{2}\right).
\]

Their common gamma and scale factors cancel in the comparison:

\[
\rho(s)=\frac{m_-(s)}{m_+(s)}
=-i\tan\left(\frac{\pi s}{2}\right).
\]

Thus the relative weight of the two additive parity lifts is elementary and
independent of the gamma amplitude.

## Exact equal-weight theorem

Write (s=\sigma+it\). The standard identity

\[
|\tan(x+iy)|^2
=\frac{\cosh(2y)-\cos(2x)}
{\cosh(2y)+\cos(2x)}
\]

gives

\[
|\rho(s)|^2
=\frac{\cosh(\pi t)-\cos(\pi\sigma)}
{\cosh(\pi t)+\cos(\pi\sigma)}.
\]

Therefore

\[
|\rho(s)|=1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\cos(\pi\sigma)=0.
\]

Inside the open critical strip (0<\sigma<1\), this is equivalent to

\[
\sigma=\frac12.
\]

Moreover,

\[
0<\sigma<\frac12
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
|\rho(s)|<1,
\]

while

\[
\frac12<\sigma<1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
|\rho(s)|>1.
\]

The two half-planes are therefore the two dominance chambers of the hidden
additive-parity double. The critical line is their unique equal-weight wall.

## Explanatory consequence

Locally, this derives the offset (1/2\) without referring to the zero set. The line is
not selected because zeros were observed there. It is selected because even
and odd additive realizations of the same positive-ray Mellin state have equal
transport magnitude exactly there.

The local construction also explains why the line is a wall rather than a third
sector. Crossing it reverses which parity lift dominates. On the wall their
comparison is unitary.

## Scope boundary

More basically, the Riemann zeta source globally retains only the even
archimedean parity channel. Equal transport magnitude does not imply that the scalar theta section can
vanish only on the wall. A scalar matrix coefficient of a unitary comparison
may vanish, and a dominant channel can still cancel against other source
components. The RH-bearing theorem must show that an authorized zero-state
requires parity balance, rather than merely showing where parity balance
occurs.

The new hard target is therefore:

If the completed scalar section vanishes, prove from the labelled source and
boundary incidence that ( |m_-(s)|=|m_+(s)|\).

Combined with the theorem above, that implication would force the critical
line. A hostile multiplier preserving the scalar functional equation but not
the parity-incidence relation remains the decisive discriminator.
