## Positive source

Fix (t>0) and take the positive half-line atomic source

\[
f=\delta_0+\operatorname{sech}(t)\delta_1.
\]

Its completed even readout is

\[
X_t(z)=2\left(1+\operatorname{sech}(t)\cosh z\right).
\]

At

\[
z_t=t+i\pi,
\]

one has

\[
\cosh(z_t)=-\cosh t,
\]

and therefore (X_t(z_t)=0). This is a genuine off-seam zero generated by a
strictly positive source. Reality and evenness supply the full quartet.

The zero is simple because

\[
X_t'(z_t)
=2\operatorname{sech}(t)\sinh(t+i\pi)
=-2\tanh t\ne0.
\]

## The forcing is the conormal

There is one ordered source separation, of length one. Its positive
autocorrelation mass is (2\operatorname{sech}(t)), so the odd doubled forcing
is

\[
\mathcal K_t(z)
=2\operatorname{sech}(t)\sinh z.
\]

Thus

\[
\mathcal K_t(z)=X_t'(z)
\]

identically. At the off-seam zero, the source-generated forcing is exactly the
nonzero divisor conormal:

\[
\mathcal K_t(z_t)=X_t'(z_t)=-2\tanh t.
\]

Its oriented sign is

\[
\operatorname{Re}(z_t)\operatorname{Re}(\mathcal K_t(z_t))
=-2t\tanh t<0.
\]

That is precisely the sign required by the doubled Green identity for an
off-seam zero. The conormal current does not automatically obstruct the zero;
for this positive source it is the mechanism that balances it.

Three proposed explanations are now finitely falsified:

1. positivity of the source autocorrelation;
2. existence of a nonzero transverse conormal at a simple zero;
3. covariance of that conormal under the determinant line.

All three coexist with exact off-seam zeros in the smallest two-cell source.
The missing theta theorem must constrain how all labelled separations combine.
