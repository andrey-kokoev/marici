# The Second Fourier Quarter-Turn Reconstructs but Does Not Cancel the Seam Defect

## Comb reconstruction in the finite model

For the unnormalized Fourier transform on `Z/N`, let `R` denote reflection of
the label coordinate. The exact identities are

\[
F_N\Omega=Ne_0,
\qquad
F_Ne_0=\Omega,
\qquad
F_N^2=NR.
\]

Thus the first Fourier quarter-turn sends the augmentation line to the control
line, and the second expands the control point back into the augmentation
line, with source reflection.

## Exterior current

For every endpoint state `g`, two applications give

\[
(\Lambda^2F_N)^2(\Omega\wedge g)
=(F_N^2\Omega)\wedge(F_N^2g)
=N^2\Omega\wedge Rg.
\]

Therefore the input–control–output route is a coherent chain after two
quarter-turns. No arbitrary comparison between `e_0` and `Omega` is needed;
Fourier itself supplies both directed maps.

## Conservation, not cancellation

If `g` is scalar-null, then `Rg` is scalar-null. If its augmentation bivector
is nonzero, the reconstructed bivector is also nonzero. Fourier sewing has
transported and rescaled the boundary defect; it has not annihilated it.

This closes the Poisson chain-map question while ruling out another shortcut:
comb reconstruction alone cannot make the completed endpoint flux vanish.

## Multi-tower meaning

The three state towers participate in a quarter-turn sequence:

\[
\text{input augmentation}
\longrightarrow
\text{control point}
\longrightarrow
\text{reflected output augmentation}.
\]

The two arrows are source-derived, and their composite is reflection. This is
the first explicit coherence cell spanning all three state towers.

The remaining RH-bearing boundary law must introduce a typed arithmetic
current whose incidence has the opposite completed flux. It cannot be obtained
from Fourier transport alone, since Fourier conserves the faithful defect.
