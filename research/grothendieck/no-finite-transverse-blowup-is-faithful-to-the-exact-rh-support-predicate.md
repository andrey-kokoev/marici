# No Finite Transverse Blow-Up Is Faithful to the Exact RH Support Predicate

## The next hostile family

Let the escaping quartet have height \(R\) and transverse displacement

\[
\delta_R=R^{-k}.
\]

Its roots are

\[
\pm R\pm i\delta_R.
\]

Every root is off the seam for every finite \(R\). Yet in the seam-normal
boundary chart, \(\delta_R\to0\), so the quartet lands on the same boundary
stratum as genuine seam zeros.

Suppose a refined compactification records the first \(m\) transverse rates

\[
\delta_R, R\delta_R, \ldots, R^m\delta_R.
\]

Choosing \(k>m\) makes all of them tend to zero. Thus every finite tower of
rate blow-ups admits an off-seam sequence indistinguishable from seam escape.

## Aspect's query-dependent horizon

Aspect's theorem says that a marked port is usable only relative to a declared
future target and a nonvanishing reconstruction margin. Here the target is the
exact predicate

\[
\operatorname{Im}w=0
\quad\text{versus}\quad
\operatorname{Im}w\neq0.
\]

Its margin can shrink faster than any finite family of chosen scales. Hence
there is no finite query horizon for exact RH support at spectral infinity.
Rank and finite-rate separation can remain correct while the operational
margin tends to zero.

This is the analytic counterpart of Aspect's coherent-storage example: the
distinction remains algebraically present at every finite stage, while every
fixed reconstruction instrument becomes arbitrarily ill-conditioned.

## Consequence for the pullback tower

The sequence

\[
\text{affine}
\longleftarrow
\text{projective}
\longleftarrow
\text{seam-normal}
\longleftarrow
\text{higher-rate blow-ups}
\]

does not terminate at any finite rung. Each finite completion consumes a port
needed by a faster off-seam approach.

The correct response is not to guess another finite wall. It is to retain the
full marked divisor carrier—or an equivalent formal/pro-object containing all
transverse scales—until the source theorem itself proves that the off-seam
part is empty.

## Three distinct targets

The programme must now keep three claims separate:

1. Total divisor charge at infinity is retained by projective degree.
2. Off-seam displacement bounded away from zero is retained by the
   seam-normal boundary.
3. Exact RH support requires the uncompleted transverse germ at all scales.

The first two are valid finite observables. Neither is equivalent to the
third.

## Source-current formulation

The full divisor need not be introduced by listing zeros. For a
source-constructed entire readout \(X\), its divisor current is determined
distributionally by

\[
\mathcal D_X=\frac{1}{2\pi}\Delta\log|X|.
\]

This retains exact support without choosing a finite transverse resolution.
It is derived from the theta readout rather than from a zero census. The
remaining noncircular problem is to transport this current through Newman
heat and compare it with the seam-supported source reference without assuming
its support in advance.

The archimedean source already supplies the smooth counting reference

\[
F(T)=\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\frac{T}{2\pi}
-\frac{T}{2\pi}+\frac78.
\]

But subtracting that scalar density controls only total growth. It cannot
erase or orient the transverse divisor current. The full current, not its
counting pushforward, must remain the marked carrier.

## Revised hard theorem

The best target is now:

> The theta-derived divisor current evolves under Newman heat through a
> source-defined correspondence whose restriction to either open half-plane
> has no support at physical time zero, while its complete all-scale germ at
> infinity is retained.

This is deliberately not compressed into a finite boundary statistic. The
hostile family proves that no such statistic can be uniformly faithful.

## Scope

The theorem here is a no-go result for every fixed finite tower of transverse
blow-ups. It does not prove the support theorem for the theta divisor current.
It identifies the necessary carrier and prevents another cycle of lossy finite
completion.
