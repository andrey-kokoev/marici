# The vacuum obstructs zero-state exclusion annihilation

## Bounded question

Does a scalar theta zero force the canonical labelled coefficient packet to
have zero energy in every positive primitive-exclusion curvature?

## Canonical Mellin transport preserves the vacuum coefficient

Let the source-labelled packet be

\[
\Omega=\sum_{n\geq1}\Omega_ne_n,
\qquad
\Omega_1\ne0.
\]

Mellin transport acts diagonally:

\[
M_ze_n=n^{-z}e_n.
\]

For the transported packet \(c_z=M_z\Omega\), the vacuum component is

\[
(c_z)_1=1^{-z}\Omega_1=\Omega_1.
\]

It is independent of \(z\) and never vanishes.

The same conclusion survives Clark differentiation by any polynomial in the
logarithmic degree whose value at zero is nonzero. In particular, the factor
\(1-a\log n\) equals one at \(n=1\).

## Every primitive exclusion sees the vacuum

For every prime \(p\),

\[
P_{p\nmid n}e_1=e_1.
\]

Therefore

\[
\|P_{p\nmid n}c_z\|^2
\geq |\Omega_1|^2
>0
\]

whenever the packet lies in the declared Hilbert domain. The finite-support
version is exact and requires no completion.

Thus the canonical transported source packet cannot satisfy

\[
P_{p\nmid n}c_z=0
\]

for even one prime, let alone every prime.

## Scalar cancellation does not remove a labelled component

A scalar zero is a cancellation after aggregation of labelled amplitudes. It
does not delete the \(n=1\) source component. Hence

\[
X(z)=0
\]

does not imply vanishing primitive-exclusion energy for the raw source packet.

Any alternative zero-state packet engineered to remove the vacuum must be
derived by an independent source operation. Quotienting out \(e_1\) merely
because it obstructs the desired implication would discard a component that
the staircase readout and Gaussian source both detect.

## Result

The positive curvature

\[
[T_p^*,T_p]=P_{p\nmid n}
\]

is a genuine arithmetic boundary observable, but its annihilation is not the
zero-state boundary condition. The vacuum supplies the smallest exact
falsifier.

This closes the direct route from scalar zero to simultaneous exclusion-kernel
membership.

## Remaining interpretation

The exclusion family can still participate in a relative identity in which
its positive vacuum contribution is paired with an independently derived seam
or archimedean term. The correct target is therefore a balanced difference,
not zero exclusion energy:

\[
E_{\rm exclusion}(c_z)-E_{\rm boundary}(c_z)=0.
\]

Both terms must be constructed before inspecting zeros, and the hostile test
must retain the vacuum rather than quotient it away.
