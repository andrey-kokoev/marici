# Divisor-Split Variance Is Prime-Diagonal

For

\[
k=\prod_{p^a\parallel k}p^a,
\]

the divisor-variance coefficient from the two-copy theta block is

\[
V(k)
=
\sum_{d\mid k}(2\log d-\log k)^2.
\]

Choose a divisor uniformly by choosing each exponent
\(j_p\in\{0,\ldots,a\}\) independently. Then

\[
2\log d-\log k
=
\sum_{p^a\parallel k}(2j_p-a)\log p.
\]

Every centered exponent has mean zero, so all cross-prime terms vanish.
Moreover,

\[
\frac1{a+1}\sum_{j=0}^a(2j-a)^2
=
\frac{a(a+2)}{3}.
\]

Since the number of divisors is \(d(k)=\prod_{p^a\parallel k}(a+1)\),

\[
V(k)
=
\frac{d(k)}3
\sum_{p^a\parallel k}
a(a+2)(\log p)^2.
\]

Thus the apparent global divisor-split channel is exactly a sum of local
prime-square variances. Unique factorization kills every mixed covariance.

This identifies it with the arithmetic Fisher or square-current layer already
present in the Tate filtration. It supplies positive local metric data in the
Dirichlet chamber, but no new cross-prime coupling capable of orienting the
analytically continued scalar section.

Consequently the two-copy reduction has only:

1. primitive archimedean curvature;
2. a prime-diagonal square-current metric;
3. scalar aggregation and continuation coupling them.

Any genuinely global RH force must occur in the third operation. It cannot be
hidden inside the divisor variance itself.

The falsifier is exact: a nonzero mixed term
\((\log p)(\log q)\) for distinct primes in \(V(k)\) would disprove the
prime-diagonal statement. Centered exponent independence makes every such
coefficient zero.
