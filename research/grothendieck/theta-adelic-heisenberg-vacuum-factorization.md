## 1. Local finite-place vacuum

Fix a prime \(p\). Let \(\psi_p\) be the standard additive character of
\(\mathbb Q_p\) with conductor \(\mathbb Z_p\), and choose the self-dual
additive Haar measure. Then

\[
 \widehat{\mathbf 1_{\mathbb Z_p}}
 =
 \mathbf 1_{\mathbb Z_p}.
\]

This local vacuum is characterized without referring to an Euler factor.

**Theorem.** If a Bruhat--Schwartz function
\(\varphi\in\mathcal S(\mathbb Q_p)\) is invariant under

\[
 x\longmapsto x+a,
 \qquad a\in\mathbb Z_p,
\]

and under every modulation

\[
 \varphi(x)\longmapsto\psi_p(bx)\varphi(x),
 \qquad b\in\mathbb Z_p,
\]

then

\[
 \varphi=c\,\mathbf 1_{\mathbb Z_p}
\]

for some scalar \(c\).

Indeed, translation invariance makes \(\varphi\) constant on
\(\mathbb Z_p\)-cosets. Modulation invariance forces its support into the
annihilator of \(\mathbb Z_p\), which is \(\mathbb Z_p\) itself. Thus only the
constant function on \(\mathbb Z_p\) remains.

This is the finite-place analogue of the unique joint-fixed real comb
boundary.

## 2. Local Mellin factor

Normalize multiplicative Haar measure by

\[
 \operatorname{vol}(\mathbb Z_p^\times)=1.
\]

Then, for \(\operatorname{Re}s>0\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
 Z_p(s)
 &=
 \int_{\mathbb Q_p^\times}
 \mathbf 1_{\mathbb Z_p}(x)|x|_p^s\,d^\times x\\
 &=
 \sum_{k\ge0}p^{-ks}\\
 &=
 \frac1{1-p^{-s}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The Euler factor is therefore the dilation/Mellin readout of the unique
local Heisenberg-fixed vacuum.

The missing theorem must be a positivity or orientation law on the global
quotient, not a collection of local inequalities.
