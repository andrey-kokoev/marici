# The Poisson zero fiber has three hidden coherence channels

## Four-channel state

Write the pre-scalar Tate--Poisson incidence as

\[
v=(A,B,C,D),
\]

where

\[
A=A_f(s),
\quad
B=A_{\widehat f}(1-s),
\quad
C=\frac{\widehat f(0)}{s-1},
\quad
D=-\frac{f(0)}s.
\]

Reciprocal Fourier transport acts by

\[
\tau(A,B,C,D)=(B,A,D,C).
\]

The completed scalar readout is

\[
L(v)=A+B+C+D.
\]

## Reciprocal representation decomposition

The (+1) eigenspace of (	au) has basis

\[
s_{\mathrm{bulk}}=(1,1,0,0),
\qquad
s_{\mathrm{pole}}=(0,0,1,1).
\]

The (-1) eigenspace has basis

\[
a_{\mathrm{bulk}}=(1,-1,0,0),
\qquad
a_{\mathrm{pole}}=(0,0,1,-1).
\]

The scalar readout sees only the line

\[
s_{\mathrm{bulk}}+s_{\mathrm{pole}}.
\]

It is blind to three independent directions:

\[
h_1=(1,1,-1,-1),
\qquad
h_2=(1,-1,0,0),
\qquad
h_3=(0,0,1,-1).
\]

All three lie in (ker L), and they span it. Hence

\[
\dim\ker L=3.
\]

## Meaning of the hidden channels

The three directions are typed:

1. symmetric bulk--pole exchange;
2. antisymmetric direct--dual bulk imbalance;
3. antisymmetric polar-boundary imbalance.

A scalar zero does not mean the four-channel incidence disappears. It means
the state lies entirely in this three-dimensional hidden fiber.

## Faithful four-port transform

Define the four readouts

\[
\begin{aligned}
R_0&=A+B+C+D,\\
R_1&=A+B-C-D,\\
R_2&=A-B,\\
R_3&=C-D.
\end{aligned}
\]

They reconstruct the channel state:

\[
\begin{aligned}
A&=\frac{R_0+R_1+2R_2}{4},\\
B&=\frac{R_0+R_1-2R_2}{4},\\
C&=\frac{R_0-R_1+2R_3}{4},\\
D&=\frac{R_0-R_1-2R_3}{4}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus the scalar completed function is exactly one port short of each of three
independent comparison capabilities.

## Why positive norm is insufficient

Every nonzero vector in (ker L) has positive Euclidean norm. Therefore a
positive quadratic form on the full channel state does not contradict scalar
nullity. The missing theorem must relate the hidden coordinates to the seam
parameter or exclude their simultaneous source-admissibility off the seam.

This closes another tempting shortcut:

\[
L(v)=0
\quad\not\Longrightarrow\quad
\lVert v\rVert=0.
\]

## Source-specific transversality target

Let (mathcal M_f(s)subset\mathbb C^4) be the source-admissible incidence
locus generated by the completed theta source. RH becomes the statement

\[
\mathcal M_f(s)\cap\ker L
\ne\varnothing
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{Re}s=\frac12,
\]

with the intersection understood for the distinguished channel state rather
than arbitrary vectors.

Reciprocity alone preserves (ker L) and cannot prove this. The next rung
must impose a source-derived relation among (R_1,R_2,R_3), preferably a
Green or Clifford conservation law whose coefficient is
(2\operatorname{Re}s-1).

## Smallest hostile tests

Any proposed transversality law must reject separately:

- pure symmetric bulk--pole cancellation (h_1);
- pure direct--dual bulk imbalance (h_2);
- pure polar imbalance (h_3);
- arbitrary mixtures of these three directions.

A theorem controlling only one hidden channel leaves a two-dimensional
off-critical cancellation fiber.
