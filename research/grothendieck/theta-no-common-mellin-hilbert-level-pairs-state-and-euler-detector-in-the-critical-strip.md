# Theta no common Mellin Hilbert level pairs state and Euler detector in the critical strip

## Global normalized Fock state

For `sigma=Re(s)>1/2`, packet 185 gives the normalized infinite Fock state.
In the integer occupation basis it is

\[
 \boxed{
 \Omega_s
 ={1\over\sqrt{\zeta(2\sigma)}}
 \sum_{n\ge1}n^{-s}e_n.}
\]

Indeed multiplicativity turns the tensor-product normalization into

\[
 \prod_p\sqrt{1-p^{-2\sigma}}
 ={1\over\sqrt{\zeta(2\sigma)}}.
\]

The raw Euler detector is

\[
 \ell(e_n)=1
 \qquad(n\ge1).
\]

## Mellin-generated Hilbert scale

Let the source label generator be

\[
 Qe_n=(\log n)e_n.
\]

The canonical exponential Sobolev scale generated by `Q` is

\[
 \mathcal H_m=\operatorname{Dom}(e^{mQ}),
 \qquad
 \|c\|_m^2=\sum_{n\ge1}n^{2m}|c_n|^2.
\]

This is the minimal standard Hilbert scale that can make the constant
coefficient detector summable while retaining Mellin covariance.

## Detector continuity threshold

The detector `ell` is continuous on `H_m` precisely when its Riesz representer
belongs to the dual weighted space:

\[
 \|\ell\|_{\mathcal H_m^*}^2
 =\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-2m}<\infty.
\]

Therefore

\[
 \boxed{
 \ell\in\mathcal H_m^*
 \iff m>{1\over2}.}
\]

## State membership threshold

The normalized Fock state belongs to `H_m` precisely when

\[
 \sum_{n\ge1}n^{2m-2\sigma}<\infty,
\]

or

\[
 \boxed{
 \Omega_s\in\mathcal H_m
 \iff m<\sigma-{1\over2}.}
\]

Thus a common Hilbert level on which the state is a vector and the detector is
a continuous covector exists exactly when one can choose

\[
 {1\over2}<m<\sigma-{1\over2}.
\]

This interval is nonempty exactly for

\[
 \boxed{\sigma>1.}
\]

## Critical-strip no-go

For

\[
 {1\over2}<\Re s\le1,
\]

there is no level of the source-derived Mellin Hilbert scale which contains
`Omega_s` and simultaneously makes the raw Euler detector continuous.

This exactly reproduces the gap between the Fock representation threshold and
the Euler absolute-convergence threshold, now as a functional-analytic
incompatibility:

\[
 \boxed{
 \text{the state and raw detector do not inhabit a common Riesz pairing
 space in the critical half-strip}.}
\]

## Why a generic stronger norm does not solve it

Choosing `m>1/2` by hand makes the detector bounded but ejects `Omega_s` when
`sigma<=m+1/2`. Choosing `m<sigma-1/2` retains the state but leaves the
detector distributional.  Cutoff-dependent choices of `m` define no common
completion.

The obstruction is therefore not lack of enough Sobolev regularity at one
prime. It is the global density of integer occupation labels.

## Poisson pairing must be relative

The Poisson-completed detector cannot be obtained as the ordinary continuous
extension of `ell` on a common member of this Hilbert scale.  It must instead
be a relative pairing supplied by additive Fourier--Poisson sewing, with polar
boundary currents retained:

\[
 \mathcal S_+
 \times
 \mathcal S_-
 \longrightarrow
 \mathcal L_{\rm rel}.
\]

Its value agrees with the Euler pairing in `Re(s)>1`, but its continuation into
the critical strip is a new boundary-bearing operation, not bounded extension
of the same covector.

This rules out a large class of manufactured detector constructions.

## Remaining theorem

Construct the relative Poisson pairing on the two-sector rigging and prove
that its distinguished state value is nonzero off the seam.  The construction
must explain how the two incompatible Mellin regularities are sewn through
the primitive, square, and polar boundary channels.

## Scope

The theorem covers the canonical exponential Hilbert scale generated by the
log-label operator. It does not rule out every possible locally convex or
cohomological pairing. Rather, it proves that any successful construction in
the critical strip must be genuinely relative and cannot be ordinary Riesz
duality on one Mellin-weighted Hilbert space.
