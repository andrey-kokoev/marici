# Hurwitz forbids zero creation by stable Euler completion

## Bounded question

Can a boundary-bearing determinant section with critical-strip zeros arise as
a completion-stable locally uniform limit of finite nowhere-zero Euler
sections?

## Finite sections

For a finite prime set \(S\), the Euler section

\[
Z_S(s)=\prod_{p\in S}(1-p^{-s})^{-1}
\]

is holomorphic and nowhere zero wherever its local factors are finite. Any
fixed nowhere-zero archimedean unit preserves this property.

The same statement holds for a finite determinant-line realization whose
scalar representative differs from \(Z_S\) by a nowhere-zero transition unit.

## Hurwitz gate

Let \(D\) be a connected domain and suppose a sequence of nowhere-zero
holomorphic sections \(F_N\) converges locally uniformly on \(D\) to a
holomorphic function \(F\). Hurwitz's theorem implies:

- either \(F\) is nowhere zero on \(D\);
- or \(F\) vanishes identically on \(D\).

Therefore a nonzero holomorphic section with isolated zeros cannot be produced
as such a limit.

## Consequence for completed zeta

The completed zeta section is not identically zero and has isolated nontrivial
zeros. Hence no domain containing one of those zeros admits a locally uniform
approximation by the ordinary finite nowhere-zero Euler sections.

This does not contradict convergence in the ordinary Euler chamber. There the
Euler product converges locally uniformly to a nonzero function. The obstruction
appears when one asks the same finite product system to cross into the critical
strip.

## Exact completion fork

Any source-derived determinant programme producing the completed divisor must
therefore use at least one of the following:

1. finite approximants that themselves acquire zeros;
2. a topology weaker than locally uniform holomorphic convergence;
3. cutoff-dependent transition data that are not nowhere-zero units on the
   completed domain;
4. a changed completed object, such as a relative determinant with explicit
   boundary channels;
5. a non-normal family whose completion is not represented by ordinary
   function convergence.

Each choice is mathematically substantive and must be derived from theta/Tate
constructors. None is automatic Euler completion.

## Relation to escaping partners

This is the analytic form of the earlier completion-at-infinity warning.
Finite sections can remain invertible while inverse norms become unbounded.
If convergence stays locally uniform and the limit remains holomorphic, that
escape cannot create an isolated zero. The topology or object must change at
the same boundary where the partner escapes.

## Result

Stable finite Euler determinants cannot manufacture the completed zero divisor.
The missing determinant carrier must already contain nontrivial boundary or
relative structure before the critical-strip limit.

This is a construction constraint, not RH: it explains why ordinary Euler
factor multiplication cannot be the desired zero-state complex.

## Sharp next gate

Among the allowed forks, the source programme already points to a relative
determinant retaining primitive, square, seam, and archimedean channels. The
next construction must specify finite approximants whose transition from Euler
sections to relative sections is explicit and whose zeros are not inserted
from \(X\).
