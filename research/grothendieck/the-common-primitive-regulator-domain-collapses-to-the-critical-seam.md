# The Common Primitive Regulator Domain Collapses to the Critical Seam

## Mellin character

Write

\[
z=s-\frac12.
\]

On logarithmic packets, the Mellin character acts by

\[
(M_zf)(q)=e^{zq}f(q).
\]

Translation satisfies

\[
T_aM_z
=
e^{za}M_zT_a.
\]

At a prime scale \(a=\log p\), the direct character therefore contributes the
factor

\[
p^z.
\]

The reciprocal sector carries the reflected character \(M_{-\overline z}\)
and contributes

\[
p^{-\overline z}.
\]

## Regulated primitive exponents

In the \(\varepsilon\)-rigging, the unmodulated first valuation correction is
bounded at scale

\[
p^{-1-\varepsilon/2}.
\]

After Mellin modulation, the direct primitive layer has magnitude scale

\[
p^{-1-\varepsilon/2+\Re z},
\]

while the reciprocal layer has magnitude scale

\[
p^{-1-\varepsilon/2-\Re z}.
\]

## Joint summability strip

The direct prime sum converges when

\[
\Re z<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
\]

The reciprocal prime sum converges when

\[
\Re z>-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
\]

Therefore both sector currents coexist in one regulated relative object
precisely in the strip

\[
|\Re z|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
\]

As the regulator is removed,

\[
\varepsilon\downarrow0,
\]

the common domain collapses to

\[
\Re z=0,
\]

equivalently

\[
\Re s=\frac12.
\]

## Meaning

The critical line is the unique locus where the two primitive all-prime
currents reach their common completion threshold simultaneously.

This conclusion does not use zero locations. It follows from:

- opposite sector riggings;
- Mellin covariance;
- the exact primitive comb defect;
- the divergence threshold of the prime harmonic series.

The half-line is therefore both the Fourier-centered dilation seam and the
common renormalization boundary of the two arithmetic primitive currents.

## What happens on the seam

At \(\Re z=0\), both primitive layers are logarithmically critical with matching
magnitude. They are not separately summable at \(\varepsilon=0\); a reciprocal
relative renormalization is still required.

Away from the seam, one layer becomes more singular while the other becomes
less singular. A symmetric subtraction cannot be inherited from the common
regulator without retaining that imbalance as explicit boundary data.

## Scope boundary

Common-domain collapse explains why the critical line is the only locus of
balanced two-sector completion. It does not imply that scalar zeros occur
there, nor that off-seam analytic continuation is impossible. Separate
meromorphic continuations may exist outside the common relative domain.

RH still requires a bridge showing that a scalar zero must define a state in
the common completed two-sector object.

## Falsifier

The result fails if Mellin modulation does not shift the two prime exponents
with opposite signs, or if both prime sums converge outside the displayed
strip. Translation covariance and the prime-series convergence threshold
decide both.
