# Hurwitz Forces Zeta Zeros into the Completion Operation, Not the Finite Euler Systems

## The finite product chart

For a finite prime set (P\), the Euler transfer is

\[
Z_P(s)=\prod_{p\in P}\frac{1}{1-p^{-s}}.
\]

It is holomorphic and nonzero wherever none of its local denominators vanish.
Multiplying by a nowhere-zero holomorphic normalization unit preserves this
zero-free property.

## Hurwitz obstruction

Let (D\) be a connected open domain. Suppose zero-free holomorphic functions

\[
u_P(s)Z_P(s),
\qquad
u_P(s)\ne0,
\]

converge locally uniformly on (D\) to a holomorphic limit (F\). Hurwitz's
theorem implies that (F\) is either zero-free on (D\) or identically zero.

Therefore a completed zeta section with an isolated zero cannot arise on a
neighborhood of that zero by locally uniform convergence of finite Euler
transfers times nowhere-zero units.

At least one of the following must occur:

1. convergence fails to be locally uniform in the zero neighborhood;
2. the normalization carries a divisor or singular boundary contribution;
3. completion enlarges the system and the scalar is formed afterward as a
   Schur complement or selected matrix coefficient;
4. the operation is not the finite Euler product limit at all, but a different
   source-derived sewing such as Poisson or theta completion.

## Why naive cross-prime addition also fails

Aspect supplied the complementary hostile result. Individual evenized prime
cells can have seam-only zeros, while a positive two-prime scalar sum already
has an off-seam zero. Thus replacing product completion by independent scalar
addition does not preserve confinement either.

The two failures point in opposite directions:

- product limits preserve zero-freeness too strongly under local uniformity;
- scalar sums create uncontrolled off-wall cancellation too easily.

The missing operation must retain cross-prime incidence before scalar
aggregation.

## Interpretation

Nontrivial zeta zeros are completion events. They are absent from every finite
Euler product system and cannot be born through a benign locally uniform
zero-free limit. They arise precisely where the operation “complete and then
read out” differs from “read out every finite cutoff and then take the limit.”

This refines the earlier Mertens-completion square. The anomaly is not merely a
constant renormalization mismatch: isolated zeros certify a failure of
zero-free local-uniform descent or the appearance of new boundary state.

## Revised hard target

Construct a boundary-bearing completion functor on the full Rosenbrock packet
such that:

1. finite Euler input, state, and output ports embed coherently;
2. primitive, square, and archimedean boundary channels remain explicit;
3. the completed selected transfer is \(\Xi\) up to a nowhere-zero source unit;
4. every new transmission-zero state is classified by the added boundary
   channels;
5. the classification forces any dark selected port to occur only at the
   simultaneous-loss wall.

Without the fourth condition, completion may create arbitrary selected-output
zeros while complementary channels remain bright.
