# A Two-Mode Mellin Zero Line Is Set Exactly by Amplitude Balance

## Two Gaussian rates

Let

\[
F(x)=a e^{-\lambda_1x}+b e^{-\lambda_2x},
\qquad
0<\lambda_1<\lambda_2,
\qquad
a,b>0.
\]

Its Mellin transform is

\[
M(s)=\Gamma(s)
\left(a\lambda_1^{-s}+b\lambda_2^{-s}\right)
\]

in the convergence chamber and thereafter by continuation. The gamma factor
has no zeros, so the transmission zeros solve

\[
1+\frac{b}{a}
\left(\frac{\lambda_2}{\lambda_1}\right)^{-s}=0.
\]

Writing \(r=\lambda_2/\lambda_1>1\), all zeros are

\[
s_k=
\frac{\log(b/a)}{\log r}
-\frac{(2k+1)\pi i}{\log r},
\qquad
k\in\mathbb Z,
\]

up to the sign convention for the imaginary indexing. Thus every zero lies on
the vertical line

\[
\Re s=\frac{\log(b/a)}{\log r}.
\]

## The half-density balance

The line is \(\Re s=1/2\) exactly when

\[
\frac{b}{a}=r^{1/2}.
\]

Therefore the half-offset is an amplitude-to-rate scaling law. Positivity of
the two source modes guarantees neither that offset nor any universal line;
changing one positive amplitude moves the entire zero lattice horizontally.

## Reciprocal centered pair

The same statement is more natural in the centered coordinate

\[
z=s-\frac12.
\]

A source-normalized reciprocal pair has the form

\[
c_ne^{-zq_n}+c_ne^{zq_n}
=2c_n\cosh(zq_n).
\]

Its two centered amplitudes are exactly equal, so every zero lies on

\[
\Re z=0.
\]

This is the elementary destructive-interference model behind the seam. The
two reciprocal realizations have equal relationship weight and opposite phase
there.

## Why this does not globalize automatically

A sum of many individually balanced reciprocal pairs is

\[
\sum_nc_n\cosh(zq_n).
\]

Its zeros need not remain on the imaginary axis. Aspect's positive two-prime
hostile gives an explicit off-seam zero of precisely this type. Pairwise
balance is therefore necessary local grammar but insufficient global
coherence.

The missing theorem must constrain the relative amplitudes across different
rates, not merely within each reciprocal pair.

## Explanatory consequence

The critical line has two distinct explanations now:

1. it is the simultaneous Hilbert-escape wall of the direct and reciprocal
   Euler vacua;
2. it is the destructive-interference line of each source-normalized
   reciprocal two-mode packet.

These are genuinely related by half-density scaling. But agreement of the two
local explanations does not prove that the completed scalar sum preserves the
line. The remaining RH content is exactly the all-label coherence law.

## Sharp falsifier

Perturb one reciprocal amplitude by a positive factor \(e^\eta\). The zero line
moves by

\[
\Delta\sigma=\frac{\eta}{\log r}.
\]

Any proposed source theorem that does not detect this perturbation cannot
explain the critical offset. Any theorem that detects pairwise imbalance but
admits Aspect's balanced two-prime hostile cannot explain global confinement.
