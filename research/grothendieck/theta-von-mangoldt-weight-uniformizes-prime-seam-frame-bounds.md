# Theta von Mangoldt weight uniformizes prime seam frame bounds

## Bounded question

Can the full seam Gram control arbitrary phased packets on each prime orbit,
and can that control remain uniform as the prime varies?

## Translate-frame symbol

Fix a prime \(p\) and put \(L_p=\log p\). The bilateral translate family
generated by the theta source has Gram symbol

\[
W_p(\theta)
=\frac1{L_p}\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z}
\left|\widehat\Phi
\left(\frac{\theta+2\pi m}{L_p}\right)\right|^2,
\qquad -\pi\le\theta\le\pi.
\]

Up to the fixed cut realization, this is the lower-frame symbol governing
phased coefficient packets on the \(p\)-power seam orbit.

## Zero-alias bound

Every term is nonnegative, so retaining only \(m=0\) gives

\[
W_p(\theta)
\ge
\frac1{L_p}
\left|\widehat\Phi\left(\frac\theta{L_p}\right)\right|^2.
\]

Since \(p\ge2\),

\[
\left|\frac\theta{L_p}\right|
\le\frac\pi{\log2}.
\]

Define the compact baseband reserve

\[
c_{\Phi}
=\min_{|\xi|\le\pi/\log2}|\widehat\Phi(\xi)|^2.
\]

For the standard Riemann theta normalization, this interval lies strictly
below the first real zero of the completed Fourier readout. Hence
\(c_{\Phi}>0\). This is a bounded low-frequency fact, independent of RH and of
all high zeros.

It follows that

\[
W_p(\theta)\ge\frac{c_{\Phi}}{\log p}.
\]

Thus every individual prime translate family has a positive lower frame bound.
The feared modulated prime-orbit packet cannot make the full seam Gram vanish.

## Exact all-prime normalization

The raw bound deteriorates as \(p\to\infty\). Multiplying the prime-resolved
seam energy by the von Mangoldt weight cancels this loss exactly:

\[
(\log p)W_p(\theta)\ge c_{\Phi}.
\]

Therefore the labelled direct sum of prime seam histories, equipped with its
source-derived \(\log p\) weight, has a prime-uniform lower frame reserve.

This is not a fitted weight. The same \(\log p\) is the primitive arithmetic
current and the logarithmic increment in the seam-selective prime-dilation
oscillator law from packet 220.

## Structural synthesis

Three previously separate facts now form one exact pattern:

1. oscillator transport produces the seam coefficient
   \((1-2\Re s)\log p\);
2. unweighted seam translates lose a factor \((\log p)^{-1}\) in their
   elementary frame bound; and
3. the primitive von Mangoldt current restores a prime-uniform seam norm.

The arithmetic logarithm is therefore simultaneously transport generator,
current weight, and frame renormalizer.

## Typing cautions

The theorem uses prime-resolved seam ports. If all primes are scalar-summed
before forming the Gram norm, cross-prime cancellations may return. Retaining
the labels is essential.

The periodized formula is for the bilateral translate model. Passing to the
actual half-line tail--seam cut requires the already derived exact cut
isometry; the boundary channel must not be discarded.

Finally, a uniform positive state norm does not by itself orient scalar zeros.
The remaining RH-bearing gate is whether the doubled Green identity contains
this weighted labelled seam Gram with coefficient \(2\Re s-1\) and vanishing
total flux on a scalar-zero state.

## Falsifier

The frame theorem fails under a different normalization if
\(\widehat\Phi\) has a zero in the compact baseband

\[
[-\pi/\log2,\pi/\log2].
\]

The all-prime theorem fails if the source-authorized seam completion erases
prime resolution before the norm is formed, or if its actual measure differs
from the \(\log p\) primitive-current weight.

## Scope

This packet reduces phased prime-orbit stability to a bounded baseband reserve
and proves that von Mangoldt weighting makes the resulting lower bound uniform
over primes. It does not derive the weighted seam term inside the global Green
identity, control scalar-summed cross-prime cancellation, prove the zero-state
boundary condition, or prove RH.
