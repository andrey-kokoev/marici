# The Centered Euler Generator Explains the Half-Offset

## Euler construction of the current

Let

\[
g(\rho)=e^{-\pi\rho^2},
\qquad
D=\rho\frac{d}{d\rho}.
\]

Then

\[
Dg=-2\pi\rho^2g
\]

and

\[
D^2g=
\left(
-4\pi\rho^2+4\pi^2\rho^4
\right)g.
\]

Therefore the frontier current is

\[
F
=
\frac1{2\pi}D(D+1)g.
\]

Indeed,

\[
\frac1{2\pi}
\left(
D^2+D
\right)g
=
\rho^2(2\pi\rho^2-3)e^{-\pi\rho^2}.
\]

## Fourier–Tate centering

Fourier transformation reverses the Euler generator with the Haar correction:

\[
\mathcal F D\mathcal F^{-1}
=
-D-1.
\]

Define the centered generator

\[
K=D+\frac12.
\]

Then

\[
\mathcal F K\mathcal F^{-1}=-K.
\]

The half-offset is therefore forced: it is the unique scalar shift making
dilation odd under Fourier exchange.

The current operator becomes

\[
D(D+1)
=
K^2-\frac14.
\]

Thus the quarter threshold is the scalar remainder produced by squaring the
Fourier-odd centered generator.

## Mellin symbol

For Mellin transform in a common convergence chamber,

\[
\mathcal M(Df)(s)=-s\,\mathcal Mf(s).
\]

Hence

\[
\mathcal M(D(D+1)f)(s)
=
s(s-1)\mathcal Mf(s).
\]

Equivalently,

\[
s(s-1)
=
\left(s-\frac12\right)^2-\frac14.
\]

The completion polynomial in \(\xi(s)\) is exactly the Mellin symbol of the
Fourier-invariant quadratic Euler operator generating the frontier current.

## Geometric meaning

The two half-planes are the two orientations of the centered dilation
generator \(K\). Fourier–Tate exchange reverses that orientation. The critical
line is the locus where the Mellin character has zero real displacement from
the centered generator:

\[
\Re\left(s-\frac12\right)=0.
\]

The shift by \(1/2\) is not inferred from observed zeros. It is the modular
Haar correction required to make the two dilation orientations exact
opposites.

The number \(1/4\) is not a separate mystery. It is the quadratic scalar
created when the two oriented sectors are folded through \(K\mapsto-K\).

## Scope boundary

This explains why the seam is centered at \(1/2\) and why the natural quotient
coordinate contains the offset \(1/4\). It does not prove that every zero lies
on the seam. The arithmetic comb can still make the canonical matrix
coefficient vanish away from the fixed locus.

The missing theorem must prohibit such vanishing while respecting the
source-derived \(K\leftrightarrow-K\) pairing.

## Falsifier

The explanation fails if the frontier current is not generated by
\(D(D+1)\), if Fourier conjugation does not send \(D\) to \(-D-1\), or if a
different centering makes the generator odd. Direct calculation establishes
the first two, and uniqueness of the scalar shift establishes the third.
