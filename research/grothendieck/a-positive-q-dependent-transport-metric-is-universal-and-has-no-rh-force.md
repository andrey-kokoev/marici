# A positive q-dependent transport metric is universal and has no RH force

## Universal pullback construction

Let `U(q)` be the invertible fundamental matrix of any real two-dimensional first-order system. For any positive terminal metric `H0`, define

\[
H(q)=U(q)^{-T}H_0U(q)^{-1}.
\]

Then

\[
H'+A^TH+HA=0,
\]

so the quadratic form is conserved. With `H0=I`, exact symbolic calculation gives

\[
H_{11}=\frac{u_{21}^2+u_{22}^2}{\det(U)^2}>0,
\qquad
\det H=\frac1{\det(U)^2}>0.
\]

Thus `H(q)` is positive for every invertible flow, independently of theta arithmetic, reciprocal sewing, or zero location.

## Hostile consequence

The same construction applies to the positive two-atom sources already known to have off-seam zeros. Therefore existence of a positive coordinate-dependent conserved metric cannot imply RH or distinguish the canonical theta source.

## Surviving gate

The content must lie in boundary compatibility, not bulk metric transport. A noncircular candidate must specify terminal or seam data before inspecting zeros and prove that the source-derived metric simultaneously:

1. respects reciprocal modular sewing;
2. gives the endpoint line a fixed definite type;
3. gives the transported source line an incompatible type throughout an open half-plane; and
4. fails one of these conditions on the hostile two-atom source.

## Disposition

Do not search for an unconstrained Lyapunov solution: one always exists. Search for an overdetermined two-boundary sewing problem whose compatibility is theta-specific. The first useful residual is the mismatch between the metric transported from the terminal boundary and the metric independently prescribed at the reciprocal seam.
