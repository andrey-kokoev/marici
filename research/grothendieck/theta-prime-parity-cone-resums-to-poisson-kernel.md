# The full prime parity cone resums sign-indefinite depths into a positive Poisson kernel

## 1. Fixed-depth character

At one prime, let

\[
  c=a+b,
  \qquad
  d=a-b,
  \qquad
  a,b\ge0.
\]

At fixed product depth \(c\), the allowed relative labels are

\[
  d=-c,-c+2,\ldots,c.
\]

Their phase sum is

\[
  \chi_c(\theta)
  =
  \sum_{a+b=c}e^{-i(a-b)\theta}
  =
  \frac{\sin((c+1)\theta)}{\sin\theta}
  =
  U_c(\cos\theta),
\]

with the continuous limiting value at \(\sin\theta=0\).

These fixed-depth characters are sign-indefinite. Already

\[
  \chi_1(\theta)=2\cos\theta
\]

is negative on half of the circle.

Therefore no theorem can orient every product-depth band separately.

## 2. Full positive-Fock resummation

Include every symmetric-power depth with half-density parameter
\(0<r<1\):

\[
  \sum_{c\ge0}r^c\chi_c(\theta)
  =
  \sum_{a,b\ge0}
  r^{a+b}e^{-i(a-b)\theta}.
\]

The two label sums factor:

\[
  \sum_{a,b\ge0}
  r^{a+b}e^{-i(a-b)\theta}
  =
  \frac1{1-re^{-i\theta}}
  \frac1{1-re^{i\theta}}.
\]

Hence

\[
  \boxed{
  \sum_{c\ge0}r^cU_c(\cos\theta)
  =
  \frac1{1-2r\cos\theta+r^2}
  >0.}
\]

Up to the positive normalization \(1-r^2\), this is the Poisson kernel of
the disk.

## 3. Arithmetic half-density

For the prime \(p\), the critical half-density gives

\[
  r=p^{-1/2}.
\]

The full two-copy local factor is therefore

\[
  \frac1{
  1-2p^{-1/2}\cos\theta+p^{-1}}
  =
  \left|1-p^{-1/2}e^{i\theta}\right|^{-2}.
\]

Thus the product--ratio cone has a canonical positive harmonic completion:

\[
\boxed{
\text{sign-indefinite depth characters}
\xrightarrow{\text{positive symmetric-power completion}}
\text{positive Poisson kernel}.}
\]

This is a hard-to-vary explanation of why truncating depths can obscure the
orientation generated by the complete source.

## 4. Exact representation-theoretic meaning

The functions

\[
  \chi_c(\theta)=U_c(\cos\theta)
\]

are the characters of the \(c\)-th symmetric power of the two reciprocal
phase directions. Positivity appears only after taking the full bosonic Fock
sum.

The local mechanism therefore unifies:

1. the parity cone;
2. positive symmetric-power provenance;
3. the half-density \(p^{-1/2}\);
4. reciprocal phase pairing; and
5. harmonic Poisson positivity.

## 5. Why this is not RH

The infinite product of the local critical factors is not convergent:

\[
  \prod_p
  \left|1-p^{-1/2-i x}\right|^{-2}
\]

does not define the completed critical-line object by an ordinary Euler
product. Prime phases and degree terms require renormalized archimedean and
endpoint sewing.

Moreover, in the undecomposed theta current the radial factor

\[
  S\sinh(yS)
\]

couples product depth to the base variables. The local Poisson resummation
orients the pure relative-phase grammar, not the completed cross-\(S\)
interaction.

Thus Nima's prime-transport Lakatos closure remains valid at the global
level.

## 6. New global target

Construct finite-place products of the positive Poisson kernels while
retaining their product-depth labels. Couple them to the archimedean
two-copy current before taking the prime limit. The desired theorem is that
the source-derived endpoint--gamma counterterm renormalizes only the shared
degree direction and preserves Poisson positivity on its orthogonal
relative-phase fibers.

This is stronger than scalar Euler continuation and weaker than assuming the
Weil/Pick kernel positive.

## 7. Falsifiers

The mechanism fails globally if:

1. archimedean sewing mixes relative-phase fibers with an indefinite sign;
2. renormalization requires subtracting a non-degree direction;
3. squarefree fill-in destroys the symmetric-power character decomposition;
   or
4. the resulting completed kernel is merely the RH-equivalent scalar Weil
   form with no precompression positivity.

## 8. Scope

function, and local Poisson positivity are exact. They prove a local
finite-prime orientation mechanism, not convergence of the critical Euler
product, positivity of the completed theta current, or RH.
