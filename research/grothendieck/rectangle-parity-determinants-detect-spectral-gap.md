# Rectangle parity determinants detect the first spectral gap

## Claim boundary

This is an exact conditional spectral decomposition under a positive on-line Weil measure, plus a source-side numerical observation. It is not an unconditional proof of positivity or RH.

## Rank-two collapse

Under the positive spectral representation, the normalized stationary kernel has the form

`k_t(a)=sum_j w_j(t) cos(gamma_j a) / sum_j w_j(t)`,

where `w_j(t)=m_j exp(-t gamma_j^2)` for positive ordinates counted once.

For one ordinate `gamma`, the four-point Gram on displacements `0,log2,log3,log6` is generated by the two vectors `cos(gamma a)` and `sin(gamma a)`. Its rank is at most two. Consequently both `2x2` parity determinants vanish exactly. This explains why the normalized rectangle approaches the positivity boundary when one conjugate pair dominates.

## First correction

Let `gamma_1<gamma_2` be the first two supported ordinates and set

`Delta=gamma_2^2-gamma_1^2`.

After normalization, the second spectral pair enters with relative weight proportional to

`exp(-t Delta)`.

Unless the chosen displacements annihilate its exterior-product coefficient, each parity determinant therefore has leading asymptotic form

`D_+(t)=C_+ exp(-t Delta)+lower terms`,

`D_-(t)=C_- exp(-t Delta)+lower terms`.

Thus consecutive source-side determinant values give the estimator

`Delta_eff(t_1,t_2)=log(D(t_1)/D(t_2))/(t_2-t_1)`.

This is not physical-time evolution: `t` is the Gaussian smoothing parameter.

## Source-side observation

The high-precision explicit-formula scout gives stable positive determinants through `t=0.2`. Between `t=0.08,0.12,0.16,0.2`, both parity channels yield the same effective squared-ordinate gap, approximately `242.1357`. Agreement of two independent parity channels is a stringent internal check on the source evaluation and explains the exponential loss of conditioning.

The rectangle therefore does more than test positivity: its distance from the rank-two boundary resolves the next spectral component. A false negative from insufficient prime-tail or quadrature precision is expected once the numerical error exceeds `exp(-t Delta)`.

## Disposition

The sampled rectangle supports, but does not certify, positivity. Its near-singularity is explained rather than treated as a defect. Certification should target rescaled quantities `exp(t Delta_*)D_+` and `exp(t Delta_*)D_-` with a source-derived enclosure `Delta_*`, rather than subtracting nearly equal normalized terms directly.
