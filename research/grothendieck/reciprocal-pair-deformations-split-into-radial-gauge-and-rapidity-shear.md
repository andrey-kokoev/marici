# Reciprocal-pair deformations split into radial gauge and rapidity shear

## The two independent deformation channels

Write a reciprocal pair as

\[
I_+=Re^\chi,
\qquad
I_-=Re^{-\chi}.
\]

There are two elementary deformations compatible with exchange of the two
channels.

First, an even common factor (h(-z)=h(z)) acts by

\[
I_+\longmapsto hI_+,
\qquad
I_-\longmapsto hI_-.
\]

It changes

\[
R\longmapsto hR
\]

while leaving \(\chi\) and the projective rapidity

\[
r=\tanh\chi=\frac{I_+-I_-}{I_++I_-}
\]

unchanged. Call this the radial gauge channel.

Second, an odd function (g(-z)=-g(z)) acts by

\[
I_+\longmapsto e^gI_+,
\qquad
I_-\longmapsto e^{-g}I_-.
\]

It changes

\[
\chi\longmapsto\chi+g
\]

while preserving the reciprocal invariant

\[
I_+I_-=R^2.
\]

Call this the rapidity shear channel.

Locally, any nowhere-zero reciprocal-pair deformation factors into these two
operations by taking the even and odd parts of the logarithms of its two
channel multipliers. The source conic therefore has two independent
deformation directions before scalar aggregation.

## Why either one can move the scalar intersection

The completed scalar channel is

\[
S=\frac12+2PR\cosh\chi.
\]

Under radial gauge it becomes

\[
S_h=\frac12+2PhR\cosh\chi.
\]

Thus a common factor can create or remove an intersection with (S=0)
without changing rapidity at all. Any attempted RH mechanism based only on
the projective coordinate (r) is therefore incomplete.

Under rapidity shear it becomes

\[
S_g=\frac12+2PR\cosh(\chi+g).
\]

Thus an opposite channel shear can create or remove an intersection while
preserving the Lorentz radius. Any mechanism based only on the conic radius
or its quadratic invariant is likewise incomplete.

The hostile symmetric multiplier and the hostile relative-phase deformation
are therefore not unrelated counterexamples. They probe the two independent
coordinates erased by the scalar readout.

## Universal real-axis dynamics

Let (L(z)=I_+(z)) and choose a real (z). Positivity of the half-density
gives

\[
\frac{d^2}{dz^2}\log L(z)
=\operatorname{Var}_z(q)>0.
\]

Consequently,

\[
\chi'(z)
=\frac12\left((\log L)'(z)+(\log L)'(-z)\right)>0,
\]

and

\[
(\log R)'(z)
=\frac12\left((\log L)'(z)-(\log L)'(-z)\right)
\]

has the sign of (z). Hence the positive source universally has increasing
rapidity and a radius increasing with (|z|) on the real boost axis.

This is genuine dynamics, but it is not theta-specific. Every nondegenerate
positive half-density has it, including hostile carriers. It cannot provide
the missing off-seam confinement theorem by itself.

## The source-rigidity target

The minimal theta source must do more than fix the conic or make its real
boost flow monotone. It must couple radial gauge and rapidity shear so that
neither can be changed independently by an authorized source constructor.

The sharpened Deutsch–Popperian conjecture is:

> The completed labelled theta constructor determines the pair \((R,\chi)\)
> jointly, up to a nowhere-zero source unit whose radial and rapidity parts
> cannot change the intersection divisor of
> \(1/2+2PR\cosh\chi\). Every reciprocal deformation that inserts an
> off-seam zero fails this joint constructor law in at least one typed source
> channel before scalar aggregation.

The immediate falsifier is constructive. Produce a nontrivial radial gauge
or rapidity shear that is generated by the admitted theta operations—label
transport, Fourier reflection, completion, and restricted-product sewing—and
that changes the scalar divisor. Such a deformation would refute canonical
section rigidity directly.
