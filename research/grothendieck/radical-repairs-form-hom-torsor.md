# Pairing-preserving radical repairs form a Hom-complex torsor

Epistemic-graph event: 1348.

## Classification theorem

Retain Ledger 1328's setup and suppose at least one radical-valued correction
`C_0` solves

`delta C_0=-Omega`.

Then every pairing-preserving repair is uniquely of the form

`C_0+Z`, with `Z in Z^0(Hom(C(G),R))`.

Indeed, two solutions differ by a radical-valued degree-zero map `Z` with
`delta Z=0`, hence by a chain map into the radical.  Conversely, adding any
such `Z` preserves the repair equation.  Thus the set of literal repairs is
an affine torsor under the group of radical-valued chain maps.

If repairs differing by radical-valued chain homotopy are identified, their
classes form a torsor under

`H^0(Hom(C(G),R))`.

Consequently a repair, when it exists, is unique up to radical chain homotopy
exactly when this degree-zero Hom cohomology vanishes.

## Small hostile nonuniqueness

Let both source and radical be the one-term complex `Z` in degree zero.  The
differentials and initial defect vanish.  Every integer multiplication
`C_m:m in Z` is a radical-valued repair, and no nontrivial chain homotopies
exist.  The repair set is a `Z`-torsor, not a distinguished point.

Thus degeneracy can replace an existence obstruction by a canonicity
obstruction even after the boundary square is repairable.

## Physical consequence

A physical construction using a degenerate relative pairing must provide
two certificates:

1. vanishing of the Ledger 1328 obstruction class;
2. either vanishing of `H^0(Hom(C(G),R))` or an independent geometric
   normalization selecting one torsor point.

Perfect pairing makes `R=0`, so both repair freedom and this canonicity issue
disappear.  The frozen five-site evidence supplies neither the physical
radical nor a normalization choosing among radical repairs.
