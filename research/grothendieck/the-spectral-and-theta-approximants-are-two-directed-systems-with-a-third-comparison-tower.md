# The Spectral and Theta Approximants Are Two Directed Systems with a Third Comparison Tower

## Separation forced by the finite falsifier

Ordinary labelled theta cutoffs form an analytic directed system

$$
\Theta_1\longrightarrow\Theta_2\longrightarrow\cdots
\longrightarrow\Xi.
$$

They can have off-seam zeros. Consequently they cannot be characteristic
determinants of finite self-adjoint operators.

Any viable Hilbert--Pólya construction must use a different directed system:

$$
\mathcal A_1\longrightarrow\mathcal A_2\longrightarrow\cdots
\longrightarrow\mathcal A,
$$

where every $\mathcal A_N$ is a source-derived compression of an already
boundary-bearing operator and its characteristic zeros are real. Its finite
determinants need not equal $\Theta_N$.

The RH-bearing object is therefore a third tower of comparison cells

$$
\kappa_N:
\det(\mathcal A_N-\lambda)
\rightsquigarrow
\Theta_{r(N)}(\lambda),
$$

whose completed descent must yield the framed Xi section. There can be no
termwise divisor-preserving isomorphism $\kappa_N$, because the two finite
divisors disagree.

This is the precise multi-tower form of the current programme:

1. the analytic source tower constructs Xi;
2. the spectral tower constructs a self-adjoint limit;
3. the comparison tower proves that their completed determinant sections
   coincide.

The third tower is not optional bookkeeping. It must record exactly how
finite off-seam zeros escape, cancel against background divisors, or leave the
comparison chart.

## Transition data do not select the section

Let $\Delta_+$ and $\Delta_-$ be local determinant sections with seam
transition

$$
g=\frac{\Delta_+}{\Delta_-}.
$$

For any entire $h$,

$$
(\Delta_+,\Delta_-)
\longmapsto
(h\Delta_+,h\Delta_-)
$$

leaves $g$, its reciprocal law, its seam modulus, and every Čech cocycle
unchanged. If $h$ has zeros, the global divisor changes.

Thus two distinct authority problems have been conflated:

- transition authority determines the determinant line and chart coherence;
- section authority determines the distinguished divisor-bearing section.

A normalization stated only in terms of the transition cannot solve the
second problem. Nor do symmetry and finite order suffice: symmetric
polynomial factors preserve the principal analytic type while changing the
divisor.

## Required source construction

The distinguished section must be produced as the determinant of an actual
source morphism, not selected among sections afterward. A valid comparison
tower must therefore provide:

1. source-derived spectral compressions $\mathcal A_N$;
2. strong or norm resolvent convergence to a fixed self-adjoint $\mathcal A$;
3. regularized determinant convergence in each resolvent chart;
4. reciprocal transition maps derived before determinant compression;
5. a canonical determinant vector, vacuum wedge, or boundary morphism fixing
   the local sections rather than only their ratio;
6. a completed comparison with the independently constructed theta section;
7. rejection of common multiplication by every divisor-bearing $h$ at the
   source-morphism level.

If item 5 is absent, the two-chart programme proves only that a determinant
line exists. It does not identify Xi or constrain its zeros.

## Categorical consequence

Poisson completion cannot be an exact divisor-preserving colimit of ordinary
theta cutoffs into the spectral tower. It must act as a nonconservative
localization or derived completion: some finite divisor information is not
transported as divisor information into the completed spectral object.

That loss is admissible only if the comparison tower types it explicitly.
Otherwise the phrase “spectrality emerges at completion” merely restates RH.

The next concrete target is the smallest source-defined spectral compression
and its canonical determinant vector. The first falsifier is whether two such
compressions with identical transition data admit a common divisor-changing
factor while preserving all declared constructor laws.
