# The Lagrangian Seam Splitting Has a Three–Four–Three Quadratic Tower

## Linear seam rank

Let \(L=\Gamma_R\) be the two-dimensional Lagrangian graph of the compatible
reciprocal-sector pair inside the four-dimensional doubled phase space. A
Lagrangian complement \(N\) is also two-dimensional. It represents the
smallest transverse extension capable of distinguishing departure from the
Fourier-compatible graph.

Thus the seam extension is rank two at the linear level:

\[
W=L\oplus N,
\qquad
\dim L=\dim N=2.
\]

## Quadratic control decomposition

Quadratic controls on \(W\) split canonically by bidegree:

\[
\operatorname{Sym}^2(W^*)
=
\operatorname{Sym}^2(L^*)
\oplus
(L^*\otimes N^*)
\oplus
\operatorname{Sym}^2(N^*).
\]

Their dimensions are

\[
3+4+3=10.
\]

These are exactly the ten quadratic generators underlying
\(\mathfrak{sp}_4\):

- three tangent or bulk controls;
- four mixed bulk–seam comparison controls;
- three pure seam controls.

The four-dimensional layer is therefore not another state sector. It is the
space of pairwise comparisons between the two-dimensional bulk and
two-dimensional seam sectors.

## Correction to the wall intuition

The “fourth wall” language was close but imprecise. What has rank four is the
mixed coherence layer. The new state carried by the seam has rank two. Once
that state is admitted, its three internal quadratic controls and four mixed
comparisons complete the original three bulk controls to ten.

This gives a source-typing template:

```text
bulk state:          2
seam state:          2
bulk controls:       3
comparison controls: 4
seam controls:       3
```

## Why this matters for scalar zeros

A scalar readout formed only from the tangent three cannot distinguish a
state moving in a normal seam direction. Conversely, retaining only the four
mixed comparisons without the rank-two seam state would treat relations as
free-standing observables. Both truncations lose meaning.

The complete quadratic object retains state, comparison, and control levels
together. This is the smallest finite symplectic model consistent with the
earlier finding that the seam cannot be reconstructed from the tail.

## Scope and falsifier

This is a canonical linear-algebra consequence of a rank-two Lagrangian graph
and complement. It does not prove that the completed theta seam has rank two
in its analytic or arithmetic topology.

The theta application fails if the source-derived normal object has different
rank, if no Lagrangian complement is admitted by the completion, or if the
mixed tensor products do not extend to the boundary domain.
