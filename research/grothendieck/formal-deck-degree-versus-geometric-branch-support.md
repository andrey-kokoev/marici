# Formal deck degree versus geometric branch support

## Reconciliation

The mod-two deck algebra

\[
\mathbf F_2[\epsilon_1,\ldots,\epsilon_5]/(\epsilon_i^2)
\]

has 31 nonzero square-free monomials with formal degree profile
\((5,10,10,5,1)\). This remains an exact Loewy and kernel-norm theorem.

It does not imply the existence of 31 geometric simultaneous branch strata.
For the source equations \(F_i=\det(H)(\ell-r_i)^2\), differences
\(F_i-F_j\) are affine-linear in three loop coordinates. Consequently the
generic complex geometry realizes singles, pairs, and triples, while
quadruples and the quintuple are generically empty. On the real
positive-definite chamber, distinct branches do not meet unless external
points collide.

## Supported higher degrees

On the Gram-nondegenerate chart, degree four can acquire branch support only
on

\[
R_0=d^T\operatorname{adj}(G)d=0.
\]

Degree five additionally requires \(C_5=0\). Thus Loewy degree is a
potential supported coefficient grade, not generic loop-base codimension.

On the rank-two chart, \(\det G=0\) alone is not support. After imposing
affine consistency, the local geometry supplies the intrinsic anti-invariant
line and fifth-point selector

\[
w^2+R=0,
\qquad
Nw=C_p,
\qquad
C_p^2+N^2R=0.
\]

The odd Kummer coordinate and odd normal incidence transport together to an
even scalar pairing. This is a positive algebraic coefficient-plus-selector
case, but no physical current or relative-chain map follows.
