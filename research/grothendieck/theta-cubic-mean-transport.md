# Theta cubic mean transport

Status: live bounded successor to `theta-cubic-plucker-transport.md`.

## 1. Source data

For the tilted completed theta law

\[
 dQ_t(u)=Z_t^{-1}u^{2t}\Phi(u)\,du,
 \qquad
 R=u^2,quad H=\log u,quad W=\frac{V'(u)}u,quad V=-\log\Phi,
\]

put

\[
 A=\mathbb E_tR,quad M=\mathbb E_tW,quad
 B=\mathbb E_tRW,quad C=B-AM,quad \delta=C/B.
\]

The proved degree-two theorem gives `C>0`.  The cubic differential target is

\[
 \left|\frac{d}{dt}\delta^{-1/2}\right|\le1.
                                                        \tag{1}
\]

## 2. The two canonical laws

Let `nu_t` be the `RW`-size-biased law:

\[
 d\nu_t(u)=\frac{R(u)W(u)}B,dQ_t(u).
\]

Let `mu_t` be the normalized Andréief separation law on two source copies:

\[
 d\mu_t(u_1,u_2)
 =\frac{(R_1-R_2)(W_1-W_2)}{2C}
   dQ_t(u_1)dQ_t(u_2).
\]

Its density is nonnegative because the source theorem makes `W` increasing
with `R`.  Define one-dimensional pushforward laws

\[
 \alpha_t=\operatorname{Law}_{\mu_t}(H_1+H_2),
 \qquad
 \beta_t=\operatorname{Law}_{\nu_t}(H_0+h_t),
 \qquad h_t=\mathbb E_tH.
                                                        \tag{2}
\]

Direct differentiation gives

\[
 D_t:=\mathbb E\alpha_t-\mathbb E\beta_t
 =\frac12\frac d{dt}\log\delta_t.
                                                        \tag{3}
\]

Hence (1) is exactly

\[
 \boxed{|D_t|\le\sqrt{\delta_t}.}                       \tag{4}
\]

No variance or auxiliary Hilbert norm appears in (4).

## 3. Faithful transport certificate

Kantorovich duality supplies the sufficient theorem

\[
 \boxed{W_1(\alpha_t,\beta_t)\le\sqrt{\delta_t}.}       \tag{5}
\]

Indeed the identity function is one-Lipschitz, so
`|D_t|<=W_1(alpha_t,beta_t)`.  Since the state space is one-dimensional,

\[
 W_1(\alpha_t,\beta_t)
 =\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
   |F_{\alpha_t}(s)-F_{\beta_t}(s)|\,ds.                \tag{6}
\]

This differs decisively from the discarded independent second-moment bound:
the monotone coupling cancels common internal spread and charges only the
displacement required to transport one source law into the other.

## 4. Exact source form of the CDFs

Writing `q_t` for the density of `Q_t`, (2) gives

\[
 F_{\alpha_t}(s)
 =\frac1{2C}
   \iint_{u_1u_2\le e^s}
   (u_1^2-u_2^2)(W(u_1)-W(u_2))
   q_t(u_1)q_t(u_2)\,du_1du_2,                          \tag{7}
\]

whereas

\[
 F_{\beta_t}(s)
 =\frac1B\int_0^{e^{s-h_t}}
   u^2W(u)q_t(u)\,du.                                   \tag{8}
\]

Thus the missing theorem is an explicit comparison between a hyperbolic
product cut in the oriented two-copy source and a one-copy size-biased cut.
There is no free comparison measure left to choose.

## 5. Attack order

The next source lemma should be attempted in this order:

1. disintegrate (7) at fixed product `p=u_1u_2` using
   `u_1=sqrt(p)e^v`, `u_2=sqrt(p)e^{-v}`;
2. use monotonicity of `W` to retain a positive `v`-kernel;
3. apply the reciprocal theta sewing before integrating in `p`;
4. compare the resulting product marginal with the shifted `RW` bias in
   (8);
5. integrate the signed CDF difference only after its canonical crossings
   have been identified.

The sharp falsifier for (5) is a tilt at which the CDF area in (6) exceeds
`sqrt(C/B)`.  Failure of (5) would not falsify cubic Jensen positivity: it
would show only that full Wasserstein displacement is still stronger than
the required barycentre displacement (4).  The exact fallback remains (4),
not a repaired variance theorem.

## 6. Product disintegration collapses the two-copy law

Use the ordered product-ratio chart

\[
 u_1=\sqrt p\,e^v,\qquad
 u_2=\sqrt p\,e^{-v},\qquad p>0,\quad v\ge0.             \tag{9}
\]

The full two-copy integral is symmetric under exchange of the labels.  More
importantly, the chart has the exact Jacobian

\[
 |du_1du_2|=dp\,dv.                                    \tag{10}
\]

Let `q_t(u)=Z_t^{-1}u^{2t}\Phi(u)`.  Since
`u_1^{2t}u_2^{2t}=p^{2t}`, the entire tilt dependence leaves the ratio
coordinate.  Define the fixed positive product kernel

\[
 k(p)=2p\int_0^\infty
 \sinh(2v)
 \bigl[W(\sqrt p e^v)-W(\sqrt p e^{-v})\bigr]
 \Phi(\sqrt p e^v)\Phi(\sqrt p e^{-v})\,dv.             \tag{11}
\]

Then `k(p)>0`, and the product marginal of `mu_t` is simply

\[
 dP_t(p)=\frac{p^{2t}k(p)\,dp}
                   {\int_0^\infty p^{2t}k(p)\,dp}.      \tag{12}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \boxed{\alpha_t=\operatorname{Law}_{P_t}(\log p).}    \tag{13}
\]

Thus the apparent two-copy moving measure is a one-dimensional exponential
family generated by one fixed source kernel.  No `t`-dependent conditional
law in the ratio variable survives after disintegration.

For comparison, if `x=\log u`, the unshifted logarithmic coordinate under
`nu_t` has density proportional to

\[
 e^{(2t+3)x}W(e^x)\Phi(e^x)\,dx.                        \tag{14}
\]

Therefore both sides of the transport problem are fixed-base exponential
families with the same slope parameter `2t`; only their base potentials and
the canonical translation `h_t` differ.  Explicitly, with

\[
 \kappa(r)=e^r k(e^r),\qquad
 \zeta(x)=e^{3x}W(e^x)\Phi(e^x),                        \tag{15}
\]

we have

\[
 \alpha_t(dr)\propto e^{2tr}\kappa(r)\,dr,\qquad
 (\beta_t-h_t)(dx)\propto e^{2tx}\zeta(x)\,dx.          \tag{16}
\]

This is the first real combinatorial compression of the cubic transport
problem: prove a uniform Wasserstein comparison between two natural
exponential families built from the same completed theta source.

## 7. The next exact comparison object

Let

\[
 K_t(s)=
 \frac{\int_{-\infty}^s e^{2tr}\kappa(r)\,dr}
      {\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{2tr}\kappa(r)\,dr},
\]

and

\[
 Z_t(s)=
 \frac{\int_{-\infty}^{s-h_t}e^{2tx}\zeta(x)\,dx}
      {\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{2tx}\zeta(x)\,dx}.
\]

The faithful sufficient theorem (5) is now the scalar inequality

\[
 \boxed{
 \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|K_t(s)-Z_t(s)|\,ds
 \le\sqrt{\delta_t}.
 }                                                       \tag{17}
\]

The immediate structural question is whether the likelihood ratio

\[
 \frac{\kappa(s)}{\zeta(s-h_t)}                         \tag{18}
\]

has a source-forced single crossing after modular sewing.  Exponential
tilting cancels from this ratio.  The translation `h_t`, however, does not:
a single-crossing theorem must hold uniformly along the source-determined
translation orbit.  It is not enough to check one unshifted pair of bases.

## 8. Orientation and magnitude must remain separate

Suppose the normalized densities in (16), after translation by `h_t`, have
one likelihood-ratio crossing.  They are then stochastically ordered, so

\[
 W_1(\alpha_t,\beta_t)
 =|\mathbb E\alpha_t-\mathbb E\beta_t|=|D_t|.           \tag{19}
\]

This makes the Wasserstein certificate faithful: it removes the excess
spread charged by the independent quadratic coupling.  But crossing alone
does **not** prove the magnitude bound `|D_t|<=sqrt(delta_t)`.  It determines
the orientation of the CDF difference, not its area.  A second source law is
still needed to control that area.

The magnitude has a particularly clean partition-function form.  Put

\[
 \mathcal Z_t=\int_0^\infty u^{2t}\Phi(u)\,du,
\]

\[
 \mathcal J_t=\int_0^\infty u^{2t+2}W(u)\Phi(u)\,du,
 \qquad
 \mathcal I_t=\int_0^\infty p^{2t}k(p)\,dp.             \tag{20}
\]

The definitions and the ordered-chart symmetry give exactly

\[
 B_t=\frac{\mathcal J_t}{\mathcal Z_t},qquad
 C_t=\frac{\mathcal I_t}{\mathcal Z_t^2},qquad
 \boxed{\delta_t=\frac{\mathcal I_t}
                         {\mathcal Z_t\mathcal J_t}}.   \tag{21}
\]

Moreover,

\[
 \mathbb E\alpha_t=\frac12(\log\mathcal I_t)',
 \quad
 \mathbb E\beta_t=\frac12(\log(\mathcal Z_t\mathcal J_t))',
\]

and hence

\[
 \boxed{D_t=\frac12(\log\delta_t)'.}                   \tag{22}
\]

The cubic differential theorem is therefore the scalar partition inequality

\[
 \boxed{|(\log\delta_t)'|\le2\sqrt{\delta_t}.}          \tag{23}
\]

Equivalently, for the inverse separation length

\[
 L_t=\delta_t^{-1/2},
\]

it is simply

\[
 \boxed{|L_t'|\le1.}                                   \tag{24}
\]

The source task has now split without ambiguity:

1. modular sewing or a likelihood-ratio theorem must orient the transport;
2. a partition-function comparison must limit its displacement speed.

Confusing these would reproduce the earlier mistake in which positivity of a
measure was asked to orient its oscillatory transform.  Here orientation is
single crossing; coercivity is the unit-speed law (24).

## 9. The `RW` channel is an exact universal carrier

The score definition contains an integration-by-parts collapse:

\[
 W(u)\Phi(u)=-\frac{\Phi'(u)}u.
\]

Therefore, with the endpoint terms vanishing for `t>=0`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal J_t
 &=-\int_0^\infty u^{2t+1}\Phi'(u)\,du\\
 &=(2t+1)\int_0^\infty u^{2t}\Phi(u)\,du
 =(2t+1)\mathcal Z_t.
\end{aligned}                                           \tag{25}
\]

Hence

\[
 \boxed{B_t=2t+1},qquad
 \boxed{\delta_t=\frac{\mathcal I_t}
 {(2t+1)\mathcal Z_t^2}}.                              \tag{26}
\]

All arithmetic dependence has disappeared from the normalization of the
one-copy comparison channel.  It survives only in the source partition
`Z_t` and the separation partition `I_t`.

There is also an exact CDF identity.  If `Q_t(u)` denotes the source CDF and
`N_t(u)` the CDF of `nu_t`, then

\[
\begin{aligned}
 N_t(u)
 &=\frac{-\int_0^u s^{2t+1}\Phi'(s)\,ds}
         {(2t+1)\mathcal Z_t}\\
 &=Q_t(u)-\frac{u^{2t+1}\Phi(u)}
                  {(2t+1)\mathcal Z_t}.
\end{aligned}                                           \tag{27}
\]

Thus

\[
 \boxed{N_t(u)<Q_t(u)\quad(u>0),}                       \tag{28}
\]

so the `RW`-biased coordinate is canonically stochastically larger than the
ordinary source coordinate.  This ordering is exact and source-independent;
the positive gap is a single boundary-current profile.

For `t>1/2`, a second integration by parts gives

\[
 M_t=(2t-1)\frac{\mathcal Z_{t-1}}{\mathcal Z_t},
 \qquad
 A_t=\frac{\mathcal Z_{t+1}}{\mathcal Z_t},             \tag{29}
\]

and hence

\[
 \boxed{
 \delta_t
 =1-\frac{2t-1}{2t+1}
 \frac{\mathcal Z_{t-1}\mathcal Z_{t+1}}
      {\mathcal Z_t^2}.
 }                                                       \tag{30}
\]

Equation (27) identifies the one-copy side of the desired transport with a
source CDF plus one explicit seam current.  The remaining nontrivial step is
now sharply localized: show that the product-separation CDF (13), after the
translation by `h_t`, differs from this ordered source-plus-seam presentation
by an area no larger than the intrinsic length in (26).

## 10. Carrier displacement versus separation displacement

The universal identity `J_t=(2t+1)Z_t` also fixes the logarithmic barycentre
of the one-copy channel.  Since exponential-family differentiation gives

\[
 \mathbb E_{\nu_t}H
 =\frac12(\log\mathcal J_t)',
\]

we obtain

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathbb E_{\nu_t}H=h_t+\frac1{2t+1}.
 }                                                       \tag{31}
\]

Thus the translated comparison law has barycentre

\[
 \mathbb E\beta_t=2h_t+\frac1{2t+1}.                   \tag{32}
\]

On the separation side,

\[
 \mathbb E\alpha_t
 =2h_t+\frac{C_t'}{2C_t}.                               \tag{33}
\]

Consequently the exact tangent is

\[
 \boxed{
 D_t=\frac{C_t'}{2C_t}-\frac1{2t+1}.
 }                                                       \tag{34}
\]

The cubic differential theorem is therefore

\[
 \boxed{
 \left|
 \frac{C_t'}{2C_t}-\frac1{2t+1}
 \right|
 \le\sqrt{\frac{C_t}{2t+1}}.
 }                                                       \tag{35}
\]

This identifies the two velocities that were hidden inside the Wronskian:

- `C_t'/(2C_t)` is the displacement of the logarithmic sum coordinate caused
  by orienting two copies with their separation determinant;
- `1/(2t+1)` is the exact displacement of the universal score carrier caused
  by the boundary current (27).

The continuous sufficient route to degree three asks that their mismatch fit
inside one intrinsic quadratic separation length.  The explanation sought is
no longer merely “two CDFs are close.”  It is

\[
 \boxed{
 \text{oriented pair separation tracks the universal carrier speed
 to within its own square-root reserve.}
 }                                                       \tag{36}
\]

The separation partition itself has the exact Turan form.  Combining
`I_t=C_t Z_t^2`, (26), and (29) yields, for `t>1/2`,

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal I_t
 =(2t+1)\mathcal Z_t^2
 -(2t-1)\mathcal Z_{t-1}\mathcal Z_{t+1}.
 }                                                       \tag{37}
\]

Hence (35) is a differential inequality for one explicit adjacent Turan
determinant of the theta Mellin partition.  This is not a new coordinate
conjecture: it is the continuous form of the cubic adjacent-coherence gate.

## 11. Return to the exact discrete gate

The unit-speed differential law remains only sufficient.  The Jensen problem
requires integer tilts, and the universal carrier makes its exact condition
considerably simpler.  Since

\[
 L_t=\delta_t^{-1/2}=\sqrt{\frac{2t+1}{C_t}},
\]

the cubic gate at consecutive tilts is precisely

\[
 \boxed{
 \left|
 \sqrt{\frac{2t+3}{C_{t+1}}}
 -\sqrt{\frac{2t+1}{C_t}}
 \right|\le1.
 }                                                       \tag{38}
\]

Equivalently, using the Turan partition (37),

\[
 \boxed{
 \left|
 \sqrt{\frac{(2t+3)\mathcal Z_{t+1}^2}{\mathcal I_{t+1}}}
 -\sqrt{\frac{(2t+1)\mathcal Z_t^2}{\mathcal I_t}}
 \right|\le1.
 }                                                       \tag{39}
\]

This is the faithful theorem to attack first.  It permits the separation
length to move faster than unit speed between integers provided it returns
inside the next gate; the continuous inequality (35) forbids that and is
therefore strictly more demanding.

The revised priority is:

1. derive an adjacent-tilt transport directly between `mu_t` and `mu_{t+1}`;
2. exploit that their Radon--Nikodym derivative depends only on the product:
   `dmu_{t+1}/dmu_t` is proportional to `p^2`;
3. compare the resulting change of `C_t` with the exact carrier increment
   `(2t+1) -> (2t+3)`;
4. use the continuous CDF theorem only if the discrete transport cannot be
   closed directly.

Thus the remaining combinatorics is an adjacent size-bias operation on one
positive product kernel, not an uncontrolled change of a two-copy measure.
