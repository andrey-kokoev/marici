# Erratum: the anti-holomorphic seam selects a current quadrature; it does not annihilate the current

## The conflation

Ledger 3605 correctly proved the holomorphic sign law

\[
J(w^{-1})=-J(w).
\]

But its initial interpretation conflated two involutions:

\[
I(w)=w^{-1},
\qquad
R(w)=\frac1{\bar w}.
\]

The fixed points of `I` are only `w=±1`. The RH seam `|w|=1` is the fixed
locus of the anti-holomorphic involution `R`.

## Correct seam law

For real source weights,

\[
J(Rw)
=J(1/\bar w)
=-\overline{J(w)}.
\]

At a seam point `Rw=w`, this gives

\[
J(w)=-\overline{J(w)}.
\]

Therefore `J(w)` is purely imaginary. It need not vanish.

For example, take

\[
w=\frac{3+4i}{5}.
\]

Then `|w|=1` and

\[
w-w^{-1}=\frac{8}{5}i\ne0.
\]

So the relative current remains a genuine seam degree of freedom.

## Revised geometry

There are now three different operations:

1. holomorphic inversion pairs reciprocal spectral points and reverses the
   current;
2. conjugation supplies the real structure;
3. their anti-holomorphic composition fixes the unit seam and selects the
   imaginary current quadrature.

Scalar orbit summation erases the first relation. Treating anti-holomorphic
fixedness as literal current nullity erases the third. Both are unfaithful
compressions.

## Revised RH target

The seam is not characterized by disappearance of the relative relationship.
It is characterized by that relationship becoming purely tangential: only one
quadrature survives the real reciprocal identification.

Accordingly, the next theorem cannot be “a zero forces `J=0`.” It must compare
the normal and tangential quadratures of the sign-local current and show that a
source-admissible zero has no normal component. That still does not prove the
zero lies on the seam unless the current is faithful to radial displacement;
the smallest hostile test must search for off-seam zeros whose current is
accidentally purely imaginary.

This correction moves the programme from current annihilation to current
polarization.
