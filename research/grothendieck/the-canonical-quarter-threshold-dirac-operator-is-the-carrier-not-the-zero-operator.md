# The Canonical Quarter-Threshold Dirac Operator Is the Carrier, Not the Zero Operator

## Adjoint first-order sectors

Work initially on compactly supported smooth functions in

\[
L^2(\mathbb R_+,d\rho).
\]

For

\[
D=\rho\frac{d}{d\rho},
\]

integration by parts gives

\[
D^*=-D-1.
\]

Thus the two first-order factors exchanged by Fourier–Tate duality are also
Hilbert adjoints up to sign.

## Two-sector Dirac operator

Set

\[
A=D,
\qquad
A^*=-D-1,
\]

and form

\[
\mathcal Q
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&A^*\\
A&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

On its natural closure, \(\mathcal Q\) is self-adjoint. Its square is

\[
\mathcal Q^2
=
\begin{pmatrix}
-D(D+1)&0\\
0&-D(D+1)
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Define the self-adjoint centered dilation generator

\[
H=-i\left(D+\frac12\right).
\]

Then each diagonal block is

\[
L=-D(D+1)=H^2+\frac14.
\]

Therefore

\[
L\geq\frac14.
\]

## Source current

For the Gaussian vacuum \(g=e^{-\pi\rho^2}\), the theta frontier current is

\[
F
=
\frac1{2\pi}D(D+1)g
=
-\frac1{2\pi}Lg.
\]

Thus the current is generated by the canonical quarter-threshold Dirac square.
The lower bound is source-derived rather than fitted to the critical line.

## Spectrum audit

Under logarithmic coordinates with the unitary Haar correction, \(H\) becomes
the ordinary momentum operator on the real line. Hence

\[
\operatorname{spec}(H)=\mathbb R
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{spec}(L)
=
\left[\frac14,\infty\right].
\]

This is continuous spectrum.

The desired Riemann-zero operator would instead need a source-derived cyclic
spectral measure concentrated at the discrete values

\[
\gamma^2+\frac14.
\]

The operator \(L\) supplies the correct threshold and carrier geometry but not
that discrete measure.

## Consequence

The programme now separates two operator roles:

- \(L=H^2+1/4\) is the canonical archimedean carrier generating the completion
  current;
- the integer comb must impose an additional arithmetic boundary condition,
  extension, quotient, or scattering determinant if discrete zero data are to
  emerge.

Calling \(L\) the Hilbert–Pólya operator would conflate carrier spectrum with
comb-selected resonance data.

## Falsifier

The threshold theorem fails if \(D^*\ne-D-1\) or if logarithmic
unitarization does not turn \(H\) into real momentum. The zero-operator
identification fails because \(L\) has continuous rather than discrete
spectrum.
