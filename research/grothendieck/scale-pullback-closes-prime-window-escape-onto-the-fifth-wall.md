# Scale pullback closes prime-window escape onto the fifth wall

## Question

Can the widening reciprocal windows be completed without hiding their
large-prime limit inside an untyped bulk closure?

## Compactified prime scale

Replace logarithmic scale \(L=\log p\) by

\[
r=e^{-L}=p^{-1}.
\]

Finite primes lie at \(r>0\), while the large-prime direction becomes the
ordinary boundary point \(r=0\). Define

\[
\mathcal W(r,q)
=
H(q-\log r)-H(q+\log r)
\qquad(r>0).
\]

Since \(-\log r=L\), this is the reciprocal window

\[
\mathcal W(r,q)=H(q+L)-H(q-L).
\]

For every compact set of \(q\)-values,

\[
\mathcal W(r,q)\longrightarrow-1
\qquad(r\downarrow0).
\]

Hence the family has the canonical boundary value

\[
\mathcal W(0,q)=-1.
\]

## The pullback object

Let \(B=[0,1/2]\) be the compactified scale base. The arithmetic label map is

\[
\lambda:\mathbb P\longrightarrow B,
\qquad
\lambda(p)=p^{-1}.
\]

Let \(\overline{\mathcal E}_{\mathrm{tail}}\to B\) be the boundary-bearing
family whose finite fibers contain \(W_{-\log r}\) and whose boundary fiber
contains the constant carrier \(-1\). The source object is the pullback

\[
\mathcal P
=
\mathcal A_{\mathrm{arith}}
\times_B
\overline{\mathcal E}_{\mathrm{tail}}.
\]

An element retains both its arithmetic label and its reciprocal window. The
restricted-product completion is taken in this graph object, not in the
unlabelled image inside one Gaussian bulk space.

## Why the fifth wall is exactly the scale boundary

The boundary value is the negative constant. Under Fourier transform,

\[
\mathcal F(-1)=-\delta_0.
\]

Thus the compactified large-prime fiber lands in the already derived
constant–delta exchange plane. No sixth simultaneous component is required.
The fifth wall is reinterpreted as the Fourier-stable boundary at infinite
prime scale.

This also explains why the four-role affine description failed. Before scale
compactification the unit appeared only as a relation between opposite tail
orientations. Completion promotes it to an actual boundary fiber, so it must
be a linear coordinate from the start.

## Graph completion versus image completion

The image-only map forgets \(r\):

\[
(r,W_r)\longmapsto W_r.
\]

Its completion sees a sequence of Gaussian bulk vectors approach a constant
but cannot say which source direction produced that limit. The graph
completion instead retains

\[
(r,W_r)\longrightarrow(0,-1).
\]

The limit is therefore typed as scale-boundary incidence, not as an
unexpected bulk state.

The pullback does not make divergent prime coefficients summable. It makes
their possible defect land in a named boundary coordinate. Primitive and
prime-square currents still require their separate pro-Gram grades, but any
failure of completion now has a definite target: the constant–delta wall at
\(r=0\).

## Universal-property reading

A constructor into the completed source must provide two compatible pieces:

1. its arithmetic scale map into \(B\);
2. its five-cell state above that scale.

Compatibility forces the state at escaping prime scale to specialize to the
declared boundary fiber. A hostile completion that sends the same labels to
zero, or to an unrelated constant, does not factor through the pullback.

## Remaining gate

The next question is quantitative but now correctly typed: do the primitive
and square weighted sections extend to distributions on \(B\) whose boundary
value is cancelled by the archimedean current? Equivalently, does the full
modular section have zero incidence in the constant–delta fiber?

This must be proved before pushing the pullback to a scalar readout. A scalar
regularization performed first would again erase which boundary fiber
absorbed the divergence.

## Result

Compactifying prime scale and pulling back the five-cell family converts the
large-prime window escape into an explicit boundary specialization
\((0,-1)\). Fourier transport places that specialization in the delta wall.
The construction prevents hidden loss of scale provenance, while leaving the
actual primitive/square boundary cancellation as the next theorem.
