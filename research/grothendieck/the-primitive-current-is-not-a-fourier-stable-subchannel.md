# The Primitive Current Is Not a Fourier-Stable Subchannel

## Global duality versus channel stability

Fourier transform is an automorphism of the Schwartz rigging and its dual, so
the full distributional pairing is preserved when both arguments are
transported. This does not imply that a source-labelled primitive subchannel
is invariant by itself.

The distinction matters for the proposed full Green-packet mate. Demanding
separate equality of the `k=1`, `k=2`, and connected-tail contributions may be
incorrect if Fourier sewing mixes those types.

## Smallest exact orbit witness

On `Z/4`, take the primitive-like odd-support vector

\[
p=(0,1,0,1).
\]

The unnormalized Fourier transform gives

\[
F_4p=(2,0,-2,0).
\]

The image is supported on the complementary even-frequency control sector and
is not proportional to `p`. A second transform returns the reflected original
vector up to the usual factor:

\[
F_4^2p=4Rp=4p.
\]

Thus the smallest Fourier-stable packet generated by the primitive vector is
at least its two-component orbit

\[
\operatorname{span}\{p,F_4p\}.
\]

## Consequence for the current filtration

The three analytic regularity levels remain necessary, but they should not be
assumed to be Fourier-invariant blocks. The sewing map may be filtered or
triangular, with primitive distributional data mapping into a dual control
distribution and returning only after the second quarter-turn.

The correct requirement is therefore:

- unitarity or duality of the complete boundary-bearing packet;
- explicit cross-level transport matrix;
- recovery of the original filtration after the full reciprocal cycle;
- no deletion of the distributional orbit component.

Blockwise equality of `k=1` currents is too strong and would falsely reject
the native Fourier rotation.
