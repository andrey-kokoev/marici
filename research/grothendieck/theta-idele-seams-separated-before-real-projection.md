# Finite places separate seams that become dense in the real shadow

## 1. The apparent contradiction

Two exact statements coexist:

\[
  \mathbb Q^\times\ \text{is discrete in the ideles},
\]

while

\[
  \log|\mathbb Q_{>0}|_\infty
  \ \text{is dense in }\mathbb R.
\]

There is no contradiction. The second map forgets all finite valuations.
Distinct rational scales that are separated adelically can approach each
other arbitrarily closely in the real logarithmic coordinate.

## 2. Faithful scale coordinate

A positive rational number has the factorization

\[
  q=\prod_p p^{v_p(q)}.
\]

Its full scale label is not merely \(\log q\), but the valuation packet

\[
  \bigl(\log q,(v_p(q))_p\bigr),
\]

with finite support in the prime coordinates and constrained by the product
formula. The real coordinate is a scalar compression:

\[
  (v_p(q))_p
  \longmapsto
  \log q=\sum_p v_p(q)\log p.
\]

This compression is injective on exact rational labels, but its image is not
topologically discrete. It preserves identity while destroying separation.

That distinction matches the programme's durable rule:

\[
\boxed{
\text{faithful label coordinate}
\ne
\text{topologically faithful scalar readout}.}
\]

## 3. Meaning of the two half-planes

The two analytic half-planes see only the archimedean magnitude and reciprocal
orientation. They are therefore shadows of two adelic polarization sectors,
not the complete sector spaces themselves.

In the full carrier, a rational scale arrow retains its prime-valuation
address. After projection to the real logarithmic line, those addressed seams
form a dense orbit. What looks like an impossible dense family of physical
defects is consequently a projection artifact.

This gives a precise version of the operator's intuition that RH may involve
two sectors secretly presented as one scalar plane:

\[
\boxed{
\text{adelic reciprocal sectors}
\xrightarrow{\text{forget finite valuations}}
\text{two half-plane shadows}.}
\]

## 4. Correct boundary architecture

The boundary construction must occur before this forgetful projection:

\[
  \mathbb Q^\times\curvearrowright\mathbb A^\times
  \longrightarrow
  \text{adelic moving-seam descent}
  \longrightarrow
  \text{archimedean character family}.
\]

At the first stage, seam labels are discrete and composable. The product
formula supplies coherence between real and finite fluxes. Only after descent
may one compress to the spectral parameter \(z\).

Thus the appropriate Green cocycle is place-indexed:

\[
  \Omega_{\mathbb A}
  =
  \Omega_\infty+\sum_p\Omega_p,
\]

with the sum interpreted in the restricted-product/source-completed sense.
A residual real seam term may be cancelled or oriented only by its finite
companions; discarding them first makes the cancellation mechanism
unavailable.

## 5. New falsifier

Take two distinct valuation packets whose real logarithms are arbitrarily
close. A purely real boundary model must treat them as nearly coincident,
whereas the adelic model must retain orthogonal or separately addressable
prime labels. If the proposed boundary trace factors continuously through
the real coordinate alone, it has erased source information before proving
descent and is inadmissible.

The positive theorem would instead show that the all-place Green cocycle
descends under diagonal \(\mathbb Q^\times\) and that its archimedean
compression is the completed theta boundary form.

## 6. Scope

The separation-versus-projection distinction and valuation factorization are
exact. They repair the dense-seam interpretation: density is a property of
the real shadow, not of the full rational source. No all-place Green cocycle,
maximal self-adjoint descent, determinant comparison, or RH theorem is yet
proved.
