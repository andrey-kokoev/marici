# Labelwise tail dynamics do not generate the cross-tower Ward cell

## The proposed pullback

For a finite label set (X), let each source tail satisfy

\[
(\partial_q+s)G_n(q)+f_n(q)=0.
\]

Let the scalar endpoint readout be

\[
L_X(G)=\sum_{n\in X}G_n(0).
\]

The previous reduction showed that a Ward map cannot factor through (L_X)
on the ambient coefficient module. The natural repair was to restrict it to
the pullback cut out by the source equations, the endpoint domain, and
(L_X(G)=0).

## Smallest hostile dynamical packet

That restriction is still insufficient when the dynamics is labelwise.
Choose two nonzero decaying source tails with

\[
G_1(0)=a,
\qquad
G_2(0)=-a,
\qquad
a\ne0,
\]

and define their forcings by

\[
f_j=-(\partial_q+s)G_j.
\]

Then both labelwise tail equations hold exactly, both tails satisfy the
condition at infinity, and the aggregate endpoint output vanishes. Yet a
label-separating Ward port sees either (a) or (-a), so its packet is
nonzero.

This is not an arbitrary violation of the differential equation. The
inhomogeneous tail equation permits each label independently; it supplies no
relation between two endpoint amplitudes.

## Consequence

The desired cross-tower cell cannot be generated by the product of the
labelwise tail equations and scalar endpoint nullity. A new source law must
couple distinct arithmetic labels before scalar aggregation.

The corrected finite object is a further pullback

\[
Z_X^{\mathrm{arith}}(s)
=
Z_X^{\mathrm{tail}}(s)
\mathop{\times}_{\mathcal A_X}
\mathcal C_X^{\mathrm{coh}},
\]

where (mathcal C_X^{\mathrm{coh}}) is the source-authorized arithmetic
coherence locus and (mathcal A_X) retains the labelled endpoint packet.
The RH-bearing cell would be

\[
Z_X^{\mathrm{arith}}(s)\longrightarrow\ker W_X.
\]

The extra pullback is not a fifth independent tower. It is the horizontal
incidence law relating the input and control towers before the output tower
forgets labelwise information.

## What the new law must contain

A viable arithmetic coherence constructor must:

1. act on the labelled endpoint packet, not only its sum;
2. relate different labels through prime-scale, divisibility, or
   Fourier--Tate sewing data;
3. be defined before assuming a scalar zero;
4. commute with finite-cutoff restriction;
5. reject the two-label cancellation without merely requiring every label to
   vanish by definition.

The primitive and square currents are candidates for coordinates of this
law, but their existence alone is not the coupling theorem.

## Falsifier

At the smallest source cutoff, construct the actual arithmetic coherence map
(A_X). If there is a nonzero packet satisfying the labelwise tail equations,
both endpoint conditions, scalar nullity, and (A_X=0), while (W_X\ne0),
then that proposed cross-label law fails.
