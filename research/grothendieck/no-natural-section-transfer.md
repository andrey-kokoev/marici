# Even a split quotient need not admit a natural section transfer

## Naturality obstruction

Let `q:G->H` be a split finite-group surjection. A section-based transfer
preserving frozen selection depends on a section `s:H->G`. To be canonical
under source symmetries over `H`, the section must satisfy

`alpha compose s=s`

for every automorphism `alpha` of `G` with `q alpha=q`.

Such an invariant section need not exist, even when group-homomorphic sections
exist.

## Smallest split hostile control

Take `G=C2 x C2`, `H=C2`, and `q(a,b)=a`. There are exactly two
identity-preserving sections:

`s_0(1)=(1,0)`, `s_1(1)=(1,1)`.

Both are group homomorphisms. The base-preserving shear

`alpha(a,b)=(a,a+b)`

interchanges them. Hence neither is invariant, and there is no natural
section-based transfer under all automorphisms over the base.

## Consequence and scope

Existence of a split quotient does not supply a canonical
selection-preserving coefficient split. One needs a marking, framing,
orientation, chamber, or other source-derived lift datum that breaks the
automorphism symmetry. No such physical datum or Betti chain lift is produced
here.
