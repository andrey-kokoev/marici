# Rectangle parity determinants are spectral wedge sums

## Question

What is the exact positive decomposition behind the two mixed-prime parity determinants, conditional on a positive spectral measure?

## One spectral pair

Put `A=log p`, `B=log q`, and let one normalized stationary character be

`k_gamma(a)=cos(gamma a)`.

Define

`x_gamma=cos(gamma(A+B)/2)`,

`y_gamma=cos(gamma(B-A)/2)`,

`u_gamma=sin(gamma(A+B)/2)`,

`v_gamma=sin(gamma(B-A)/2)`.

The addition identities give the parity blocks

`G_+(gamma)=2 [x_gamma,y_gamma]^T[x_gamma,y_gamma]`,

`G_-(gamma)=2 [u_gamma,v_gamma]^T[u_gamma,v_gamma]`.

Thus each conjugate spectral pair contributes rank one to each parity channel. The full four-cell Gram has rank at most two for one pair.

## Positive mixtures

Let positive unnormalized spectral weights be `w_j` and `Z=sum_j w_j`. Then

`G_+=(2/Z) sum_j w_j X_j X_j^T`,

`G_-=(2/Z) sum_j w_j U_j U_j^T`,

where `X_j=(x_j,y_j)` and `U_j=(u_j,v_j)`.

The two-dimensional Cauchy--Binet identity yields exact determinant decompositions

`D_+=(4/Z^2) sum_(i<j) w_i w_j (x_i y_j-x_j y_i)^2`,

`D_-=(4/Z^2) sum_(i<j) w_i w_j (u_i v_j-u_j v_i)^2`.

Every term is a nonnegative squared spectral wedge. This proves rectangle positivity conditional on positive spectral weights and identifies the radical exactly: a parity determinant vanishes precisely when all positively weighted parity vectors are collinear.

## Gaussian weights and the first gap

For translated Gaussian tests,

`w_j(t)=m_j exp(-t gamma_j^2)`.

If the first two supported parity vectors are not collinear, the leading determinant term is

`D_+(t) ~ 4 (m_2/m_1) exp[-t(gamma_2^2-gamma_1^2)] (x_1 y_2-x_2 y_1)^2`,

and similarly with `(u,v)` for `D_-`. This gives the exact coefficient behind the effective-gap observation.

## Relation to forest--cycle factorization

This is a genuine positive cycle decomposition, but its atoms are exterior products of distinct spectral parity vectors. They are not route holonomies and are not generated by multiplying one-prime edge correlations. The rectangle cycle detects spectral diversity: one conjugate pair lies in the radical, while two noncollinear pairs create positive area.

A source-side proof cannot assume these positive weights without assuming the on-line spectral representation. The arithmetic task is therefore to reproduce or dominate these wedge sums directly from endpoint, archimedean, and prime terms. The formula states the target decomposition but does not derive positivity from the explicit formula.

## Disposition

The first mixed-prime rectangle admits an exact conditional sum-of-squares factorization. This supports a revised forest--cycle programme only if `cycle` means parity-sector spectral wedge energy. The original edge-composition and route-holonomy interpretations remain rejected.
