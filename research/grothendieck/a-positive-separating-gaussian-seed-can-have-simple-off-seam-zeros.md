# A Positive Separating Gaussian Seed Can Have Simple Off-Seam Zeros

## Hostile pointed source

\[
\phi_a(u)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi a}}
\exp\left(-\frac{u^2}{4a}\right)
\]

and define the strictly positive even seed

\[
\Phi(u)=\phi_a(u)+c\phi_b(u),
\qquad c>0.
\]

Its Fourier transform is

\[
F(z)=e^{-az^2}+ce^{-bz^2}.
\]

The seed is nonzero everywhere and therefore separates the bounded even heat
commutant exactly as the theta seed does.

## Explicit off-seam divisor

The zeros satisfy

\[
e^{-(a-b)z^2}=-c,
\]

so

\[
z^2=-\frac{\log c+(2k+1)\pi i}{a-b}.
\]

For \(c=1\), every zero has both nonzero real and imaginary parts. The divisor
is closed under \(z\mapsto-z\) and conjugation, but it is not confined to the
imaginary seam.

The zeros are simple. At a zero,

\[
F'(z)=2c(a-b)z e^{-bz^2}\ne0.
\]

Thus the Clark conormal is nonzero and the local rank-two seam normal form is
perfectly regular at the hostile off-seam zero.
