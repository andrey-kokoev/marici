# The Two Euler Hilbert Vacua Meet by Simultaneous Escape on the Critical Line

## Direct source vacuum

At prime (p\), the endpoint-normalized direct valuation vacuum is

\[
v_{p,s}^+=\sum_{k\ge0}p^{-ks}e_k.
\]

Writing \(\sigma=\Re s\), its squared local Hilbert norm is

\[
\|v_{p,s}^+\|^2
=\sum_{k\ge0}p^{-2k\sigma}
=\frac{1}{1-p^{-2\sigma}}.
\]

Therefore the unnormalized restricted-product vacuum has formal norm

\[
\|v_s^+\|^2
=\prod_p\frac{1}{1-p^{-2\sigma}}
=\zeta(2\sigma).
\]

It is a Hilbert vector exactly when

\[
\sigma>\frac12.
\]

## Reciprocal source vacuum

The reciprocal local parameter is (p^{s-1}\), giving

\[
v_{p,s}^-=\sum_{k\ge0}p^{k(s-1)}e_k
\]

and

\[
\|v_s^-\|^2
=\prod_p\frac{1}{1-p^{-2(1-\sigma)}}
=\zeta(2-2\sigma).
\]

It is a Hilbert vector exactly when

\[
\sigma<\frac12.
\]

## The simultaneous-loss wall

The two ordinary Hilbert realizations occupy complementary open sectors:

\[
v_s^+\in\mathcal H
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Re s>\frac12,
\]

\[
v_s^-\in\mathcal H
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Re s<\frac12.
\]

On the critical line both norms contain the harmonic Euler product

\[
\prod_p(1-p^{-1})^{-1}
\]

and diverge. Thus \(\Re s=1/2\) is exactly the locus where the direct and
reciprocal ordinary Hilbert meanings fail simultaneously. The completed Tate
object is a relative sewing of two sectors, not one Hilbert state analytically
continued through an innocent coordinate wall.

This derives the half-offset from the square norm of the local half-density.
The exponent (2\sigma\) reaches the prime harmonic threshold at one.

## Normalization is not harmless

Each local vacuum could be divided by its norm, but that changes the endpoint
normalization and multiplies the scalar source by the divergent product of
local normalization factors. Such normalization moves the primitive and
prime-square boundary currents rather than eliminating them. It is therefore
not a source-preserving cure.

The full tensor product of local inverse operators is an even stronger and
usually divergent demand. The theorem concerns the distinguished
endpoint-normalized source vacuum, which is the smallest state-level
implementability test.

## Scope boundary

Simultaneous Hilbert escape explains why the critical line is the sewing wall.
It does not explain why the completed relative scalar vanishes there or only
there. A relative object can remain nonzero on its sewing locus, and scalar
cancellation could still occur away from it after completion.

The RH-bearing target is now precise: prove that any zero of the completed
relative section represents a loss of pairing that can occur only when neither
ordinary Euler vacuum is available. The zero-to-relative-defect bridge remains
the missing theorem.
