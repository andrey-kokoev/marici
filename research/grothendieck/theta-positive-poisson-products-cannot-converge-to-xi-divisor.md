# Positive local Poisson products cannot converge normally to the \(\xi\) divisor

## 1. Finite-place positivity

For a finite prime set \(S\), the complete two-copy relative-phase factor is

\[
  P_S(x)
  =
  \prod_{p\in S}
  \left|1-p^{-1/2-ix}\right|^{-2}.
\]

Every factor and therefore every finite product is strictly positive for real
\(x\). Its holomorphic sector representatives are zero-free wherever their
Euler denominators are defined.

## 2. Normal-limit obstruction

Hurwitz's theorem gives a sharp constraint. A locally uniform limit on a
connected domain of holomorphic zero-free functions is either zero-free or
identically zero.

The completed theta scalar \(X\) is not identically zero and has known real
zeros. Therefore no sequence of zero-free finite Euler products, multiplied
by nowhere-zero holomorphic renormalization units, can converge locally
uniformly to \(X\) on a domain containing one of those zeros.

\[
\boxed{
\text{the }\xi\text{ divisor cannot arise from normal convergence of
positive local scalar factors}.}
\]

This is independent of RH because critical-line zeros are already known to
exist.

## 3. Consequence for completion

At least one of the following must occur:

1. scalar convergence fails near the divisor;
2. the renormalization ceases to be a nowhere-zero holomorphic unit;
3. the limit is taken only after passing to an operator or distributional
   object; or
4. zero formation occurs through loss of uniform invertibility in an
   infinite-dimensional limit.

The third and fourth possibilities are compatible with the unitary defect
theorem. Every finite approximation may have a strictly positive coercivity
reserve, while the infinite excited compression develops a kernel:

\[
  \varepsilon_N(x)>0,
  \qquad
  \varepsilon_N(x)\longrightarrow0.
\]

The zero is then not cancellation of finitely many positive scalar factors.
It is closure of an infinite operator gap.

## 4. Why the local Poisson theorem still matters

The local theorem controls grammar and orientation before the limit:

- every prime Fock cone contributes a positive relative-phase kernel;
- no finite depth or prime creates a zero;
- negative primitive or squarefree channels remain forbidden; and
- any zero must be a genuinely global completion event.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{local arithmetic determines admissible positive transport;}
\quad
\text{global completion determines spectral closure}.}
\]

This is a stronger separation than “Euler product versus analytic
continuation.” It specifies the categorical change required at completion:
from scalar products to an infinite operator limit.

## 5. Revised RH target

Construct a directed system of finite-place excited-sector compressions
\(D_S(z)\) such that:

1. every local transition is derived from the positive Poisson/Fock packet;
2. the completed archimedean boundary supplies compatible connecting maps;
3. \(D_S(x)^*D_S(x)>0\) at every finite stage;
4. the strong/resolvent limit \(D(x)\) satisfies
   \[
   \det(D(x)^*D(x))
   =
   |X(x)/X(0)|^2;
   \]
5. modular contractivity prevents kernel formation off the unitary boundary.

The last item is RH-bearing. The preceding items explain how zeros can appear
without any finite local negative factor.

## 6. Falsifiers

The architecture fails if:

1. a finite source-authorized stage already requires a negative local
   channel;
2. connecting maps erase prime labels or create squarefree primitives;
3. the operator limit depends on an arbitrary exhaustion;
4. its kernel is unrelated to the theta vacuum defect; or
5. off-axis kernel exclusion is obtained only by assuming the divisor.

## 7. Scope

Finite-place positivity and the normal-limit obstruction are exact. They show
that scalar positive-factor completion cannot produce the known theta
divisor. No directed operator system, exhaustion-independent limit,
contractive off-axis theorem, or RH result is constructed.
