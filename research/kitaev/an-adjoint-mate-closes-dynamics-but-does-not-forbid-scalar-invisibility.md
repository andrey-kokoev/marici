# An adjoint mate closes dynamics but does not forbid scalar invisibility

## Question

The previous packet isolated a missing reverse incidence

\[
p:F\longrightarrow Y,
\qquad
q:Y\longrightarrow F.
\]

Grothendieck sharpened this to an adjoint-mate coherence cell: source-derived
nondegenerate pairings on `F` and `Y` should determine `q` from `p`.  This
packet asks the next Deutschian question: does that cell itself make a
nonzero scalar-invisible state impossible?

The answer is no.  It solves reciprocity and conservative closure.  A second,
independent port-collocation law is needed to confine scalar zeros.

## Exact mate theorem

Let `F` and `Y` be finite-dimensional complex spaces with positive Gram
matrices `H_F` and `H_Y`.  For every forward incidence `p:F -> Y`, there is a
unique mate

\[
q=H_F^{-1}p^*H_Y
\]

satisfying

\[
\langle pf,y\rangle_{H_Y}=\langle f,qy\rangle_{H_F}.
\]

Consequently

\[
K=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&p\\
-q&0
\end{pmatrix}
\]

is skew-adjoint in the direct-sum metric `diag(H_Y,H_F)`.  Its evolution is
norm preserving.  The mate fixes the sign and phase of reverse incidence once
the two metrics are frozen.

This result is strong but information-neutral: arbitrary positive choices of
`H_F` and `H_Y` manufacture a mate for every `p`.  Source authority resides in
the derivation of the metrics and their boundary deltas, not in the algebraic
mate formula.

## Minimal counterexample

Take one-dimensional `F` and `Y`, unit metrics, and `p=q=1`.  Then

\[
K=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1\\
-1&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Starting from `x(0)=(1,0)`, the conservative trajectory is

\[
x(t)=(\cos t,-\sin t).
\]

For the scalar observer `c=(1,0)`, the output is `cos t` and vanishes at
`t=pi/2`, although the state has unit norm, the mate law is exact, and the
closed dynamics is skew-adjoint.  Therefore:

> exact reciprocity plus positive conservation does not imply pointwise
> scalar visibility.

Even observability of `(K,c)` only says that an output history determines the
initial state.  It does not say that one output sample, or one analytic matrix
coefficient, is zero-free.

## The missing port law

Grothendieck's three-point hostile model shows the invariant problem.  A
self-adjoint carrier `A` with distinct source and observer ports `v` and `u`
produces the cross coefficient

\[
u^*(A-z)^{-1}v.
\]

Self-adjointness of `A` does not confine its zeros.  The zero-confined object
is instead a diagonal positive coefficient

\[
v^*H(A-z)^{-1}v,
\qquad H>0,
\]

with the appropriate self-adjointness relation between `A` and `H`.

Thus the required extra cell is not merely `q=p^dagger`.  It must collocate
the endpoint observer with the metric dual of the source port:

\[
u=Hv.
\]

In finite dimensions, a positive definite Hermitian `H` satisfying `Hv=u`
can exist only if

\[
v^*u>0
\]

is real.  This condition is also sufficient.  It is therefore the smallest
port-compatibility falsifier before any spectral calculation.  A phase,
negative value, or zero in `v^*u` prevents positive collocation in the frozen
frame.

For theta/Tate, the relevant `H` cannot be fitted after observing the scalar
section.  It must be obtained from the injective Schwartz--Bruhat trace range,
retain primitive, square, seam, and endpoint currents, and survive the rigged
completion.  Ordinary local weighted Hilbert metrics have already been ruled
out.

## Effect-space formulation

Let `W` be the span of calibrated scalar effects generated by authorized
constructor words on the acted-on probe module.  A rival state difference
`Delta` is behaviorally real only if some `E` in `W` has nonzero pairing with
`Delta` and the theory predicts the response.

Adjoining the mate `q` can enlarge `W` through mixed words such as `pq`, `qp`,
and boundary-return paths.  Merely writing the formal adjoint does not.  The
hard-to-vary test is therefore:

1. derive the mate metrics from the source;
2. show that mixed forward-return words are admitted perturbations;
3. show that they add an effect transverse to the previous behavioral kernel;
4. derive the endpoint/source collocation cell;
5. prove the resulting diagonal coefficient survives completion.

If `Delta` remains in `W`-perp after all authorized mixed words, it is quotient
data, not a hidden mechanism.

## Revised bottom

Reverse incidence was correctly identified as a missing causal capability,
but it is not by itself the zero-confining explanation.  There are now three
separate gates:

1. **Mate gate:** derive a closable reverse incidence from source pairings.
2. **Collocation gate:** identify the endpoint observer with the positive
   metric dual of the source port.
3. **Cyclicity and completion gate:** ensure the collocated port sees the
   admissible carrier and retains a uniform positive type after completion.

The first gate closes the noncommutative constructor algebra.  The second
turns a cross coefficient into a positive diagonal coefficient.  The third
prevents invisible invariant sectors and loss of strictness at infinity.

## Falsifiers

- A unitary two-port rotation with an instantaneous scalar zero.
- A self-adjoint carrier with a nonreal zero in a cross-resolvent coefficient.
- `v^*u` not positive real in the frozen source frame.
- A mate produced by a fitted metric rather than source constructors.
- A valid algebraic mate whose adjoint domain is not dense after rigged
  completion.
- A collocated port that is noncyclic on an admissible invariant sector.
- Finite positive collocation whose lower type bound tends to zero with the
  cutoff.

## Verdict

The new Deutschian question is sharper:

> What source-derived boundary colligation simultaneously authorizes the
> adjoint return path and proves that the theta endpoint observer is the
> positive metric dual of its forcing port, with cyclicity and strict type
> preserved by completion?

Without the collocation cell, reverse incidence explains how information can
return, but not why scalar invisibility is impossible.
