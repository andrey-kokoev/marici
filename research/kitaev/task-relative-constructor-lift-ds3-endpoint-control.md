# Task-relative constructor lift for D(S3) endpoint control

Owner: `marici.Kitaev`

## Question

Must a physical realization independently implement the six ideal gauge
quadrature families and two flux projectors, or is there a weaker exact
criterion for compiling the endpoint operations?

## Claim boundary

Independent generator-by-generator lifting is sufficient but not necessary.

Let `P` project onto the frozen endpoint module and let
`H_0,H_1,...,H_m` be the source-authorized drift and controlled
Hamiltonians. There are two distinct compiler regimes.

An invariant-path compiler requires

\[
(1-P)H_jP=0
\]

for every admitted Hamiltonian. Define the compressed generators

\[
h_j=PH_jP
\]

and their real dynamical Lie algebra

\[
\mathfrak g_{\rm src}
=
\operatorname{Lie}_{\mathbb R}(ih_0,ih_1,\ldots,ih_m).
\]

Write the frozen endpoint algebra as

\[
\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb C\oplus
M_2(\mathbb C)^4\oplus M_3(\mathbb C)^2.
\]

Then the compiler requirement depends on the requested operation.

For arbitrary within-block conjugations, the exact algebraic gate is

\[
[\mathfrak g_{\rm src},\mathfrak g_{\rm src}]
=
\mathfrak{su}(2)^4\oplus\mathfrak{su}(3)^2.
\]

It is immaterial whether the microscopic generators resemble flux projectors
or gauge quadratures. Only the generated projective action matters.

A return-only compiler may leave the endpoint module during a pulse sequence.
For that regime, generatorwise invariance is not necessary. Let
`G_{\rm reach}` be the reachable subgroup on the full microscopic Hilbert
space and define its endpoint stabilizer

\[
G_P=\{U\in G_{\rm reach}:UPU^\dagger=P\}.
\]

The compiled endpoint group is the restriction of `G_P` to `P\mathcal H`.
The same projective and central tests apply to this restricted group. A
holonomic excursion can therefore be a valid lift even when every
instantaneous generator has nonzero off-diagonal blocks relative to `P`.
For the identity component, the relevant infinitesimal algebra is

\[
\mathfrak g_P
=
\{X\in\mathfrak g_{\rm reach}:[X,P]=0\},
\]

restricted to `P\mathcal H`. This can strictly exceed the Lie algebra
generated by the individually compressed Hamiltonians `PH_jP`: brackets
formed through the excursion space can return as new endpoint actions.
Disconnected stabilizer elements must be audited at group level and cannot
be inferred from `\mathfrak g_P`.

For sector dephasing, projective completeness is insufficient. The central
eigenvalue signatures available from `\mathfrak g_{\rm src}` must separate
all eight sector labels. One separating central direction beyond the existing
two-port ideal algebra is enough in the frozen model.

For arbitrary coherent relative phases, the central trace projection must
have rank eight. The ideal two-port algebra has rank five, so three independent
central covectors are absent.

These are coefficient-level reachability criteria. They do not alone prove
that admissible controls contain signed neighborhoods, that required pulse
times are finite, or that approximations are uniform under faults. If control
amplitudes do contain a neighborhood of zero, the Lie algebra is the correct
local accessibility object. With one-sided or quantized controls, the
reachable semigroup—not merely its formal Lie closure—must be audited.

## Disposition

Replace the generator-by-generator lift programme by a task-relative
constructor morphism:

\[
\text{authorized source controls}
\longrightarrow
\text{compressed endpoint dynamics}
\longrightarrow
\text{requested quotient action}.
\]

For an invariant-path claim, the first exact falsifier is leakage:
a source state `v` in the protected module and an admitted control
`H_j` with

\[
(1-P)H_jv\ne0.
\]

For a return-only claim, transient leakage is not a falsifier. The exact
boundary falsifier is a claimed pulse word `U` with

\[
(1-P)UP\ne0
\]

at its declared completion time.

After the applicable invariance gate, the next falsifier is a nonzero
covector on the requested
target Lie algebra that annihilates every generated bracket. For dephasing it
is instead a pair of sector labels with identical central signatures.

This criterion leaves the microscopic Hamiltonian formula open. It states
exactly what such a formula must prove and permits fewer, differently shaped
source controls than the eight ideal endpoint generators.
