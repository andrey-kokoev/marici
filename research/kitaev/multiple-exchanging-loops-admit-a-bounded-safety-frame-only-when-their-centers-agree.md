# Multiple Exchanging Loops Admit a Bounded Safety Frame Only When Their Centers Agree

An exchanging loop acts on the logarithmic relative coordinate by an affine
reflection

\[
r_i(x)=-x+\ell_i.
\]

Each individual reflection has the unique fixed point

\[
c_i=\frac{\ell_i}{2}.
\]

Thus one odd loop can always be centered algebraically. Compatibility becomes
nontrivial only when more than one exchanging loop is present.

## Two-loop obstruction

For two odd holonomies,

\[
r_j\circ r_i(x)
=x+\ell_j-\ell_i.
\]

Their product is an even translation. The two reflections have a common fixed
point if and only if

\[
\ell_i=\ell_j,
\]

equivalently

\[
c_i=c_j.
\]

If \(\delta=\ell_j-\ell_i\ne0\), iterating the even composite gives

\[
(r_jr_i)^N(x)=x+N\delta,
\]

which escapes linearly. Every individual loop has a fixed frame, but no single
frame is compatible with both.

## Holonomy-group theorem

Let \(H\) be a subgroup of the affine isometry group of the line generated by
loop holonomies

\[
x\longmapsto\varepsilon x+\ell.
\]

The following are equivalent:

1. \(H\) has a bounded orbit.
2. \(H\) has a global fixed point.
3. Every even holonomy has zero translation and all odd holonomies have the
   same center.

Indeed, any nonzero translation has an unbounded orbit. If all translations
vanish, either \(H\) is trivial or it consists of the identity and reflections
about one common point.

This is the complete finite compatibility criterion for a bounded twisted
safety frame.

## Gauge behavior

Changing the affine origin by \(q\) sends

\[
\ell_i\longmapsto\ell_i+2q
\]

for every odd loop. Individual centers shift, but their differences do not:

\[
(c_j+q)-(c_i+q)=c_j-c_i.
\]

Therefore the absolute common center is frame-dependent, while center mismatch
and the induced even translation are invariant.

This also clarifies the safety-margin typing. A numerical origin for \(x\)
requires a fixed local comparison threshold. The invariant obstruction is not
the value of one reflection center but failure of all source loops to share
one.

## Minimal hostile fixture

Take

\[
r_1(x)=-x,
\qquad
r_2(x)=-x+2.
\]

The loops separately fix \(0\) and \(1\), but

\[
r_2r_1(x)=x+2.
\]

After \(N\) repetitions, the coordinate is \(x+2N\). Local fixed frames do
not glue.

## Source-authority boundary

The theorem does not assert that theta/Tate transport has multiple odd loops.
The source graph must provide its actual loop generators and their affine
holonomies. If it has only a tree or one odd generator, this obstruction is
absent; if it has several, their center differences are the first exact audit.

## Falsifiers

- Two odd loop holonomies with distinct centers under a claimed bounded global
  frame.
- A nonzero even translation in the holonomy group.
- Choosing a separate origin for every loop and calling the result global.
- Reporting an individual odd displacement as gauge-invariant.
- Checking each loop separately without checking their composites.
- Inferring physical positivity from existence of an affine fixed point before
  the comparison threshold is typed.
