# Cutoffwise Compatible Actuators Need Not Form a Pro-Constructor

Let (X_{N+1}\to X_N) and (Y_{N+1}\to Y_N) be the source restriction maps,
written (p_N) and (q_N). A completed actuator requires a natural family

\[
B_N:Y_N\to X_N
\]

satisfying

\[
p_NB_{N+1}=B_Nq_N.
\]

Cutoffwise residual cancellation, symmetry, nondisturbance, uniqueness, and a
uniform norm bound do not imply this restriction law.

The smallest hostile uses one-dimensional spaces and identity bonding maps.
Suppose the finite source constraints uniquely select

\[
B_N=(-1)^N.
\]

Then every stage has a unique correction and (\|B_N\|=1), but

\[
B_{N+1}-B_N=\pm2.
\]

No adjacent square commutes and no inverse-limit actuator exists. This is not
inverse-norm escape; it is coherence escape.

For approximate completions, define the naturality defect

\[
\eta_N=\|p_NB_{N+1}-B_Nq_N\|.
\]

Exact pro-construction requires (eta_N=0). A Banach-limit construction needs
an explicitly sufficient Cauchy or summability condition in the transported
source norms. Mere (eta_N\to0) may still be insufficient if accumulated
defects are not controlled.

If finite solution sets are torsors rather than singletons, the compiler must
solve the inverse-limit selection problem inside those torsors. Choosing a
different lift independently at every cutoff is not a constructor.

## Falsifiers

- Unique bounded finite lifts alternate or drift under restriction.
- Adjacent naturality defects do not vanish.
- Approximate defects vanish but accumulate without a Cauchy estimate.
- Coherence is achieved only by cutoff-dependent unauthorized shears.
- Scalar outputs converge while typed actuator graphs do not.
