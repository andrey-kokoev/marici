# One noncentral spectral tag can split repeated simple control blocks

## Question

What additional Hamiltonian is sufficient to break a diagonal correlation across \(k\) identical simple Lie blocks?

Let \(\mathfrak g\) be a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra. Assume the available controls already generate the diagonal subalgebra

\[
\Delta\mathfrak g
=
\{(X,\ldots,X):X\in\mathfrak g\}
\subseteq
\mathfrak g^{\oplus k}.
\]

Add one noncentral weighted control

\[
H_\lambda
=
(\lambda_1H,\ldots,\lambda_kH),
\qquad
0\ne H\in\mathfrak g.
\]

## Claim boundary

Bracketing \(H_\lambda\) with diagonal controls gives

\[
[(K,\ldots,K),H_\lambda]
=
(\lambda_1[K,H],\ldots,\lambda_k[K,H]).
\]

Because \(\mathfrak g\) is simple, the ideal generated by nonzero \(H\) is all of \(\mathfrak g\). Therefore the Lie closure contains

\[
(\lambda_1X,\ldots,\lambda_kX)
\]

for every \(X\in\mathfrak g\).

Since \(\mathfrak g=[\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g]\), repeated brackets generate

\[
(\lambda_1^mX,\ldots,\lambda_k^mX)
\]

for every positive integer \(m\) and every \(X\in\mathfrak g\). Together with the diagonal \(m=0\) directions, the available block-coefficient vectors contain

\[
(1,\ldots,1),\quad
(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_k),\quad\ldots,\quad
(\lambda_1^{k-1},\ldots,\lambda_k^{k-1}).
\]

If the \(\lambda_i\) are pairwise distinct, the Vandermonde matrix is invertible. Linear combinations isolate every block coefficient vector \(e_i\). Hence the generated Lie algebra is

\[
\mathfrak g^{\oplus k}.
\]

If the weights take only \(r\) distinct values, blocks with equal weights remain locked and the generated algebra contains only \(r\) independently controlled copies of \(\mathfrak g\).

A central weighted control

\[
(\lambda_1I,\ldots,\lambda_kI)
\]

cannot perform this repair: it commutes with the diagonal algebra, so no coefficient powers or block-selective noncentral directions are generated.

For \(D(S_3)\), this gives a sufficient repair signature:

- one noncentral source control with four distinct weights across the four \(\mathfrak{su}_2\) blocks;
- one with two distinct weights across the two \(\mathfrak{su}_3\) blocks.

One physical Hamiltonian may satisfy both conditions if its projections have the required aligned nonzero directions and distinct weights. This theorem is sufficient, not necessary; more general nonaligned block projections may decouple blocks by other mechanisms.

## Disposition

The missing block selector is not a central label. It is a noncentral spectral tag whose adjoint orbit and unequal block weights enter the Lie closure.

The first falsifier is one of:

1. \(H=0\) in one repeated-block family;
2. \(\mathfrak g\) is not simple or \(H\) generates a proper ideal;
3. equal weights are claimed to separate their blocks;
4. a central weighted control is substituted for a noncentral one;
5. Vandermonde inversion is used when weights are not source-fixed scalars;
6. the sufficient aligned-control theorem is reported as necessary.

For actual \(D(S_3)\) ports, the next test is to extract their block projection weights and adjoint-orbit directions. Distinct central characters alone do not pass; the asymmetry must occur in a switchable noncentral Hamiltonian.
