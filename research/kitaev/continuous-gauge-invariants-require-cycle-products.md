# Continuous Gauge Invariants Require Cycle Products

Let (G=(V,E)) carry complex Hermitian edge coordinates (z_e), with the
channel-frame torus acting by

\[
z_{uv}\longmapsto g_u z_{uv}\overline{g_v}.
\]

There are two different finite readout problems.

On a fixed stratum where a chosen spanning forest is nonzero, edge magnitudes
and the normalized holonomies of its fundamental cycles reconstruct the
coupling packet up to channel gauge. This reconstruction is rational: it uses
division by tree-edge magnitudes.

On the full affine closure, division is not continuous at support collapse.
The polynomial invariant algebra is instead generated by

\[
|z_e|^2
\]

and the unnormalized products around directed simple cycles,

\[
W_C=\prod_{e\in C}z_e^{\epsilon_e}.
\]

To see this, a gauge-invariant monomial must have equal incoming and outgoing
phase exponent at every vertex. Its exponent data are therefore an integral
circulation. Every finite integral circulation decomposes into directed
cycles; two-edge backtracks give the magnitude generators. Hence every
invariant monomial is a product of edge magnitudes and cycle monomials.

Because the acting torus is compact, these invariant polynomials separate its
orbits. They provide a continuous, support-aware quotient. The target is not
a fixed torus: cycle products automatically vanish when their support cycle
breaks.

## Why a fundamental-cycle list is insufficient on the closure

Take vertices (1,2,3,4), the nominal tree

\[
T=\{12,23,34\},
\]

and chords (13,14). Its fundamental products are

\[
W_{123}=z_{12}z_{23}\overline{z_{13}},
\qquad
W_{1234}=z_{12}z_{23}z_{34}\overline{z_{14}}.
\]

If (z_{12}=0), both vanish. But the surviving support still contains the
cycle (1\to3\to4\to1), whose invariant

\[
W_{134}=z_{13}z_{34}\overline{z_{14}}
\]

can be nonzero. Recovering it from the nominal fundamental products would
require division by the vanished tree coordinate.

An exact positive Hermitian pair is obtained with diagonal entries (3),

\[
z_{12}=z_{24}=0,
\quad
z_{23}=z_{34}=z_{14}=1/2,
\quad
z_{13}=\pm i/2.
\]

The two matrices are complex conjugates. They have identical edge
magnitudes, identical characteristic polynomials, and both nominal
fundamental products equal to zero. Yet

\[
W_{134}=\pm i/8.
\]

Thus a generic cycle basis is not a completion-stable observation list.

## Minimality must be typed

There are now three notions of minimal interface:

- on one fixed nonzero support stratum, \(\beta_1\) normalized phases;
- for polynomial generation on a declared finite graph, edge magnitudes and
  directed simple-cycle products;
- for a particular authorized constructor family, only those invariant
  combinations actually consumed downstream.

The second list can be redundant on the generic stratum. Its redundancy is
the price of avoiding singular division while support changes. Optimization
must therefore specify whether it minimizes generic coordinates, invariant
algebra generators, or operative downstream observations.

Carrier geometry. The choice of (U(1)), a real (C_2) locus, or another

## Falsifiers

- Using one nominal fundamental-cycle list after one of its tree edges
  vanishes.
- Dividing by a coupling without a uniform nonvanishing certificate.
- Calling generic-stratum coordinate minimality a generating theorem for the
  affine invariant algebra.
- Retaining every cycle product without checking downstream authority.
- Inferring a theta cycle from the abstract invariant-ring theorem.
