# Continuous bridge control is enough for a Weyl bus but not for its conditional use

Owner: `marici.Kitaev`

## Bounded question

Does an internal electric fusion qutrit require a primitive cyclic-shift gate,
or does the previously isolated off-block bridge already suffice to compile a
Weyl bus?

## Verdict

Under a signed continuous Hamiltonian-control model, one nonzero off-block
bridge is enough. It enlarges the pinned electric control Lie algebra to
`u(3)`, and full `U(3)` reachability then includes an exact clock and shift.

Under a discrete gate model, the same conclusion does not follow. A single
fixed bridge unitary may generate only a proper or nondense subgroup. In that
model a directly derived cyclic-incidence gate, or a separate universality
proof for the discrete gate set, is still required.

In neither model does local qutrit controllability supply the two conditional
system-bus interactions used by the catalytic commutator. The remaining hard
constructor is therefore incidence between the bus actuator and the two
external predicates, not the abstract qutrit Weyl algebra itself.

## Continuous control theorem

Let the electric fusion qutrit split as

\[
\mathcal H_B=\mathbb C|u\rangle\oplus W.
\]

The pinned continuously pulsed braid Hamiltonians generate

\[
\mathfrak g_0=\mathfrak u(1)_u\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)_W.
\]

Admit one Hermitian bridge `H` satisfying

\[
(I-P_u)HP_u\ne0.
\]

The established single-bridge lemma gives

\[
\operatorname{Lie}_{\mathbb R}(\mathfrak g_0\cup\{iH\})
=\mathfrak u(3).
\]

Assume each admitted Hamiltonian has independently variable signed,
piecewise-constant amplitude. The reachable identity component is then the
connected Lie group with this Lie algebra, namely `U(3)`. Consequently one may
choose an orthonormal basis and reach

\[
Z=\operatorname{diag}(1,\omega,\omega^2)
\]

and

\[
X|j\rangle=|j+1\bmod3\rangle.
\]

These obey

\[
ZX=\omega XZ
\]

up to the harmless convention that exchanges `omega` with its inverse.

Thus a primitive cyclic-incidence operation is a high-value direct target,
but it is not an additional algebraic necessity once continuous signed bridge
control has been physically established.

## Why the discrete case is different

Suppose only the six braid unitaries and one fixed bridge unitary `G` are
available. The associative algebra generated by their matrices may be all of
`M_3` while the generated unitary group is finite, nondense, or dense without
an exact finite word for the chosen Weyl generators.

Therefore none of the following implies the next:

```text
nonzero off-block matrix element
associative span M3
dense discrete subgroup of U(3)
exact finite synthesis of X and Z
fault-tolerant synthesis with bounded cost
```

The continuous theorem crosses the middle gaps only because arbitrary signed
pulse times are part of its control authority.

## The true remaining incidence

Let `P` be the anyonic charge predicate and `Q` the classical or fusion flag
predicate. The catalytic phase compiler requires

\[
U_P=(I-P)\otimes I+P\otimes Z
\]

and

\[
V_Q=(I-Q)\otimes I+Q\otimes X.
\]

Full local control on the bus gives `X` and `Z`, but it does not create either
tensor-product interaction. Independent access to `P` and `Z` supplies only
separable words. Those words cannot entangle an initially unentangled system
and bus, whereas `U_P` generally can. The same applies to `Q` and `X`.

The minimal physical question has therefore moved:

> Which localized source interaction produces a nonzero conditional
> Hamiltonian `P` tensor `h_Z`, and which produces `Q` tensor `h_X`, with the
> two interactions addressing the same bus and preserving its return path?

If arbitrary bus conjugation is available, the exact choice of nontrivial bus
Hamiltonian in each coupling is flexible. Conjugation can change its bus
basis. What cannot be synthesized from bus-only controls is the external
predicate incidence itself.

## A stronger reduction under conditional controllability

Suppose an admitted interaction has the form

\[
P\otimes H_0
\]

and bus-only controls generate `u(3)`. Commuting and conjugating by bus controls
produces conditional Hamiltonians in the Lie ideal generated by `H_0` under
`u(3)`. If the traceless part of `H_0` is nonzero, simplicity of `su(3)` makes
that ideal all of `su(3)`. Hence one non-scalar conditional bus Hamiltonian is
enough to obtain every determinant-one bus operation conditioned on `P`.

The same statement holds separately for `Q`. Central conditional phases may
require an additional traceful interaction, but the qutrit clock and shift can
be chosen with determinant one after multiplication by global phases.

Therefore the two marginal couplings need not be primitive controlled `Z` and
controlled `X`. It suffices to derive:

1. full local continuous qutrit control;
2. one non-scalar conditional bus Hamiltonian for `P`;
3. one non-scalar conditional bus Hamiltonian for `Q`.

This is a materially smaller source compiler target.

## Bus return and reference stability

After compiling the conditional Weyl operations, their group commutator acts
as the desired joint phase on the external predicates and as the identity on
the bus. Because the equality is an operator identity, return holds for mixed
bus states and for buses entangled with an untouched reference.

Compilation error changes this conclusion. If the implemented conditional
operations are approximate, the residual bus channel can retain predicate
information. Fault containment must therefore bound both the external phase
error and the failure of the bus-return channel to equal the identity.

## Exact control-model ledger

| Authority | Weyl bus conclusion |
|---|---|
| Braid words only | Impossible because `P_u` is conserved |
| Braid Hamiltonians with signed times | Still reducible |
| Signed braid Hamiltonians plus one off-block bridge | Full local `U(3)` control; exact Weyl pair reachable |
| Braid gates plus one fixed bridge gate | Undecided without discrete group analysis |
| Local Weyl pair only | No conditional predicate coupling |
| Local control plus one non-scalar `P`-conditional interaction | Arbitrary special-unitary control conditioned on `P` |
| Separate conditional control for `P` and `Q` on one bus | Catalytic joint phase reachable, subject to fault and locality audit |

## Highest-information next source test

The best next test is no longer to search specifically for a microscopic
three-cycle shift matrix. Search for any continuously tunable localized
Hermitian interaction crossing `P_u`. If found, test whether the microscopic
source also supplies one non-scalar bus coupling conditioned on each external
predicate.

This sequence separates three obstructions cleanly:

1. internal bus controllability;
2. system-to-bus incidence;
3. coherent bus return under physical errors.

## Falsifiers

- The bridge Lie closure remains a proper subalgebra of `u(3)` despite a
  nonzero off-block bridge and the stated signed-control assumptions.
- `U(3)` is not reachable from a driftless signed control system whose dynamic
  Lie algebra is `u(3)`.
- A separable word of system-only and bus-only gates implements an entangling
  controlled bus unitary.
- A non-scalar conditional bus Hamiltonian fails to generate conditional
  `su(3)` when arbitrary local `su(3)` conjugations and signed pulses are
  admitted.
- The source already supplies both predicate-conditioned interactions on the
  same bus with a complete fault contract.

## Claim boundary

This packet is a control-theoretic reduction. It assumes signed continuous
Hamiltonian access and does not derive that access from the lattice. It does
not claim an exact finite pulse word, a robust synthesis algorithm, or a
fault-tolerant implementation.

Its exact contribution is to relocate the bottleneck. Once one physical
off-block Hamiltonian is continuously controllable, the qutrit Weyl pair is a
compilation consequence. The irreducible missing source data are then two
predicate-conditioned incidences on the same bus.

No build, checker, or Git operation was used.
