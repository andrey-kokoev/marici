# Exact return control does not commute with reachable closure

Owner: `marici.Kitaev`

## Question

May a control compiler replace the executable reachable group by its topological
closure before taking the endpoint stabilizer and restriction?

## Claim boundary

No. Let `G_exec` be the group generated by finite authorized control words
on the microscopic Hilbert space, and let `P` be the endpoint projector.
Define

\[
S_{\rm exec}
=
G_{\rm exec}\cap\operatorname{Stab}(P)
\]

and

\[
S_{\rm cl}
=
\overline{G_{\rm exec}}\cap\operatorname{Stab}(P).
\]

Then

\[
\overline{S_{\rm exec}}\subseteq S_{\rm cl},
\]

but equality need not hold. Closure can create new exact-looking return
operations from limits of words that never return exactly at any finite
stage. The endpoint restriction must therefore be typed as either exact,
approximate, or robust; these are different constructor claims.

A minimal central-phase witness is the irrational flow

\[
U(t)=\operatorname{diag}(e^{it},e^{i\alpha t}),
\qquad
\alpha\notin\mathbb Q.
\]

Finite execution supplies only the one-parameter subgroup
`G_exec={U(t)}`, while its closure is the full two-torus of diagonal phases.
For `\alpha=\sqrt2`, the target

\[
\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)
\]

lies in the closure but is not executable: its first entry forces
`t=2\pi n`, while its second would require
`n\sqrt2=m+1/2`, which is impossible for integers `m,n`.

Thus a closed-group central rank of two can coexist with only one executable
continuous parameter. The closure has not found another control knob; it has
forgotten finite-word attainability.

For the D(S3) endpoint programme, the exact compiled action is the restriction
of `S_exec` to the endpoint module. Approximate compilation may use the
closure, but it must additionally report a word-length or time-to-accuracy
law. Robust compilation requires a uniform law under the admitted control
faults. Neither follows from Lie rank or density.

The stabilizer Lie algebra

\[
\{X\in\mathfrak g:[X,P]=0\}
\]

must also name which group supplies `\mathfrak g`. The Lie algebra of an
immersed executable subgroup can be strictly smaller than the Lie algebra of
its closure.

## Disposition

Introduce three typed endpoint images:

1. exact finite-word image;
2. closure image with an approximation modulus;
3. robust image with a fault-uniform approximation modulus.

Reject any physical-control claim that reports only the closure image while
calling its elements executable. The first machine witness is a target
endpoint unitary, a proof that it lies in the closed image, and an arithmetic
or algebraic obstruction showing that no finite authorized word reaches it.

For the existing D(S3) two-port result, projective Lie completeness remains an
ideal continuous-control theorem. Its physical lift must still state whether
pulse amplitudes and durations generate the exact subgroup, only a dense
subgroup, or a robustly approximating family.
