# Strong Real-Structure Transport Does Not Control Moving Tail Frames

## The completion escape

Let (H=\ell^2(\mathbb N)) with standard conjugation (J). For (N\ge2), let
(J_N) agree with (J) on every coordinate except the (N)-th, where

\[
(J_Nx)_N=-\overline{x_N}.
\]

Every (J_N) is an antiunitary involution. For each fixed (x\in\ell^2),

\[
\|(J_N-J)x\|=2|x_N|\longrightarrow0.
\]

Thus (J_N\to J) strongly. Nevertheless

\[
\|J_N-J\|=2
\]

for every (N), witnessed by the moving unit vector (e_N).

If identity transport is tested from (J) to (J_N), every fixed
finite-support packet eventually has zero intertwiner residual. The normalized
tail state (e_N) always has residual norm two. This is the real-frame version
of finite coherence with completion escape.

## Fixed-real-subspace gap

Let (P=(I+J)/2) and (P_N=(I+J_N)/2), regarded as real-linear orthogonal
projections onto the fixed real forms. On the (N)-th complex coordinate,
(P) selects the real axis while (P_N) selects the imaginary axis. Hence

\[
\|P_N-P\|=1
\]

for every (N), although (P_N\to P) strongly.

The distinguished unit (e_1) is fixed by every (J_N), and any observation
using only the first (k) coordinates is exactly blind once (N>k). Vacuum
transport and all fixed finite observations therefore coexist with maximal
drift of an unobserved real direction.

## Exact completion gate

Pointwise transport on a dense union is insufficient for any theorem
quantified over all normalized cutoff states. One needs at least one of:

1. uniform intertwiner control
   \[
   \|D_NJ_{+,N}-J_{-,N}D_N\|\to0;
   \]
2. gap convergence of the transported fixed real subspaces;
3. a source topology in which every moving residual witness has norm tending
   to zero;
4. a uniformly observable family whose rows detect every drifting frame
   direction.

These are typed alternatives, not interchangeable phrasings. Operator-norm
control implies uniform statewise residual control. A weaker source topology
may legitimately quotient the tail witness, but then it must also prove that
all admitted constructors and currents descend through that quotient.

## Relation to pro-Gram completion

The hostile does not prove that the theta/Tate completion fails. It supplies
the exact falsifier Grothendieck's finite-stage argument must exclude:

\[
\forall x\text{ fixed},\quad \mathcal R_Nx\to0,
\qquad
\exists x_N,\ \|x_N\|=1,
\quad \|\mathcal R_Nx_N\|=2.
\]

If the pro-Gram norm makes (e_N) negligible, the residual may be harmless;
if a seam, primitive, or square-current row still detects (e_N), then the
moving frame is operative and strong convergence is inadequate. This is the
same tail-plus-seam observability split in real-structure form.

## Falsifiers

- Intertwining is checked only on each fixed finite-support vector.
- Vacuum transport is used to infer convergence of the full real form.
- Strong convergence of conjugations is reported as gap convergence.
- A moving normalized state retains nonzero residual.
- The analytic norm kills the witness while an admitted discrete or seam port
  still detects it.
- A quotient removes the witness without proving constructor descent.

## Claim boundary

This is an exact Hilbert-space hostile theorem. It does not determine the
theta/Tate source topology, prove that its tail witnesses survive, construct
the conjugations, or prove RH.
