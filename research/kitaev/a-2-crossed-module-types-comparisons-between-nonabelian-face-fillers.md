# A 2-crossed module types comparisons between nonabelian face fillers

Owner: marici.Kitaev

## Question

What is the next constructor rung when crossed-module face fillers themselves
have task-visible comparisons, and strict Peiffer interchange is no longer
allowed to erase those comparisons?

## Claim boundary

The natural algebraic object is a 2-crossed module of groups

\[
L
\xrightarrow{\partial_2}
H
\xrightarrow{\partial_1}
G,
\qquad
\partial_1\partial_2(l)=e_G.
\]

The group \(G\) acts on \(H\) and \(L\), the two boundary maps are
\(G\)-equivariant, and there is a \(G\)-equivariant Peiffer lifting

\[
\{-,-\}:H\times H\to L.
\]

This packet uses the convention recorded by Arvasi and Ulualan in their
definition equivalent to Conduché's. Their 2006 paper also states the
equivalence with simplicial groups whose Moore complex has length two and gives
the associated homotopy groups. The primary source is:

<https://tcms.org.ge/Journals/JHRS/volumes/2006/n1a1/v1n1a1hl.pdf>

### What the new group \(L\) does

In an ordinary crossed module, the Peiffer law forces mixed 1--2 transport to
agree strictly.

In a 2-crossed module, the Peiffer lifting supplies an \(L\)-valued witness for
that comparison. Its boundary is the Peiffer commutator in \(H\).

With the source convention, the first mandatory law is

\[
\partial_2\{h,h'\}
=
(\partial_1h\triangleright h')\,
h\,
h'^{-1}\,
h^{-1}.
\]

Thus an apparent failure of \(H\to G\) to be a crossed module is not discarded.
It is lifted to an explicitly typed \(L\)-constructor.

The second mandatory law is

\[
\{\partial_2l,\partial_2l'\}
=
[l',l].
\]

Hence comparisons whose \(H\)-boundaries already come from \(L\) recover the
commutator structure inside \(L\).

The remaining published axioms enforce:

- multiplicativity of the lifting in each \(H\) argument;
- compatibility when one argument lies in \(\operatorname{im}\partial_2\);
- \(G\)-equivariance.

These laws generate an induced \(H\)-action on \(L\) for which

\[
L\xrightarrow{\partial_2}H
\]

becomes a crossed module.

Because the multiplicativity formulas are convention-sensitive in the
placement of actions and inverses, a future checker must copy one complete
published convention verbatim. Mixing formulas from left-action and
right-action conventions is a first-invalid-constructor defect.

### The rung hierarchy

The three coefficient levels have distinct roles:

- \(G\): ordered edge transport;
- \(H\): face fillers comparing edge paths;
- \(L\): volume-level comparisons between face-filler composites.

The Peiffer lifting is not a decorative bilinear form. It is the constructor
that lifts a failure of strict interchange into the next coefficient level.

This is exactly the case that justified going higher: two lower coherencers
have the same \(G\)-boundary, while their difference remains visible in \(H\)
and requires an \(L\)-cell.

### Homotopy sectors

For the 2-crossed module

\[
L\to H\to G,
\]

the associated connected homotopy \(3\)-type has

\[
\pi_1
\simeq
G/\operatorname{im}\partial_1,
\]

\[
\pi_2
\simeq
\ker\partial_1/\operatorname{im}\partial_2,
\]

and

\[
\pi_3
\simeq
\ker\partial_2.
\]

These have direct constructor meanings.

- \(\pi_1\) is residual edge transport not filled by faces.
- \(\pi_2\) is face-filler ambiguity not repaired by volume constructors.
- \(\pi_3\) is volume-comparison ambiguity with no lower boundary.

The sequence therefore internalizes the programme's recurring pattern:

\[
\text{obstruction}
\longrightarrow
\text{filler torsor}
\longrightarrow
\text{higher automorphism}.
\]

### Strict-collapse fixture

Take a genuine crossed module

\[
H\xrightarrow{\partial_1}G
\]

and set

\[
L=1
\]

with trivial Peiffer lifting.

This is admissible only when the Peiffer commutator in \(H\) is already trivial
under the crossed-module law. Then

\[
\pi_3=1.
\]

The new rung contains no independent state. This fixture proves that adding
the symbol \(L\) does not force a higher logical sector.

### Pure higher-sector fixture

Let \(A\) be an abelian group and take

\[
A\to1\to1
\]

with trivial boundary maps, actions, and lifting.

Then

\[
\pi_1=1,
\qquad
\pi_2=1,
\qquad
\pi_3=A.
\]

All edge and face data are trivial, while a pure volume-level logical sector
survives.

This is the smallest exact witness that lower closure can coexist with
nontrivial next-rung state.

For

\[
A=C_2,
\]

the model carries one higher logical bit with no edge or face syndrome.

### Peiffer-generated fixture

Start with a pre-crossed module

\[
H\xrightarrow{\partial_1}G
\]

that satisfies equivariance but not the strict Peiffer identity.

Let \(L\) be generated by symbols

\[
\{h,h'\}
\]

whose boundaries are the corresponding Peiffer commutators, modulo the
published multiplicativity, boundary, and equivariance relations.

Then \(L\) records exactly the missing interchange constructors needed to
promote the pre-crossed module to a 2-crossed module.

This is the explanatory fixture: the next rung is generated by a named failure
of the previous rung rather than appended freely.

### Four-simplex boundary

The crossed-module tetrahedral law compares composites of face fillers. In the
2-crossed-module rung, distinct tetrahedral comparison routes can differ by an
\(L\)-valued 3-transport.

A \(4\)-simplex supplies the first closed boundary on which those
volume-comparison cells must compose coherently. The resulting identity is the
next geometric audit.

This packet does not freeze an explicit ordered \(4\)-simplex word. Such a word
depends on:

- simplex orientation;
- base vertex;
- whiskering convention;
- left versus right actions;
- ordering of tetrahedral cells;
- the chosen published 2-crossed-module convention.

Inventing that word from the desired cancellation would be a typing defect.
The next finite packet must derive it from a simplicial group or a fully frozen
higher-holonomy convention.

### Rotation test

Cellular duality can again exchange geometric dimensions. It cannot eliminate:

- three coefficient groups \(L,H,G\);
- two boundary maps;
- the Peiffer lifting;
- induced \(H\)-action on \(L\);
- ordered mixed-composition laws.

Therefore the 2-crossed-module coefficient lens survives Carrier rotation as a
genuine model of a homotopy \(3\)-type.

The pure fixture \(A\to1\to1\) is especially decisive: no lower-dimensional
incidence syndrome can reconstruct the nontrivial \(\pi_3=A\) without adding
the \(L\)-port.

### Relation to the recursive 2+1 architecture

The coefficient complex

\[
L\to H\to G
\]

is a nonabelian counterpart of the recursive bridge hierarchy.

- a \(G\)-mismatch asks for an \(H\)-bridge;
- two \(H\)-bridges ask for an \(L\)-comparison;
- two \(L\)-comparisons differ by \(\ker\partial_2\).

Unlike a generic stack of comparators, the Peiffer lifting specifies why the
next bridge exists: it lifts the mixed-composition defect of the preceding
rung.

### Source-authority boundary

Nothing in the present \(D(S_3)\) packets proves that its physical compiler
uses a particular 2-crossed module.

The Hopf associator, braiding, and pair-channel data may already satisfy strict
coherence, in which case \(L=1\) is sufficient. Alternatively, two
source-derived implementations may differ by a task-visible 3-transport,
requiring nontrivial \(L\).

The compiler must derive:

- \(G,H,L\);
- both boundary maps;
- all actions;
- the Peiffer lifting;
- its physical or algebraic implementation;
- the \(4\)-simplex coherence word.

The higher algebra is a requirement language, not authority to invent those
constructors.

### Hostile fixtures

The first-failed law must be reported under these cases:

1. \(\partial_1\partial_2(l)\ne e_G\);
2. a boundary map is not \(G\)-equivariant;
3. the Peiffer lifting is not \(G\)-equivariant;
4. the boundary of \(\{h,h'\}\) is not the frozen Peiffer commutator;
5. \(\{\partial_2l,\partial_2l'\}\ne[l',l]\);
6. multiplicativity fails in either \(H\) argument;
7. an \(L\)-cell is inferred from scalar cancellation;
8. a nontrivial \(\pi_3\) is contracted without an authorized higher cell;
9. a \(4\)-simplex word is assembled using inconsistent action conventions;
10. finite algebraic 3-type coherence is presented as physical execution or
    uniform completion.

## Disposition

The programme has now gone one genuine rung above crossed modules.

The bounded compiler packet is:

- finite groups \(L,H,G\);
- complex law \(\partial_1\partial_2=1\);
- frozen \(G\)-actions;
- Peiffer lifting;
- the complete five published axiom families in one convention;
- \(\pi_1,\pi_2,\pi_3\);
- strict-collapse and pure-\(\pi_3\) fixtures;
- source authority;
- unresolved ordered \(4\)-simplex law;
- quantitative and physical status.

The decisive result is conditional but sharp:

- if every task-visible Peiffer defect is uniquely and coherently lifted, the
  new rung self-closes;
- if \(\ker\partial_2\ne1\), a genuine volume-comparison logical sector
  survives;
- if no source-derived lifting exists, the proposed higher compiler fails
  before the \(4\)-simplex calculation.

The next move should derive the ordered \(4\)-simplex audit from one frozen
finite 2-crossed module, rather than move immediately to another abstract rung.
