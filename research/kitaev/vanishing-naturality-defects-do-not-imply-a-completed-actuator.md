# Vanishing Naturality Defects Do Not Imply a Completed Actuator

After transporting adjacent cutoff actuators into one Banach chart, write

\[
\eta_N=\|B_{N+1}-B_N\|.
\]

The condition (\eta_N\to0) says only that adjacent restriction squares
asymptotically commute. It does not imply that (B_N) converges, even when the
actuators are uniformly bounded.

An exact rational hostile concatenates triangular waves. In block (m), the
values rise

\[
0,\frac1m,\frac2m,\ldots,1
\]

and then fall back to zero in steps of (1/m). As (m\to\infty), every
adjacent defect tends to zero and all actuator norms are at most one. Yet block
endpoints give a subsequence converging to zero and block peaks give a
subsequence identically one. No completed actuator exists.

A sufficient norm-completion certificate is summable total variation:

\[
\sum_N\eta_N<\infty.
\]

Indeed,

\[
\|B_M-B_N\|\le\sum_{k=N}^{M-1}\eta_k,
\]

so (B_N) is Cauchy. Summability is sufficient, not asserted necessary; a
source-specific compactness plus uniqueness theorem could replace it.

In a genuine inverse system, defects must be computed after the declared
bonding maps and in transported source norms. Scalar-output convergence cannot
substitute for graph convergence of the typed actuator.

## Falsifiers

- Adjacent defects tend to zero but total tail variation does not vanish.
- Bounded actuator families have two distinct subsequential limits.
- Convergence holds only after scalar compression.
- A compactness subsequence is reported as a canonical full limit without
  uniqueness.
- Defects are measured in cutoff-dependent nonuniform charts.
