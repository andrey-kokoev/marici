# A frame syndrome records either invariant error or a selected gauge sheet

Owner: `marici.Kitaev`

## Bounded question

When does repeated environmental recording of an `S3` frame syndrome create
an objective record of the relative error, and when does it instead decohere
and select part of the relational frame itself?

The distinction is determined by the pointer coordinate. Recording the exact
group difference preserves coherence inside each invariant syndrome sector.
Recording the cheaper component difference leaks the global reflection sheet
whenever the relative rotation is nonzero.

## Frozen coordinates

For two frames, write

\[
g=(e,k),
\qquad
h=(f,l),
\]

with (e,f\in\mathbb Z_2) and (k,l\in\mathbb Z_3). Their exact relative
coordinate is

\[
d=g^{-1}h=(\delta,c),
\]

where

\[
\delta=e+f,
\qquad
c=(-1)^e(l-k).
\]

Define the raw component difference

\[
u=l-k.
\]

Then

\[
u=(-1)^e c.
\]

The exact coordinate (d) is invariant under common left gauge action. The raw
coordinate (u) changes sign under a common reflection.

## States in one invariant syndrome sector

Fix (d=(\delta,c)). Every state supported in that sector has the form

\[
|\Psi_d\rangle
=
\sum_{e,k}\alpha_{e,k}
|e,k;\ e+\delta,\ k+(-1)^e c\rangle.
\]

The amplitudes retain the global frame coordinate. In particular, coherence
between (e=0) and (e=1) is coherence between the two global reflection sheets
while the relative displacement remains fixed.

Write

\[
|\Psi_d\rangle=|\psi_0\rangle+|\psi_1\rangle,
\]

where (|\psi_e\rangle) collects the terms with first-frame parity (e).

## Invariant syndrome dilation

An exact pointer dilation acts as

\[
V_{\mathrm{inv}}|g,h\rangle|0\rangle
=
|g,h\rangle|g^{-1}h\rangle.
\]

On the fixed sector,

\[
V_{\mathrm{inv}}|\Psi_d\rangle|0\rangle
=
|\Psi_d\rangle|d\rangle.
\]

The pointer contains the relative error and no global-frame coordinate. The
system remains unentangled with the pointer inside the fixed sector. Hence all
coherence within (\Pi_d) survives.

For a superposition of different (d), the pointer distinguishes the sectors
and the unobserved record dephases their cross terms. The recording instrument
selects the commutative algebra generated by the invariant projectors
(\{\Pi_d\}). It does not select a basis within each sector.

## Raw component dilation

The cheaper component extractor acts as

\[
V_{\mathrm{raw}}|e,k;f,l\rangle|0\rangle
=
|e,k;f,l\rangle|\delta,l-k\rangle.
\]

On the same fixed invariant sector,

\[
V_{\mathrm{raw}}|\Psi_d\rangle|0\rangle
=
|\psi_0\rangle|\delta,c\rangle
+
|\psi_1\rangle|\delta,-c\rangle.
\]

When (c\neq0), the two pointer states are orthogonal. Tracing out one ideal
pointer removes the cross terms

\[
|\psi_0\rangle\langle\psi_1|
,
|\psi_1\rangle\langle\psi_0|.
\]

Thus a single perfect raw-syndrome record completely decoheres the global
reflection-sheet coherence within that fixed physical error sector.

When (c=0), both branches write the same pointer value. No reflection-sheet
information leaks. The raw extractor is harmless on the identity and pure
reflection relative sectors, but not on the four sectors with a nonzero
rotation coordinate.

## Imperfect pointer and redundancy rate

Let one environment fragment receive normalized pointer states

\[
|\eta_c\rangle,
\qquad
|\eta_{-c}\rangle,
\]

with overlap

\[
\gamma=\langle\eta_{-c}|\eta_c\rangle.
\]

After (n) conditionally independent fragments record the same raw label, the
sheet cross term is multiplied by

\[
\gamma^n.
\]

Hence the surviving coherence magnitude obeys

\[
\|\rho_{01}^{(n)}\|
=
|\gamma|^n\|\rho_{01}^{(0)}\|.
\]

For (0<|\gamma|<1), achieving residual coherence at most (\varepsilon) needs

\[
n
\geq
\frac{\log\varepsilon}{\log|\gamma|}.
\]

For equal prior sheet weights, the optimal discrimination error available
from the (n) fragments is

\[
p_{\mathrm{err}}^{(n)}
=
\frac12
\left(
1-\sqrt{1-|\gamma|^{2n}}
\right).
\]

The same overlap therefore controls both decoherence of the protected frame
and increasing accessibility of the copied sheet distinction.

## Redundancy creates a classical record algebra

Once the pointer states are distinguishable, their labels lie in a commuting
algebra and can be fanned out to many consumers. Redundancy makes the selected
distinction public; it does not make the rest of the frame state copyable.

For the invariant extractor, the copied algebra consists of functions of
(d). For the raw extractor, the copied algebra contains the signed component
(u). Within a known nonzero (c) sector, the pair ((c,u)) determines the global
reflection parity:

\[
(-1)^e=uc^{-1}.
\]

Thus combining the intended error label with the raw component record turns
the environment into a reference for the previously coherent (C_2) sheet.
It does not record the full global (S_3) orientation: the common (\mathbb Z_3)
rotation coordinate (k) remains hidden in this interaction.

## Invariant versus covariant recording Hamiltonians

A collision-model interaction for the exact record may be written
schematically as

\[
H_{\mathrm{inv}}
=
\sum_d\Pi_d\otimes h_d.
\]

Every (\Pi_d) commutes with the common left gauge action. The pointer may be
gauge inert while the joint interaction remains symmetric.

For raw component projectors (Q_{\delta,u}), a common reflection exchanges

\[
Q_{\delta,u}
\longleftrightarrow
Q_{\delta,-u}.
\]

A raw interaction

\[
H_{\mathrm{raw}}
=
\sum_{\delta,u}Q_{\delta,u}\otimes h_{\delta,u}
\]

has only two symmetry-compatible options.

1. Identify (h_{\delta,u}=h_{\delta,-u}). The apparatus retains only the
   unsigned coarse record and cannot perform exact signed correction.
2. Let the pointer transform by (u\leftrightarrow-u). The joint interaction is
   covariant, but the pointer is now a transported frame reference rather than
   an invariant classical label.

If the pointer labels are held fixed while the system alone transforms, the
signed raw interaction explicitly breaks the common reflection symmetry.

## Objective record or symmetry selection

The finite criterion is therefore:

- an invariant pointer creates an objective record of a relational error;
- a covariant signed pointer creates a relational record jointly with an
  external reference frame;
- a fixed signed pointer dynamically selects a gauge sheet;
- an unsigned pointer preserves symmetry but loses exact correction data.

“Objective” here means redundantly broadcastable and available to independent
future consumers within the declared pointer calibration. Stability, spatial
independence, and thermodynamic amplification remain physical obligations.

The clean invariant branch is not free. In the frozen qubit--qutrit resource
theory, computing (c=(-1)^e u) requires controlled inversion. The raw branch
is component-Clifford but leaks the sheet. The finite apparatus therefore
faces a real resource tradeoff rather than a choice of notation:

- pay the nonstabilizer resource to record only the invariant error;
- consume or classicalize the frame through raw measurement;
- retain a covariant pointer as an additional reference system;
- or accept an unsigned record that detects but does not exactly correct.

## Future-carrier consequence

Recording changes the future carrier in two distinct ways.

The invariant record adds a classical port (d) while preserving every quantum
degree of freedom inside the selected relative sector. Future constructors may
condition on (d) without acquiring a global frame sheet.

The raw signed record adds a port (u) and removes reflection-sheet coherence
from the reduced frame. Future constructors can condition on a selected sheet
relative to the pointer, but operations requiring coherent superposition of
the two sheets are no longer available unless the environmental fragments are
recollected and coherently erased.

The record is therefore not a passive copy of pre-existing source data. It is
a carrier transition that determines which part of the controller becomes
classical and which counterfactual controls disappear.

## Fault-tolerance consequence

A component-syndrome circuit may correctly identify every basis-state right
shift and still fail as a quantum error-correction instrument because it leaks
logical frame information. Classical truth-table correctness is insufficient.

The recovery contract must specify both:

- which error label becomes available;
- which within-code coherences the extraction channel preserves.

For coherent frame protection, the required nondemolition algebra contains
the global left-frame action within each relative sector. The exact invariant
syndrome preserves it; the raw signed syndrome does not.

## DPC: a diagnosis can manufacture the classical frame it presupposes

The conjecture is:

> A syndrome becomes an objective error record only when its pointer algebra
> is invariant under the protected reference symmetry. If the cheaper pointer
> is merely covariant, redundant recording does more than reveal an error: it
> transports or selects a reference frame and exponentially suppresses the
> coherence between its symmetry-related branches.

The `S3` model supplies an exact finite mechanism. The controlled sign that
makes the syndrome gauge invariant is also what prevents the diagnostic
environment from learning the global reflection sheet.

## Critics

### Gauge coherence is unphysical, so losing it is harmless

Without any external reference, gauge-related branches are redundant
descriptions. The raw pointer becomes such a reference by carrying a signed
label. Relative to later noncentral constructors, the sheet then has
operational meaning. Whether its coherence matters is fixed by the admitted
future constructor family, not by calling it gauge.

### The raw syndrome identifies the same basis-state fault

Basis truth tables ignore off-diagonal terms. Two instruments can agree on
every basis state while differing exactly on the coherence that makes the
frame a quantum reference.

### Forgetting the pointer should restore the symmetry

Forgetting removes access to the label but leaves the reduced system dephased.
Reversal requires coherent control of every environment fragment that retained
which-sheet information.

### More copies only improve reliability

They improve accessibility of the selected classical distinction while
suppressing the conjugate sheet coherence by the same overlap power.

### A covariant pointer does not break symmetry

Correct for the enlarged system--pointer carrier. It does mean that the
pointer is part of the reference frame. Treating its signed label as an inert
absolute record would discard that transformation law.

## Exact falsifiers

- Failure of the raw difference to change sign under a common reflection.
- A nonzero relative rotation sector in which the two sheet branches write the
  same ideal raw pointer state.
- An invariant exact syndrome pointer decohering states within one fixed
  relative sector.
- An imperfect repeated record whose sheet cross term does not acquire the
  factor (\gamma^n) under conditionally independent collisions.
- A signed raw pointer interaction commuting with system-only reflection while
  its pointer labels remain fixed and distinct.
- Destructive basis-state correctness promoted to coherent nondemolition
  correction without an off-diagonal test.
- Redundant raw records claimed to reveal the common (\mathbb Z_3) rotation;
  this interaction leaks only the reflection-sheet coordinate.

## Claim boundary

This packet proves the finite collision-model decoherence and covariance
statements. It does not derive a microscopic bath Hamiltonian, a macroscopic
stability time, independence of environment fragments, or the thermodynamic
cost of amplification.

Its structural conclusion is that objective error recording and dynamical
frame selection are two branches of the same syndrome interaction, separated
by whether the pointer records an invariant group coordinate or a covariant
component coordinate.
