# The minimal common carrier is a Fourier-saturated multigraph rigging, not one Hilbert space

## Question

Can one source topology make Fourier sewing and the primitive, square, seam,
archimedean, and endpoint rows jointly continuous or closable without forcing
their distinct regularity types into one Hilbert norm?

There is a canonical abstract construction: the minimal Fourier-saturated
graph topology on a common source core.  It solves the locally convex
continuity problem while leaving positivity, Hilbert adjoints, determinant
class, and completion stability as separate gates.

## Why one positive Hilbert carrier fails

Aspect's minimal adjacent-Weyl model takes

\[
C_a=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&a\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Metric self-adjointness forces

\[
H=
\begin{pmatrix}
x&y\\
y&ax
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det H=ax^2-y^2.
\]

A positive metric exists only for `a>0`.  For `a<0`, every compatible metric
is indefinite, and at the seam `a=0` the compatible form degenerates.

Therefore the reciprocal sectors cannot be compressed into one globally
positive bordered-Weyl carrier.  They require separately typed sector
completions and a seam relation.  This independently supports the paired-
tower architecture.

## Common algebraic core

Let `D` be a source-authorized dense core stable under Fourier transform
`F`, with

\[
\mathcal F^4=I.
\]

Let the finite admitted boundary family be

\[
\mathcal L=
\{L_P,L_Q,L_{seam},L_\infty,L_{end}\},
\]

where each row has its own target locally convex or Hilbert space `Y_i`.
The primitive target may be exponential or distributional; the square target
may be Hilbert; endpoint and seam targets retain their own trace topology.

Do not identify the `Y_i`.

## Fourier-saturated graph topology

Start with a separating source seminorm family `p_0` on `D`.  Add

\[
p_{i,k}(x)=q_i(L_i\mathcal F^k x),
\qquad
i\in\mathcal L,
\qquad
k=0,1,2,3,
\]

for every defining seminorm `q_i` of `Y_i`.  Let `tau_G` be the locally convex
topology generated by `p_0` and all `p_i,k`.

This topology has three exact properties.

1. Every admitted row `L_i` is continuous.
2. Fourier transform is continuous and permutes the added seminorms cyclically.
3. `tau_G` is the weakest locally convex topology stronger than `p_0` having
   properties 1 and 2.

The third property is the universal minimality theorem: any other topology
making every `L_i` and `F` continuous must make every composite `L_i F^k`
continuous, and therefore contains `tau_G`.

## Completion as a multigraph

The completion embeds naturally into the closure of the diagonal graph

\[
x\longmapsto
\left(
x,
(L_i\mathcal F^k x)_{i,k}
\right)
\]

inside the product of the source completion and all typed target spaces.
Each boundary row extends as a coordinate map.  Fourier extends by cyclic
permutation of the four orbit coordinates.

This is a multigraph rigging, not a common Hilbert completion.  It retains the
asymmetric facts:

- `P` may be distributional or exponentially rigged;
- `Q` may be an ordinary Hilbert coordinate;
- seam and endpoint traces may be continuous only on their declared graph
  domains;
- reciprocal sectors can carry positive and Krein metrics separately.

## What the construction does and does not prove

It proves an abstract minimal topology once a common invariant core and typed
rows are supplied.  It does not prove:

- that the common core is nontrivial and dense in every required pivot;
- that the rows are source-authorized;
- that Hilbert adjoint domains are dense;
- that seam compression is compact or Fredholm;
- that a determinant exists;
- positive port collocation;
- uniform observability or compact-open determinant convergence.

In particular, continuity in the multigraph topology must not be reported as
boundedness on additive adelic `L2`.

## Avoiding a topology-by-fiat objection

Any chosen family of maps can be made continuous by a graph topology.  The
construction becomes explanatory only because the admitted row family is
frozen independently by source incidence and is finite up to the four-element
Fourier orbit.

The hard-to-vary requirements are:

1. no row may be added merely to kill a kernel;
2. the target topology of each row is fixed before the common completion;
3. cutoff maps preserve the row labels and their Fourier orbit;
4. reciprocal reflection acts on the same multigraph packet;
5. counterfactual source perturbations have predicted coordinate responses.

If these fail, the graph topology is a convenient realization space rather
than a source-derived carrier.

## Finite-cutoff naturality

For `X subset Y`, let `j_YX:D_X -> D_Y` be the source inclusion.  The
multigraph completions form a directed system only if every row square closes:

\[
L_{i,Y}\mathcal F_Y^k j_{Y,X}
=
t_{i,Y,X}L_{i,X}\mathcal F_X^k
\]

for declared target transports `t_i,Y,X`.  Scalar agreement after summing the
rows is insufficient.

Uniform continuity constants are needed only when a quantitative completion
or inverse theorem is claimed.  Exact graph naturality is the first gate.

## Relation to closability

On a chosen Hilbert pivot, each row still has a closability test: its adjoint
domain must be dense.  The multigraph completion can retain a row continuously
even when that row is not closable as an operator on the pivot.  In that case
it is a boundary correspondence, not a Hilbert operator.

This distinction is useful rather than defective.  It prevents the primitive
current or idelic trace from being forced into an invalid bounded-operator
type.

## Falsifiers

- The proposed common core is not Fourier invariant.
- A boundary row is undefined or infinite on the core.
- Fourier has an orbit larger than the declared finite saturation because of
  omitted sheet or phase data.
- Cutoff inclusions fail one typed row square.
- A target topology is strengthened only to make a domination inequality pass.
- Multigraph continuity is misreported as Hilbert closability.
- A determinant is formed before seam compactness or an operator ideal is
  established.
- One global positive metric is imposed across the sign-changing minimal
  Weyl model.

## Verdict

The weakest plausible common carrier is not one Hilbert space.  It is the
Fourier-saturated multigraph completion of a source-authorized common core,
with each boundary current retaining its own target topology.
