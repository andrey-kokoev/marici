# Coherent two-route measurement halves a cube-root bridge phase exactly

Owner: `marici.Kitaev`

## Bounded question

Can the relational sector bit and an oriented `C3` phase port turn two
full-rank charged routes into a new deterministic qutrit gate without an
arbitrary analog waiting time?

Yes, conditionally and exactly. Coherently control two unitary bridge routes,
then measure the route bit in an oriented phase basis. The branch operators
are linear combinations of the two route unitaries. They are proportional to
unitaries precisely when one rotated Hermitian part of the relative holonomy
is scalar.

For a relative cube-root projector phase, the `C3` choice `z=-omega` produces
an exact square-root phase with success probability `3/4`. The failure branch
is the inverse cube-root gate, so it can be undone and the protocol repeated.
The expected number of attempts is `4/3`.

## Claim boundary

The linear-combination theorem and cube-root phase-halving gadget are exact
finite-dimensional results. They assume coherent route control, a clean route
bit, oriented conjugate-basis measurement, full-rank unitary bridge branches,
and an admitted inverse of the failure gate.

The packet does not construct those primitives microscopically in `D(S3)`,
prove that the relative bridge holonomy has the required projector form, or
show that the resulting fixed gate set is universal or fault tolerant. It
provides a finite compiler target and branchwise falsifiers.

## Coherent two-route control

Let `R` and `S` be unitary qutrit actions of two charged bridge routes with the
same typed endpoints. Prepare a route qubit in

\[
|+\rangle
=
\frac{|0\rangle+|1\rangle}{\sqrt2}.
\]

Apply the controlled route

\[
W
=
R\otimes|0\rangle\langle0|
+
S\otimes|1\rangle\langle1|.
\]

For a unit complex number `z`, measure the route qubit in the basis whose bras
are

\[
\langle m_+|
=
\frac{\langle0|+z\langle1|}{\sqrt2},
\]

and

\[
\langle m_-|
=
\frac{\langle0|-z\langle1|}{\sqrt2}.
\]

The induced qutrit branch operators are

\[
M_+
=
\frac{R+zS}{2},
\qquad
M_-
=
\frac{R-zS}{2}.
\]

This coherent sum is unavailable if the route label has already become a
classical record or leaked into an environment.

## Exact unitary-branch criterion

Define the relative holonomy

\[
H=R^*S.
\]

Then

\[
M_+^*M_+
=
\frac14
\left(
2I+zH+\overline zH^*
\right),
\]

and

\[
M_-^*M_-
=
\frac14
\left(
2I-zH-\overline zH^*
\right).
\]

Both nonzero branches are proportional to unitaries exactly when

\[
zH+\overline zH^*=\lambda I
\]

for one real scalar `lambda` with absolute value below two. Their
input-independent probabilities are then

\[
p_+=\frac{2+\lambda}{4},
\qquad
p_-=\frac{2-\lambda}{4}.
\]

The criterion is both necessary and sufficient. It distinguishes a reversible
measurement-assisted gate from an input-dependent filter.

## Spectral interpretation

Let the eigenvalues of `H` be

\[
e^{i\theta_j}.
\]

Write

\[
z=e^{i\alpha}.
\]

The unitary-branch criterion becomes

\[
2\cos(\alpha+\theta_j)=\lambda
\]

for every eigenvalue. After one common rotation, all eigenvalues of `H` must
have the same real part. On the unit circle this permits at most two conjugate
phase values, with arbitrary degeneracies.

Thus not every pair of charged routes admits reversible linear-combination
measurement. Their relative spectrum is the first finite gate.

## Hermitian-involution example

If

\[
H=H^*,
\qquad
H^2=I,
\]

choose

\[
z=i.
\]

Then `lambda=0`, both outcomes have probability `1/2`, and the normalized
branch gates are

\[
U_+=\frac{R+iS}{\sqrt2},
\qquad
U_-=\frac{R-iS}{\sqrt2}.
\]

For `R=I` and `S=H`, these are fractional involution gates. If `H` is a
coordinate transposition, one obtains an exact quarter-turn generated by that
swap. The required phase `i` is a `C4` orientation and is not supplied by the
`C3` dyon port alone.

## Cube-root projector holonomy

Take the first route as the canonical logical reference, so `R=I` in the
chosen qutrit frame, and let the second route be `S=H`. Let `P` be an
orthogonal qutrit projector and suppose the relative charged route is

\[
H
=
e^{2\pi iP/3}
=
I+(\omega-1)P,
\qquad
\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}.
\]

Its eigenvalue is `omega` on the image of `P` and one on its complement.
Choose the oriented route-measurement phase

\[
z=-\omega=e^{-i\pi/3}.
\]

For both eigenvalues of `H`, the real part of `zH` is `1/2`. Hence

\[
zH+\overline zH^*=I,
\]

so

\[
p_+=\frac34,
\qquad
p_-=\frac14.
\]

The probabilities are independent of the unknown qutrit state.

## Success branch is the exact square root

On the complement of `P`, the normalized plus branch has phase

\[
e^{-i\pi/6}.
\]

On the image of `P`, it has phase

\[
e^{i\pi/6}.
\]

After removing the common global phase,

\[
U_+
\sim
e^{i\pi P/3}.
\]

Therefore

\[
U_+^2
\sim
H.
\]

The measurement gadget exactly halves the cube-root projector phase. No
continuously selected dwell time appears.

## Failure branch is correctable

The normalized minus branch is, up to global phase,

\[
U_-
\sim
e^{-2\pi iP/3}
=
H^*.
\]

If `H` is an admitted exact gate, apply `H` after the minus outcome. This
restores the input state up to global phase, and the attempt can be repeated.

After `n` attempts, the probability of no success is

\[
4^{-n}.
\]

The expected attempt count is

\[
\frac{1}{3/4}=\frac43.
\]

Thus the ideal protocol is an exact repeat-until-success square-root
constructor with bounded expected cost and an exponentially decaying tail.

## Vacuum-line specialization

If

\[
P=P_A
\]

is the native first-pair vacuum projector, the injected success gate is

\[
Q_A=e^{i\pi P_A/3}.
\]

In the monomial qutrit basis, `P_A` is an off-axis edge projector. Therefore
`Q_A` is nonmonomial. Discrete electric braid permutations conjugate it onto
all three qutrit edges.

This is a substantially better fixed-gate candidate than an axis-aligned
sixth-root phase: it couples coordinates rather than only relabeling their
phases.

The remaining algebraic question is whether the finite braid and cube-root
group together with `Q_A` has finite or dense projective closure. The current
packet does not assume the answer.

## Where the two independent ports enter

The relational `B`-charge system supplies the coherent route bit and balances
the sector displacement. The oriented `C3` port supplies

\[
z=-\omega
\]

and distinguishes it from its conjugate.

Neither resource alone implements the gadget:

- without the `B` reference, the two charged routes cannot be coherently
  superposed inside one global charge sector;
- without the oriented `C3` phase, the required measurement basis is absent;
- without relative projector holonomy `H`, the linear combination acts only
  on the route bit or produces a scalar qutrit phase.

The gate is generated by their typed interaction.

## Environment closure condition

Suppose the two route branches return environment states `e_R` and `e_S`.
Before route-bit measurement the joint state contains

\[
R\psi\otimes|0\rangle\otimes e_R
+
S\psi\otimes|1\rangle\otimes e_S.
\]

The advertised qutrit branch operator `R plus or minus zS` exists only when
the environment overlap has unit modulus and its phase is included in the
declared value of `z`. Exact clean interference requires the two environment
states to agree up to one calibrated phase.

If they are orthogonal, route measurement produces no coherent linear
combination on the qutrit. If their overlap has modulus below one, the branch
is a noisy channel rather than the displayed unitary operator.

## Fault accumulation under retry

The ideal expected attempt count is small, but one physical fault may persist
across retries through the shared reference, route controller, or qutrit.
Fault locations are not independent merely because attempts have distinct
classical labels.

A filtered implementation must report:

- one-attempt branch-channel residuals;
- correction error after the minus branch;
- reference and environment return before retry;
- the stopping-time tail;
- maximum fault spread over repeated attempts;
- common-mode orientation and phase-calibration errors.

The geometric success tail does not by itself imply a threshold theorem.

## General deterministic-feed-forward criterion

When both branch operators are proportional to unitaries, the outcome record
selects known gates `U_+` and `U_-`. To make the overall logical action one
deterministic target `T`, the constructor family must contain corrections

\[
C_+U_+=T,
\qquad
C_-U_-=T.
\]

Alternatively, one branch may be exactly undoable and retried, as in the
cube-root gadget.

Input-independent probabilities are necessary for this clean random-unitary
interpretation. If the scalar-norm criterion fails, the outcome probability
depends on the unknown input and the branch is a filter, not merely a random
gate.

## Exact falsifiers

- The route label is classical or environmentally recorded before coherent
  recombination.
- The branch operators differ from `(R plus or minus zS)/2`.
- `zH` plus its adjoint is non-scalar while both branches are called unitary
  up to normalization.
- Branch probabilities depend on the unknown input while deterministic
  feed-forward is claimed.
- For the cube-root projector case, `z` differs from the oriented value
  `-omega` without rechecking the scalar-norm equation.
- The success and failure probabilities differ from `3/4` and `1/4`.
- The plus branch fails to square projectively to `H`.
- The minus branch is retried without first undoing `H*`.
- The oriented measurement phase is inferred from the `B` reference alone.
- A global ancillary cube-root phase is treated as a qutrit projector
  holonomy.
- The nonmonomial vacuum-line gate is declared universal without computing its
  generated closure.
- Ideal geometric retry is promoted to independent physical fault trials.

## Disposition

The relational sector workspace and oriented `C3` port can jointly implement
an exact new qutrit gate. When two charged routes differ by a cube-root phase
on a projector, oriented route measurement with `z=-omega` produces the
projector-phase square root with probability `3/4`; the other branch is
exactly undoable and retryable.

For the native vacuum projector this yields a fixed nonmonomial sixth-root
edge gate without arbitrary analog waiting. The construction is still a
compiler target: it requires coherent charged routes, an independently
oriented measurement basis, clean environment return, exact failure
correction, and a separate closure calculation for the resulting discrete
gate group.
