# The resolved cube-root bridge and electric braids generate a finite monomial qutrit group

Owner: `marici.Kitaev`

## Bounded question

If the resolved magnetic bridge

\[
D_C=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,\omega)
\]

were cleanly executable on the protected electric fusion qutrit, would it and
the two native electric braids already give universal qutrit control?

No. The exact reachable unitary group is the finite monomial complex
reflection group

\[
G(3,1,3)
\cong
C_3^3\rtimes S_3.
\]

It has order 162 and projective order 54. Its complex linear span is the full
matrix algebra, but its executable word set is finite and therefore not dense
in `U(3)`.

## Frozen generators

Use the protected fusion basis

\[
|A_L\rangle,
\qquad
|B_L\rangle,
\qquad
|C_L\rangle.
\]

The exact electric data gives

\[
B_{12}=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,1)
\]

and

\[
B_{23}=F B_{12}F^T,
\]

where

\[
F=
\begin{pmatrix}
1/2&1/2&1/\sqrt2\\
-1/2&-1/2&1/\sqrt2\\
1/\sqrt2&-1/\sqrt2&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The resolved `C`-`G` monodromy candidate supplies either `D_C` above or its
complex conjugate. The choice does not alter the generated group.

## Hidden monomial basis

Define

\[
|q_0\rangle=|C_L\rangle,
\]

\[
|q_1\rangle
=
{|A_L\rangle-|B_L\rangle\over\sqrt2},
\]

and

\[
|q_2\rangle
=
{|A_L\rangle+|B_L\rangle\over\sqrt2}.
\]

These vectors form an orthonormal basis. Direct application of the frozen
matrices gives

\[
B_{12}:
q_1\longleftrightarrow q_2,
\qquad
q_0\longmapsto q_0,
\]

and

\[
B_{23}:
q_0\longleftrightarrow q_1,
\qquad
q_2\longmapsto q_2.
\]

Thus the two electric braids generate every permutation of the three `q`
axes.

The magnetic bridge acts as

\[
D_C|q_0\rangle=\omega|q_0\rangle,
\]

\[
D_C|q_1\rangle=|q_1\rangle,
\qquad
D_C|q_2\rangle=|q_2\rangle.
\]

Conjugating `D_C` by braid permutations supplies the independent cube-root
phase on each axis.

## Exact group classification

The diagonal subgroup generated by the three conjugates of `D_C` is

\[
\left{
\operatorname{diag}
(\omega^{a_0},\omega^{a_1},\omega^{a_2})
:
a_j\in\mathbb Z/3
\right}
\cong C_3^3.
\]

The braid group image is `S3`, acting by permutation of the three diagonal
coordinates. Therefore every reachable word has the unique monomial form

\[
\operatorname{diag}
(\omega^{a_0},\omega^{a_1},\omega^{a_2})P_\sigma,
\qquad
\sigma\in S_3.
\]

Conversely every such monomial matrix is generated. Hence

\[
\langle B_{12},B_{23},D_C\rangle
=
C_3^3\rtimes S_3
=
G(3,1,3).
\]

Its order is

\[
3^3\cdot6=162.
\]

The scalar center contained in the group is

\[
\{I,\omega I,\omega^2I\}.
\]

Quotienting global phase gives projective order

\[
162/3=54.
\]

The complex-conjugate bridge generates the same diagonal subgroup because
`omega^2` is the inverse of `omega`.

## Full associative span but finite reachability

The monomial representation is irreducible. Any operator commuting with the
independent diagonal phases must be diagonal in the `q` basis. Commuting also
with every permutation forces all three diagonal entries to be equal.

Thus the commutant consists only of scalars. By finite-dimensional Burnside
closure, the complex associative span of the group is

\[
M_3(\mathbb C).
\]

This does not make the gate group universal. Linear combinations of reachable
unitaries are not executable words. The actual group has only 162 elements.

This is a smallest concrete separation among:

- full associative operator generation;
- finite projective gate reachability;
- dense unitary controllability.

## Continuous phases still do not suffice with discrete braids

Suppose the cube-root bridge were upgraded to an arbitrary continuous phase

\[
D_C(t)=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,e^{it}).
\]

Conjugation by the discrete braid permutations gives independent phases on
the three `q` axes. The reachable group becomes

\[
U(1)^3\rtimes S_3.
\]

It is still monomial and is not all of `U(3)`.

The earlier Lie-algebra theorem assumes continuously pulsed Hermitian braid
generators, not merely access to the completed exchange unitaries
`B_12,B_23`. Fractional evolution under a reflection Hamiltonian is an
additional physical capability. A topological exchange path does not
automatically supply its continuously timed logarithm.

Thus there are three separate actuator models.

1. Discrete braids plus discrete cube-root phase: finite group of order 162.
2. Discrete braids plus continuous pair phase: monomial group
   `U(1)^3 semidirect S3`.
3. Continuously pulsed braid Hamiltonians plus a nonzero bridge Hamiltonian:
   full Lie algebra under the previously frozen rank condition.

Only the first model follows from the proposed resolved topological
monodromy, and even that remains conditional on clean corridor compilation.

## What the finite group is good for

The group is not computationally universal, but it is operationally useful.

- It leaves the purely real tester surface.
- It supplies both signs of the missing complex quadrature.
- It permutes three mutually orthogonal phase axes.
- It gives a finite exact calibration group for process and corridor tests.
- Its finite relations provide hostile checks for phase and braid-frame
  faults.

Using the bridge to certify its own physical corridor remains circular. Once
independently verified, it can certify other real-only constructors.

## Fault implications

### Character conjugation

Replacing `omega` with `omega^2` leaves the abstract reachable group unchanged
but conjugates signed phase labels.

### Axis-frame permutation

A common braid-frame fault permutes the `q` axes and preserves every abstract
group relation. An independent logical-basis anchor is required to identify
which physical channel is called `C`.

### Fractional-pulse overclaim

Knowing an involutory braid unitary does not authorize

\[
e^{-itH_{\rm braid}}
\]

for arbitrary `t`. A chosen logarithm is not a source-derived Hamiltonian
path.

### Associative-span overclaim

Tomographic linear span or Burnside irreducibility does not imply that sums of
gate words can be executed coherently as deterministic unitaries.

## Exact falsifiers

- The three displayed `q` vectors failing orthonormality.
- Either braid acting other than as the stated transposition.
- The resolved bridge failing to phase only `q_0`.
- A reachable word having more than one nonzero entry in a row or column in
  the `q` basis.
- The discrete group having order other than 162 or projective order other
  than 54.
- Full associative span promoted to dense gate reachability.
- A continuous pair phase with discrete braids claimed to leave the monomial
  group.
- Fractional braid Hamiltonians inferred from completed topological exchanges.

## Result

The resolved magnetic phase would be a genuine new protected qutrit gate, but
not a universal one. Together with electric braiding it generates exactly the
finite group `G(3,1,3)`. The group spans every qutrit matrix algebraically
while reaching only 54 projective gates. Full controllability requires a
stronger continuous Hamiltonian model that has not been physically compiled.
