# Positivity Improvement Must Precede One-Dimensional Trace

## Correction

Suppose the direct and reciprocal endpoint rows already have the same
one-dimensional image (L=\mathbb C u\), and arithmetic aggregation acts on
that line by

\[
A_s u=m(s)u.
\]

Then every post-aggregation strict-overlap test is a scalar divisor test. For
nonzero (u),

\[
\langle u,A_su\rangle=m(s)\|u\|^2.
\]

Consequently nonzero overlap is equivalent to (m(s)\ne0). On a real ordered
ray, positivity improvement is equivalent to (m(s)>0); on a complex line it
is not even defined without an independently supplied phase or real cone.
Neither version explains nonvanishing of the completed scalar section.

This dissolves the proposed principal-angle route in the common-trace model.
The trace lines coincide before aggregation, so their angle reserve is already
maximal. Any loss occurs entirely in the scalar multiplier.

## The stronger higher-rank theorem

Let (V) be a finite-dimensional real vector space with a proper cone (C),
and let (C^*\) be its dual cone. A source-derived correspondence
(\Phi:V\to V) is positivity improving when

\[
\Phi(C\setminus\{0\})\subset\operatorname{int}C.
\]

For every nonzero (x\in C) and every nonzero (f\in C^*), this implies

\[
f(\Phi x)>0.
\]

This is genuinely stronger than positivity, positive-semidefinite kernel
factorization, complete positivity, unitality, trace preservation, or unitary
sewing. It can carry explanatory content only when (\Phi), (C), and the
evaluation functional are derived before scalar trace from authorized source
constructors.

For a cutoff family, pointwise positivity improvement is still insufficient.
One needs a fixed normalization and a uniform interior margin. In the matrix
positive cone a usable finite version is

\[
\Phi_N(X)\succeq \delta\,\operatorname{Tr}(X)I
\quad(X\succeq0)
\]

with one (\delta>0) independent of (N). A family with
(\delta_N\downarrow0) is strictly improving at every finite stage but does
not preserve the reserve under completion.

## Exact hostile model

On (M_2(\mathbb C)), let

\[
U=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\Phi_0(X)=UXU^*.
\]

The channel is completely positive, unital, trace preserving, invertible, and
implemented by a unitary. With (P=|e_1\rangle\langle e_1|), however,

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(P\Phi_0(P))=0.
\]

Thus all those weaker conditions admit the quarter-turn blind state. Its Choi
matrix is positive semidefinite but rank one, and (\Phi_0(P)) remains on the
boundary of the positive cone.

For (0<\varepsilon\le1), the depolarized channel

\[
\Phi_\varepsilon(X)
=(1-\varepsilon)UXU^*+
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\operatorname{Tr}(X)I
\]

is positivity improving and obeys

\[
\Phi_\varepsilon(X)\succeq
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\operatorname{Tr}(X)I.
\]

But choosing (\varepsilon_N=1/N) makes the reserve collapse. The
depolarizing term is a mathematical repair fixture, not an authorized
theta/Tate constructor.

## Source-authority boundary

The admissible route is therefore:

\[
\text{source arithmetic module}
\longrightarrow
\text{higher-rank ordered correspondence}
\longrightarrow
\text{scalar trace}.
\]

Tracing first and then requiring strict overlap is rejected as circular. The
actual theta/Tate source must independently provide the higher-rank module,
proper cone or ordered operator system, primitive correspondence, and uniform
completion estimate. This packet supplies no authority for those objects and
does not prove RH.

## Falsifiers

- The correspondence is only positive, CP, unital, trace preserving, or
  unitary, and a nonzero positive state has zero tested overlap.
- The claimed cone is introduced only after observing the scalar multiplier.
- The construction lives on the one-dimensional common trace image.
- Positivity improvement holds cutoffwise but its interior margin tends to
  zero.
- Strictness requires an unauthorized depolarizing or discrete constructor.
- The scalar trace agrees while typed pre-trace correspondences differ.
