# Authorized robustness is a control distance

## Bounded question

How does the ambient distance to failure change when perturbations must be
generated by an ordered, source-authorized control family?

## Frozen control model

Let \(X\) be a smooth finite-dimensional realization manifold with a closed
failure set \(F\). Let the authorized reversible local controls be vector
fields

\[
f_1,\ldots,f_m.
\]

For the driftless model,

\[
\dot x(t)=\sum_{j=1}^m u_j(t)f_j(x(t)),
\]

freeze an admissible control class and cost, for example

\[
c(u)=\int_0^T\|u(t)\|\,dt.
\]

The authorized robustness radius is

\[
d_{\rm auth}(x,F)
=
\inf\{c(u):x(0)=x,\ x(T)\in F\}.
\]

It is infinite when no authorized trajectory reaches failure.

This is a sub-Finsler or sub-Riemannian control distance when the usual
regularity and bracket-generation hypotheses hold. The exact geometry depends
on the declared norm, drift, reversibility, and amplitude bounds.

## Ambient versus authorized distance

If every authorized trajectory has ambient length no larger than its declared
control cost, then

\[
d_{\rm ambient}(x,F)
\leq
d_{\rm auth}(x,F).
\]

Equality requires a compiler theorem showing that an ambient shortest path can
be synthesized by authorized controls at equal cost. It is not automatic.

Three cases occur:

- the nearest ambient failure direction is directly actuated;
- it is not directly actuated but is generated by ordered brackets;
- it lies outside the full reachable orbit and failure is prohibited.

These correspond to direct vulnerability, higher-order vulnerability, and an
invariant control barrier.

## First-order test

Suppose locally the safe region is described by a smooth margin

\[
\phi(x)>0
\]

and failure by \(\phi=0\). The instantaneous change under control \(f_j\) is

\[
L_{f_j}\phi=d\phi(f_j).
\]

If some \(L_{f_j}\phi\neq0\), a directly authorized perturbation changes the
margin at first order.

If every \(L_{f_j}\phi=0\) at a point, the direct control distribution is
tangent to the local margin surface there. This does not yet prove robustness:
ordered compositions can generate Lie brackets not present among the original
directions.

## Ordered commutator route

Let \(\Phi_f^t\) and \(\Phi_g^t\) be local flows. A four-step commutator word
has the expansion

\[
\Phi_g^{-\varepsilon}
\circ\Phi_f^{-\varepsilon}
\circ\Phi_g^{\varepsilon}
\circ\Phi_f^{\varepsilon}(x)
=
x+\varepsilon^2[f,g](x)+O(\varepsilon^3),
\]

up to the sign fixed by the flow-composition convention.

Therefore

\[
L_f\phi=L_g\phi=0,
\qquad
L_{[f,g]}\phi\neq0
\]

means that failure is invisible to every individual infinitesimal control but
visible to an ordered second-order word.

This is not addition of control effects. It is noncommutative product syntax:
reordering the same four flows reverses or destroys the bracket displacement.

## Bracket depth

Let \(\mathfrak L_k\) be the span of authorized iterated brackets of depth at
most \(k\). Define the local transverse depth at \(x\) by the least \(k\) for
which some \(V\in\mathfrak L_k\) satisfies

\[
d\phi_x(V)\neq0.
\]

Depth one is direct actuation. Depth two is a commutator route. Larger depth
requires nested ordered words.

The depth is a local structural invariant relative to the frozen generators.
It is not by itself the exact finite control cost. Under regular bracket
geometry, displacement in a weight-\(k\) direction typically has cost scaling
like the \(k\)-th root of its coordinate size. Precise constants require the
nilpotent approximation and control norm.

## Heisenberg witness

On \(\mathbb R^3\), take

\[
f=\partial_x-\frac y2\partial_z,
\qquad
g=\partial_y+\frac x2\partial_z.
\]

At the origin, both direct fields lie in the \(xy\)-plane, but

\[
[f,g]=\partial_z.
\]

A small commutator loop returns \(x\) and \(y\) to leading order while changing
\(z\) by order \(\varepsilon^2\). A vertical failure plane can therefore be
reached even though no direct control points vertically at the origin.

For a small vertical separation \(\delta\), ambient Euclidean distance is
order \(\delta\), while horizontal control cost is order \(\sqrt\delta\).
The nearest mathematical perturbation is substantially cheaper than the
authorized ordered implementation.

## Orbit barrier theorem

Let \(\mathcal O(x)) be the orbit generated by finite compositions of
authorized local flows. If

\[
\mathcal O(x)\cap F=\varnothing,
\]

then failure is unreachable and

\[
d_{\rm auth}(x,F)=\infty.
\]

The orbit is the nonlinear analogue of the smallest invariant reachable
subspace in the finite constructor theorem.

A sufficient local certificate is a source-derived invariant \(I\) satisfying

\[
L_VI=0
\]

for every vector field \(V\) in the Lie algebra generated by the authorized
controls, with the source and failure set lying on disjoint invariant levels.

Testing only the original \(f_j\) is insufficient unless their common
invariants are proved closed under all generated brackets.

## Drift and irreversible controls

With drift,

\[
\dot x=f_0(x)+\sum_ju_jf_j(x),
\]

or with one-sided semigroup controls, inverse flows in the commutator word may
be unavailable. Lie-algebra accessibility can then overestimate executable
reachability.

The correct object is the reachable semigroup or control cone, together with
time and amplitude constraints. A formal bracket synthesized using negative
time is unauthorized unless reversible implementation is independently
available.

This is the control analogue of treating an inverse operator as present merely
because a finite-stage map is injective.

## Quantum constructor instance

For coherent quantum control with Hamiltonians \(H_j\), the infinitesimal
generators act by

\[
\mathcal L_j(\rho)=-i[H_j,\rho].
\]

Commutators of superoperators correspond to nested Hamiltonian commutators.
An ordered pulse sequence can generate an effective direction absent from every
individual Hamiltonian.

If the dynamical Lie algebra preserves a code sector or superselection block,
that orbit is an exact barrier. If a bracket leaves it, the bracket depth gives
the first infinitesimal order at which the declared control family breaks the
selection rule.

Open-system channels and measurement feedback require semigroup or instrument
trees rather than reversible Lie words. The same order-typing warning remains,
but the Lie theorem does not automatically transfer.

## Toric-code interpretation

Local Pauli generators preserve topological indistinguishability below the
support required for a noncontractible word. The relevant cost is discrete
support weight, not a smooth control length. Code distance is therefore the
discrete protected-sector analogue of transverse bracket depth:

- local generators do not directly cross the logical barrier;
- an ordered or supported composite first reaches a nontrivial logical class;
- below that cost, the stabilizer/syndrome structure certifies prohibition.

The analogy concerns generated directions and minimum authorized cost, not a
literal smooth Lie approximation of Pauli support.

## Failure-stratum vulnerability

For several closed failure strata \(F_j\), define

\[
d_{\rm auth}(x,F_j)
\]

separately. The first executable failure is the stratum with least authorized
cost, not necessarily the one closest in ambient norm.

This can reverse the ambient ordering. A mathematically nearby defect may be
protected by an invariant, while a more distant defect lies along a directly
actuated direction.

Thus a source-native robustness report should include both:

- ambient nearest failure;
- authorized least-cost failure route or orbit barrier.

## DPC: ordered vulnerability explanation

The conjecture is:

> A capability's physical robustness is explained by the least-cost authorized
> word or control path reaching its typed failure stratum, or by an invariant
> reachable orbit proving that no such path exists. Ambient distance is only a
> lower-bound diagnostic unless the nearest perturbation is compiled from the
> authorized controls.

The finite reversible smooth case supports this through control distance,
bracket accessibility, and orbit barriers. The open research boundary is
irreversible, stochastic, infinite-dimensional, and resource-constrained
control.

## Critics

### Lie brackets are infinitesimal, not finite operations

Correct. A bracket word gives a local asymptotic direction. Finite reachability
and cost require flow-domain and error-control theorems.

### Bracket generation does not imply controllability with drift

Correct. Reversibility and drift hypotheses are essential. Accessibility,
small-time local controllability, and global controllability are distinct.

### The metric may ignore laboratory cost

Correct. The control norm, amplitude bounds, time cost, and allowed inverses
must be source-authorized.

### A deep bracket can be unusably expensive

Correct. Structural reachability is separate from robust or practical
actuation. Bracket depth supplies order, not full pricing.

## Exact falsifiers

- Ambient nearest-failure distance reported as executable control cost without
  a synthesis theorem.
- First-order tangency presented as an invariant barrier before checking Lie
  brackets or semigroup closure.
- A bracket word using inverse flows when controls are irreversible.
- Lie-algebra accessibility promoted to global controllability without the
  required hypotheses.
- A bracket-depth claim contradicted by a shallower transverse authorized word.
- An orbit barrier intersecting the failure set.
- A physical robustness ranking based on ambient distance when authorized costs
  reverse that order.

## Deutschian explanation

An unavailable direct perturbation can still be synthesized by order. The
system moves around a small loop in allowed directions; noncommutativity prevents
the loop from closing and leaves a displacement in a new direction. That
residual is the physical content of the bracket.

Conversely, a true barrier is not absence of a visible arrow. It is an invariant
orbit closed under every authorized ordered composition. The distinction
explains both why operation order creates capabilities and why ambient geometry
alone misprices robustness.

## Claim boundary

This packet treats finite-dimensional smooth reversible control exactly at the
local algebraic level and distinguishes it from drift, semigroup, and quantum
instrument control. It does not prove global controllability or laboratory pulse
costs.
