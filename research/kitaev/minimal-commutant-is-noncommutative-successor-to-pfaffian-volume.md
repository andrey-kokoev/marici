# Minimal commutant is the noncommutative successor to Pfaffian volume

## Question

What operator-valued criterion plays the role of nonzero Pfaffian volume when pairwise relations are noncentral and cannot be encoded by one alternating scalar form?

## Claim boundary

For a finite-dimensional authorized constructor family \(\mathcal G\subset\operatorname{End}(H)\), let

\[
\mathcal A=\operatorname{Alg}(\mathcal G)
\]

be its generated unital algebra and

\[
\mathcal A'
=
\{T\in\operatorname{End}(H):TA=AT\text{ for all }A\in\mathcal A\}
\]

its commutant.

The noncommutative relational geometry is full relative to the declared superselection structure exactly when the commutant contains no operators beyond the authorized center or multiplicity algebra.

### Relation to Pfaffian nondegeneracy

In the toric Weyl representation, the radical of the alternating form is

\[
\operatorname{Rad}\omega
=
\{v:\omega(v,w)=0\text{ for all }w\}.
\]

A radical vector labels a Weyl operator commuting with every generated Weyl operator. Thus

\[
\operatorname{Rad}\omega=0
\]

is equivalent to absence of a nontrivial logical commuting direction. In the irreducible representation, the commutant is then only \(\mathbf C I\).

The finite Pfaffian condition

\[
\operatorname{Pf}(\omega)\ne0
\]

is therefore the Abelian-coordinate expression of minimal commutant.

This supplies the desired bridge:

\[
\text{nonzero Pfaffian}
\quad\leadsto\quad
\text{minimal commutant}.
\]

The right-hand criterion survives noncentral commutators.

### Electric D(S3) qutrit

On the three-C total-C fusion space, the braid generators act as the permutation representation

\[
\mathbf1\oplus\mathbf2.
\]

The generated associative algebra is

\[
\mathbf C\oplus M_2(\mathbf C)
\]

of dimension five. Its commutant is

\[
\mathbf C\oplus\mathbf C,
\]

generated by the projectors onto the invariant line and standard doublet.

This excess commutant is the exact shadow of incomplete relational volume. Braiding distinguishes and acts within the doublet but cannot couple it to the invariant line.

### One bridge fills the operator volume

Let

\[
H_{12}=\operatorname{diag}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
\]

in the intermediate-channel basis. The pinned incidence between the invariant line \(u\) and a doublet vector \(w_2\) is

\[
\langle w_2|H_{12}|u\rangle
=
\frac{\sqrt2}{3}(\alpha-\gamma).
\]

When \(\alpha\ne\gamma\), the pair-channel constructor breaks the invariant decomposition. Together with continuous braid Hamiltonians it generates \(\mathfrak u(3)\), hence the full associative algebra

\[
M_3(\mathbf C).
\]

The commutant collapses to

\[
\mathbf C I.
\]

This is a noncommutative volume-opening transition. The added arrow does not merely increase matrix rank; it removes the final relational superselection between two previously disconnected constructor orbits.

### Relative minimality with superselection

For the full D(S3) endpoint algebra, eight simple blocks are physically typed. If cross-sector mixing is not authorized, the minimal admissible commutant is not one scalar globally but one scalar per superselection block.

With one copy of each simple module, full blockwise relational volume means

\[
\mathcal A'
=
Z(\mathcal A)
\cong
\mathbf C^8.
\]

An additional commutant direction inside a block signals an unobserved or uncontrollable multiplicity. Removing the authorized center itself would instead change the superselection theory.

Thus the criterion is task-relative:

\[
\text{commutant}
=
\text{declared unavoidable center}.
\]

### Why this is better than determinant

A determinant tests invertibility of individual operators. Minimal commutant tests collective irreducibility of an operator family.

An invertible monodromy with zero trace can still leave invariant subspaces. Conversely, a family of singular projectors can generate a full matrix algebra. Relational volume belongs to the generated family, not to one matrix coefficient.

### Four-system interpretation

In the scalar skew case, three perfect matchings interfere through one Pfaffian. In the ordered case, the corresponding matching products enlarge a generated algebra. The appropriate questions are:

- what algebra do all matching routes generate?
- what is its commutant?
- which residual projectors commute with every route?
- does a new cross-route arrow collapse that excess commutant?

This retains factorization and order rather than compressing them prematurely.

### Quantitative completion

In a cutoff family, pointwise minimal commutant is only algebraic faithfulness. Uniform relational volume requires a gap for the commutant-detecting superoperator.

For example, average conjugation defects can define

\[
\mathcal L_X(T)
=
\sum_{U\in\mathcal G_X}[U,T]^*[U,T].
\]

Its kernel is the commutant. Completion stability requires a cutoff-independent lower bound on \(\mathcal L_X\) transverse to the authorized center.

This is the operator-algebraic analogue of a uniform Pfaffian or smallest-singular-value bound.

### Authority boundary

Minimal abstract commutant proves algebraic separation or control span. It does not prove that every generator is physically executable, fault-tolerant, or source-closed.

In the qutrit packet the pair-channel Hamiltonian is algebraically sufficient but its microscopic corridor remains uncompiled. The operator volume is therefore mathematically full conditional on a constructor whose physical lift is still open.

## Disposition

Minimal commutant is the robust noncommutative successor to Pfaffian volume. It reproduces the toric criterion when commutators centralize to an alternating form, and it correctly diagnoses the electric D(S3) qutrit: braid-only volume is incomplete, while one source-typed pair-channel bridge fills M3 and collapses the commutant to scalars.

This gives the programme a coefficient-lens-independent question: after quotienting only the authorized superselection center, which operators still commute with every admitted constructor? Those residual operators are the shadows of missing relational dimensions.
