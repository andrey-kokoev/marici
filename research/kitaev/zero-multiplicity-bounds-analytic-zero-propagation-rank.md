# Zero multiplicity bounds analytic zero-propagation rank

## Result

A scalar holomorphic zero of multiplicity \(m\) directly forces only the first \(m\) jet coordinates to vanish. Functional-equation or symmetry images with nonvanishing multipliers transport those constraints but do not generically increase their independent rank.

Consequently, a simple zero supplies rank one. It cannot by itself force a faithful rank-two or higher feature packet to vanish.

## Jet budget

Let \(f\) be holomorphic near \(s_0\). A zero of exact order \(m\) means

\[
f^{(k)}(s_0)=0
\]

for \(0\le k<m\), while

\[
f^{(m)}(s_0)\ne0.
\]

The forced jet packet therefore has exactly \(m\) scalar coordinates before additional source identities are imposed.

A simple zero forces only

\[
f(s_0)=0.
\]

It does not force \(f'(s_0)=0\).

## Symmetry transport

Suppose a local symmetry \(g\) obeys

\[
f(g(s))=\chi_g(s)f(s)
\]

with \(g'(s_0)\ne0\) and \(\chi_g(s_0)\ne0\). Then \(g(s_0)\) is a zero of the same multiplicity.

Differentiating the covariance law produces an invertible triangular relation between the \(m\)-jets at \(s_0\) and \(g(s_0)\). Hence the orbit copies the same multiplicity data in transported frames. It does not generically multiply the intrinsic zero-propagation rank by the orbit size.

For a simple zero, all symmetry-related value equations remain generated by one local vanishing coordinate once the covariance arrows are included.

## Functional-equation consequence

A reciprocal pair \(s_0\) and \(1-s_0\), or a larger orbit including conjugates, may contain several distinct points. But if their scalar values are tied by nonzero functional-equation multipliers, the vanishing equations are transported copies of one source equation.

Counting four points in a zero orbit therefore does not yield four independent constraints on the lifted source fiber.

## Observability comparison

Let a finite-dimensional state satisfy

\[
\partial_q\Psi=A\Psi,
\qquad
y=L\Psi.
\]

The output jets obey

\[
y^{(k)}=LA^k\Psi.
\]

State observability may require the stacked rows

\[
L,\;LA,\;\ldots,\;LA^{n-1}
\]

to have rank \(n\). But a simple zero of \(y\) at one point forces only the first row to vanish. It does not force the observability tower to vanish.

Thus observability answers whether a complete output trajectory determines a state. It does not imply that one instantaneous output zero determines the state.

## Rank obstruction for positive features

Suppose a proposed contradiction uses a feature map

\[
J_s:V_s\longrightarrow\mathbb C^r
\]

and positive energy

\[
E_s(v)=\lVert J_sv\rVert^2.
\]

If a scalar zero of multiplicity \(m\) is claimed to force \(J_s\Psi_s=0\), the derivation must supply at least the effective rank of \(J_s\) in independent forced equations. When \(m<\operatorname{rank}J_s\), multiplicity alone is insufficient.

In particular, a simple zero cannot directly annihilate two independent Clark quadratures, a tail-plus-seam pair, or primitive and square channels.

## Ways additional rank can arise

Extra rank must come from an independently derived law, not merely from relabelling the zero orbit. Candidates include:

- a boundary condition that vanishes at every scalar zero;
- an independent seam equation;
- a source identity coupling value and derivative;
- a conserved current whose value is fixed separately;
- a nonlinear Green identity whose defect terms vanish for source reasons;
- a proven restriction of the admissible source fiber to dimension at most \(m\).

Each candidate must be audited for source authority and algebraic independence from the scalar functional equation.

## Hostile fixture

Take

\[
f(s)=s-s_0.
\]

Let the lifted feature vector be

\[
J(s)=(f(s),1).
\]

At \(s_0\), the scalar vanishes but

\[
\lVert J(s_0)\rVert^2=1.
\]

Adding any number of symmetry-labelled copies \(\gamma_gf(s)\) preserves the independent constant feature. No scalar orbit stacking forces the full energy to vanish.

## Diagnostic

For every proposed RH contradiction of the form scalar zero implies positive energy zero, report:

1. assumed zero multiplicity;
2. forced scalar jet rank;
3. symmetry-orbit rank after covariance relations;
4. independently sourced boundary or current rows;
5. rank of the target positive feature map;
6. the first unsupported rank increment.

## Verdict

The zero-propagation budget is controlled by multiplicity plus genuinely independent source laws, not by the number of reciprocal sheets or symmetry-related points. Any architecture intended to cover arbitrary nontrivial zeros must work for simple zeros unless it independently proves higher multiplicity. Therefore a rank-two or larger positive network requires an additional source-derived vanishing mechanism beyond scalar covariance.
