# Analytic composition cannot raise the conormal rank of one regular zero

## Result

The linear scalar-to-network factorization obstruction has a local analytic extension. Near a regular scalar-zero hypersurface, every analytic network output universally forced to vanish by that scalar zero is locally divisible by the scalar equation. Consequently, arbitrarily elaborate analytic composition does not create an additional transverse vanishing direction.

## Regular zero setup

Let \(M\) be a complex manifold of admissible source parameters or states and let

\[
f:M\longrightarrow\mathbb C
\]

be holomorphic. Assume \(p\in M\) is a regular zero:

\[
f(p)=0,
\qquad
df_p\ne0.
\]

Then a local coordinate system exists in which

\[
f=z_1.
\]

The zero locus is the smooth hypersurface \(z_1=0\).

## Local analytic factorization theorem

Let \(h:M\to W\) be a holomorphic vector-valued network output. Suppose on a neighborhood of \(p\),

\[
f(x)=0\Longrightarrow h(x)=0.
\]

Then there is a local holomorphic map \(a:M\to W\) such that

\[
h=af.
\]

In coordinates, expand each component of \(h\) as a power series in \(z_1\). Vanishing at \(z_1=0\) removes the constant term, so every component is divisible by \(z_1=f\).

At the zero,

\[
dh_p=a(p)\,df_p.
\]

Thus all component differentials of \(h\) lie in the one-dimensional conormal span generated by \(df_p\).

## Conormal-rank no-go theorem

Let \(h_1,\ldots,h_r\) be any finite family of holomorphic outputs universally forced to vanish on the regular zero locus of \(f\). Then

\[
\operatorname{rank}
\begin{pmatrix}
dh_1(p)\\
\vdots\\
dh_r(p)
\end{pmatrix}
\le1.
\]

The family may contain many labelled outputs, nonlinear composites, sheet images, loop amplitudes, or current expressions. Their first-order vanishing information remains rank one when their vanishing is derived solely from \(f=0\).

## Composition consequence

Serial, parallel, feedback, polynomial, determinant, and analytic functional composition can transform or amplify the scalar equation. If every resulting feature vanishes universally whenever \(f=0\), each feature remains in the local principal ideal generated by \(f\).

The composition tower can create new operational capabilities. It cannot create new codimension from a single regular vanishing law.

## Positive-energy consequence

Suppose

\[
E(x)=\sum_{j=1}^r|h_j(x)|^2
\]

and every \(h_j\) is forced to vanish by \(f=0\). Locally,

\[
E(x)=|f(x)|^2\sum_{j=1}^r|a_j(x)|^2.
\]

Such an energy may be useful for conditioning estimates, but its vanishing is algebraically inherited from the scalar. It does not independently explain why \(f\) cannot vanish.

To obtain a contradiction, one still needs an independent lower bound incompatible with \(f=0\), derived from source normalization or another transverse law.

## Noncommutative qualification

Ordered operator products can create commutators and holonomy absent from scalar multiplication. But if a scalar zero is claimed to force an operator-valued analytic output \(H\) to vanish universally, every matrix coefficient of \(H\) is locally divisible by \(f\). Noncommutativity changes the available outputs, not the codimension supplied by the scalar premise.

The escape is for the network output not to be universally forced by \(f=0\), but instead to satisfy an independent source identity coupling several objects.

## Singular and restricted cases

The theorem does not settle:

- singular zero loci where \(df_p=0\);
- nonreduced multiplicity structure;
- implications valid only on one canonical source trajectory rather than a neighborhood;
- discontinuous or nonanalytic constructors;
- additional boundary equations cutting the admissible space transversely.

These are exactly the places where extra zero-propagation rank could enter. Each requires separate source authority.

## RH hostile interpretation

If a hypothetical simple off-seam zero is regular in the relevant source parameter space, then no finite analytic network packet universally derived only from scalar vanishing can supply more than one transverse constraint. Reciprocal sheets and composition may rearrange that constraint, but they do not raise its conormal rank.

An RH-bearing network therefore needs at least one independent transverse source law: seam, boundary, current, normalization, or a nonlinear identity whose non-scalar term is fixed without using the desired conclusion.

## Verdict

The fourth composition tower can generate new operations and observables, but it cannot manufacture independent vanishing information from one regular scalar equation. The critical resource is not compositional complexity alone; it is source-authorized transverse incidence.
