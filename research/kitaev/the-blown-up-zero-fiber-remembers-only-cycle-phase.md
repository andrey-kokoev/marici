# The Blown-Up Zero Fiber Remembers Only Cycle Phase

Let a nominal incidence graph (G=(V,E)) carry edgewise polar coordinates

\[
z_e=r_eu_e,
\qquad
r_e\in[0,\infty),
\quad
u_e\in U(1).
\]

The edgewise oriented blow-up retains a phase (u_e) even when (r_e=0).
These edge phases are not all intrinsic. Channel frames

\[
g=(g_v)_{v\in V}\in U(1)^V
\]

act by

\[
u_{uv}\longmapsto g_u u_{uv}\overline{g_v}.
\]

One constant phase on each connected component acts trivially. Hence the
effective gauge dimension is \(|V|-c\), where (c) is the number of connected
components.

The quotient of the full angular boundary is

\[
U(1)^E/\delta U(1)^V
\simeq
H^1(G;U(1))
\simeq
U(1)^{\beta_1},
\qquad
\beta_1=|E|-|V|+c.
\]

Thus the blow-up of an all-zero analytic coupling packet remembers one phase
per independent nominal cycle, not one phase per vanished edge.

## Triangle gauge reduction

For oriented edges (12,23,13), the invariant boundary holonomy is

\[
h=u_{12}u_{23}\overline{u_{13}}.
\]

Two vertex-frame choices make (u_{12}=u_{23}=1). The remaining edge phase is
then determined by (h). Therefore the zero fiber of a blown-up triangle,
after channel gauge, is one copy of (U(1)).

For a tree,

\[
\beta_1=0,
\]

and every retained zero-edge phase is removable by channel gauge. A tree has
no intrinsic phase-provenance port even in the oriented blow-up.

For (K_4), the all-zero blown-up fiber has dimension

\[
6-4+1=3.
\]

This equals the generic cycle rank, but its meaning differs: at positive
radii it is coherent coupling holonomy; at zero radii it is boundary
provenance retained by the finer source topology.

## Blow-down versus support quotient

Ordinary analytic blow-down sends every boundary point to the same zero
coupling packet and erases the entire (H^1(G;U(1))) fiber. By contrast, the
usual quotient formed only from the nonzero support graph has cycle rank

\[
\beta_1(\operatorname{supp}r),
\]

which drops as edges vanish.

These are two different completions:

- support quotient: phases on vanished edges cease to exist;
- blown-up quotient: their gauge-invariant cycle combinations remain as
  source-boundary memory.

The second is strictly richer and cannot be inferred from the first after
completion.

## Real coefficient lens

If a source-derived real structure restricts edge phases to (C_2), the
boundary quotient becomes

\[
H^1(G;C_2)\simeq C_2^{\beta_1}.
\]

This is the exact graph-level form of the torsor-bit theorem. One bit per
nominal independent cycle survives the real blown-up zero fiber; tree-edge
signs remain gauge.

## Authority and decoder boundary

The theorem counts intrinsic boundary phase only after both a nominal graph
and an edgewise phase-preserving topology have been authorized. It does not
derive either structure for theta/Fock completion. Nor does a nontrivial zero
fiber restore nonzero cycle amplitudes or bounded inverses.

Grothendieck's scalar one-in/one-out defect-port ceiling remains an independent
implementation constraint: representing (U(1)^{\beta_1}) boundary memory in
that model requires a matrix lift or typed internal state.

## Falsifiers

- Counting one intrinsic phase for every zero edge before quotienting channel
  frames.
- Retaining a tree phase as gauge-invariant provenance.
- Identifying the blown-up zero fiber with the ordinary support quotient.
- Treating boundary holonomy as a nonzero Wilson amplitude.
- Reducing (U(1)) to (C_2) without a real coefficient structure.
- Inferring a phase-preserving theta topology from the abstract graph count.
