# The complete scalar context tower reconstructs a minimal ordered realization

## Bounded question

Can a scalar readout ever recover ordered constructor dynamics, and if so, what
does it determine and what physical structure remains unresolved?

## Frozen scalar process

Let \(E\) be a finite alphabet of admitted constructors and let \(E^*\) be the
free word monoid. Suppose every executable word \(w\) has a scalar response

\[
f(w)\in\mathbb F.
\]

One scalar value \(f(w)\) is a severe quotient of the history. The complete
function

\[
f:E^*\longrightarrow\mathbb F
\]

is a different object: it records the scalar response under every admitted
constructor word.

## Hankel matrix

Index rows and columns by words and define

\[
H_f(u,v)=f(uv).
\]

The row indexed by \(u\) is the future-response function after prefix \(u\):

\[
v\longmapsto f(uv).
\]

Two prefixes are predictively equivalent exactly when their Hankel rows agree.
Thus the row space is the linear space of distinct scalar futures exposed by
all right contexts.

## Finite linear realization

A dimension-\(d\) linear realization consists of a row vector \(\alpha\), a
column vector \(\beta\), and matrices \(M_e\) such that

\[
f(e_n\cdots e_1)
=
\alpha M_{e_n}\cdots M_{e_1}\beta.
\]

For words \(u,v\), write the corresponding products as \(M_u,M_v\). Then

\[
H_f(u,v)=\alpha M_uM_v\beta.
\]

Every finite Hankel submatrix factors through the \(d\)-dimensional state
space. Therefore

\[
\operatorname{rank}H_f\leq d.
\]

The scalar context tower places an exact lower bound on every ordered linear
realization.

## Hankel realization theorem

If

\[
\operatorname{rank}H_f=r<\infty,
\]

then \(f\) has an \(r\)-dimensional linear realization. Moreover, every minimal
linear realization has dimension \(r\), and any two minimal realizations are
related by an invertible similarity transformation.

One construction uses the span of left residuals

\[
f_u(v)=f(uv).
\]

Appending a constructor defines the transition on residuals. Evaluation at the
empty word defines the terminal functional, and the residual of the empty word
defines the initial state.

The construction is ordered: the transition matrices need not commute. The
noncommutative information is stored in how scalar futures change under all
contexts, not in any one output value.

## Minimality conditions

For a proposed realization, define the reachable span

\[
\mathcal R
=
\operatorname{span}\{M_w\beta:w\in E^*\}
\]

and the unobservable subspace

\[
\mathcal N
=
\bigcap_{w\in E^*}\ker(\alpha M_w).
\]

The realization is minimal exactly when

\[
\mathcal R=\mathbb F^d
\]

and

\[
\mathcal N=\{0\}.
\]

Reachability says every internal direction is generated by some constructor
history. Observability says every internal direction affects some future scalar
context. Removing either inaccessible or invisible directions produces a
smaller realization with the same complete scalar process.

## Similarity is the exact abstract gauge

If \(T\) is invertible, define

\[
M'_e=T^{-1}M_eT,
\qquad
\alpha'=\alpha T,
\qquad
\beta'=T^{-1}\beta.
\]

Then every word response is unchanged:

\[
\alpha'M'_w\beta'=\alpha M_w\beta.
\]

For minimal realizations, this similarity is the only abstract linear gauge.
The complete scalar context tower therefore determines the minimal predictive
linear system up to change of internal coordinates.

It does not determine a preferred basis, tensor factorization, spatial
locality, energy, positivity cone, unitary structure, physical port names, or
implementation cost. Those structures may reduce similarity gauge, but only
when included in the frozen source packet.

## Order can appear in scalar responses

Use

\[
M_a=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1\\
0&1
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
M_b=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0\\
1&1
\end{pmatrix},
\]

with

\[
\alpha=(1,0),
\qquad
\beta=(1,0)^{\mathsf T}.
\]

Then

\[
\alpha M_aM_b\beta=2,
\qquad
\alpha M_bM_a\beta=1.
\]

A scalar readout distinguishes the two orders because the experimenter can
execute both constructor words. The earlier loss-of-order theorem applied to an
additive or determinant character, not to an arbitrary context-indexed scalar
process.

## Two-sided contexts separate internal histories

Let \(w,w'\) be two internal histories. The complete scalar context family tests

\[
f(uwv)
\]

against

\[
f(uw'v)
\]

for all admitted prefixes \(u\) and suffixes \(v\).

If every such value agrees, then \(w\) and \(w'\) are indistinguishable in the
minimal predictive realization. If some context differs, the scalar interface
can expose their order-sensitive distinction.

This is precisely constructor-context closure. Scalar tomography becomes
powerful by varying the constructors around the hidden process, not by assigning
more meaning to one terminal number.

## Finite observations versus the complete tower

Any bounded experiment sees only a finite Hankel submatrix. Its rank is a lower
bound on the full realization dimension. Apparent rank stabilization at finite
word length is not by itself proof that no longer context adds a new direction.

A finite certificate needs an independent bound on realization dimension or a
closure theorem showing that the observed row and column spaces are invariant
under every constructor shift. Without that bound, an arbitrarily long finite
prefix can agree with a higher-rank process that diverges later.

This is the context analogue of finite-stage invertibility without a uniform
completion theorem.

## Process equality versus physical realization

Complete equality of \(f(w)\) for all words establishes equality of the minimal
predictive linear realizations up to similarity. It does not establish identity
of nonminimal implementations.

An implementation may contain hidden modes that are unreachable, unobservable,
or both. It may also carry named internal ports whose effects are excluded from
the scalar experiment family. Reopening such a port changes the process packet
and may distinguish implementations previously equivalent.

The correct hierarchy is:

1. one scalar output value;
2. a finite scalar context packet;
3. the complete scalar word process;
4. a minimal predictive realization up to similarity;
5. a source-labelled realization with named internal structure;
6. a physical implementation.

Only the third and fourth levels are equivalent under the finite-rank theorem.

## Control-theory translation

For a switched linear system, constructor letters select transition matrices.
The scalar Markov parameters over all switching words form the Hankel matrix.
Reachability and observability remove internal directions that never participate
in an input-output experiment.

Minimal realization explains everything the complete scalar process predicts.
It remains an input-output model, not a unique physical plant. Similarity
preserves predictions while changing state coordinates; additional physical
structure is needed to select one realization frame.

## Software-architecture translation

A single API response does not determine the service state machine. A complete
black-box suite that executes every command word and records every response can,
under a finite-state linear model, reconstruct the minimal predictive machine
up to internal renaming or similarity.

Private caches, redundant state, provenance, deployment topology, and fault
domains may remain invisible. The recovered machine is the smallest behavioral
contract, not the source code or infrastructure realization.

## Toric-code instance

One syndrome snapshot does not determine the logical operator algebra. A
context family that composes candidate Pauli strings with star, plaquette, and
loop probes can distinguish more histories.

The full Pauli response table can reconstruct the represented logical algebra
up to the admitted representation gauge. It still does not identify a physical
gate sequence, locality layout, or fault-tolerant implementation.

## Theta/Tate hostile instance

One completed scalar Tate section cannot determine the tail-seam operator pair.
A complete source-authorized scalar context tower could, in principle,
determine a minimal ordered linear realization if its Hankel rank is finite and
the required contexts survive completion.

This changes the research question. The missing lift need not be guessed from
one scalar section. One may instead ask whether the theta/Tate constructor
monoid supplies enough left and right contexts to make the completed Hankel
process finite-rank, faithful, and completion-stable.

Even success would recover the minimal predictive operator system only up to
similarity. Source-native seam, Fock, topology, and unit structure must still
select the physically meaningful frame.

## Critics

### The complete word tower is physically impossible

Often yes. Then the theorem is a boundary result: it states what additional
contextual evidence would be sufficient and why a finite available packet falls
short.

### Similarity may destroy quantum positivity or unitarity

Correct. Plain linear realization forgets those structures. A quantum or
positive realization theorem must restrict the gauge and prove compatible
structure from the source process.

### Finite rank may fail at completion

Correct. Then no finite-dimensional linear realization exists for the complete
process, though finite cutoffs may each have one. Uniform rank and continuity
are separate completion gates.

### One-sided contexts should suffice

They suffice only under additional reachability or observability assumptions.
Two-sided contexts expose how an internal history interacts with both preparation
and future testing.

### Minimal realization is the true mechanism

No. It is the smallest predictive model in the declared category. Physical
mechanism includes source-labelled structure that input-output equivalence may
quotient away.

## Exact falsifiers

- One terminal scalar used to infer an ordered transition realization.
- A finite Hankel rank plateau used as a global rank theorem without a dimension
  bound or shift-closure certificate.
- A realization called minimal despite an unreachable or unobservable
  direction.
- Similarity-equivalent minimal models presented as physically identical when
  named structure is not transported.
- A complete scalar process used to infer a unique nonminimal implementation.
- Contexts added mathematically although no source constructor executes them.
- Finite-cutoff realizations used to infer a completion-stable finite-rank
  process without a uniform theorem.

## Deutschian explanation

A lone scalar hides the mechanism because many internal histories can end with
the same value. Context changes that. By placing every available constructor
before and after the hidden history, the experiment asks how that history
changes all possible futures.

The collection of those scalar futures is an abstract state. When their span is
finite, constructor action on that span reconstructs the minimal ordered
dynamics. What remains hidden is not predictive behavior but the choice of
physical coordinates and implementation behind it.

## Claim boundary

This packet states the finite-rank Hankel realization theorem and its
reachability-observability boundary. It does not prove finite rank, source
authority, positivity, unitarity, or completion stability for any infinite
sector.
