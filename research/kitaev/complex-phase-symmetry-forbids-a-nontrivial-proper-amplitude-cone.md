# Complex Phase Symmetry Forbids a Nontrivial Proper Amplitude Cone

## Phase-cone obstruction

Let (V) be a nonzero complex vector space, regarded as real, and let
(C\subset V) be a convex cone. If (C) is invariant under the full scalar
phase action, then in particular it is invariant under multiplication by
(-1). Hence

\[
x\in C\Longrightarrow -x\in C.
\]

Therefore (C\cap(-C)=C). If (C) is pointed, this forces (C=\{0\}).
There is no nontrivial proper amplitude cone compatible with the full
(U(1)) phase symmetry.

This is not a quantitative completion problem. Positivity on complex
amplitudes first requires a source-derived reduction of symmetry: a real form,
a phase frame, a conjugation, or an ordered sector. Choosing such a frame from
the desired sign of the completed scalar readout is circular.

## The density-operator exit

The canonical phase-invariant replacement is the quadratic lift

\[
q(\psi)=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|.
\]

It lands in the proper cone of positive semidefinite Hermitian operators and
satisfies

\[
q(e^{i\theta}\psi)=q(\psi).
\]

This removes the phase obstruction, but changes the type of the state space.
The lift is not additive:

\[
q(\psi+\phi)
=q(\psi)+q(\phi)
+|\psi\rangle\langle\phi|
+|\phi\rangle\langle\psi|.
\]

The cross terms are precisely the interference data. A diagonal-only density
packet therefore does not descend from the linear amplitude theory without an
explicit polarization or two-variable kernel constructor.

The lift is also phase-blind and hence not faithful on amplitude vectors. It
is faithful only on projective rays, with the zero vector retained as a
separate stratum.

## Scalar multiplier theorem

For a nonzero reference amplitude (\psi) and scalar multiplier (m(s)),

\[
q(m(s)\psi)=|m(s)|^2q(\psi).
\]

Thus the density output vanishes exactly when (m(s)=0). If this operator is
constructed only after scalar Tate completion, positivity of its trace merely
squares the divisor and remains circular. It becomes explanatory only if an
operator-valued two-variable kernel is independently derived from source atoms
before the scalar multiplier is formed.

## Minimal phase-sensitive fixture

For orthogonal (e_1,e_2), the states

\[
\psi_+=e_1+e_2,
\qquad
\psi_-=e_1-e_2
\]

have identical coordinate intensities but opposite off-diagonal coherences:

\[
q(\psi_+)-q(\psi_-)
=2\bigl(|e_1\rangle\langle e_2|
+|e_2\rangle\langle e_1|\bigr).
\]

Any purported positive lift retaining only diagonal intensities identifies
these states. The minimal repair is not another scalar positivity claim but a
typed sesquilinear kernel or a polarization-complete observation family.

## Consequences for the theta/Tate programme

Exactly two noncircular routes remain.

1. Derive a canonical real form or phase frame from source units and prove
   Fourier–Tate transport preserves it through completion.
2. Derive a phase-invariant operator kernel before scalar completion, retain
   its mixed coherences, and prove its positive correspondence has uniform
   gain and the required separation property.

The first route risks the previously isolated torsor-frame compiler problem.
The second risks replacing an amplitude identity by a quadratic shadow and
losing interference. Neither route follows from scalar positivity.

## Falsifiers

- A claimed nonzero proper amplitude cone is invariant under multiplication by
  (-1).
- The real or phase frame is chosen from the sign of the desired scalar answer.
- A density lift is called linear or amplitude-faithful.
- Only diagonal intensities are retained while mixed coherence is discarded.
- The kernel (q(m\psi)) is constructed downstream from the scalar divisor.
- Projective-ray faithfulness is reported as vector faithfulness.

## Claim boundary

This finite algebraic theorem does not construct a theta/Tate real form,
source kernel, Fourier–Tate phase transport, or completion estimate. It does
not prove RH.
