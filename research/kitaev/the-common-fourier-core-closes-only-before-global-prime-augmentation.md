# The common Fourier core closes only before global prime augmentation

## Question

Can the abstract Fourier-saturated multigraph rigging be instantiated from
existing theta/Tate source data?  Yes, but only after correcting the codomain
of the primitive and square channels: they are vector-valued boundary
incidences before they are scalar currents.

## Source-native common core

At the archimedean place, use the Fourier-invariant Beurling-type
Gelfand--Shilov core `G_infinity`: functions and Fourier transforms decay
faster than every exponential together with all polynomially weighted
derivatives.  It is dense in `L2`, stable under Fourier transform, reflection,
and every complex character multiplier.

At the finite places, use the algebraic restricted Schwartz--Bruhat core: all
but finitely many local factors are the declared vacuum.  Equivalently in the
bilateral valuation presentation, use finite-excitation vectors in the local
chains before restricted-product completion.

Their algebraic restricted tensor product gives a core `D_0` that is stable
under the source Fourier operation, reciprocal reflection, finite Euler
cutoff inclusions, and every finite local incidence map.

This is the concrete core required by the preceding multigraph theorem.

## Correct codomains of the three prime grades

The vacuum cocycle determines three different global targets.

- The primitive grade `k=1` produces a sequence whose squared norm contains
  `sum_p p^-1`; it belongs only to a distributional or exponentially weighted
  boundary sequence space `Y_P`.
- The square grade `k=2` produces a genuine Hilbert sequence in `Y_Q`, but its
  scalar amplitudes are not absolutely summable.
- The connected grades `k>=3` land in an absolutely summable determinant
  target `Y_conn` in the established seam window.

Thus the source maps are incidence maps

\[
I_P:D_0\longrightarrow Y_P,
\qquad
I_Q:D_0\longrightarrow Y_Q,
\qquad
I_{conn}:D_0\longrightarrow Y_{conn}.
\]

They are not three ordinary scalar rows.

## Why scalar augmentation must be delayed

The local augmentation `ell_p(e_j)=1` recovers each scalar prime-power term.
Globally, however, summing these augmentations across all primes is precisely
where the regularity hierarchy becomes singular:

- primitive augmentation is not a continuous Hilbert functional;
- square augmentation is not an absolutely convergent determinant trace;
- only the connected tail has an ordinary scalar sum in its source window.

Therefore the common graph topology must be generated by `I_P`, `I_Q`, and
`I_conn` with their separate codomains.  Replacing them by completed scalar
prime sums imports the missing boundary exponentiation and can import the
zeta divisor.

This corrects the phrase “all five rows are defined on one core.”  The seam,
endpoint, and archimedean traces may be scalar or finite-dimensional boundary
rows; the two exceptional arithmetic channels are typed vector coordinates.

## Fourier-saturated instantiation

Generate the common topology on `D_0` from:

- the Gelfand--Shilov and finite Schwartz--Bruhat source seminorms;
- `I_P F^k` into `Y_P`;
- `I_Q F^k` into `Y_Q`;
- `I_conn F^k` into `Y_conn`;
- seam, endpoint, and archimedean traces after `F^k`;
- `k=0,1,2,3`.

Fourier transform cyclically permutes this finite orbit of typed seminorm
families.  Reciprocal reflection acts between the two sector copies.  The
completion is the closure of one diagonal multigraph in the product of all
targets.

The construction preserves the asymmetric prime topologies rather than
forcing `P` and `Q` into a common scalar norm.

## What remains genuinely missing

The pre-scalar common carrier now has an explicit candidate.  The missing map
is the boundary exponentiation or augmentation correspondence

\[
\operatorname{Aug}_{\partial}:
Y_P\times Y_Q\times Y_{conn}\times Y_\infty
\rightharpoonup
\operatorname{Fr}(L_+).
\]

It must produce a framed determinant-line unit while retaining primitive and
square boundary data.  Its domain, reciprocal covariance, cutoff naturality,
and odd exponential frame remain open.

This is consistent with the paired-tower architecture:

```text
common pre-scalar incidence core
  -> direct and reciprocal typed sector completions
  -> fifth-level boundary augmentation and sewing.
```

No sixth level is required to state the missing map.

## Relation to the rigged transpose

For fixed source forcing `f` in the test space, the canonical transpose

\[
B_f^\times(\lambda)=\lambda(f)
\]

is defined on every boundary grade embedded continuously in the source dual.
This gives one common reverse variance operation without choosing a global
Hilbert adjoint.  Aspect's sign calculation still prevents interpreting all
sector realizations through one positive metric.

The transpose is therefore compatible with the multigraph carrier, but its
dynamic executability and completion-stable feedback interpretation remain
separate.

## Falsifiers

- Treating the primitive vacuum cocycle as an `l2` vector.
- Treating the square amplitudes as an `l1` determinant coefficient.
- Applying global prime augmentation before the typed sequence completion.
- Defining `Y_P` by analytic continuation of the scalar zeta current.
- A Fourier image leaving the declared common core or one typed target.
- Cutoff maps that commute only after scalar prime summation.
- A boundary exponentiation fitted from the completed section.

## Verdict

The common Fourier-invariant core is not the missing object.  It already
exists at the test-space and vector-incidence level.  The obstruction begins
when the primitive and square boundary coordinates are exponentiated or
augmented into one scalar determinant frame.

The next exact audit should therefore freeze `Y_P` and `Y_Q`, verify their
four-step Fourier orbit and cutoff naturality, and only then classify the
domain of `Aug_partial`.  Asking for one common scalar current space would
erase the very regularity distinction the source has derived.
