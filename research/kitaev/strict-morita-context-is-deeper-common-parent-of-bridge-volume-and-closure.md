# Strict Morita context is the deeper common parent of bridge, volume, and closure

Owner: marici.Kitaev

## Question

Can the three clauses of dualizable task completeness be derived as aspects of
one standard algebraic structure, rather than retained as independent axioms?

## Claim boundary

Yes, provisionally: the finite algebraic common parent is a **strict Morita
context**, and its completion-stable successor is a quantitatively full
imprimitivity-type bimodule.

Let \(A\) be the authorized source-constructor algebra and \(B\) the
task-localized behavior or test algebra. Let

\[
{}_A M_B,
\qquad
{}_B N_A
\]

be the forward interface and its backward requirement interface. A Morita
context supplies two typed pairings

\[
\mu:M\otimes_BN\to A,
\qquad
\nu:N\otimes_AM\to B,
\]

subject to the compatibility identities

\[
\mu(m\otimes n)m'=m\nu(n\otimes m'),
\]

\[
\nu(n\otimes m)n'=n\mu(m\otimes n').
\]

These are not decorative associativity equations. They say that performing a
source-side comparison and then acting gives the same interface state as
acting through the behavior-side comparison. Thus the context square already
contains forward transport, backward requirements, and their evaluation mate.

### Why the bridge is forced

The bimodules \(M\) and \(N\) are the bridge. The maps \(\mu\) and \(\nu\)
reconstruct source and behavior endomorphisms from paired interface passages.
Changing either pairing while fixing the actions generally violates a Morita
identity. The bridge is therefore constrained by composition on both sides,
not selected from a scalar readout.

In categorical language, this is a dual pair of 1-morphisms in the bicategory
of algebras and bimodules. The cup and cap maps are the evaluations; the Morita
identities are their snake or triangle laws.

### Why relational volume is fullness

The context is strict when both pairings are onto:

\[
\mu(M\otimes_BN)=A,
\qquad
\nu(N\otimes_AM)=B.
\]

Surjectivity says every authorized action is assembled from interface
passages. Together with the usual faithful finite-projective hypotheses, it
makes \(M\) a progenerator and yields

\[
A\simeq\operatorname{End}_B(M),
\qquad
B^{\mathrm{op}}\simeq\operatorname{End}_A(M).
\]

This is the double-centralizer form of task completeness. Each side is the
full commutant of the other. The permitted center is shared under Morita
equivalence. Hence minimal authorized commutant is not an independent volume
postulate: it is the operator-algebraic shadow of full pairing.

When a pairing is nondegenerate but not full, it can distinguish every vector
inside its image while still failing to generate an entire constructor block.
That is precisely the difference between pointwise faithfulness and relational
completeness.

### Why self-closure is the Morita identity

The two Morita identities compare the first nontrivial alternate composites.
Once they hold, longer zigzags reduce by associativity and the triangle laws.
No new independent coherencer is required in the strict bicategory: the tower
self-closes because every longer comparison is generated by the same cups,
caps, and module actions.

If the identities hold only up to an invertible 2-cell, the next rung is an
associator or cocycle. If that cell itself is coherent, one obtains a weak or
higher Morita context. If it is not, the residue is genuine path dependence.
This locates exactly when an additional tower rung is warranted.

### What the earlier three-coordinate signature becomes

The earlier obstruction coordinates now have a common typing:

- evaluation drift is failure of a Morita compatibility identity;
- excess commutant is failure of fullness or faithfulness;
- mate residue is failure of coherence after base change.

They remain logically separable as defects, but they are defects of one
constructor object rather than unrelated tests.

### Finite sector fit

For the toric logical sector, the raw homology--cohomology pairing is not yet
an ordinary Morita equivalence. Passing through the finite Heisenberg or Pauli
algebra turns the nondegenerate symplectic pairing into an irreducible module.
The logical Pauli algebra and its representation then obey the expected
double-centralizer statement. Local syndrome alone corresponds to a
non-full interface: it omits the two global loop directions.

For the pure-electric \(D(S_3)\) qutrit, braid constructors generate
\(\mathbb C\oplus M_2\). Their commutant \(\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb C\) records
the failure of fullness across the \(1\oplus2\) split. An unequal pair-channel
supplies the missing off-diagonal incidence; the generated algebra becomes
\(M_3\), and the commutant becomes scalar. This is exactly the finite passage
from a non-full context to a full one.

### Completion changes the theorem

Algebraic Morita equivalence is insufficient in an infinite or completed
system. The pairings can be onto at every finite cutoff while their frame
bounds tend to zero. The appropriate quantitative condition is a uniform
fullness estimate. In Hilbert-module language, finite interface elements must
generate approximate identities with cutoff-independent control; equivalently,
the relevant frame or Gram operator must have a uniform positive lower bound
on the task-localized module.

Thus finite strictness and completion-stable strictness must not be conflated.
The topological analogue is closer to a strong Morita equivalence or
imprimitivity bimodule than to a bare algebraic bimodule.

## Disposition

Replace the loose common-parent conjecture by a sharper programme:

1. identify \(A\), \(B\), \(M\), and \(N\) from authorized source data;
2. derive both typed pairings rather than fitting them from scalar shadows;
3. test the two Morita identities;
4. test fullness, faithfulness, and the double-centralizer equalities;
5. only then test uniform fullness under completion and physical execution.

The decisive hostile models are now canonical:

- a nondegenerate but non-surjective pairing, giving faithfulness without
  relational completeness;
- a surjective scalar shadow whose typed pairing violates a Morita identity;
- a strict finite context whose smallest frame bound tends to zero;
- a full algebraic context whose physically executable sub-bimodule is not
  full.

The most informative next calculation is the \(D(S_3)\) qutrit Morita defect:
write the braid-generated \(1\oplus2\) module and the pair-channel augmentation
as explicit bimodule pairings, then identify the exact image ideal missing
before augmentation. That calculation would turn “the commutant shrinks” into
a constructor explanation of which relational passage was absent.
