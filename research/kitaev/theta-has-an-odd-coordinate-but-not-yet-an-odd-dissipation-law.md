# Theta has an odd coordinate but not yet an odd dissipation law

## Question

Does the pinned Clark and seam geometry already supply the odd observation
required by character-resolved boundary observability?

## Three notions that must not be merged

An odd object can occur at three progressively stronger levels:

1. an odd coordinate exists in the source carrier;
2. an independently retained output reads that coordinate;
3. the output participates in a passive defect identity with a uniform
   observability margin.

Only the third level excludes a boundary unit mode by the telescoping
observability theorem.

## Pinned Clark parity

Write the two Clark features in their common source frame as

\[
H_a+f=X+iY,
\qquad
H_{-a}+f=X-iY,
\]

where

\[
X=G+f,
\qquad
Y=a\,\partial_zG.
\]

Under the sheet action \(a\mapsto-a\),

\[
X\mapsto X,
\qquad
Y\mapsto-Y.
\]

Thus \(Y\) is a genuine linear odd coordinate. This is stronger than merely
knowing that the two scalar sheet energies differ.

Their quadratic expansions are

\[
\lvert X\pm iY\rvert^2
=
\lvert X\rvert^2+\lvert Y\rvert^2
\pm2\operatorname{Im}(X\overline Y).
\]

The trace is even, while the cross term is odd.

## The Wronskian is diagnostic, not automatically an output row

For

\[
P_a=F+iaF',
\qquad
Q_a=F-iaF',
\]

the oriented Wronskian

\[
W_a=Q_aP_a'-P_aQ_a'
=2ia\bigl(FF''-(F')^2\bigr)
\]

reverses under \(a\mapsto-a\). It is an odd quadratic or bilinear
diagnostic. It is not, merely by changing sign, a linear output \(O\) in a
passive identity

\[
I-L^*L=O^*O.
\]

Indeed \(O^*O\) is even. The odd representation is carried by the signed
output space of \(O\), not by the parity of its positive square.

Therefore the Wronskian can certify orientation or cross coherence while
still failing to measure the norm lost by an odd boundary state.

## Seam parity

The pinned bilateral decomposition has tail cells \(P_y,Q_y\) and seam cell
\(M_y\). Reciprocal exchange swaps \(P_y,Q_y\) and fixes \(M_y\). Hence

\[
P_y+Q_y
\]

and \(M_y\) are even, while

\[
P_y-Q_y
\]

is odd.

The completed scalar plus seam observation retains only the even pair and
has antisymmetric kernel spanned by the tail difference. The seam is an
independent output, but it is not the missing odd output.

The coherency rank-one relation can reconstruct the magnitude of this odd
coordinate from the even data, leaving a sign torsor. That nonlinear
reconstruction still does not provide a linear dissipative row or prove that
the sign is preserved dynamically. The raw tail sign is known to cross away
from the seam, so source parity alone is not a conservation law.

## Why two sheet energies do not settle observability

Retaining both scalar energies recovers the even trace and the odd cross
term. It does not in general reconstruct the full pair \((X,Y)\). Different
feature pairs can have the same norm sum and cross term.

Consequently:

- feature-space faithfulness of \((X,Y)\) is stronger than faithfulness of
  the two scalar energies;
- an odd quadratic statistic is weaker than a signed linear odd port;
- pointwise retention of \(Y\) is weaker than dynamical observability of
  every odd boundary state.

This is the same scalar-versus-constructor distinction in character form.

## Relational sewing interpretation

Aspect's paired-wing instrument supplies the operational analogue. Each wing
has flat local marginals, while a coherent joint even-odd analyzer detects a
parity fringe. The odd observable lives in the sewing relation, not in either
local scalar projection.

This suggests the theta odd output may likewise be a fifth-level relational
port. If so, searching for it among single-sector scalar currents is
mis-typed. The source must derive a coherent cross-sheet incidence, not a
post hoc comparison of two completed numbers.

\[

For every row, establish an intertwining law with the sheet action. Then test:

## Disposition

Theta already contains the missing odd information at the Clark feature
level. The unresolved theorem is no longer existence of an odd coordinate.
It is promotion of that coordinate to a source-authorized boundary loss port
whose repeated returns are uniformly observable.
