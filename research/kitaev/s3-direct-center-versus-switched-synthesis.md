## Bounded question

Is the optimal sector generator

\[
Z=\operatorname{diag}(-8,1,2,3,6,7,20,5)
\]

available as one simultaneous linear combination of the declared source
Hamiltonians?

Reconstruct the exact endpoint representation and take the Hermitian source
surface consisting of the real and imaginary gauge quadratures, `B^t`,
`B^c`, and the `G/H` current `K_c`.  There are 15 listed controls but their
matrix span has rank nine.

Impose the linear equations that every off-diagonal entry within each simple
block vanish and every diagonal entry within a block agree.  The intersection
of the direct primitive span with the block center has sector-signature rank

\[
\boxed{1}.
\]

It contains only the common identity direction.  Adjoining the target `Z`
raises the signature rank to two.  Therefore `Z` is not a direct primitive
linear combination, and no single simultaneous source pulse implements it.

## Why this does not contradict Lie rank 34

The dynamical Lie closure contains a six-dimensional separating center, and
therefore contains `iZ`.  But obtaining its traceless-block cancellations
uses noncentral switched motion.  Lie membership proves the target belongs to
the analytic control algebra; it does not provide pulse order, durations, a
word-length bound, calibration, or an exact effective compiler.

Under the strong continuous-control contract—arbitrary real durations for
every primitive one-parameter subgroup—the connected analytic subgroup
generated by those flows contains `exp(-2 pi i Z/8)`.  This is an existential
finite-product statement, not the named finite compiler required by the
instrument audit.  Under a fixed digital gate alphabet, even that conclusion
does not follow without a separate exact-synthesis theorem.

The source hierarchy is now:

\[
\text{direct central signature rank }1
\;<\;
\text{Lie-accessible central rank }6
\;<\;
\text{full block center rank }8.
\]

Adding `P1 tensor Z` as a primitive conditional Hamiltonian closes both the
switched-word and controlization gaps in three pulses for powers `1,2,4`, but
