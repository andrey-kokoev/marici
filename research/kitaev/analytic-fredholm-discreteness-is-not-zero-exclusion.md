# Analytic Fredholm Discreteness Is Not Zero Exclusion

Let \(\Omega\subset\mathbb C\) be connected and let

\[
K(s):\Omega\to\mathcal K(X)
\]

be a norm-analytic family of compact operators on one fixed Banach space
\(X\). Set \(A(s)=I-K(s)\). The analytic Fredholm theorem gives exactly two
alternatives:

1. \(A(s)\) is noninvertible for every \(s\in\Omega\); or
2. \(A(s)^{-1}\) is meromorphic, and the noninvertible parameters form a
   discrete subset whose principal parts have finite rank.

One verified invertible base point excludes the first alternative. It does
not exclude the discrete exceptional set. Therefore analytic Fredholm theory
can localize completion failures but cannot prove their absence from an
off-seam region.

## Smallest exact model

For

\[
K(s)=\operatorname{diag}(s,1/2),
\qquad
A(s)=\operatorname{diag}(1-s,1/2),
\]

the base point \(s=0\) is invertible, while \(s=1\) is an isolated
noninvertible parameter with kernel generated by \((1,0)\). The inverse has a
simple pole there. This is the desired Fredholm conclusion and simultaneously
a hostile to zero exclusion.

If

\[
K(s)=\operatorname{diag}(1,s/2),
\]

then \(I-K(s)\) is singular everywhere. Analyticity and compactness alone do
not select the meromorphic-inverse branch; an invertible base point is a
mandatory constructor.

## Scalar readout is separately typed

An operator kernel need not appear in a chosen scalar matrix element. In the
first model,

\[
\langle e_2,A(s)e_2\rangle=1/2
\]

never vanishes, even though \(A(1)\) has a kernel. Conversely, an arbitrary
scalar functional may vanish where the operator is invertible. Identification
of the scalar Tate section with an operator obstruction therefore requires a
source-derived theorem, not matching zero sets after the fact.

Compactness alone also does not construct a determinant. The ordinary
Fredholm determinant \(\det(I-K(s))\) requires trace-class control (or an
explicit regularized determinant with its own hypotheses). Analytic Fredholm
theory supplies meromorphic inversion without supplying that scalarization.

## Domain and trivialization gate

The theorem acts on one fixed Banach space with norm-analytic dependence. A
family of rigged domains, graph norms, or source completions depending on
\(s\) must first be transported into a fixed authorized trivialization. A
pointwise family of compact matrices at finite cutoff does not prove compact
norm-analytic dependence after completion.

## Theta/Tate consequence

To use this lane, Grothendieck must prove:

1. one fixed completed source space over the region, or a source-authorized
   analytic trivialization;
2. a norm-analytic compact correlation remainder;
3. one genuinely invertible base point;
4. if a scalar zero equation is desired, trace-class or regularized-
   determinant control and equality with the completed Tate section.

Even all four steps show only that exceptional parameters are zeros of the
typed determinant. An additional symmetry, positivity, or exclusion theorem
is still required to place them on the seam.

## Falsifiers

- Compactness and analyticity are proved only cutoffwise.
- No invertible base point is supplied.
- Varying domains are silently treated as one Banach space.
- Compactness is used to claim an ordinary Fredholm determinant.
- A scalar Tate zero is equated with an operator kernel without a typed
  equality theorem.
- Discreteness of bad parameters is reported as their absence off seam.
