# The native half-density supply is tangent to the neutral boundary

## Question

Does the source-derived additive-versus-multiplicative Haar half-density provide the inward-pointing resource law needed to forbid an off-seam neutral invisible state?

It provides the exact critical exponent and direction of modular gain. By itself it does not provide a strict neutral-boundary barrier.

## Typed relative dilation

Under \(x=e^q\), additive Haar measure becomes

\[
dx=e^q,dq,
\]

whereas multiplicative Haar measure is \(dq\). The unitary additive half-density is multiplication by \(e^{q/2}\).

For prime dilation, the additive and multiplicative representations therefore differ by the modular half-character \(p^{1/2}\), once their source roles are independently fixed and a common diagonal gauge is forbidden.

Combining this comparison with the Mellin character produces the relative amplitude multiplier

\[
m_p(s)=p^{1/2-s}.
\]

Its quadratic multiplier is

\[
\lambda_p(s)=|m_p(s)|^2=p^{1-2\operatorname{Re}s}.
\]

This response is source-local and zero-independent. A multiplicative-only model lacks the half-density factor, so the relative two-sector package makes a discriminating perturbation prediction.

## Discrete supply law

Let \(q_k\) be a quadratic relationship charge after \(k\) prime-dilation steps in one fixed prime orbit. Exact covariance gives

\[
q_{k+1}=\lambda_p(s)q_k.
\]

Hence the one-step supply is

\[
q_{k+1}-q_k
=
\bigl(p^{1-2\operatorname{Re}s}-1\bigr)q_k.
\]

The sign of the coefficient detects the critical seam exactly.

## Tangency no-go theorem

On the neutral boundary \(q_k=0\), the native modular supply vanishes:

\[
q_{k+1}-q_k=0.
\]

Therefore half-density covariance is tangent to the neutral boundary. It preserves a neutral sector rather than repelling it.

In continuous prime-step time, the same law has the form

\[
\dot q=(1-2\operatorname{Re}s)(\log p)q.
\]

Again, \(q=0\) is invariant. No strict inward-pointing condition can be obtained from this homogeneous supply alone.

## Completion asymmetry

Iteration gives

\[
q_k=p^{k(1-2\operatorname{Re}s)}q_0.
\]

For \(\operatorname{Re}s>1/2\), the magnitude tends to zero as \(k\to\infty\). Thus any lower bound based only on this single-sheet charge collapses under completion in that half-sector.

For \(\operatorname{Re}s<1/2\), it grows. The reciprocal sheet reverses these roles. Doubling records the gain imbalance but supplies only an indefinite exchange conservation unless another cross-current controls the neutral boundary.

## What the half-density does explain

It explains, without zero inspection:

- why the critical offset is \(1/2\);
- why direct and reciprocal prime transports are adjoint-matched only on the seam;
- which half-sector contracts and which expands under a prime step;
- why additive and multiplicative Haar roles cannot be collapsed without losing the modular comparison.

These are counterfactual predictions of the relative source package.

## What it does not explain

It does not show that the actual endpoint-reachable source state has nonzero charge. It does not prevent an initially neutral state. It does not distinguish the antisymmetric scalar-zero null generator from another null direction. It does not provide a uniform completion margin.

Therefore the half-density is an orientation coefficient, not the missing barrier current.

## Required transverse supply

A genuine barrier needs an independently source-derived term \(r_k(v)\) such that

\[
q_{k+1}-q_k
=
\bigl(\lambda_p(s)-1\bigr)q_k+r_k(v),
\]

with a strictly oriented sign for \(r_k(v)\) on normalized endpoint-reachable, source-invisible states at the flagged neutral boundary.

The transverse term must come from an incidence channel not proportional to \(q\), such as a seam, primitive--archimedean, square-current, or Clark relationship supply. Its typing and sign must be derived before scalar evaluation.

## Hostile checks

- If the proposed supply is proportional to \(q\), it is tangent and cannot be a strict barrier.
- If the half-density factor disappears under a common admissible frame change, the sectors were not independently typed.
- If the single-sheet lower bound is claimed uniform for \(\operatorname{Re}s>1/2\), prime iteration falsifies it.
- If reciprocal doubling merely exchanges contraction and expansion, it has not supplied transverse neutral-boundary force.
- If the added current is chosen after observing a zero, the repair is circular.

## Verdict

The native modular half-density supplies the correct moving orientation and the first discriminating source perturbation response, but its balance is homogeneous and neutral-boundary tangent. The remaining Deutschian mechanism must be an independently typed transverse resource current whose value does not vanish with the Krein charge itself.
