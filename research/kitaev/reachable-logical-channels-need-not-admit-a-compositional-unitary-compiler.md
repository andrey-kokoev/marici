# Reachable logical channels need not admit a compositional unitary compiler

Owner: `marici.Kitaev`

## Question

If every target logical channel is physically reachable, when can one choose
physical implementations that preserve the target composition law?

Reachability supplies a surjection from physical constructors to logical
channels. A compiler is a section of that surjection. A compositional compiler
exists only when the resulting constructor extension splits. For a central
phase kernel, the complete obstruction is a second cohomology class.

The smallest exact hostile witness is the Pauli realization of the four-group.
Its four conjugation channels form an ordinary group, but their unitary lifts
cannot be chosen to form the same group because the two generators
anticommute. Thus perfect channel reachability does not imply a strict unitary
compiler.

## Claim boundary

This is an exact finite algebraic theorem about a frozen constructor kernel.
It does not prove that the relevant `D(S3)` physical constructors exist, nor
does it determine their microscopic phase, record, or holonomy kernel.

The theorem applies after the source has supplied an actual physical
constructor group or groupoid and a well-defined quotient to target logical
operations. It does not infer that structure from scalar readouts.

## Constructor extension

Let `E` be a group of accepted closed physical constructors and let `G` be the
target logical operation group. Suppose logical evaluation is a surjective
homomorphism

\[
q:E\longrightarrow G.
\]

Its invisible constructor kernel is

\[
K=\ker q.
\]

Elements of `K` act trivially at the chosen logical quotient but may carry a
global phase, a returned ancilla transformation, a record-frame change, or a
closed-corridor holonomy invisible to that quotient.

The data form an extension

\[
1\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow E
\overset{q}{\longrightarrow}G\longrightarrow1.
\]

A set-theoretic compiler chooses one physical representative for every target:

\[
s:G\longrightarrow E,
\qquad
q\circ s=\operatorname{id}_G.
\]

Such a choice need not preserve composition.

## Composition defect

For a normalized choice with `s(1)=1`, define

\[
\kappa(g,h)
=
s(g)s(h)s(gh)^{-1}.
\]

Because `q` is a homomorphism,

\[
\kappa(g,h)\in K.
\]

This is the exact residual between compiling a product and composing the two
compiled constructors. A compiler is strict precisely when

\[
\kappa(g,h)=1
\]

for every pair.

When `K` is not central, `G` acts on `K` through conjugation by the chosen
lifts, and associativity gives the noncommutative factor-set law

\[
\kappa(g,h)\kappa(gh,k)
=
\alpha_g\bigl(\kappa(h,k)\bigr)\kappa(g,hk).
\]

Changing representatives changes both the action representative and the
factor set. The splitting question is then a nonabelian extension problem,
not an ordinary scalar phase fit.

## Central phase theorem

Suppose

\[
K\subseteq U(1)
\]

is central. The action `alpha` is trivial, and associativity gives

\[
\kappa(g,h)\kappa(gh,k)
=
\kappa(h,k)\kappa(g,hk).
\]

Thus `kappa` is a normalized `U(1)`-valued two-cocycle.

Replace the representatives by

\[
s_b(g)=b(g)s(g),
\qquad
b(g)\in U(1).
\]

Then

\[
\kappa_b(g,h)
=
b(g)b(h)b(gh)^{-1}\kappa(g,h).
\]

The representative choice changes the cocycle only by a coboundary. Hence the
class

\[
[\kappa]\in H^2(G,U(1))
\]

is independent of the chosen lifts.

A strict compositional unitary compiler exists if and only if

\[
[\kappa]=0.
\]

Proof: if a strict section exists, its cocycle is one. Conversely, a trivial
class supplies a rephasing `b` for which `kappa_b` is one, so `s_b` is a
homomorphism.

This is stronger than checking every generator separately. The obstruction
lives on relations and closed multiplication loops.

## Minimal Pauli witness

Let

\[
G=C_2\times C_2
=
\{1,x,z,xz\}.
\]

Represent its logical action by qubit conjugation channels generated by the
Pauli matrices `X` and `Z`:

\[
\mathcal X=\operatorname{Ad}_X,
\qquad
\mathcal Z=\operatorname{Ad}_Z.
\]

Although

\[
XZ=-ZX,
\]

their conjugation channels commute:

\[
\mathcal X\mathcal Z
=
\mathcal Z\mathcal X.
\]

The four channels therefore realize `G` exactly.

Any unitary implementing `Ad_X` is of the form

\[
e^{i\alpha}X,
\]

and any unitary implementing `Ad_Z` is of the form

\[
e^{i\beta}Z.
\]

Their commutator remains

\[
(e^{i\alpha}X)(e^{i\beta}Z)
(e^{i\alpha}X)^{-1}(e^{i\beta}Z)^{-1}
=
-I.
\]

Rephasing cannot turn the two lifts into commuting unitaries. Therefore no
homomorphic section from the logical four-group to the unitary constructors
exists.

The cocycle class is nontrivial. Every individual logical channel is
reachable, and every channel multiplication table entry is correct, yet the
unitary compiler fails on a closed relation.

## Why the four-group is the first useful witness

A single cyclic generator does not expose a commutator loop. Its lift can be
rephased to satisfy a finite-order relation. The four-group adds the smallest
pair of independent commuting logical generators whose lifts can carry a
nontrivial central commutator.

The first failed word is

\[
xzx^{-1}z^{-1}=1
\]

in the target while its physical lift evaluates to

\[
-I.
\]

This gives an exact meaning to “first failed”: it is the shortest target
relation, under a frozen generator and word-order convention, whose lifted
evaluation lies nontrivially in the constructor kernel.

## Channel control versus coherent control

The phase `-I` is invisible to an uncontrolled conjugation channel. It is not
invisible in every executable context.

For a controlled implementation,

\[
|0\rangle\langle0|\otimes I
+
|1\rangle\langle1|\otimes U,
\]

replacing `U` by `-U` changes the relative control phase. Interference on the
control can detect the difference. The projective channel quotient therefore
forgets information needed by coherent higher-order use.

This separates two compiler targets:

- a channel compiler may work projectively because central phases cancel in
  conjugation;
- a coherent controlled-operation compiler needs a phase frame or a lifted
  central extension.

Calling both targets “the same gate set” erases the obstruction.

## Three legitimate repairs

### Accept a projective compiler

Retain the cocycle as a coherence cell and compile only contexts insensitive
to `K`. Composition is weak rather than strict. This is adequate only when
every admitted downstream context factors through the same quotient.

### Enlarge the target

Replace `G` by the central extension represented by the physical lifts. The
compiler becomes strict because the formerly hidden phase is now part of the
target operation. This changes the specification rather than removing the
phase.

### Add a reference and compensate

Supply a source-derived phase reference and a corrective constructor whose
kernel action cancels `kappa`. This trivializes the cocycle only if the
corrector is physically admitted and composes with the required fault bounds.
Fitting an abstract phase function after observing the desired answer is not a
constructor.

## Application to the `D(S3)` control frontier

The established two-flux Lie result has two levels.

- Its derived algebra supplies full `su(d_a)` control on every nontrivial
  simple block.
- Three independent central phase directions remain absent from full
  blockwise unitary control.

For within-block conjugation and random-unitary twirling, those missing phases
cancel. Projective control is sufficient at that quotient. It is not
sufficient for arbitrary coherent relative phases between superselection
blocks, coherently controlled block operations, or any compiler whose
downstream contexts observe the central kernel.

The next microscopic audit should therefore not ask only whether the two flux
ports generate the desired channels. It must compute the composition defect
of their physical lifts on a presentation of the target control group:

\[
\kappa(r_i,r_j)
=
s(r_i)s(r_j)s(r_ir_j)^{-1}.
\]

If the defect is a phase, compute its cohomology class. If it includes record,
ancilla, or corridor transformations, retain the full noncentral kernel and
test extension splitting there.

## Fault-filtered refinement

An algebraically split extension may still fail as a fault-tolerant compiler.
A correcting resection `b(g)` can require unbounded time, nonlocal support, a
fragile common reference, or a constructor whose error spread grows with code
size.

Thus the physical hierarchy is:

1. every target operation has a lift;
2. the constructor extension splits algebraically;
3. a splitting exists inside the authorized constructor set;
4. the splitting preserves the fault filtration;
5. its resource and error bounds remain uniform.

No lower item implies the next.

## Hostile fixtures

### Generatorwise success, relation failure

Verify physical lifts for every generator but never evaluate a presentation
relation. The Pauli four-group passes every generator test and fails the
commutator relation by `-I`.

### Phase-blind validation

Validate only conjugation channels and then claim controlled-unitary access.
The hidden cocycle becomes a relative control phase.

### Scalar correction without a constructor

Choose numbers `b(g)` that trivialize a displayed cocycle but provide no
physical phase-reference operation implementing them.

### Central assumption on a record kernel

Treat a route record transformation as a scalar phase. If records are
permuted or acted on conditionally, the kernel is noncentral and ordinary
second cohomology is the wrong type.

### Cutoffwise splitting without uniformity

Trivialize the extension at every finite size using a correction whose depth
or support diverges. Algebraic splitting survives while the executable
compiler disappears in the limit.

### Changing the target silently

Replace the intended logical group by its physical central extension and call
the original compiler problem solved. The enlarged target is a legitimate
repair only when the specification change is explicit.

## Falsifiers

- Surjective reachability is claimed to supply a compositional section.
- A projective representation is treated as an ordinary representation
  without evaluating its cocycle.
- Rephasing is claimed to change the central commutator of two lifts.
- Channel equivalence is promoted to controlled-operation equivalence.
- A noncentral record or holonomy kernel is reduced to `U(1)` without proof.
- A coboundary is called physically executable without an admitted corrective
  constructor.
- Finite splitting is promoted to uniform fault-filtered splitting.

Shared Carrier geometry supplies ordered composition, closed relation loops,

## Disposition

The compiler gap has an exact algebraic core. Reachable logical operations
admit a strict compositional physical compiler precisely when the constructor
extension splits. For a central phase kernel, the obstruction is the
cohomology class of the composition cocycle. The Pauli four-group is the
smallest finite witness: all four logical channels are reachable, but no
choice of unitary representatives preserves their group law.

For `D(S3)`, projective block control remains valid for phase-blind
conjugation contexts. Promotion to coherent controlled use requires the
physical port lifts, their kernel, and their relation cocycles. This is the
next finite audit after microscopic port construction and before any claim of
a compositional fault-tolerant compiler.
