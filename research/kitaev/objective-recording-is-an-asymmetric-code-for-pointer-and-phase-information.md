# Objective recording is an asymmetric code for pointer and phase information

Owner: `marici.Kitaev`

## Question

What information becomes locally objective when a pointer label is fanned out,
and what information remains nonlocal or fragile?

The GHZ record state has an exact asymmetric access structure. Any nonempty
fragment can read the pointer label perfectly. No proper fragment contains any
relative phase information. The label has singleton access, while its
conjugate coherence requires the complete carrier.

Equivalently, the repetition subspace has logical bit-flip distance `n` and
logical phase-flip distance one. It robustly distributes one commuting
classical algebra while making the conjugate quantum coordinate globally
encoded but maximally erasure-fragile.

## Claim boundary

This is a finite theorem about the ideal GHZ/repetition encoding produced by a
controlled copy tree. It does not assert a collapse mechanism, uniquely actual
record value, macroscopic stability, thermal robustness, or fault-tolerant
preparation.

The phrase “objective” refers only to redundant local availability of the
commuting pointer label to disjoint fragments. It does not mean that all
information in the source state has become classical or locally available.

## Encoded state

Let the total number of source-plus-record qubits be

\[
n=N+1.
\]

The fanout isometry maps a source qubit to

\[
|\Psi\rangle
=
\alpha|0\rangle^{\otimes n}
+
\beta|1\rangle^{\otimes n}.
\]

Write

\[
|0_L\rangle=|0\rangle^{\otimes n},
\qquad
|1_L\rangle=|1\rangle^{\otimes n}.
\]

The two-dimensional repetition subspace is the carrier of the original source
qubit before records are irreversibly read or discarded.

## Proper-fragment theorem

Let `F` be any proper subset of the `n` qubits, with nonempty complement
`F^c`. Tracing out the complement gives

\[
\rho_F
=
|\alpha|^2
|0\rangle\langle0|^{\otimes|F|}
+
|\beta|^2
|1\rangle\langle1|^{\otimes|F|}.
\]

The cross term vanishes because

\[
\langle0|1\rangle^{|F^c|}=0.
\]

Therefore every proper reduced state is independent of the relative phase
between `alpha` and `beta`.

In particular, the states

\[
|+_L\rangle
=
\frac{|0_L\rangle+|1_L\rangle}{\sqrt2}
\]

and

\[
|-_L\rangle
=
\frac{|0_L\rangle-|1_L\rangle}{\sqrt2}
\]

have identical reductions on every proper subset. No measurement confined to
such a subset can distinguish their logical phase.

## Pointer-label access

For the branch ensemble

\[
\{|0_L\rangle,|1_L\rangle\},
\]

the reduction to any nonempty subset contains orthogonal states

\[
|0\rangle^{\otimes|F|},
\qquad
|1\rangle^{\otimes|F|}.
\]

Thus any single qubit is an authorized reader of the pointer label. Larger
fragments contain redundant copies of the same classical coordinate.

The access structures are therefore complementary:

- pointer label: every nonempty fragment is authorized;
- relative phase: only the complete `n`-qubit carrier is authorized.

This is not full quantum secret sharing because small fragments do learn the
pointer populations. It is an asymmetric split of classical and conjugate
quantum information.

## Logical-operator weights

On the repetition subspace, one may choose

\[
Z_L=Z_j
\]

on any single qubit `j`, since it distinguishes `|0_L>` from `|1_L>`.

A logical bit flip is

\[
X_L=X_1X_2\cdots X_n.
\]

No lower-weight `X` product exchanges both codewords. Hence the minimum
logical weights are

\[
w(Z_L)=1,
\qquad
w(X_L)=n.
\]

The encoding protects the classical pointer label against a bounded number of
independent bit flips through majority decoding, but it has no protection
against even one unknown phase flip.

This is an asymmetric code, not a quantum memory of distance `n`.

## Erasure asymmetry

Erasing any `n-1` qubits still leaves one perfect copy of the branch label.
For a source promised to be `|0_L>` or `|1_L>`, the classical record survives
until every share is lost.

By contrast, erasing any one qubit removes all relative phase from the
remaining reduced state. The two phase states `|+_L>` and `|-_L>` become
identical on the survivors, so no recovery acting only on those survivors can
restore an unknown encoded qubit.

Classical erasure robustness and quantum erasure correction are therefore
opposite in this encoding.

## Information-algebra decomposition

The locally available algebra on a proper fragment contains the diagonal
logical observable generated by `Z_L`. It does not contain the off-diagonal
logical operators `X_L` and `Y_L`.

The pointer algebra has been pushed downward in access threshold until one
site suffices. The conjugate algebra has been pushed upward until all sites are
required.

Objective recording is therefore not simply information duplication. It is a
redistribution of operator support across an access structure.

## Local agreement versus global coherence

The pairwise checks

\[
Z_1Z_j
\]

verify that every record bit agrees with the source bit. They generate the
stabilizer of the classical repetition relation.

The remaining logical phase is measured by the global operator

\[
X_1X_2\cdots X_n.
\]

Every local agreement check can pass while this global eigenvalue is wrong.
A single `Z` fault changes it without altering any pointer-basis record.

This is the same local-syndrome/global-logical separation as in a topological
code, though the repetition code itself is not topologically protected.

## Multi-label theorem

For `r` orthogonal pointer labels, consider

\[
|\Psi_r\rangle
=
\sum_{a=1}^{r}
\alpha_a|a\rangle^{\otimes n}.
\]

Every nonempty fragment perfectly distinguishes the branch labels `a`. Every
proper fragment has reduced state

\[
\rho_F
=
\sum_a|\alpha_a|^2
|a\rangle\langle a|^{\otimes|F|}.
\]

All inter-label coherences disappear because the discarded conditional states
are orthogonal.

Thus the complete diagonal label algebra is locally broadcast, while every
off-diagonal matrix unit

\[
|a\rangle\langle b|
\]

with `a` different from `b` remains a full-carrier correlation.

## `D(S3)` sector split

Let the accepted endpoint carrier decompose into eight simple sectors:

\[
\mathcal H
=
\bigoplus_aV_a.
\]

An ideal central record isometry has the form

\[
W_N
=
\sum_aP_a\otimes|a\rangle^{\otimes N}.
\]

For a state with sector blocks

\[
\rho_{ab}=P_a\rho P_b,
\]

every nonempty record fragment reveals the central label `a`. Tracing even one
orthogonal record copy removes the cross-sector blocks with `a` different from
`b`.

Within-sector states are untouched because the pointer depends only on `P_a`.
The full matrix algebra on each `V_a` remains available. Hence the encoding
creates this access split:

- central sector label: locally redundant;
- within-sector quantum state: retained in the endpoint carrier;
- cross-sector coherence: global across endpoint and all record shares.

If a superselection rule excludes cross-sector coherence, the last coordinate
is not in the admitted physical domain. The record theorem still cannot be
used to derive that superselection rule.

## What remains protected

The phrase “information protected from record formation” now has three exact
meanings.

### Fixed within-sector algebra

The record coupling acts as identity on each simple block, so within-sector
operations remain locally available at the endpoint.

### Global cross-sector correlation

Before any share is lost, inter-sector coherence exists only as a joint
correlation across the endpoint and every record fragment.

### Excluded source domain

A superselection rule may remove some cross-sector states entirely. This is a
domain restriction, not dynamical protection.

These cases must not be conflated.

## Redundancy and fragility

Increasing `N` improves availability of the central label under fragment
erasures. At the same time it increases the number of shares whose loss is
sufficient to make cross-sector coherence unrecoverable from the retained
carrier.

In the ideal orthogonal model, one escaped record already causes complete
dephasing. Further copies do not make the reduced coherence “more zero,” but
they make coherent reversal require control of more ports and therefore make
recoherence operationally less plausible.

Redundancy is robustness for one algebra and fragility for its conjugate.

## Fault interpretation

Independent final bit flips can be corrected by majority on the pointer
records. Correlated fanout-tree faults can defeat that decoder.

Phase faults are different. A single phase flip is a weight-one logical
operator of the repetition subspace and is invisible to every local pointer
agreement check.

Therefore a record code needs two separately declared fault contracts:

- integrity of the broadcast classical label;
- preservation or intentional disposal of the conjugate coherent coordinate.

Protecting only the first is sufficient for a classical record and
insufficient for reversible quantum control.

## Deutschian explanation

A classical fact becomes objective in this model because the dynamics changes
its access structure. It moves one commuting distinction from a single source
into many low-threshold fragments. The same unitary moves the incompatible
phase information into a high-order correlation that no local observer can
access.

Nothing mystical or merely semantic happened. The constructor redistributed
which counterfactual measurements can succeed on which subsystems.

The explanation is incomplete until the source Hamiltonian, causal fanout,
and irreversible escape of record shares are derived. But the finite model
identifies exactly what those dynamics must accomplish.

## Hostile fixtures

### Every fragment called a quantum copy

Observe that every leaf reveals the pointer label and infer that it carries
the original unknown pure state. Its reduced state contains no relative phase.

### Classical erasure robustness called quantum correction

Recover the branch label from one surviving qubit after many erasures and
claim recovery of an arbitrary encoded superposition.

### Local agreement called full-state certification

Verify every `Z_1Z_j` check and omit the global `X` coordinate.

### Global phase called locally hidden but robust

Note that the phase requires all shares and infer protection. Losing any one
share destroys recoverability from the remainder.

### Sector recording called within-sector tomography

Read the `D(S3)` central label from many fragments and infer the unknown state
inside its simple block.

### Dynamical dephasing called superselection

Trace out one record share, observe vanished cross-sector blocks, and infer
that those blocks were never physically admissible.

### More records called more quantum memory

Increase `N` and quote the classical repetition distance while ignoring the
weight-one phase logical operator.

## Falsifiers

- A proper fragment of the ideal GHZ encoding distinguishes `|+_L>` from
  `|-_L>`.
- A nonempty fragment fails to distinguish the two ideal branch labels.
- The minimum weight of logical `Z_L` exceeds one or that of logical `X_L` is
  below `n` in the frozen repetition subspace.
- Erasure of one share leaves recoverable arbitrary logical phase on the
  survivors.
- Local pointer agreement checks determine the global phase coordinate.
- An ideal central sector copier leaks within-sector state information to the
  record fragments.
- Redundant classical access is promoted to redundant access to the full
  noncommutative source algebra.

## Shared Carrier geometry and coefficient lens

Shared Carrier geometry supplies fragment subsets, access thresholds, erasure
cuts, fanout incidence, and local-versus-global support. The quantum
coefficient lens supplies complementary logical operators, partial trace,
GHZ coherence, no-cloning, and noncommutative algebra restriction.

The access-structure pattern can be reused for other coefficients, but the
identification of pointer and conjugate observables requires the quantum lens.

## Disposition

Objective recording is an asymmetric encoding transformation. It gives one
commuting pointer algebra singleton access across many fragments while moving
conjugate coherence into a full-carrier correlation. The resulting classical
record is highly erasure-tolerant; the encoded quantum phase is destroyed by
one erasure.

For `D(S3)`, an ideal central-sector copier makes the eight-sector label
redundantly available without leaking or disturbing within-sector quantum
states. The missing many-body theorem must derive that selective coupling and
show how record shares become stable and independently accessible without
silently claiming protection of excluded or globally fragile coherences.
