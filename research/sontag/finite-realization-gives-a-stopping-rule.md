# Finite Realization Gives a Stopping Rule

## Bounded question

When may finite-depth continuation agreement certify agreement at every future
depth for two candidate Marici realizations?

## Difference-system theorem

Suppose two candidates have finite-dimensional linear realizations of
dimensions `n1` and `n2`, with the same finite input alphabet and scalar
records. Form their block-diagonal comparison system:

- state space: the direct sum of the two realization spaces;
- transition for each input: the direct sum of the two candidate transitions;
- initial state: the pair of initial states;
- readout: the first record minus the second.

Let `N = n1 + n2`. The reachable subspaces generated by words of length at
most `k` form an increasing chain inside an `N`-dimensional space. Once two
successive members are equal, the chain is transition-invariant and can never
grow again. Starting from a nonzero initial state, strict growth can occur at
most `N - 1` times.

Therefore, if the difference readout vanishes on every word of length at most
`N - 1`, it vanishes on every word. If the candidates differ behaviorally,
some word of length at most `N - 1` separates them.

The sharper operational bound is not the ambient `N - 1`, but one less than
the dimension of the reachable comparison space. That dimension can be found
by exact rank stabilization. The argument certifies future closure because
stabilization proves invariance; it does not extrapolate a numerical pattern.

## Pauli orientation audit

Ordinary and opposite multiplication on two-by-two matrices give a comparison
space of ambient dimension eight. With `X`, `Y`, and `Z` as inputs, exact rank
growth across depths zero through seven is recorded by the checker. The first
record separation occurs at depth three, safely before the general ambient
bound of seven.

This explains why the earlier finite prefix was meaningful only after the
realization dimension and transition closure were frozen. Without those
conditions, Nima's hostile construction still applies: a new distinction can
always be placed just beyond the inspected horizon.

## Marici consequence

There are now two different notions of closure:

1. Source closure fixes which generators, transitions, and probes are
   authorized.
2. Realization closure proves that their reachable comparison space has
   stabilized.

Only their conjunction turns finite testing into an all-futures statement.
Source closure without rank stabilization leaves an uncertified prefix. Rank
stabilization without source closure proves completeness only for an
arbitrarily truncated protocol.

For a finite linear Marici object, the certificate should therefore carry:

- the source-typed input and probe generators;
- the two candidate transition representations or one proposed quotient;
- the exact reachable-rank sequence;
- the first stabilization index;
- zero residual of the relevant difference readout on a spanning basis;
- the authority boundary under which the generator set is complete.

## Explanatory gain

The stopping rule is not “test sufficiently many cases.” It is: construct the
finite comparison state, close it under every authorized transition, and stop
when exact span growth ceases. At that point later words cannot create a new
state direction, so every later record is already controlled by the tested
span.

## Claim boundary

This is a finite-dimensional linear-realization theorem. It does not give a
finite bound for nonlinear, infinite-dimensional, or unfrozen-generator
systems. Those require an independent finite realization theorem, a symbolic
invariant closure, or an explicitly finite/resource-indexed claim.
