# Elimination orders differ symplectically but have no discrete holonomy

## Comparison problem

The chain skew form can be reduced by eliminating adjacent hyperbolic pairs in different orders. Let

\[
P_\alpha^TMP_\alpha=H
\]

and

\[
P_\beta^TMP_\beta=H
\]

be two exact reductions to the same block-diagonal minimal form. Their comparison is

\[
G_{\alpha\beta}=P_\alpha^{-1}P_\beta.
\]

It satisfies

\[
G_{\alpha\beta}^THG_{\alpha\beta}=H,
\qquad
\det G_{\alpha\beta}=1.
\]

Thus elimination-order comparisons are automorphisms of the hyperbolic target.

## Exact result

The checker enumerates every order of the adjacent pairs at sizes four, six, and eight. All 32 reductions land in the same weighted hyperbolic normal form. All comparison maps are symplectic with determinant one, but some are nonidentity.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{the minimal object is canonical, but its identification with the original presentation is not strictly canonical.}
}
\]

## Higher-categorical consequence and correction

Different elimination routes are connected by invertible comparison cells labelled by \(G_{\alpha\beta}\). A subsequent exact test verifies

\[
G_{\alpha\beta}G_{\beta\gamma}=G_{\alpha\gamma}
\]

and

\[
G_{\alpha\beta}G_{\beta\alpha}=I
\]

for every tested route triple and loop. This is also immediate from \(G_{\alpha\beta}=P_\alpha^{-1}P_\beta\).

Therefore nonidentity comparison matrices do **not** by themselves constitute holonomy. The discrete comparison category generated by these canonical matrices is thin: there is one declared arrow between each pair of reductions, and every loop composes to identity.

The ambient real symplectic group does have nontrivial topology, but that topology is not imported automatically. A Maslov or metaplectic phase could arise only after supplying additional continuous paths or lift choices. No such phase has been constructed here.

The current minimalization groupoid is consequently equivalent to a point whenever it is nonempty. The ordered boundary also supplies a canonical left-to-right representative, so no extra polarization is needed at this finite discrete level.
