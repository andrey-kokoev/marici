# Stable observer growth requires movable cutoffs

## Tail observers as gated endpoint moments

At multiplicative positions \(y_j=e^{x_j}\), the canonical right-tail observer is

\[
B_{ij}
=
\mathbf 1_{j\ge i}\frac{y_i}{y_j}.
\]

Thus

\[
B_i(u)
=
y_i\sum_{j\ge i}u_j/y_j.
\]

It is the reciprocal endpoint moment \(q_-\), but restricted to the tail beginning at context \(i\).

The stable observer bank is therefore generated by one movable cutoff applied to one endpoint law:

```text
choose cutoff i
restrict packet to j >= i
apply reciprocal endpoint moment
renormalize by y_i
```

## Why global moments became unstable

The Laurent ladder used globally supported covectors

\[
q_\lambda=\sum_j u_jy_j^\lambda.
\]

To isolate one local innovation, many such global moments must cancel almost exactly, producing Vandermonde ill-conditioning.

A tail observer performs the same separation geometrically with a cutoff. No high-order cancellation is required.

Hence the relevant resource tradeoff is

```text
global analytic observers:
  simple support, growing algebraic order, poor conditioning

movable-cutoff observers:
  fixed exponential law, growing spatial addressability, exact conditioning
```

## Minimal constructor extension

Beyond the endpoint pair, stable observer growth needs a restriction constructor

\[
C_i:u\mapsto(u_j\mathbf 1_{j\ge i})_j.
\]

Then

\[
B_i=y_iq_-C_i.
\]

Reversal transports this to the left-tail observer

\[
\widetilde B_i(u)
=y_i^{-1}\sum_{j\le i}u_jy_j.
\]

Thus a reversal-closed stable bank contains transported right- and left-cutoff observations.

## Complexity conservation

The stable bank does not eliminate complexity. It relocates it:

- the observation formula remains first-order exponential;
- the number of cutoff locations grows with context count;
- each cutoff requires labelled spatial control;
- forgetting cutoff labels collapses the bank back to the two endpoint moments.

So stable recovery of \(n\) labelled contexts requires \(n\) independently addressable cutoff positions, even though every observer uses the same local kernel.

## Continuous limit

For dense contexts, the discrete tail family approaches

\[
B_x(u)=e^x\int_x^\infty e^{-t}u(t)\,dt.
\]

Differentiation recovers the local density:

\[
\frac{d}{dx}B_x=B_x-u(x).
\]

The infinite observer bank is therefore governed by a first-order transport equation. This is dual to the first-order Markov innovation recursion and remains compatible with the second-order Green law.

## Consequence

The most economical stable architecture is not “add higher moments.” It is:

```text
one endpoint kernel
+ a movable boundary/cutoff
+ labelled cutoff control
```

A boundary that can move becomes a complete observer.
