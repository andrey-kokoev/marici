# The stationary residual double is a Clifford–Lorentz module

## Canonical metric

On the incoming/outgoing double, define

\[
H=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The two charge lines are null, while their mutual pairing is nonzero. Translation and reversal satisfy

\[
M(z)^THM(z)=H,
\qquad
R^THR=H.
\]

Thus both contexts act through \(O(1,1)\).

In the null basis,

\[
M(e^s)=\begin{pmatrix}e^s&0\\0&e^{-s}\end{pmatrix}.
\]

After changing to sum/difference coordinates this is the usual hyperbolic rotation

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
\cosh s&\sinh s\\
\sinh s&\cosh s
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

No temporal interpretation of \(s\) is required; it is the logarithmic displacement parameter.

## Clifford generators

Let

\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}
\]

be the infinitesimal weight grading and let

\[
R=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}
\]

exchange the two orientations. Then

\[
A^2=R^2=I,
\qquad
AR+RA=0.
\]

Therefore \(A\) and \(R\) generate the real Clifford algebra \(\mathrm{Cl}_{1,1}\) in this convention.

## Meaning

The doubled type is not merely a convenient pair of coordinates. Its context laws force:

- two null directions: incoming and outgoing;
- a hyperbolic pairing between them;
- reciprocal weights under displacement;
- an involution exchanging the null directions;
- a Clifford action generated by weight and reversal.

So the smallest stationary completion of a two-way residual is automatically a spinorial two-component object for one hyperbolic plane.

This explains why fermionic language emerged twice from independent directions:

1. primitive incidence used exterior creation and Pfaffian pairing;
2. context completion of the residual produced a Clifford module.

The first acts on many primitive labels; the second acts on the retained incoming/outgoing type. Their compatibility is now the next nonredundant test.
