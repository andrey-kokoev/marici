# Coherent Gaussian inputs require a density kernel

Let \(|+d\rangle\) and \(|-d\rangle\) be normalized Gaussian packets of
position variance \(a\).  Their overlap is

\[
S=\exp\!\left(-\frac{d^2}{2a}\right),
\]

and

\[
\langle+d|Q^2|+d\rangle=a+d^2,
\qquad
\langle+d|Q^2|-d\rangle=aS.
\]

The normalized even and odd cat states have the same diagonal component
weights but

\[
\langle Q^2\rangle_+
=\frac{a+d^2+aS}{1+S},
\qquad
\langle Q^2\rangle_-
=\frac{a+d^2-aS}{1-S}.
\]

Their difference is nonzero for \(d\ne0\).  Therefore the classical joint
weight table from Entries 1713–1718 cannot encode coherent input states.  The
minimal coefficient object is the Hermitian component density kernel
\(\rho_{jk}\), paired with the transition generating kernels between packet
occurrences.  This is source-derived matrix structure, not a new Cut carrier
incidence.
