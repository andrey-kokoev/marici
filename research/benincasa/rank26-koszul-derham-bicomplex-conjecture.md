# Rank-26 Koszul–de Rham bicomplex conjecture

## Explanatory claim

The marked-pole, Cayley–Menger multiplication, and fiber-IBP relations are not independent repair families. They are the horizontal and vertical faces of one source-defined Koszul–de Rham bicomplex.

Let the multiplication direction be generated by the six frozen factors

\[
K,q_1,q_2,q_3,q_{23},q_{31},
\]

and let the de Rham direction be generated by the two retained fiber derivatives

\[
\partial_a,\partial_b.
\]

The total differential is forced by:

\[
Kq_i=q_iK,
\qquad
q_iq_j=q_jq_i,
\]

\[
\partial_r(fg)=(\partial_rf)g+f\partial_rg,
\]

and

\[
\partial_a\partial_b=\partial_b\partial_a.
\]

These identities predict a total complex before any finite residual is inspected.

## Hard-to-vary conjecture

For every fixed multidegree bounded by numerator degree (d), Cayley–Menger pole depth (k), and labelled marked-pole depths \(\ell_i\), the complete source Koszul–de Rham complex is exact at the first relation term once all three cutoff types exceed that multidegree by the largest shift required by the declared differential.

Absolute homology in a finite truncation is permitted only in the moving boundary layer of the joint numerator/pole-depth filtration. No stable multi-interior class is permitted.

## Finite falsifier

1. Derive every mixed IBP/multiplication square from Leibniz, with occurrence labels retained.
2. Construct the complete finite total differential along a cofinal sequence of numerator, Cayley–Menger-pole, and labelled marked-pole cutoffs.
3. Filter first homology by the complete labelled multidegree.
4. Fix an interior multidegree bound before comparing cutoffs.
5. Track the induced class under cutoff inclusion.

The conjecture fails if a nonzero class supported in the frozen multi-interior persists under three successive cofinal cutoff inclusions.

A class that moves outward with the cutoff is truncation-boundary homology and does not falsify the source complex.
