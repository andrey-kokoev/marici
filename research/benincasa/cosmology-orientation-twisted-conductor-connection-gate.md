# A single conductor frame cannot identify central and moving walls

The central walls `b+x` and `a+y` meet the exceptional divisor at the same
point \(r=0\). Their moving counterparts meet it at \(r=1\) and \(r=-1\).
A map of the exceptional coordinate cannot simultaneously satisfy
\(f(0)=1\) and \(f(0)=-1\). Thus direct exceptional pullback cannot preserve
both labelled moving residues and cannot define the required
residue-compatible connection.

The replacement is a normalized conductor cospan: one central branch at
\(r=0\), retaining both wall occurrences, and separate moving branches at
\(r=1\) and \(r=-1\). It requires distinct residue maps, orientation-local
system signs, comparison through the bulk complement, and Gauss–Manin
compatibility on each leg.

Until that cospan and its continued Leray tubes are constructed, the weighted
pairing remains undefined.
