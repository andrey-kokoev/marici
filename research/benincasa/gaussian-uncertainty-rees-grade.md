# Gaussian uncertainty requires the second Rees grade

For one mode, write the anomalous coordinate as \(\beta=x+iy\) and the
statistical occupation as \(n\).  The uncertainty excess over the vacuum is

\[
\mathfrak U=(n+\tfrac12)^2-|\beta|^2-\tfrac14
=n+n^2-x^2-y^2.
\]

Hence

\[
\operatorname{gr}^{(1)}\mathfrak U=n,
\qquad
\operatorname{gr}^{(2)}\mathfrak U=n^2-x^2-y^2.
\]

The statistical direction is constrained at first order.  Pure Bogoliubov
directions are tangent to the vacuum boundary and are invisible to the first
uncertainty grade.  Their physical completion requires the second-order
occupation \(n_2=x^2+y^2\).

Thus the first phase-space jet identifies the coefficient tangent, while the
second Rees grade decides whether that tangent integrates to a physical
Gaussian state.
