# Finite-time Eq. (19) erratum packet

## Scope

This packet records only the source-derived cubic-loop contribution at second
normal order in the initial time, \(\eta_0^2\). It does **not** propose a
replacement for the complete printed equation: bulk counterterms and the
\(\eta_0^1,\eta_0^0\) grades remain outside this claim.

The frozen source is Collins--Holman--Vardanyan, arXiv:1408.4801, especially
Eqs. (17)--(19). The interaction and initial-surface vertices are expanded
directly from Eq. (17), with the common Wightman/prefactor conversion used in
Entries 1558--1562.

## Corrected cubic second grade

For the three labelled vertex-location sectors, direct expansion gives

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{sector}&\text{oscillatory basis}&\text{zero-mode weight}\\
\hline
BB&J_1&+1\\
BS&-2J_2&-2\\
SS&-J_0&+1
\end{array}
\]

The zero-mode column sums to zero:

\[
1-2+1=0.
\]

Thus the source-derived cubic contribution at this grade is

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C^{(2)}_{\rm cubic,osc}=J_1-2J_2-J_0,
\qquad
\mathcal C^{(2)}_{\rm cubic,zero}=0.
}
\]

Eq. (19) instead displays the cubic oscillatory combination

\[
J_1-4J_2-2J_0
\]

and a nonzero \(J_0\) zero-mode term. Those displayed boundary-containing
weights are not generated by the printed source construction.

## Independently excluded repairs

1. **First-route truncation.** The bulk zero mode includes a subleading inner
   primitive; retaining it changes the bulk weight to \(+1\) and produces the
   cancellation above.
2. **Scalar endpoint normalization.** Matching the mixed sector needs
   \(w=2\), while matching the surface--surface sector needs \(w^2=2\).
3. **Endpoint-distribution convention.** A smooth one-sided regulator is
   multiplicative and reproduces the direct exponential expansion.
4. **Eq. (17) versus Eq. (18).** Their endpoint coefficients agree exactly.
5. **Missing Wick family.** The complete labelled cubic census gives the same
   fish multiplicity in all three location sectors.
6. **Independent vertex rescaling.** Every symmetric sewing
   \((V^2,2VW,W^2)\) has discriminant zero; the printed \((1,4,2)\) has
   discriminant eight.
