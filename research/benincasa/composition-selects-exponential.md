# Composition selects the exponential completion

Let (U(g)) be differentiable and satisfy

\[
U(0)=I,
\qquad
U(g+h)=U(g)U(h),
\qquad
U'(0)=-iH.
\]

Differentiating the composition law at (h=0) gives

\[
U'(g)=U(g)U'(0)=-iU(g)H.
\]

The unique solution is

\[
\boxed{U(g)=e^{-igH}.}
\]

Thus Entry 1643's completion ambiguity disappears once the source one-parameter composition law is retained.

For (H^2=I), the polar completion has projective slope

\[
r_{\rm pol}(g)=\frac{2g}{2-g^2}.
\]

Composition of two matrices (aI-ibH) combines slopes by

\[
r\star s=\frac{r+s}{1-rs}.
\]

Exact rational substitution shows

\[
r_{\rm pol}(g+h)\ne r_{\rm pol}(g)\star r_{\rm pol}(h)
\]

generically.  The polar completion is CP and has the correct second-order jet, but it fails source composition.
