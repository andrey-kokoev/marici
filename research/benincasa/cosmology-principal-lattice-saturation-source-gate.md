# Saturation recovers coordinates but not a source class

The image lattice \(2\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z\) has index two in the target
lattice \(\mathbb Z^2\). Its saturation is all of \(\mathbb Z^2\), and the
unique missing coset is represented by \((1,0)\). Thus saturation recovers the
formal primitive coordinate needed for the target.

This operation occurs entirely inside the target coordinate lattice. It does
not construct a source preimage, ordered boundary, `g23/g31` labels, or a
comparison map. Representatives \((1,b)\) and their sign-related forms differ
by the old lattice; coordinate normalization does not select a geometric
preimage.

Hence canonical saturation constructs the formal over-lattice but not a
source-authorized primitive class. The stronger remaining test is saturation
of the joint column-boundary graph rather than the column projection alone.
