# Exact contractions form a torsor without a canonical section

Let \(E:S\to R\) be exact source evaluation and let \(t\) be a target. If the
contraction fiber

\[
C_t=\{s\in S:E(s)=t\}
\]

is nonempty, then \(C_t\) is a torsor under \(K=\ker E\): two contractions
differ by a unique kernel element. The observed transported-minus-local words
are nonzero examples of these differences.

Evaluation data alone cannot select a point. In the model \(E(a,b)=a\), the
E-preserving shear

\[
(a,b)\longmapsto(a,b+a)
\]

moves every point of the target fiber \(E^{-1}(1)\). Thus no point is invariant
under all automorphisms preserving the supplied evaluation structure.

Constructor transport and strict finite coherence transport a contraction once
a base contraction has been selected; they do not create that base point. A
canonical section requires additional source data that breaks the kernel shear
symmetry, such as a sourced pairing, symmetry-fixed normalization, or order.
