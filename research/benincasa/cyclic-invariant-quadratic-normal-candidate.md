# Cyclic-invariant quadratic-normal candidate

## Bounded question

Let

\[
N_2=\langle
\nu_1\nu_2,\nu_1\nu_3,\nu_2\nu_3
\rangle.
\]

The cyclic occurrence action permutes these three labelled generators freely.
Its invariant subspace is therefore the line

\[
N_2^{C_3}
=\mathbf Q\langle
s_2
\rangle,
\qquad
s_2=\nu_1\nu_2+\nu_1\nu_3+\nu_2\nu_3.
\]

Any \(C_3\)-equivariant map from a scalar total-energy normal into \(N_2\)
must land projectively in this line. Symmetry does not prove that such a map
exists or fix its affine normalization.

Test whether Entry 2554's CM reduction kills \(s_2\), sends it into the
first-normal span, or gives it a nonzero component in the unique quadratic
quotient.

3. zero class: the candidate is exact;
4. presentation-dependent result: the proposed invariant line was mistyped.

## Result

The exact cyclic permutation matrix on the ordered labelled basis

\[
(\nu_1\nu_2,\nu_1\nu_3,\nu_2\nu_3)
\]

has a one-dimensional invariant subspace generated by \((1,1,1)\).
Reducing its generator \(s_2\) with Entry 2554's kernel relations gives a
nonzero coordinate in the unique quadratic quotient at:

- generic packet A at two primes;
- generic packet B;
- homogeneous packet HOMA;
- coordinate-soft packet SOFT1.

Thus outcome 1 survives:

\[
\boxed{
N_2^{C_3}
\longrightarrow
N_2/\langle\nu_1,\nu_2,\nu_3\rangle
\quad\text{is nonzero}.
}
\]

Because both spaces are one-dimensional, this reduction is a projective
isomorphism on the tested locus.

## Narrow interpretation

Cyclic equivariance determines the only possible projective target of a
scalar conormal map, and coefficient reduction does not kill it. It does not
establish that the source supplies a nonzero map into that target. The
remaining missing datum is existence and normalization, not target choice.
