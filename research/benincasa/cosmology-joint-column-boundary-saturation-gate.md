# Joint saturation produces exactly the occurrence shadow

Retain the principal coefficient and ordered boundary together. The literal
joint generator is
\((-2;0,2,-2)\), whose content is two. Its primitive saturated generator,
with target orientation, is
\((1;0,-1,1)\).

The boundary \((0,-1,1)\) is exactly the previously observed antisymmetric
occurrence shadow. This identifies that shadow algebraically: it is the
primitive half of the literal joint column-boundary vector.

The required formal generator is \((1;0,-2,2)\). It is not even in the rational
span of the literal joint generator, hence not in its saturation. The exact
residual is the boundary-only vector \((0;0,-1,1)\).

Thus joint saturation sharpens rather than repairs the obstruction. A
realization would need the saturated half together with an independently typed
boundary-only occurrence shadow; the existing occurrence data do not define
such a Čech connector.
