# The shadow factorization is unique but untyped

Let
\(h=(1;0,-1,1)\) be the saturated half and
\(r=(0;0,-1,1)\) the boundary-only shadow. The required generator satisfies
\[
(1;0,-2,2)=h+r.
\]
This factorization is unique over the integers: the principal coordinate forces
the coefficient of \(h\) to be one, and either nonzero boundary coordinate then
forces the coefficient of \(r\) to be one.

Conditionally, the sum has primitive principal coefficient, the required
ordered boundary, zero boundary sum, and explicit `g23/g31` routing. The defect
is entirely typing: `h` is not a source object, `r` is only an occurrence
shadow, and no occurrence-to-Čech connector exists. The existing connector
audit requires eight object components and twelve naturality squares.

Thus the arithmetic constructor is exact and unique, but it cannot be promoted
without a source realization of both summands and the connector.
