# Derivative interactions require an independently frozen quantum ordering

The classical symbol \(q^2p\) has Weyl-ordered quantization

\[
H_W
=
\frac13(q^2p+qpq+pq^2)
=
qpq.
\]

But the Hermitian family

\[
H_\lambda=qpq+\lambda\hbar q,
\qquad \lambda\in\mathbb R,
\]

has the same classical symbol and differs only at quantum order.

Its primitive Heisenberg equations are

\[
Dq=q^2,
\]

\[
\boxed{
Dp=-(pq+qp)-\lambda\hbar.
}
\]

Thus the ordering/counterterm parameter changes the primitive quantum force.

With primitive occurrence-resolved \(\hbar\), the added term \(-\lambda\hbar\) is itself primitive. It contributes zero to the co-Leibniz defect:

\[
\Delta(-\lambda\hbar)
-\bigl(-\lambda\hbar_L-\lambda\hbar_R\bigr)
=0.
\]

Therefore Cut sewing and its coherence cannot select \(\lambda\). Once an ordering is fixed, the same co-Hochschild closure applies; but the ordering must come from the primary Hamiltonian, regulator, symmetry, or renormalization prescription.

This is sector coefficient authority, not carrier geometry.
