# One-site irregular-to-Kummer comparison

## Source integral

For one labelled site with exponent \(\beta\), the regulated time factor is

\[
I_\beta(E;\epsilon)
=
\int_{-\infty}^{0}
(-\eta)^{-\beta}e^{i(E-i\epsilon)\eta}d\eta,
\qquad \epsilon>0.
\]

With \(r=-\eta\),

\[
I_\beta(E;\epsilon)
=
\int_0^\infty r^{-\beta}e^{-(\epsilon+iE)r}dr
=
\Gamma(1-\beta)(\epsilon+iE)^{\beta-1},
\]

initially for \(\operatorname{Re}\beta<1\) and elsewhere by the source's
analytic regularization.

## Endpoint form

With \(\rho=r^{-1}\), the same integral is the rapid-decay period of

\[
\rho^{\beta-2}e^{-i(E-i\epsilon)/\rho}d\rho.
\]

Thus Fourier--Laplace pushforward maps the irregular endpoint object

\[
\mathcal K_{\rho^{\beta-2}}
\otimes\mathcal E^{-iE/\rho}
\]

to the regular Kummer object with local solution

\[
(\epsilon+iE)^{\beta-1}.
\]

Its monodromy around \(\epsilon+iE=0\) is

\[
e^{2\pi i(\beta-1)}.
\]

## Match to the source site-weight representation

Equations (2.19)--(2.22) of arXiv:2402.06558 implement this transform before
assembling the universal graph integrand. The transformed coupling is supported
on the positive site-weight ray and carries density

\[
z^{\beta-1}\vartheta(z),
\]

up to the source-fixed phase and coupling normalization. Therefore the
irregular Big-Bang endpoint does not require a new energy incidence carrier at
one site. It produces the already present labelled positive site occurrence
with a sector-specific Kummer coefficient.

## Scope

This proves the primitive one-site comparison and its source normalization.

## Occurrencewise Cut compatibility

The source exponent at site \(s\) is

\[
\beta_s
=
\rho_s
+\gamma\left[2-\frac{(k_s-2)(d-1)}2\right],
\qquad
\rho_s
=
\sum_{j\text{ external at }s}l_j
+\sum_{e\text{ internal at }s}l_e.
\]

Cutting an internal edge \(e\) replaces it by one labelled external occurrence
at each endpoint. At either endpoint, the same \(l_e\) moves from the second
sum to the first. The interaction order \(k_s\) is unchanged. Hence

\[
\beta_s^{\rm before}=\beta_s^{\rm after}
\]

at both endpoints. The positive Kummer measures are therefore unchanged
occurrencewise. This proves coefficient-level commutation of the
Fourier--Laplace transform with one Cut. Propagator-residue normalization
belongs to the already separate universal-integrand Cut map.

## Two-site Gysin check

For the conformal two-site one-edge graph, direct integration of the three
source propagator terms gives, up to the frozen global source unit,

\[
\psi_{12}(x_1,x_2;y)
\sim
\frac1{(x_1+x_2)(x_1+y)(x_2+y)}.
\]

Multiplication by the two endpoint Kummer densities commutes with residue at
\(q_1=x_1+y=0\):

\[
\operatorname{Res}_{q_1=0}(f_1f_2\psi_{12})
=
f_1|_{x_1=-y}\,f_2\,
\operatorname{Res}_{q_1=0}\psi_{12}.
\]

The only exceptional locus is the collision of the Kummer branch
\(x_1=X_1\) with the residue wall, namely \(X_1+y=0\). This is the existing
singleton partial-energy support and misses the positive physical chamber.

## Resolved collision

Put

\[
q=x_1+y,
\qquad
\delta=X_1+y,
\qquad
u=x_1-X_1=q-\delta.
\]

The Gysin wall and Kummer branch are \(q=0\) and \(u=0\). Blowing up
\((q,\delta)\) and using \(q=\delta t\) gives

\[
u=\delta(t-1),
\qquad
u^\alpha\frac{dq}{q}
=
\delta^\alpha(t-1)^\alpha
\left(\frac{d\delta}{\delta}+\frac{dt}{t}\right),
\]

where \(\alpha=\beta_1-1\). The exceptional fiber has labelled points
\(t=0,1,\infty\). For nonintegral \(\alpha\), its rank-one Kummer local system
has one-dimensional \(H^1\). Thus the collision contributes a rank-one
coefficient excess generated by the existing Gysin and Kummer data.

## Physical positive-chain pairing

Near the collision, put \(u=x_1-X_1\ge0\) and
\(\delta=X_1+y\). After freezing all regular factors at the corner, the source
positive chain has the universal singular period

\[
J_\alpha(\delta)
=
\int_0^\infty\frac{u^\alpha}{u+\delta}du
=
-\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi\alpha)}\delta^\alpha.
\]

The equality holds in its convergence strip and elsewhere by the source's
analytic regularization. For \(\alpha=m+s\),

\[
J_{m+s}(\delta)
=
(-1)^{m+1}\delta^m
\left(\frac1s+\log\delta+O(s)\right).
\]

Hence the generic exceptional line has a nonzero source pairing, and the
resonant finite part contains a nonzero logarithm. The logarithmic coefficient
is independent of subtraction of the \(1/s\) local counterterm.
