# Singular amplitude maps and Hermitian costalks

For

\[
f_\varepsilon=\operatorname{diag}(1,\varepsilon),
\qquad |u\rangle=(a,b)^T,
\]

the pushed density is

\[
f_\varepsilon|u\rangle\langle u|f_\varepsilon^\dagger
=
\begin{pmatrix}
a^2&\varepsilon ab\\
\varepsilon ab&\varepsilon^2b^2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its associated grades are the Hermitian square of the image, the image–kernel
cross term, and the Hermitian square of the kernel.  A pure kernel amplitude
has zero ordinary and first density grades but survives at grade two.  Thus
ordinary pushforward legitimately forgets the direction while the existing
support-sensitive Rees costalk retains it.
