# Source-envelope delta reopening gate

Problem: distinguish a substantive source enlargement from an adjacent artifact.

Bold conjecture: any new adjacent artifact reopens the primitive-lift programme.

Rivals: relevance by proximity; reopening by one composable missing arrow; and
supersession only by a complete primitive chain.

Risky consequence: the proximity rule admits a matching abstract sign character
or another even coefficient vector although neither changes an obstruction.

Strongest falsification attempt: four representative deltas were classified.
The two proximity-only cases construct no missing arrow and leave all gates
unchanged. A labelled generator with verified connecting image reopens its
branch; a complete source-to-readout primitive chain supersedes the fixed
envelope result.

Disposition: the conjecture is falsified. A delta reopens one branch exactly
when it constructs a previously missing typed arrow with compatible interfaces.
Branch reopening does not supersede the objective-level obstruction. The next
test asks whether the source-labelled generator and connecting image is the
first and highest-leverage compositional target.
