# Finite stabilization criterion

Problem: decide whether the A4, A5, and A6 evidence justifies another brute-force
cutoff or colimit language.

Bold conjecture: the coherent diagram satisfies a preregistered sufficient
stabilization gate.

Gate: transitions must cohere; tau-p must remain nonzero; the last two rank
increments must agree; and residual support must stop growing. Passing would
allocate another cutoff but would not prove eventual stabilization.

Risky consequence: ranks and faithful normal-form supports must meet all four
conditions.

Strongest falsification attempt: ranks are 4,146, 5,888, and 7,924, with
increments 1,742 and 2,036. Residual supports are 69, 219, and 315, with
increments 150 and 96. Coherence and nonvanishing pass; equal rank increments
and zero support growth fail.

Disposition: falsified. Another brute-force cutoff is not justified by
stabilization, and colimit promotion remains unsupported. Failure of this
sufficient gate does not prove eventual stabilization impossible. The next test
must derive graded transition-cokernel contributions from labelled cutoff
differences rather than fit another total rank.
