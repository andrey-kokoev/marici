# Finite atomic measures are invariant nonlinear classical realizations

For the Hamiltonian equations

\[
\dot q=p,
\qquad
\dot p=-q^2,
\]

an (N)-atomic positive measure

\[
\mu=\sum_{s=1}^Nw_s\delta_{(q_s,p_s)}
\]

remains (N)-atomic: each atom follows the Hamiltonian trajectory and each
positive weight is fixed.

Its moments obey

\[
\frac d{dt}m_{ab}
=a,m_{a-1,b+1}-b,m_{a+2,b-1}.
\]

Thus the infinite moment hierarchy has a (3N-1)-dimensional nonlinear
positive realization, although it has no finite degree-stable linear moment
submodule.

This statement is classical. Atomic phase-space measures are not automatically
positive quantum states.
