# Quartic Gaussian first variation is a Hartree Hamiltonian tangent

Take the local Hermitian quartic interaction

\[
H_4=\lambda q^4
\]

and a centered one-mode Gaussian covariance

\[
V=
\begin{pmatrix}
V_{qq}&V_{qp}\\
V_{qp}&V_{pp}
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The quartic part of the Heisenberg covariance equations is

\[
\dot V_{qq}=0,
\qquad
\dot V_{qp}=-4\lambda\langle q^4\rangle,
\qquad
\dot V_{pp}=-4\lambda\langle\{p,q^3\}\rangle.
\]

Gaussian Wick reduction gives

\[
\langle q^4\rangle=3V_{qq}^2,
\qquad
\langle\{p,q^3\}\rangle=6V_{qp}V_{qq}.
\]

These equations equal symplectic covariance transport generated by the
state-dependent quadratic Hartree Hessian

\[
h_{qq}=12\lambda V_{qq}.
\]

Consequently

\[
\frac{d}{dt}\det V=0
\]

at first order in the quartic coupling.  The parity-even interference exists,
but it is a Hamiltonian tangent rather than statistical noise.

The checker verifies the direct Wick and Hartree formulas exactly over an
integer covariance census.  This is the one-mode \(q^4\) representative; a
general derivative quartic EFT vertex still requires its tensor-valued Wick
reduction.
