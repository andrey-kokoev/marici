# Cumulant sewing has a finite species rule but infinite coefficient rank

For a finite labelled set \(S\), moments and connected cumulants obey

\[
m_S
=
\sum_{\pi\in\Pi(S)}
\prod_{B\in\pi}\kappa_B,
\]

where \(\Pi(S)\) is the partition lattice. Nested restriction/conditioning is organized by refinement chains

\[
\pi\le\sigma\le\tau.
\]

The two parenthesizations of the incidence coproduct enumerate the same refinement chains. Coassociativity is therefore inherited from transitivity of partition refinement; it does not require a new coherence generator at each cumulant grade.

This gives one arity-uniform combinatorial rule:

\[
\boxed{\text{moments}=\operatorname{SET}(\text{connected cumulants}).}
\]

However, an arbitrary state has an independent connected cumulant coordinate at every arity. The coefficient coalgebra is not finite rank or finitely generated as an ordinary fixed-arity algebra. Its compression is species-level: one partition/refinement law governs infinitely many labelled grades.

Thus the process coefficient architecture is infinite but finitely specified, matching the distinction found for the differential moment hierarchy.
