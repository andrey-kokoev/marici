# A mod-two Bockstein cannot reach the primitive free column

For the coefficient sequence
\(0\to\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{2}\mathbb Z\to\mathbb F_2\to0\), exactness gives
\(2\beta(x)=0\) for every mod-two class. Nonzero Bocksteins can occur, as in
the complex \(\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{2}\mathbb Z\), but they land in
\(\mathbb Z/2\).

The target column belongs to the free lattice
\(\mathbb Z^2=(\Xi_{\log},-\sigma_{123})\), whose two-torsion subgroup is zero.
Therefore no coefficient Bockstein can map to the primitive element \((1,1)\).

The earlier finite-field gamma-normal derivative called a Bockstein is a
different construction and does not define this integral connecting map. The
remaining algebraic parity operation is lattice saturation, which must still
be tested for source labels.
