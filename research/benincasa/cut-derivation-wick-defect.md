# Gaussian Cut pushforward has a Wick-contraction differential defect

For three labelled modes, take

\[
H=\frac12\sum_{i=1}^3p_i^2+q_1q_2q_3
\]

and let \(C_3\) be Gaussian pushforward over mode \(3\), with

\[
C_3(q_3)=C_3(p_3)=0,
\qquad
C_3(q_3^2)=1.
\]

On the labelled observable

\[
f=p_1q_3,
\]

the Hamiltonian derivation gives

\[
Df=-q_2q_3^2+p_1p_3.
\]

Therefore

\[
C_3(Df)=-q_2,
\qquad
D_{\rm obs}(C_3f)=D_{\rm obs}(0)=0.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{[C_3,D](p_1q_3)=-q_2.}
\]

For internal variance \(\nu\), the defect is \(-\nu q_2\). It is a canonical Wick-contraction term generated by the frozen cubic incidence and internal coefficient state.

Thus Cut pushforward is not a strict morphism of the free-plus-cubic differential algebra. The retained labelled algebra remains strict; after pushforward, differential compatibility requires an induced effective term or a homotopy/coherence cell. The defect is derived from existing Cut contraction and does not justify a new carrier generator.
