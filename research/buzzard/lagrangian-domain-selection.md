## Formal increment

Lean defines the standard rational symplectic boundary form. In dimension two,
a nonzero isotropic generator represents a Lagrangian line. The first
coordinate line and the line generated by `(-1,1)` are both Lagrangian for the
same fixed bulk observation map. The map is injective on the first line and
noninjective on the second, proving that the boundary choice is part of the
kernel data.

Lean then defines reflection `diag(1,-1)`, positive Euclidean boundary energy,
and coordinate exchange. Both coordinate lines are distinct,
reflection-invariant Lagrangians with unit energy. Exchange preserves energy,
is anti-symplectic, and swaps the lines. Any exchange-invariant scalar score
must therefore assign them equal values and cannot select one uniquely.

## Type-system consequence

The Green form types admissible boundary domains but does not choose one.
Reflection invariance and positivity also fail to select a domain when the
candidates lie in one orbit of the admitted geometry. Domain selection needs
an additional orbit-separating source constructor, and restricted kernel data
must be indexed by that selected domain.

This increment specializes the Green-residual theorem into an executable
extension-domain countermodel.

## Boundary and missing interfaces

- a source-derived polarization or Calderon projector;
- an authorized coorientation breaking the exchange orbit;
- transport of Smith and barcode data under domain change.
