## Formal increment

The coefficient ring is the actual commutative ring
`MvPolynomial (Fin 2) Rat`, written informally as `Q[u,v]`. For a two-by-two
matrix, Lean defines `firstDeterminantalIdeal` as the ideal generated by the
range of its four entries, equivalently its one-by-one minors.

For

`A = diag(u,v)` and `B = diag(1,u*v)`, Lean proves:

- `det A = det B`;
- the first determinantal ideal of `B` is the unit ideal, since it contains
  the entry `1`;
- the first determinantal ideal of `A` is proper, since evaluation at the
  origin sends every entry generator to zero but does not send `1` to zero;
- hence equal determinants do not determine the first determinantal ideal.

All matrix indices are `Fin 2`; coefficients are rational; no domain, PID,
Noetherian, presentation-invariance, or geometric-source assumptions are
added beyond those already supplied by this concrete polynomial ring.

## Claim boundary

This is an exact finite algebraic counterexample. It does not prove the ideal
`(u,v)` is nonprincipal, identify module Fitting ideals, prove invariance under
presentation changes, or authorize a physical or geometric interpretation of
the two strata. Those remain separate interfaces.
