# History induces a canonical Hilbert geometry only on the cycle quotient

## Question

What metric structure is already source-derived once completed history is continuous and its kernel is exactly the cycle space?

## Claim boundary

History canonically metrizes the quotient by cycles, not the cycle residue itself. The quotient completion is the closure of the history range in the declared Hilbert target. Any metric assigned to the kernel requires additional source data.

## Exact sequence

Let \(\mathcal C\) be the completed projective source, \(\mathcal Z=\ker\partial\), and

\[
\widehat B:\mathcal C\to\mathcal H
\]

the completed all-ratio history map. Prior results give

\[
\ker\widehat B=\mathcal Z.
\]

Therefore history factors through an injective map

\[
\overline B:\mathcal C/\mathcal Z\longrightarrow\mathcal H.
\]

This is an algebraic and continuous quotient factorization. It does not assert that the image is closed or that the original quotient topology equals the norm topology induced from \(\mathcal H\).

## Canonical visible norm

Define on the algebraic quotient

\[
\|[c]\|_{\mathrm{hist}}=\|\widehat Bc\|_{\mathcal H}.
\]

This is well defined because adding a cycle does not change history. It is positive definite because the history kernel is exactly \(\mathcal Z\). Its inner product is

\[
\langle[c],[d]\rangle_{\mathrm{hist}}
=
\langle\widehat Bc,\widehat Bd\rangle_{\mathcal H}.
\]

The Hilbert completion of this normed quotient is canonically isometric to

\[
\overline{\widehat B(\mathcal C)}^{\mathcal H}.
\]

No closed-range theorem is needed for that statement.

## Radical and cycle sector

The pullback positive form

\[
G_{\mathrm{hist}}(c,d)
=
\langle\widehat Bc,\widehat Bd\rangle
\]

has radical exactly \(\mathcal Z\). Consequently it supplies no positive length to a nonzero cycle. Adding an arbitrary positive form on \(\mathcal Z\) would produce a Hilbertized augmented carrier, but its values would not be derived from common history.

A forest gives a continuous coordinate splitting within the admissible greedy class. Such a splitting identifies vectors with visible and cycle coordinates, but it does not make the decomposition orthogonal and does not select a cycle covariance.

## Reciprocal structure

The symmetric all-ratio grade and Hilbert direct-sum target make reciprocal block exchange isometric. Hence reciprocity descends to an isometry of the history quotient and its Hilbert completion. This isometry still does not determine a metric on the kernel.

## Metaphysical consequence

The carrier now has a canonical stratified geometry:

- the quotient visible to common history has a source-derived Hilbert norm;
- the invisible route residue has exact projective topology and integral presentation changes;
- no source-derived orthogonal sum joins them.

Thus route residue is not a second unmeasured Hilbert component waiting for numerical calibration by default. It is a different typed layer until an independent positive form is supplied.

## Disposition

The available analytic data determine precisely the visible quotient geometry and no more. The remaining physical covariance problem is localized to the cycle kernel. Its acceptance test is an independently sourced positive form on \(\mathcal Z\), compatible with reciprocal action, cutoff inclusions, and completed forest changes.
