# Every five-deletion core of the stored 52-vertex witness excludes six newcomers

**Claim (computational, fixed labeled matrix).** No triangle-free graph on 53 vertices with independence number at most 11 contains an induced 47-vertex subgraph of the archived Google 52-vertex (3,12) graph. This is not a universal 53-vertex exclusion.

For each 47-core H, enumerate every independent red support meeting every independent eleven in H. Vertices of red degree 11 in H cannot belong to a support: after extension their twelve red neighbors would be independent. On the resulting support graph, red compatibility means disjointness; blue compatibility means that H outside the union has no independent ten. Pairwise compatibility is necessary, not sufficient, for joint completion. Repetition in a six-tuple is allowed precisely at a compatible loop. Restoring the five original vertices gives the lower bound of five coexisting newcomers on each core.

A five-class certificate consists of five pairs (one shared vertex, one missed independent ten). Every support belongs to at least one certified class. Two members of a class cannot be joined by either color. If the nearest-anchor assignment fails, try a greedy reassignment with updated intersections and unions. If that fails, build the actual compatibility graph: cache missed tens only as witnesses for red-overlapping pairs, and perform an exact six-clique search, allowing repetition on loops. A heuristic failure never excludes a core by itself.

**Full enumeration:** 2,598,960 distinct cores, 129,002,547 single supports counted across cores. First certificate: 2,514,441 cores. Greedy certificate: 70,341 more. Direct clique search: 14,178. No six-tuple passed. Thus every compatibility graph is loop-free and has clique number exactly five. This does not imply five-colorability: twelve previously audited examples have chromatic number six. The change of test from coloring to clique exclusion is essential.
