## Why the strip cannot simply be declared common

The Tate phase rotates the pure mismatch subspace into generic-angle directions. Equal leading density

\[
L^{-1}G_{abs,L}^T
\to2G_{Pl}
\]

does not imply finite-cutoff subfeature inclusion. Nor does equality of signed Tate/reference traces imply the Douglas domination.

Therefore adjoining the same formal \(B_\alpha\) to both features would define a new positive dilation rather than identify the independently physical one.

The source theorem must prove the embedding.

## Global obstruction and filtered target

A single finite \(L\) cannot dominate the full angular carrier. On a ramified character sector, the Tate connection contains a conductor term of magnitude

\[
f(\chi_v)\log q_v.
\]

For every finite \(L\), characters exist with conductor energy greater than \(L+C_S\). Thus a global unweighted common-strip domination at fixed cutoff is false.

Use the conductor projection

\[

\]

On \(Z_F\mathscr H_S\), the negative Tate leg satisfies

\[
A_{S,-}\preceq(F+C_S)I.
\]

The admissible common-bulk target is therefore filtered:

\[
\boxed{
L\ge F+C_S.
}
\]

At each admissible \((L,F)\), the ideal common-edge form

\[
C_{L,F}=LI-A_{S,-}|_F
\]

is positive. The exact physical theorem is the regulator-level comparison identifying this abstract filtered edge with the mismatch-strip feature inside the complete Tate positive Gram.

The positive completion is consequently a directed system over

\[
\boxed{

=\{(L,n,F):L\ge F+C_S\},
}
\]

not one fixed-cutoff Hilbert inclusion on the unrestricted angular dual.
