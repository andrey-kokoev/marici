# Default-source role parity excludes the complementary marking at any origin

## Scope

The source is the default canonical seed and the existing `GenerativeAssemblyRules` operational signature: PN, inverse-PN, NE, reader-phase-advance and NN-phase-advance. Host construction of separately configured seeds is not an emitted operation.

The invariant is a fixed coloring of lattice vertices:

    chi(x) = (x0 + x2) mod 2,
    C references have chi = 1; M references have chi = 0.

The default seed satisfies it. The complementary marking used by chart7 exchanges C/M roles on the same chamber vertices and violates it. Changing packet origin or coefficient permutation does not change this geometric role observation.

## Complete finite local test

The checker enumerates six primitive shapes: both tetrahedron orientations and the four split components of an octahedron. For each shape, it enumerates all eight origin-parity classes compatible with the owner's required coordinate sum, then all24 active reference markings.

-128 are native-completable invariant sources.

Every emitted successor of those128 sources satisfies the full named-reference invariant. Coverage includes64 PN,64 inverse-PN,64 NE,64 NN advances and128 reader advances. Electron states have no outgoing operations in this signature.

There are also384 exact translation controls and256 phase controls. The earlier100-state exploration is disclosed but supplies no all-path evidence.

## Why the finite cases cover arbitrary generated states

This part is a source-level argument, not kernel-verified JavaScript semantics.

1. `makeLatticeItem` constructs every owner by integer translation of one of the listed tetrahedral or octahedral templates. Each primitive cell is one of the listed components.
2. Two admissible origins with the same coordinatewise parity differ by an even vector with coordinate sum zero. Such differences are generated by2(e0-e3),2(e1-e3),2(e2-e3). They preserve chi at every translated vertex.
3. Face matching, item keys used for equality, neighbor construction and apex extraction commute with a common integer translation: they use coordinate addition, equality and fixed templates, without a spatial cutoff. Reference completion therefore commutes with those translations. The three generator translations are checked as implementation controls for every admitted case.
4. A reachable proton or neutron chart is constructed by `completeP` or `completeN`. Its active four references are a bijective marking of its primitive cell, so one of the24 cases applies. Its outside reference is the uniquely computed apex; it is not an unenumerated free input. All emitted successors are checked for the full five- or six-reference invariant.
5. The target chart expressions in `outgoing` depend on species, cell and aliases, not packet origin or coefficient permutation. Origin affects phase validation and coefficient bookkeeping but cannot introduce a different target geometry/reference assignment. Checking phases1 and5 is a control; the all-origin claim uses this source dependency argument, not extrapolation from those samples.
6. Electron targets are terminal. Induction over finite emitted-operation paths now preserves the invariant from the default seed.

## Conclusion

Under the declared operational signature, the default seed cannot generate a proton context on the original chamber with the complementary C/M assignment at **any** origin. This removes the higher-origin escape left open by the preceding origin-zero test, without an unbounded reachability search or an origin reset.

The conclusion does not refute independently supplied complementary seeds, which are valid under a different input policy. It does not rule out a newly declared role-changing operator, a global symmetry outside this signature, altered source semantics or the broad retained-context descent conjecture.
