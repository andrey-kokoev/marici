# Atomic event-audit publication separates revocation from proof truth

Under a HYPOTHETICAL in-process event-A vault, audit validation and publication hold the SAME owner lock. A deterministic two-thread workload starts audit, lets revocation contend during its locked critical section, then releases audit. The receipt publishes at epoch 1 before revocation increments to epoch 2; a later audit is refused without changing the receipt count. When revocation acquires first, audit is refused with no receipt. A split check/commit negative control exposes the stale-publication race it would permit. Fresh `check_atomic_archive_audit.py` passes these cases; the primitive mathematical row statements remain unchanged throughout.

This is a local concurrency test of one critical-section protocol, not independent authentication of event A, a formal proof against every scheduler, or a physical execution record. The receipt from the successful race remains an earlier event-bound claim, while revocation prospectively removes the next protected audit. Provider B's identical mathematical bytes do not extend A's capability or repair a stale A commit.

The fixed dual-archive question now has a scoped answer across byte substitution, retention horizons and atomic publication. Next branch should compare process-local locked receipts with an independently exportable OFFLINE verification packet: which issuer signatures/append-only events would be required before a replay verifier could validate a receipt without the vault, and which information a hash-only receipt cannot prove. Analytic S,A,R,C,G mapping remains deferred.
