# Why an endpoint insertion needs all four square ports to break a row

**Claim.** Assume the width-nine square with its four-site outside red path is legal. Add site 10 between the two outside path ends to form a pentagon. Assume the resulting core with the four square ports is legal. Keep an individually legal old row and the prescribed port links (red to its support and to disjoint square rows). Its attachment fails exactly when it intersects every square row and omits an interior site of the old outside path.

**Core prerequisite from the labels.** The red neighbors of 10 are the two outside path ends and site 9 (count 9 only once). The ends are mutually blue, and 10 has no red link to a square port. A new red triangle therefore needs 9 and an end distinct from 9. If 9 is an outside end, no such edge exists. If it is an outside interior site, it is red to the neighboring end of the old four-site path. If 9 is covered, its only possible old red link to an outside site is the physical edge `8–9`. Thus insertion fails precisely when 9 is an interior outside site, or when 9 is covered and 8 is an outside end. A blue seven cannot add another exclusion: a blue set through 10 can use at most one interior outside site and no outside end. The square bound below allows at most four square ports and covered sites in total, so at most six vertices including 10. If neither exclusion applies, the five outside sites form the required pentagon.

**Necessity.** No old edge changes. Site 10 is blue to every old port, including the row port. Thus no new red triangle can cause the row failure. A forbidden blue seven must include both the row port and 10: deleting either recovers a legal graph. Of the five remaining vertices, at most one lies outside the square on the old path. Its ends are red to 10, and its two interior sites are red to each other. Thus at least four vertices must come from the covered sites and square ports.

Suppose a blue set takes `k` square ports. It cannot take any site in their supports. The five covered sites consist of the common fixed site and two adjacent movable pairs. With zero ports it can take at most three sites (one per movable pair and the fixed site). With one port, three sites are excluded, so at most two remain. Two distinct ports cover at least four sites, leaving at most one. Any three ports cover all five sites. In each case `k = 0, 1, 2, 3`, the sum of ports and available mutually blue sites is at most three. Only `k = 4` supplies the required four vertices. Those ports cover all five sites, so the fifth vertex must be an interior outside site. Blue links to the row port say exactly that each square support meets the row, and that the interior site is absent from it.

**Sufficiency.** Take the four square ports, a missed interior site, site 10, and the row port. The interior site is outside the square and is not a pentagon neighbor of 10. All four square ports are mutually blue and blue to 10. The two stated row conditions make every remaining link blue. These seven vertices certify failure.

**Unique label.** Let `f` be the common square site and `a–b–c–d` the old outside path. Without `f` a separated row misses at least one site in each movable pair (their two sites are consecutive). The square corner that chooses both missed sites is then disjoint from the row. Thus a failing row must contain `f`. On the old graph its port and the four square ports already form a blue five. The outside sites missed by the row must be pairwise red, or two of them complete a blue seven. The old outside path has no red triple. Hence the two remaining row sites must both be outside, and the two missed sites must form a red edge. Missing `ab` or `cd` puts the other red edge inside the row and creates a red triangle with its port. Only missing `bc` works. The sole candidate is therefore `{f,a,d}`.

Conversely, if that triple is separated in physical column order, its support has no red edge (the only added core edges are outside, and `ad` is not one). Its port has no red links to square ports, so it creates no red triangle. An old blue set through this port can use at most four square ports and covered sites in total, by the bounds above. Of the missed outside sites `b,c` it can use at most one. Including the row port gives at most six. Thus the row is legal at nine and fails at ten by the claim. If the triple is not separated it is not an Excel row, and no old row is lost.

This proof concerns one row at a time. It does not assume that all legal old rows can be installed simultaneously. [Finite check](results/ramsey_inherited_endpoint_shadow.json).
