# Tagged proof-record contract exposes but does not identify associator histories

Freeze a bounded four-leaf tagged Farkas interface with explicit primitive row roots, candidate proof matrices, ordered leaf tags, and a list of formal associator rotations. The caller can inspect public final certificate, ordered leaf tags, or replay the EXACT rotation path. The two pentagon routes return the same certificate and ordered leaves but different path records. A request to DECLARE the two histories equal is refused in the absence of an independently sourced higher pentagon filler. Fresh `check_tagged_relation_control_contract.py` checks all these controls and refuses an omitted primitive root.

This resolves the apparent conflict between retaining fine evidence and using a set-valued compositional repair: the tagged relation preserves four labelled candidate derivations even when two yield the same proof packet; the caller may replay their distinct paths, yet no unsupported equality is silently inserted. Equality of the PUBLIC ANSWER is a weaker control than equality of certificate vectors; equality of leaf tags is weaker than equality of rotation histories. The prior formal associator pentagon holds as a map but is not a historical-identification principle.

The bounded tag-and-control contract is a deliverable, not a global higher-category result. A distinct successor should attempt a source-derived higher pentagon constructor with exact boundary, check coherence against a five-leaf overlap, and refuse any self-asserted filler. An analytic S,A,R,C,G correspondence still needs independently authoritative role/edge/base data; Farkas source roots are not those analytic roles.
