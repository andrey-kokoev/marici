# Large-shift nilpotence halves the two-prime operator budget

Let `I=(-L,L)` and let `T_a` be zero-extension translation by `a>0` on
`L2(I)`. If `2a>=|I|=2L`, then `T_a^2=0`. Its initial and final spaces are
orthogonal, and on their nonzero paired fibers

\[
\frac{T_a+T_a^*}{2}
\]

is the matrix `[[0,1/2],[1/2,0]]`. Therefore

\[
\left\|\frac{T_a+T_a^*}{2}\right\|=\frac12.
\]

At `L=0.55`, both active shifts satisfy this condition:

\[
2\log2>1.1,\qquad 2\log3>1.1.
\]

Consequently the complete two-prime operator obeys

\[
\|P_L\|\le\frac12\left(\frac{\log2}{\sqrt2}
+\frac{\log3}{\sqrt3}\right)
=0.5622065862\ldots .
\]

This is an infinite-dimensional bound, not a finite-section scout. It improves
the generic symmetric-translation budget by a factor two. Joint cancellation
between the two shifts could improve it further, but is not needed for this
bound.
