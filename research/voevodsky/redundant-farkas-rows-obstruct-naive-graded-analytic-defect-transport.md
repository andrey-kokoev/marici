# Redundant Farkas rows obstruct naive graded analytic defect transport

The polyhedral presentation object `(A,r,s)` and arrow `(M,d,c)` in `polyhedral-relative-residues-form-a-farkas-category-with-a-checked-refinement-prism.md` encode inclusion of inequality-defined relations; they do not declare a chain complex, differential, or degree-labelled cofiber. A proposed analytic functor cannot define kernel/cokernel merely by treating A as a linear edge operator without an invariance proof under redundant rows.

Explicit countercontrol: the real interval K={x:0<=x<=1} has presentation A=(-1,1)^T, r=(0,1)^T at s=0. Add the redundant row x<=2, yielding A'=(-1,1,1)^T, r'=(0,1,2)^T. Both describe exactly K; adding the row and dropping it are certified Farkas inclusions in both directions. Yet as bare linear maps R->R^2 and R->R^3, A and A' each have rank one and kernel zero while their cokernels have dimensions one and TWO respectively. Thus `dim coker A` is not an invariant of the semantic polyhedral relation or its certificate-equivalence under row additions. This is NOT a proof that no enriched functor exists: a legitimate construction might quotient row syzygies or attach a different graded complex with explicit comparison quasi-isomorphisms. It proves that the naive candidate loses historical-defect meaning before touching analytic completion.
