# The canonical prime-shell cube has the minimal completion grade

## Question

Among all Boolean \(n\)-cubes generated by distinct adjacent-prime shell actions, does the cube on the first \(n\) shells complete first, and why is its final-edge grade

\[
L_n=p_2p_3\cdots p_{n-1}p_n^2p_{n+1}?
\]

## Claim boundary

The theorem covers arithmetic Boolean cubes obtained from \(n\) distinct shell actions

\[
p_i\longmapsto p_{i+1}
\]

acting multiplicatively on integer vertices. It proves minimal completion grade within this class. It does not exclude a noncubical graph configuration from creating the same probe-depth defect earlier.

## General shell set

Choose shell indices

\[
I=\{i_1<\cdots<i_n\}.
\]

The minimal base on which every selected action is independently applicable is

\[
B_I=\prod_{i\in I}p_i.
\]

For a subset \(S\subseteq I\), its cube vertex is

\[
v_S=B_I\prod_{j\in S}\frac{p_{j+1}}{p_j}.
\]

These vertices are distinct. Indeed, the prime-exponent change produced by the binary indicator of \(S\) is a discrete boundary: at prime position \(r\), the exponent change is the incoming selected indicator at \(r-1\) minus the outgoing selected indicator at \(r\). Reading from the least prime upward recovers every indicator uniquely.

## Completion grade

An edge in direction \(i\in I\), based at \(S\subseteq I\setminus\{i\}\), has grade

\[
g(S,i)=v_Sp_{i+1}.
\]

Every ratio \(p_{j+1}/p_j\) exceeds one. For fixed \(i\), the largest edge grade therefore occurs at

\[
S=I\setminus\{i\}.
\]

Cancellation gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
g(I\setminus\{i\},i)
&=B_Ip_{i+1}\prod_{j\in I\setminus\{i\}}\frac{p_{j+1}}{p_j}\\
&=p_i\prod_{j\in I}p_{j+1}.
\end{aligned}
\]

This increases with \(i\). Hence the cube completion grade is

\[
L(I)=p_{i_n}\prod_{j\in I}p_{j+1},
\]

and the unique latest edge has direction \(i_n\), based at the vertex where every other selected action has been applied.

## Minimality

For any ordered \(n\)-element index set, \(i_r\geq r\). Monotonicity of the primes gives

\[
p_{i_n}\geq p_n,
\qquad
p_{i_r+1}\geq p_{r+1}.
\]

Therefore

\[
L(I)=p_{i_n}\prod_{r=1}^{n}p_{i_r+1}
\geq
p_n\prod_{r=1}^{n}p_{r+1}=L_n.
\]

Equality forces \(i_r=r\) for every \(r\). Thus the first \(n\) shells give the unique minimally completing shell cube.

For the canonical set \(I=[n]\),

\[
L_n=p_n\prod_{r=1}^{n}p_{r+1}
=p_2p_3\cdots p_{n-1}p_n^2p_{n+1}.
\]

The first values are

\[
L_2=45,
\quad L_3=525,
\quad L_4=8085,
\quad L_5=165165.
\]

## Point--line--square--cube meaning

A vertex records a subset of prime replacements. An edge adds one replacement. A square asserts commutation of two replacements. The Boolean \(n\)-cube assembles all orders and all subsets of \(n\) commuting replacements. The completion grade is the largest arithmetic cost among its edges, not a temporal stage. At \(L_n\), the unique missing maximal edge closes the full-support cubical diagram.

Combined with the Walsh--Vandermonde theorem, this proves that the earliest literal \(n\)-shell cube carries a unique \((n-1)\)-setting blind direction and that one further setting detects it.

## Strongest falsification attempt

Enumerate all shell subsets of sizes two through six among the first ten shells. Construct every cube vertex and edge directly, verify vertex injectivity, compare the enumerated maximum grade with \(L(I)\), verify uniqueness of the latest edge, and confirm that the canonical initial segment uniquely minimizes completion grade at each dimension.

## Disposition

The arithmetic completion formula and its minimality are proved within the shell-cube class. The remaining global question is whether a noncubical subgraph can realize an \(n\)-shell full-support Walsh residue below \(L_n\).
