# Categorical RH proof bracket and the form-core gate

## Question

How tightly can the RH proof be bracketed by the residual doctrine, and which exact categorical arrow remains missing between finite packet positivity and the full Weil criterion?

## Claim boundary

This packet identifies a conditional necessary-and-sufficient bracket. It does not prove the Gaussian family is a form core, reverify the source-to-Weil comparison, or imply RH.

## Objects and functors

Let:

- \(\mathcal A\) be the arithmetic source category generated by endpoint, gamma, and labelled prime data;
- \(D(Q)\) be the source-typed domain of the completed quadratic form;
- \(\mathcal V\) be the subcategory generated by rational Gaussian translates, rational positive widths, and derivative jets;
- \(j:\mathcal V\to D(Q)\) be evaluation of those probes in the completed domain;
- \(Q\) be the completed Weil form;
- \(j^*Q\) be its family of finite Gram restrictions.

The relevant restriction functor is

\[
j^*:\operatorname{Form}(D(Q))
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Form}(\mathcal V).
\]

Packet positivity establishes only

\[
j^*Q\geq0.
\]

To infer \(Q\geq0\), the functor \(j^*\) must reflect nonnegativity on the admitted class of closed or closable forms.

## Necessary bracket

Assuming the standard source-typed Weil criterion and the completed packet comparison, the necessary chain is

\[
\mathrm{RH}
\Longrightarrow
Q\geq0\text{ on }D(Q)
\Longrightarrow
j^*Q\geq0
\Longrightarrow
G_I\geq0\quad\text{for every finite packet }I.
\]

The last arrows are restrictions and therefore lose information unless \(j\) is a core inclusion.

## Candidate sufficient bracket

The reverse chain requires additional arrows:

\[
\left[
G_I\geq0\ \forall I
\right]
+
\left[
\mathcal V\text{ is a form core for }Q
\right]
+
\left[
G_I=(j^*Q)_I
\right]
\Longrightarrow
Q\geq0
\Longrightarrow
\mathrm{RH}.
\]

Thus packet positivity brackets RH exactly only after the form-core and source-comparison cells are proved.

## Why ordinary density is insufficient

Fixed-width Gaussian translates can be dense in \(L^2\), and their nonvanishing Fourier multiplier can make them distributionally faithful. Neither property implies graph-norm density for \(Q\).

For

\[
f_n(x)=e^{-nx^2},
\]

one has

\[
f_n(0)=1,
\qquad
\|f_n\|_2^2=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2n}}
\longrightarrow0.
\]

Endpoint evaluation is therefore not continuous in the \(L^2\) topology. A graph norm containing the endpoint term does not send this sequence to zero.

The checker also verifies a finite analogue: an indefinite form can restrict positively to a proper probe subspace while retaining an unseen negative direction.

## Required core theorem

A sufficient analytic theorem must provide one common domain and topology with:

1. closed or closable operators \(A,B\) satisfying
   \[
   Q(f)=\|Af\|^2-\|Bf\|^2;
   \]
2. a well-defined rational Gaussian-jet subspace \(\mathcal V\subseteq D(A)\cap D(B)\);
3. density of \(\mathcal V\) in the graph norm controlling both \(A\) and \(B\);
4. equality between restricted forms and completed arithmetic Gram packets;
5. derivative-order tail bounds \(\epsilon_{N,k}\) justifying the completion cells.

Only then does positivity on the enumerable probe category extend to the full criterion domain.

## Categorical interpretation

The form-core theorem is a conservativity statement stronger than ordinary observer faithfulness. Distributional faithfulness says probes distinguish distributions. Form-core conservativity says restriction reflects the order relation on unbounded or closable quadratic forms.

These are different arrows:

\[
\text{jointly faithful on objects}
\not\Longrightarrow
\text{order-reflecting on forms}.
\]

The missing arrow is therefore not another observer. It is a topology-sensitive density theorem making \(j^*\) conservative for positivity.

## Proof bracket

The current categorical proof status is:

| Arrow | Status |
|---|---|
| Labelled prime data to finite arithmetic rows | constructed at finite cutoff |
| Packet coefficient quotient by duplicate labels | discharged |
| Finite cutoff to completed packets | conditional on sourced tail constants, including derivative order |
| Completed kernel to rational packet restrictions | typed by continuity |
| Rational packet PSD to all real packet PSD | follows from continuity and cone closure |
| Packet PSD to positivity on \(D(Q)\) | blocked by the form-core theorem |
| Completed Weil positivity to RH | relies on the separately sourced Weil criterion |
| Source-derived Douglas contraction or feature map | not supplied |

## Disposition

Categorical investigation narrows the proof to two possible closure routes:

- construct the global Douglas contraction directly on \(D(Q)\), bypassing core reflection;
- prove the Gaussian-jet subcategory is a graph-norm form core and establish packet positivity there from arithmetic data.

Both routes still require source comparison and differentiated tail control. Abstract \(L^2\) density, distributional faithfulness, RKHS reconstruction, and finite packet positivity do not close the missing arrow.
