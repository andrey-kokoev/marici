# Is binding to the parent's P/PN face interfaces actually difficult?

Node: `negative-direction-policy-native-descent`; governing parent: `face-chart-transition-descent`.

**Conclusion:** intrinsic mathematical hardness is NOT established. The remaining distinction can be localized to a finite reader/interface and input-policy question. Several stronger substitutes are either already solved or simply false.

## 1. What the parent actually asks

The parent's statement concerns face-specific P/PN charts of one regular chamber, typed changes of C/M admissibility domains and overlap cells, preserving geometric vertex identities. It does not demand one occupied role frame, uniqueness of a field coupling, an active physical time-reversal event, or arbitrary scalar pointwise field gluing.

Its acceptance does demand actual native witnesses with full references and law domains, rather than calling a vertex permutation or an unadmitted supplied filler a source transition. The crucial distinction is which inputs and comparisons the owning policy admits.

## 3. Fresh minimal test on one chamber

The new checker uses the current source API on all24 P markings of the same regular chamber. For each marking it:

1. constructs and validates its two actual PN ports, including the reader-prefixed port;
2. applies complete reversal to identify the dual E presentation on that SAME chamber;
3. validates that the reversed original PN interface is an actual NE interface;
4. constructs the declared complementary P marking and validates its two PN ports;
5. compares geometry, role domains and occurrence indices explicitly.

Results:

-24 of24 complementary constructions supply a full four-face cover;
-216 actual transfers validate, including the shifted-origin controls below;
-the two complementary P charts have disjoint two-face PN domains whose union is all four faces;
-the full reversal places **E**, not P, on the original P chamber;
-the old PN faces become **NE** faces, not PN faces in the complementary P chart.

The last fact has an elementary explanation valid beyond the samples: an original PN face has two M vertices. Flipping all roles gives one M on that same face. A target PN face still requires two M. Therefore a total fixed-face PN-domain equivalence between complementary role charts is impossible under that definition.

This does NOT obstruct the existing higher four-facet incidence object: it retains different parent faces and restricts to shared edges/vertices instead of pretending the two complete PN domains coincide.

## 4. The precise local interface, without an origin trap

The reversal already supplies the complementary roles. Turning its same-chamber E reader into the chosen complementary P reader can preserve all four geometric vertices and their new roles.

At origin0, that re-reading changes C2->C0 and M2->M0 while retaining C1/M1: the index changes are -2 and0. It is not a uniform origin translation or identical occurrence record.

But requiring origin0 would be an unnecessary restriction. At target origin2:

- old E's C2 and M2 occurrences are retained exactly;
- the other C1/M1 occurrences advance by2;
- the resulting P phase frame passes the actual template validator;
- its actual PN transfer also validates, for all24 markings.

Thus occurrence bookkeeping does not by itself prove impossibility. A same-chamber E_r -> P_(r+2) **comparison** is a concrete candidate interface, not a fabricated all-six-amplitude identification.

The target's outside C2 is computed by the existing `completeP` neighbor constructor. It lies among the old six reference vertices in8 cases and outside them in16. It is therefore not generally an identity map of the old reference set, but its new geometry is constructible; this is not evidence of computational hardness or a missing independent amplitude.

What the unchanged forward operation generator does NOT supply is an emitted E->P transition: its E packets are terminal. This is an operation-signature boundary, not a no-go for passive re-reading, a higher native comparison, or an explicitly admitted directional policy.
