# Independent branch anchors survive all observed small rule cases

Fresh rerun of `check_progress_premise_rule_ledger.py` expands query indices through n+2 for all support words n<=2. All 15 typed active pairs occur across 11,319 alpha graph states and 23,641 individually chosen transitions, including 419 ERASE--K edges. Both endpoints of EVERY transition pass the combined predicates: (i) every component has OUT or ERASE root and OUT_i meets channel-i Q/BOOL; (ii) Q phase principal and auxiliary roles and finite B/K successor tails are typed; (iii) original suffix is owned exclusively by COPY; (iv) cutting COPY leaves two disjoint auxiliary branches, EACH with OUT or ERASE anchor. The old coarse COPY--NIL counterexample violates (iv).

This is still bounded model checking rather than a symbolic closure theorem. A plausible rule-preservation ledger: COPY--B inserts a fresh B on each anchored branch and moves original suffix to new COPY; COPY--NIL splits the two anchored branches, attaching NIL to both; Q_B--K and Q_S--B exchange saved budget/support without losing the OUT root; Q_B--N becomes Q_R at the OUT root; Q_S--N produces BOOL--OUT plus a detached ERASE budget root; Q_R--B produces BOOL--OUT plus an ERASE support root; Q_R--N produces BOOL--OUT; ERASE--B/K transfers its eraser root to the successor; ERASE--N removes an isolated eraser/NIL component. Each case needs formal boundary disjointness and typing checks, especially when a Q/E principal is waiting on COPY auxiliary and the other channel terminates. These cases distinguish a preservation theorem from seeing one instance of every rule.
