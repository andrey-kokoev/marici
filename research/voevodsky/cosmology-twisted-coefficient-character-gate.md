# Twisted-coefficient character gate

## Question

Can a nontrivial rank-one local system make `Xi_log` exact and then specialize faithfully to trivial coefficients?

## Claim boundary

No. For character monodromies `(a,b)`, torus cochains form the Koszul complex generated by `a-1` and `b-1`. At `(1,1)`, cohomology ranks are `(1,2,1)`. If either monodromy is nontrivial over a field, the complex is acyclic.

A generic primitive necessarily divides by `a-1` or `b-1`. It therefore has a pole or becomes undefined at `(1,1)`, exactly where the primitive `Xi_log` class reappears. Over the character ring, top cohomology is the quotient by `(a-1,b-1)`, supported only at the trivial character; specialization is nonflat.

Nontrivial local systems also change the coefficient object. No integral comparison to the fixed trivial-coefficient `sigma123` is supplied.

## Disposition

Twisting kills the class rather than filling it, and no faithful regular specialization returns the primitive. The next leaf computes the two-parameter Koszul residue at the trivial character and tests whether it contains information beyond the existing unit obstruction.
