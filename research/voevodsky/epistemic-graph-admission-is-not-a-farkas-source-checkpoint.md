# Epistemic graph admission is not a Farkas source checkpoint

The tracked `.narada/epistemic/README.md` describes an immutable hash-linked graph admission ledger, but explicitly says admission is structural/provenance/evidence-location policy, NOT truth certification. Read-only inspection of the actual event `ev-000000015066-ee877f72-0cfe-4858-9efe-e633830a3ed9` confirms its `previous_hash` equals its immediately preceding stored event's hash and its kind is `proposal_admitted`. Crucially this event records `certifies_truth:false`, `identity_state.authentication.status:missing`, and `identity_state.authority.granted:false`. It opens the publication audit issue; it does not publish a primitive Farkas row attestation. Fresh `check_graph_ledger_checkpoint_scope.py` checks these exact fields and records its bounded scope.

The graph is authoritative for the COORDINATION claim that this research issue transition was admitted. It is not an independently verifiable source-row issuer checkpoint: the inspected record has no authenticated actor, no granted Farkas source authority, no signed row-manifest binding and no independently verified cross-client consistency witness. The two-file check also does not recompute the ledger's canonical event hash or prove global append-only anti-fork properties. A graph hash may preserve local provenance of a research claim without authenticating that claim's mathematical truth or its operational owner.

This is an evidence-based negative result for using the presently inspected graph admission as the requested Farkas offline receipt. Next branch should test a SOURCE-SCOPED claim type under an explicitly hypothetical owner payload and require separate independently verified authority, rather than mutate graph policy or pretend graph admission supplies it. Analytic S,A,R,C,G mapping remains deferred.
