# Hausdorff-moment three-cone lift

## Question

Can the scalar finite-difference hierarchy be reorganized into a smaller positive-matrix constructor capable of proving many complete-monotonicity inequalities simultaneously?

## Claim boundary

The difference hierarchy is lifted to the compact Hausdorff moment problem and its three Gram/localizing cones. The explicit divided-difference and positive J-fraction constructor remain unsupplied.

## Difference moments

Fix positive \(t,h\) and set

\[
a_k(t,h)=D_{k,h}H(t).
\]

If

\[
H(t)=\int_0^\infty e^{-t\lambda}\,d\nu(\lambda)
\]

with \(\nu\geq0\), define

\[
x=1-e^{-h\lambda}\in[0,1].
\]

Pushing the weighted measure \(e^{-t\lambda}d\nu(\lambda)\) to \([0,1]\) gives \(\mu_{t,h}\), and

\[
a_k(t,h)=\int_0^1x^k\,d\mu_{t,h}(x).
\]

Thus the finite-difference sequence is a compact Hausdorff moment sequence.

## Three cone families

For every matrix size, define

\[
M_0=(a_{i+j}),
\]

\[
M_x=(a_{i+j+1}),
\]

and

\[
M_{1-x}=(a_{i+j}-a_{i+j+1}).
\]

For a coefficient polynomial \(p(x)=\sum_ic_ix^i\), their quadratic forms are

\[
c^*M_0c=\int|p(x)|^2d\mu,
\]

\[
c^*M_xc=\int x|p(x)|^2d\mu,
\]

and

\[
c^*M_{1-x}c=\int(1-x)|p(x)|^2d\mu.
\]

All three matrices must therefore be positive semidefinite. They encode positivity of the measure and localization to the compact interval \([0,1]\).

## Why this is stronger operationally

Checking only \(a_k\geq0\) tests monomials separately. The Hankel matrices test every polynomial combination simultaneously. The two localizing matrices prevent a positive functional from escaping outside the intended interval.

A single source-derived Gram factor for these three families would prove infinitely many coupled scalar inequalities. A positive J-fraction could provide such a factor through positive recurrence coefficients, subject to exact identification with the arithmetic moments.

## Exact finite fixtures

For positive atoms at \(1/3\) and \(2/3\) with weights \(2\) and \(3\), the checker verifies exact factorizations

\[
M_0=VWV^*,
\qquad
M_x=VWXV^*,
\qquad
M_{1-x}=VW(1-X)V^*.
\]

Every principal minor through matrix size three is nonnegative.

For signed atoms at \(1/2\) and \(3/4\) with weights \(1\) and \(-1/2\), the two-by-two Hankel determinant is

\[
-\frac1{32}.
\]

The matrix cone detects the negative weight even when some individual moments remain positive.

## Relation to the divided-difference kernel

Grothendieck reports that the surviving arithmetic route is an order-two Stieltjes target for an outer derivative of \(H\), whose factorial moments are generated by

\[
\frac{H(x)-H(y)}{x-y}.
\]

That exact identification has not been reconstructed in this packet. It must specify:

- which derivative or normalization of \(H\) is the Stieltjes function;
- the factorial-moment convention;
- how its moments match \(a_k(t,h)\);
- which three finite Hankel/localizing conditions are required at each truncation;
- the source-derived coefficients of the proposed J-fraction.

Without those arrows, generic Hausdorff positivity cannot be transferred to the explicit arithmetic function.

## Categorical interpretation

The scalar cubical observer is lifted by a moment functor into three cone-valued matrix observers:

\[
\{a_k\}_{k\geq0}
\longrightarrow
(M_0,M_x,M_{1-x}).
\]

A positive Gram or J-fraction constructor would be a common apex whose restrictions generate all three cones naturally in matrix size. This is the same proof-compression pattern as the entire-kernel nerve: one positive object generates several infinite observer families.

## Disposition

The remaining complete-monotonicity gate has a structured matrix target rather than an unorganized list of scalar inequalities. The nonredundant candidate is a source-derived positive Gram or J-fraction factor for the three compact Hausdorff families. Existing failed positivity backends should remain frozen. The first missing typed arrow is the explicit divided-difference-to-moment identification.
