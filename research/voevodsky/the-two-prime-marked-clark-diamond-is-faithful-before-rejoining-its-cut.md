# The two-prime marked Clark diamond is faithful before rejoining its cut

## Result

The marked Clark pilot on

    2 -> 4 -> 12,
    2 -> 6 -> 12

has an explicit bounded linear receiver for each marked generator. Its eight marked paths have:

- rank six after terminal record composition;
- rank eight when the typed middle cut and its two records are retained.

The sum over markings distinguishes the two original unmarked routes already at degree two. The recording arrow has an explicit signed Green mate, different from both inverse transport and its ordinary Hilbert adjoint.

This is a fixed-prepared-forcing linear recorder and an exact finite cut comparison. Physical event access, a faithful signed metric, and extension to the full stable realization remain separate claims.

## 1. Actual source and target objects

Freeze the real forcing Phi and the upper-half-plane Clark feature map L from `a-weighted-clark-feature-history-receiver-with-convergent-comparisons.md`. The smallest common shell refinement for the diamond is

    A=[log 2,log 4],
    B=[log 4,log 6],
    C=[log 6,log 12].

It is a coarsening of the fifteen-shell catalogue; C is the union of the [6,10] and [10,12] exponential-label shells. The feature map is additive under that refinement.

At cut a use the existing fiber

    E_a=L2(a,infinity) direct-sum L2(0,a),

with unitary reassembly R_a. The source-derived seam transport is

    T_(b<-a)=R_b^(-1)R_a.

The record carrier F_<=2 is the vacuum plus the first two Hilbert tensor powers of the Clark feature space, with the declared degree weights tau^(2r). Set

    X_a=E_a tensor F_<=2.

The cut source and record memory are separate factors. The forcing Phi used to calibrate the emitted feature is fixed. Varying that forcing together with an unknown state would require a different source specification; we do not claim that the map f -> f tensor L(f) is linear.

## 2. Marked arrows

For an event e:n->np with a=log n and b=log(np), let v_e be its chamber incidence and g_e=L(v_e). Define

    B_e^0=T_(b<-a) tensor I,
    B_e^1=T_(b<-a) tensor c_R(g_e),

where c_R appends g_e and is truncated above degree two. Both maps go from X_a to X_b and are bounded. The forgotten arrow still performs the actual source transition; it is not an identity arrow at a fixed arithmetic object.

The free marked path category therefore has a uniquely determined functor to these bounded maps, by composition. No inverses of directed prime arrows are required for this extension.

For the unmarked lift e -> e^0+e^1,

    B_e^0+B_e^1=T_(b<-a) tensor (I+c_R(g_e)).

Starting at the vacuum, composing events gives the existing event signature with each letter replaced by its actual Clark feature. The same receiver can be used on a larger tensor history when more than two events are admitted.

## 3. Source moment and cut composition

Features are computed from the global source moments Phi(x) and x Phi(x) before tail completion. If x=a+r is used on a translated tail, the first moment becomes

    x Phi(x)=(r+a)Phi(r+a).

Thus its moment vector is transported by [[1,0],[a,1]], in addition to the spectral phase. Applying the Clark row (1,-kappa) gives (1-kappa a,-kappa), including the required affine-origin correction.

Successive origins add, so both paths have the same correctly sewn terminal source transform. This agrees with the reassembly identity

    T_(log12<-log4) T_(log4<-log2)
      =T_(log12<-log6) T_(log6<-log2).

The two routes differ in their record factors, not in this terminal source reassembly. All endpoint, forcing, and first-moment cross terms remain inside the independently established Clark feature pairing.

## 4. Exact eight-column calculation

Write a,b,c for the three chamber basis vectors. The first route has event vectors

    (a,b+c),

and the second has

    (a+b,c).

For each route order its four markings as 00,01,10,11. Their terminal record columns are

    route through 4:  1, b+c, a, a tensor (b+c),
    route through 6:  1, c, a+b, (a+b) tensor c.

The eight columns have rank six. Their kernel is exactly generated by

    [route4,00]-[route6,00],

and

    [route4,01]+[route4,10]-[route6,01]-[route6,10].

These are the forgotten-gap collision and the single-record interval-additivity collision. Since L is injective on the finite chamber space, and tensor degree is retained, the same ranks hold for the analytical feature records.

The unmarked lift sums each route's four markings. Its two images have difference

    a tensor b - b tensor c.

They are independent. Thus this receiver is faithful on the two-dimensional unmarked route space even though it is not faithful on all eight marked paths after terminal composition.

## 5. Retaining the actual middle cut restores marked faithfulness

Before rejoining, retain the intermediate object m=4 or m=6 and the two separate one-event records. The target is

    direct_sum_(m=4,6) (C direct-sum L(V)) tensor (C direct-sum L(V)).

Each route lives in its own intermediate-object summand. Within that summand its four markings occupy the distinct bidegrees (0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1). Every column is nonzero, so all eight are independent.

The exact coefficient implementation has 32 cut coordinates and verifies rank eight. Concatenating the two record factors and forgetting the middle label gives the preceding rank-six terminal recorder exactly:

    Join Cut = Terminal.

Both terminal-kernel vectors are nonzero in the retained cut record. Hence the marked-cut construction supplies precisely the information lost at terminal rejoining. No extra arbitrary scalar probe is needed for this finite repair; the intermediate vertex and cut placement are already part of the marked source.

This is the linear marked-cut statement. We do not infer that a cofiber of a sum splits as a sum of cofibers, or claim a full derived Clark functor from this single pilot.

## 6. The signed Green mate is explicit

Let C_Clark be the fixed four-port signature acting on the one-letter feature carrier. On the degree-zero-through-two record use

    J_record=1 direct-sum C_Clark direct-sum C_Clark^(tensor 2),

with the degree weights included in the underlying Hilbert inner product. On X_a use the positive cut-fiber pairing times this signed record form.

The forgotten arrow's mate is T_(b<-a)^(-1) tensor I, because reassembly is unitary. For a recording arrow the formula is

    (B_e^1)^sharp
      = T_(b<-a)^(-1) tensor [tau^2 a_R(C_Clark g_e)],

where a_R(h) contracts the last tensor slot against h using the unweighted one-slot Hilbert pairing. Direct tensor contraction proves

    q_b(B_e^1 x,y)=q_a(x,(B_e^1)^sharp y).

The factor tau^2 comes from the adjacent degree weights. The checker verifies the complete matrix identity for an independent complex feature.

This is a concrete Green mate. It is not an inverse: it lowers record degree. The ordinary weighted Hilbert adjoint contracts against g_e rather than C_Clark g_e. Since the signed form can be degenerate, a mate is only unique modulo maps landing in its radical; no nondegeneracy is asserted.

The opposite-polarity comparison already proved for L and C_Clark transports the same formula between the two spectral orientations. It changes left/right creation conventions by tensor reversal, while retaining the distinction between signed mate and inverse.

## 7. Disposition

The pilot supplies:

1. typed bounded marked arrows using actual seam transport;
2. the correct source-first Clark moment and origin correction;
3. an exact event-signature composition law;
4. explicit signed Green mates;
5. faithful observation of all eight marked paths when the typed middle cut is retained.

It also identifies the precise premature quotient: composing the two record factors and discarding their intermediate object lowers rank from eight to six.

The most productive next extension is to retain compatible typed cuts on the three-prime braid, with the same generator maps and mates. This tests the full-cut indexing theorem analytically before any terminal metric descent. It should keep the whole attachment diagram rather than infer its information content from its composite arrow alone.
