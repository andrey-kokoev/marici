# Minimum Farkas surplus does not commute with composition

Freeze a finite exact-rational Farkas proof policy: among nonnegative integer multiplier tuples with entries 0..4 that certify a target row, minimize surplus and then choose the lexicographically least multiplier. Let S be `0<=x<=1`, B the single row `x<=2`, and C the single row `x<=3`, with references 0, 1/4 and 1/2 respectively. For S->B the policy picks multiplier `(1,2)` on source rows `(-x<=0,x<=1)`, surplus 0. For B->C it must pick multiplier `(1)`, surplus 1. Their Farkas composite is `(1,2)` with surplus 1. Direct S->C instead picks `(2,3)` with surplus 0. Both certify the SAME inclusion at the target reference, yet the selected composite is not the selected direct proof.

Fresh `python research/voevodsky/checkers/check_canonical_farkas_policy.py` checks all coefficients in the declared finite menu, each residual with its reference shift, and direct versus composed proof packets. This counterexample does NOT depend on an unsearched smaller surplus: 0 is the global minimum possible for any nonnegative-surplus policy, and all three selected arrows with 0/1 margins satisfy the stated equations. The finite menu pins down lexicographic tie-breaking, not a universal representation-independent canonical proof.

Consequently even a deterministic local 'least surplus' selector is not a functor on proof presentations without retaining a coherencer recording comparison between selected direct and staged arrows. Source/history authority and the analytic residue cannot be inferred from either chosen proof. Next independent verifier should replay the three specific proofs without importing the searcher and test whether a comparison cell exists inside the original Farkas category (not merely semantic equality), while preserving nonnegative margins.
