# Full formal history: analytic receiver and positivity obstructions

## Outcome

The completed universal history resolves the algebraic source, but it cannot be identified indiscriminately with an analytic positive receiver.

Two precise results constrain that identification:

1. A continuous linear map from the full degree-topological history to a normed space factors through a finite jet. In particular, there is no faithful continuous bounded-operator realization of the whole formal history with the operator-norm topology.
2. More strongly, for a complete filtered star algebra with a filtration-preserving involution, **every positive scalar functional on the entire formal algebra annihilates the augmentation ideal**. This statement does not require continuity.

The second result applies to the proposed full relative history involution whenever it is admitted. It does not prove failure of the Clark kernel's positivity: the source-to-Clark star receiver has not been constructed, and a proper analytically convergent source domain need not contain all formal series used in the obstruction.

Let H be the degree-completed tensor algebra on the admitted chamber space V, with augmentation ideal F^1 H. The arithmetic histories determine

`r=S_(2,3)^(-1) S_(3,2)=1+d`.

The leading component d_2 is nonzero. Since concatenation identifies the tensor product of homogeneous spaces with the corresponding higher homogeneous space, the degree-6 component of d^3 is d_2 tensor d_2 tensor d_2 and is nonzero.

The exact checker recovers 27 nonzero degree-six terms from the actual arithmetic windows. Thus:

- d^3=0 in the four-jet;
- d^3 is not zero in the full history;
- span(1,d,d^2) is not invariant under multiplication by 1+d in the full history: d^2 maps to d^2+d^3.

The finite Jordan obstruction remains correct on its declared quotient. It cannot be applied to the full history as though that same three-dimensional subspace remained invariant.

The closed relative subalgebra generated by d is a copy of C[[d]]: the distinct powers have strictly increasing leading chamber degree, so no nonzero formal series in d vanishes. If comparison unitarity supplies the relative involution, then

`d*=(1+d)^(-1)-1=-d+d^2-d^3+...`.

Its four-jet is the previously derived -d+d^2. Relative degree in d and chamber tensor degree must not be conflated.

## 2. Continuity into one normed carrier forces a finite jet

Let L:H->Y be complex-linear and continuous, where Y is normed and H has the formal degree topology. Some F^N H is mapped into the open unit ball. For any x in F^N H and scalar t, tx also lies in F^N H, so

`||t L(x)||<1` for every t.

Hence L(x)=0. Therefore L factors through H/F^N H.

The same argument works for the product topology with ordinary finite-dimensional coefficient topologies: every basic zero neighborhood contains some full tail F^N H. It does not depend on treating the complex coefficient field as discrete.

Consequences:

- a continuous linear scalar readout sees only finitely many degrees;
- a continuous algebra map H->B(Hilbert) in operator norm kills some F^N H;
- its augmentation images form a nilpotent ideal;
- if the image of 1+d is unitary, it is a unitary of the form I+nilpotent and is therefore I.

The last assertion follows from the spectral theorem for the normal operator U-I, whose spectrum is {0}. Nontrivial indefinite finite-jet representations are not excluded.

### Strong-operator continuity does not repair a bounded full representation

Suppose pi maps H into bounded operators on a Hilbert space and is strong-operator continuous. For each vector xi, the preceding argument applied to h->pi(h)xi gives an N depending on xi with pi(F^N H)xi=0.

Set

`K_N = intersection_(h in F^N H) ker pi(h)`.

Each K_N is a closed linear subspace; their increasing union is the whole Hilbert space. Baire's theorem implies that one K_N has interior and hence is the whole space. Thus pi again kills a common F^N H.

This statement assumes bounded operators defined on the whole Hilbert space. It is not a blanket theorem about arbitrary unbounded operators on incomplete domains.

## 3. Positivity on the entire formal star algebra is stronger still

Let A be a unital complete separated filtered complex star algebra with F^0 A=A, multiplicative filtration, augmentation A/F^1 A=C, and an antilinear involution preserving each F^n A. Assume the usual characteristic-zero scalar operations. Let lambda:A->C be complex-linear and positive:

`lambda(x* x)>=0` for every x in A.

For a in F^1 A put b=a* a. Then b=b* lies in F^2 A. For every real t>0, the formal series

`g_t = sum_(k>=0) binomial(1/2,k) (-t b)^k`

exists in A. The filtration makes each coefficient calculation finite. Real coefficients and b=b* imply g_t*=g_t, and the formal binomial identity gives

`g_t* g_t = 1-t b`.

Positivity therefore implies

`0 <= lambda(1)-t lambda(a* a)`

for every t>0. Since lambda(a* a)>=0, necessarily lambda(a* a)=0. Cauchy–Schwarz for the positive form lambda(x* y) then gives lambda(a)=0.

Every x decomposes as epsilon(x)1+a with a in F^1 A, so

`lambda(x)=lambda(1) epsilon(x)`.

More generally, the entire augmentation ideal is radical for lambda(x* y). **No continuity of lambda was used.** The essential assumption is that the functional is positive on the whole formal algebra, including every g_t.

### Consequence for the proposed Clark relative functional

On C[[d]] with d*=(1+d)^(-1)-1, a positive functional on the full algebra has

`lambda(d)=lambda(d^2)=0`.

Thus it cannot provide the nonzero highest-grade coupling L=lambda(d^2) required by the nondegenerate invariant relative form.

An indefinite Hermitian functional is not excluded. Neither is a history-indexed positive family whose comparison is an isometry between different forms rather than a unitary for this one fixed star-functional.

### Consequence for star representations

If the entire A has a star representation on a common invariant pre-Hilbert domain D, every vector functional

`lambda_xi(a)=<xi,pi(a)xi>`

is positive on A. The argument implies ||pi(a)xi||^2=0 for every a in F^1 A and xi in D. Hence such a representation kills the augmentation ideal. Allowing unbounded operators on D does not evade this result if the representation and adjoint identities are defined for **all** formal elements on that same invariant domain.

This is a conditional obstruction to a proposed full star receiver, not a theorem that arbitrary analytic history representations are impossible.

## 4. Why this is not a negative result about RH or the Clark kernel

The full Clark divided-difference kernel already has an explicit signed feature realization. Its positivity on all finite Euler packets is the RH-strength condition described in the source-interface audit.

No receiver has been shown to send the entire formal history algebra, with its proposed involution, into that feature realization. The present no-go says that one cannot obtain a nontrivial positive star-functional by simply identifying these objects.

It does not show that the Clark kernel is indefinite. Nor does it permit replacing the full kernel by its rank-two numerator. Those are distinct statements about different carriers.

## 5. A constructive analytic alternative: restrict the source, do not rename its topology

Choose a norm on finite-dimensional V and the projective tensor norms on its powers. For rho>0 define

`H_rho(V) = {h : sum_n rho^n ||h_n||_projective < infinity}`.

Chronological multiplication and the projective cross-norm inequality make this a unital Banach algebra. Tensor polynomials are norm dense. It embeds algebraically into the full formal history but is generally a proper subalgebra: arbitrary factorial-growth coefficients need not belong to it.

If A is a unital Banach algebra and a linear chamber map ell:V->A obeys

`||ell(v)|| <= rho ||v||`,

then tensor evaluation extends uniquely to a bounded algebra map

`Eval_ell:H_rho(V)->A`.

Indeed the norm of the evaluated degree-n term is at most rho^n ||h_n||, giving absolute convergence and multiplicativity. The uniqueness follows from polynomial density.

For an event v satisfying rho ||v||<1, its geometric inverse belongs to H_rho. Finite products of such events and their comparisons are therefore admitted. This is a sufficient admission condition, not an assertion that the actual Clark increments satisfy it.

Unlike H, this Banach algebra need not contain sqrt(1-t a* a) for every arbitrarily large t. Nor has the eventwise formal involution been shown bounded on a chosen H_rho. Thus the formal positivity proof cannot simply be repeated here.

**Important:** H_rho is not complete for the degree filtration in the universal formal sense; its degree completion is H. Its bounded universal property is a different, explicitly stated receiver category. One must not claim a continuous full-history evaluation by silently restricting the source to H_rho.

No Clark chamber-to-operator map ell, invariant involution on the selected analytic domain, or identification with the prescribed Green form is constructed by this general Banach-algebra extension theorem.

## 6. Legitimate options for the next receiver

The full-history search now has a sharper branch point:

1. **Proper analytic source domain:** derive its norm and actual chamber operators from Clark source operations, prove product/inverse admission, and compute its full-kernel functional.
2. **History-indexed positive presentations:** comparisons transport one metric to another. A common terminal-invariant star-functional is not asserted. A compatible finite-jet/pro-system is different from a single Hilbert norm on every formal series.
3. **Indefinite formal receiver:** retain the signed pairing and establish the nonzero cross-degree coupling from the source. Positivity of a later restricted spectral image would be a separate theorem.
4. **Finite-jet receiver:** state the truncation honestly and keep track of the directions its form or representation loses.

The current shell-tail construction gives linear, first-order-domain-correct ingredients. It still needs an admitted ordered multiplication/receiver. Defining that receiver and its topology precedes imposing a positive full-history interpretation.
