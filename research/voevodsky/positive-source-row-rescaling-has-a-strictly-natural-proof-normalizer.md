# Positive source-row rescaling has a strictly natural proof normalizer

A nontrivial PRESENTATION change keeps the source set `[0,L]` fixed but replaces its primitive upper row `x<=L` by `rho*x<=rho*L`, rho>0. Proof coordinates `(a,b;c)` now obey `rho*b-a=1` and `rho*L*b+c=T`. Rescaling that row from rho to sigma transports the proof as `R_rho,sigma(a,b;c)=(a,rho*b/sigma;c)`. The source-derived zero-surplus normalizer is `N_rho(a,b;c)=(a+c/L,b+c/(rho*L);0)`. Exact substitution yields the stronger result `N_sigma R_rho,sigma = R_rho,sigma N_rho` **strictly on proof packets**: the apparently nonlinear coordinate rescaling is harmless when its multiplier and primitive bound are transported together.

Fresh rational `check_farkas_row_rescaling.py` verifies 160 squares, observes 120 naive unchanged-multiplier type failures, and rejects negative row scaling. For the concrete proof `(1,2;1)` of x<=3 at L=rho=1, doubling the primitive row yields `(1,1;1)` (not `(1,2;1)`). The normalizer becomes `(2,3/2;0)` in the doubled-row presentation, which transports back to `(2,3;0)`. Positive row rescaling is therefore a proof-coordinate isomorphism on this frozen family, not an arbitrary change of primitive source set or authority.

This result also narrows the earlier concern: failure of strict naturality under surplus-adding target weakening/source refinement does NOT imply failure under all presentation changes. Whether the analytic S,A,R,C,G comparison respects such rescalings still needs an independently typed analytic role/edge/base assignment, and no free-grammar proof-history 4-cell follows from these packet equalities.
