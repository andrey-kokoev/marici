# Two simultaneous erasers form a bounded port-graph diamond

Fresh `check_two_eraser_port_diamonds.py` extracts 24 length-three support/two-query fixtures under one priority order, locates reachable snapshots with two simultaneously enabled ERASE active pairs, and applies them in both orders on copies of the SAME full graph. All 72 encountered diamonds have equal alpha-canonical successor graphs, including detached garbage components; every rewrite audits one symmetric wire per live port. This closes the prior untested same-family eraser case for those snapshots.

The general commuting lemma has clear premises: two redexes consume disjoint agents and their RHS rewires only their own external boundary endpoints with fresh internal names. Then the two rewrite substitutions commute up to renaming of fresh agents. In this specific net, simultaneously enabled principal-principal active pairs cannot share an agent because each agent has exactly one principal port. A rewrite may create or destroy OTHER redexes, but that is not a competing pair of simultaneously enabled redexes. A formal proof requires checking every rule's boundary discipline and termination for all finite nets; the bounded checks do not supply that theorem.

Next audit the entire finite rule table (COPY, query phases, NIL, erasers) for boundary-preserving replacement and a strictly decreasing global termination measure on the intended copier/two-query input class. If both hold, local confluence plus termination would support a restricted unique-normal-form result. The local add(i)/union extension would require a NEW audit.
