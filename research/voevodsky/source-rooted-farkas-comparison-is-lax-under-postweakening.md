# Source-rooted Farkas comparison is lax under postweakening

A candidate directed comparison between the staged and direct Farkas proofs is generated by the primitive source-row relation `(a,b;c)->(a+c,b+c;0)` for `S=[0,1]`. Test postcomposition with a public target weakening `x<=T -> x<=U`, U>=T. Postwhiskering adds U-T to the surplus. Normalizing *before* postwhiskering gives `(T-1,T;U-T)`; normalizing the postwhiskered proof directly gives `(U-1,U;0)`. They are different proof packets whenever U>T. A SECOND normalization of the first route gives the latter. Hence strict naturality fails, while a directed comparison triangle joins after explicitly retaining the source-row normalization cell.

Fresh `check_farkas_comparison_whiskering.py` passes 30 rational postweakening squares, observes 18 strict failures, and verifies `normalize(post(p))=normalize(post(normalize(p)))` where both sides are typed. It refuses a foreign primitive source root and a cyclic authority-support DAG. The initial candidate script had a loop-arity error, corrected before the passing run. This is local mathematical proof rewriting; the original Farkas 1-category still has no declared 2-cells, and the support DAG checks only root reachability—not independent authentication of an owner or physical event.

The comparison thus needs at least a **lax, source-rooted 2-cell structure** with a coherent residual normalization after whiskering. Treating the normal form as a strictly functorial replacement would repeat the minimum-surplus mistake. Next: independently replay the small comparison triangle from its source equations, then test horizontal/vertical composition of two such cells and a hostile loss of the primitive row-bound dependency. Analytic S,A,R,C,G identification remains a separate typed-map problem.
