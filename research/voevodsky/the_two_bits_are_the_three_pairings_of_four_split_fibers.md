# The two bits are the three pairings of four split fibers

Let \(L_{\rm raw}\) be the lattice generated by the four component differences \(d_i\), and let

\[
L_{\rm sat}=E_7^+\cong A_1^3
\]

be its primitive closure. In the orthogonal root frame \((\alpha_{12},\alpha_{13},\alpha_{14})\), the four differences are

\[
(1,1,1),\quad(1,-1,-1),\quad(-1,1,-1),\quad(-1,-1,1).
\]

Hence

\[
L_{\rm raw}
=
\{(n_1,n_2,n_3)\in\mathbb Z^3:
 n_1\equiv n_2\equiv n_3\pmod2\}.
\]

Its inclusion in \(L_{\rm sat}=\mathbb Z^3\) has Smith diagonal

\[
(1,2,2),
\]

and therefore

\[
L_{\rm sat}/L_{\rm raw}\cong(\mathbb Z/2)^2.
\]

The three nonzero elements have a canonical geometric description. They are represented by half-sums associated with the three perfect matchings of four split fibers:

\[
\begin{aligned}
12|34&:\quad \frac{d_1+d_2}{2},\\
13|24&:\quad \frac{d_1+d_3}{2},\\
14|23&:\quad \frac{d_1+d_4}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Using the complementary pair gives the same quotient class because
\(d_1+d_2+d_3+d_4=0\). The sum of the three nonzero classes is zero, exactly as in the Klein four group.

Thus the two-bit ambiguity is not fundamentally an ordered pair of unexplained coefficients. It is the choice of whether the transported integral thimble lies in the raw component-difference lattice, and if not, which pairing of the four split fibers is required to make its half-sum integral.

This also explains why automorphisms naturally permute the three nonzero classes: they permute the three perfect matchings of four geometric fibers.

The remaining based-path problem now has a precise geometric target. Follow the physical relative path through the reflection-adapted pencil and determine which critical fibers are joined by its vanishing thimble. That pairing directly gives the quotient class; only afterward need it be translated into the conventional \((e_6,v_{\rm alg})\) coordinates.
