# Reframing the top coherencer as octahedral compression in a stable correspondence category

## The compression unit

For composable analytic maps

\[
X\xrightarrow fY\xrightarrow gZ,
\]

do not replace the path merely by \(gf\). Retain the three cone packages

\[
\operatorname{Cone}(f),
\qquad
\operatorname{Cone}(gf),
\qquad
\operatorname{Cone}(g).
\]

There are canonical comparison maps

\[
a:
\operatorname{Cone}(f)
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Cone}(gf),
\]

\[
b:
\operatorname{Cone}(gf)
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Cone}(g).
\]

Their composite is canonically nullhomotopic. This is the elementary octahedral compression cell.

The lower path becomes one higher-level arrow while its failure of invertibility remains present as cone data.

## Adjoint direction

The backward Green path supplies the mate diagram

\[
Z^*
\xrightarrow{g^*}
Y^*
\xrightarrow{f^*}
X^*.
\]

A Beck--Chevalley cell compares cone formation before and after this adjoint reversal. The required metric enhancement identifies the analytic adjoints with the independently sourced Green maps rather than defining them from the forward path.

## Cutoff curvature

At finite cutoff, projection does not commute strictly with global sewing. The residual

\[
A_X
=
P_XF(I-P_X)
\]

should be retained as the curvature 2-cell of the finite cone comparison.

The completion problem is to prove either

\[
A_X\to0
\]

in the completed graph topology, or to construct compatible transgressions \(H_X\) satisfying

\[
A_X=\partial H_X.
\]

Thus the top coherencer is a completion-level nullhomotopy of cutoff curvature, not a false strict finite equality.

## Higher-level object

The proper target is a stable correspondence system whose simplices are:

- vertices: closed state/port carriers;
- edges: closed correspondences;
- triangles: cone comparison maps;
- tetrahedra: octahedral compatibilities and adjoint mates;
- 4-cells: compatibility of these octahedra under two successive path compressions.

A literal 4-simplex is then one chart of the stable higher structure, not the primitive definition of the top coherencer.

## Metric enhancement

After stable compression, attach the Green/Hodge operator

\[
Q_D
=
D+D^*.
\]

The metric layer must prove closedness and semiboundedness. It is not generated by simplicial identities.

The Krein--Langer negative sector belongs here, not in the underlying cone combinatorics.

## Determinant and chamber

Apply the determinant functor only after cone compression:

\[
\operatorname{Cone}(gf)
\longmapsto
\operatorname{Det}
\operatorname{Cone}(gf).
\]

The complete primal--dual flag retains the right and left kernel data. Xi is one distinguished minor of this determinant package.

The chamber theorem

\[
\Delta_\Xi
\ne0
\]

off the seam is a separate open-condition theorem. It does not follow from the octahedral or Beck--Chevalley laws.

## Practical constructor sequence

1. Define closed finite-cutoff correspondences \(f_X,g_X\).
2. Form all three cone packages.
3. Construct the canonical octahedral comparison maps.
4. Insert the rank-one cutoff curvature as a specified 2-cell.
5. Prove compatibility under \(X\le Y\).
6. Pass to the closed pro-limit.
7. Identify backward cone maps with the independent Green mates.
8. Add the Hodge metric enhancement.
9. Apply the determinant/flag functor.
10. Prove the separate incidence-chamber theorem.

## Disposition

The first executable model of higher-level path compression is now implemented. The analytic top coherencer should be sought as a completion-compatible family of octahedral cone cells, not as an isolated scalar 4-simplex filler.
