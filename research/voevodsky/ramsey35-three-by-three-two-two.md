# A red K3,3 has at most three doubled pairs across both sides

In the notation of the [one-side result](ramsey35-three-by-three-side.md), the additional case (a,b)=(2,2) is excluded. The result concerns arbitrary legal 35-vertex graphs, not just incidences near the cube.

Let the two red-biclique sides be A,B. Each vertex outside the six sees only A or only B or neither in red. Write X,Y for the first two classes, Z for the third. In (2,2), |X|=|Y|=13 and |Z|=3. Each of the two 16-sets X+Z and Y+Z is blue to a blue three, hence a catalogued (3,6) graph. Each has three marked blue fives from the root neighborhoods: a middle five meeting each of two disjoint outer fives in one point. Those intersections are the two doubles. The edge count of the opposite 16-graph Y+Z is 12+e(X), by summing degrees on the deleted 19 vertices A+B+X (their internal red edge count is 9+15+e(X)).

For each meeting pair of fives there is a marked (3,7) common blue graph on 21 vertices of edge count 44+k, where k counts the red edges between their private fours. One has 7<=k<=13: the lower bound is the 21-catalogue floor, and k>=14 would give two private vertices sharing all four opposite private vertices and their own root. The third root has degree four in this 21-graph. Deleting its closed neighborhood leaves precisely the opposite 16-graph, and its four neighbors give four attachment sets in that graph. Their sizes are determined from the local side: a single-only vertex x has seven red neighbors besides its root, hence support size 7-deg_X(x) in Y+Z. The supports must agree with its observed edges to Z. This applies twice in each direction. The four attachment sets per panel are not treated as four independent choices.

The census generated 244,272 marked sides before orbit reduction and reciprocal filtering. The two 16-graphs must identify Z consistently: induced colors agree, and each glued neutral vertex has red degree eight. Of 1,081,448 such surviving identifications after reciprocal base-type checks, 112 supported all four panels separately. In each of these 112 cases a known group of eight single-only X vertices meets a group of eight single-only Y vertices. Both sets of panels prescribe the same 64 edge positions from opposite directions. **Every case forces opposite colors on at least one individual edge**, with 4 to 22 forced disagreements per case. No model of the remaining unspecified edges can repair this. A separate Boolean selector encoding checked all 112 empty intersections; no timeout was counted as negative.

Together with the previous exclusion of a fully doubled side and the identity |Z|=a+b-1, only (0,1), (0,2), (1,1), (1,2) remain, up to interchange. In particular at most two vertices can be blue to all six vertices of a red K3,3. Two quartet cells sharing three labels already consume two doubles on the root/cell side. Therefore at most **one** pair of the three shared labels can occur on a further core cell: in particular their shared triple cannot contain two whole pair masks. Neither K3,3 itself nor the remaining eight-singleton profiles are excluded by this test.
