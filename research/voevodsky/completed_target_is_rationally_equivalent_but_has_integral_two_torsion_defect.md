# The completed target is rationally equivalent but has an integral two-torsion defect

## Question

After restoring ambient descent and all two-wall symmetries, how does the completed target differ homologically from the audited incidence source?

## Claim boundary

This computes integral homology and the cone of the exact chain map. It does not interpret the residual torsion as a physical observable.

## Source and completed target

The source complex is

\[
A_1=\mathbb Z^4\xrightarrow{K}A_0=\mathbb Z^3,
\]

where \(K\) is surjective and

\[
\ker K=\mathbb Z(1,1,1,1)^T.
\]

The completed target is

\[
B_1=\mathbb Z^4\xrightarrow{D_{\rm ext}}B_0=\mathbb Z^3.
\]

The chain map has components

\[
P:A_1\to B_1,
\qquad
J:A_0\to B_0,
\]

and satisfies

\[
D_{\rm ext}P=JK.
\]

The degree-one map is unimodular, \(\det P=1\). The degree-zero map has Smith invariants

\[
(1,2,2),
\]

and cokernel \((\mathbb Z/2)^2\).

## Homology

Since \(D_{\rm ext}=JKP^{-1}\), with \(K\) surjective and \(J\) injective,

\[
\ker D_{\rm ext}=P(\ker K).
\]

Therefore

\[
H_1(B)\cong\mathbb Z,
\]

generated by

\[
P(1,1,1,1)^T=(1,1,0,0)^T
=f_{\log}+f_{\rm face}.
\]

The image is \(\operatorname{im}J\), so

\[
H_0(B)=\mathbb Z^3/\operatorname{im}J
\cong(\mathbb Z/2)^2.
\]

By contrast,

\[
H_1(A)\cong\mathbb Z,
\qquad H_0(A)=0.
\]

The chain map is an isomorphism on \(H_1\) and introduces only the degree-zero saturation defect.

## Cone calculation

With cone differentials

\[
d_2=\begin{pmatrix}P\\-K\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
d_1=\begin{pmatrix}D_{\rm ext}&J\end{pmatrix},
\]

one has \(d_1d_2=0\). Since \(P\) is unimodular, \(d_2\) is injective. If \((b,a)\in\ker d_1\), write \(b=Px\); then

\[
0=D_{\rm ext}Px+Ja=J(Kx+a).
\]

Injectivity of \(J\) gives \(a=-Kx\), so \((b,a)=d_2x\). Hence

\[
H_2(\operatorname{Cone})=H_1(\operatorname{Cone})=0,
\]

and

\[
H_0(\operatorname{Cone})\cong(\mathbb Z/2)^2.
\]

## Coefficient dependence

After tensoring with \(\mathbb Q\), \(\mathbb R\), or \(\mathbb C\), the map \(J\) becomes invertible and the cone is acyclic. The source and completed target are therefore chain-equivalent over characteristic zero.

At odd primes the same holds. Only the prime two retains the defect. Consequently a real or complex period pairing cannot by itself detect whether the integral conductor lattice has been saturated correctly.

## Physical consequence

The closed interference class is completely transported at characteristic zero. What remains unresolved is integral parity/orientation data in degree zero. Any claim that a complex-valued contour integral verifies the full integral source descent is too strong: it necessarily forgets the \((\mathbb Z/2)^2\) defect.

A decisive physical completion must therefore include a source-derived integral normalization, parity-sensitive record, or orientation datum in addition to a complex period.

## Disposition

Ambient descent, full symmetry, and characteristic-zero homology are complete. The sole exact algebraic residual is the integral two-primary saturation defect. It is not yet a physical effect or record.
