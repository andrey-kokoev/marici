# Archival loss blocks new replay without erasing a past local verdict

A process-local content-addressed archive initially supplies exact primitive row bytes and a fresh P->Q->P mathematical replay passes. Remove the rows while retaining only their digest and a previously recorded local verdict: a NEW independent replay refuses with `ARCHIVE_UNAVAILABLE`, while the retained earlier result remains a historical claim about what the local checker did. Substituting different rows under the same key refuses with `ARCHIVE_INTEGRITY_FAILURE`; restoring matching rows permits fresh replay. Fresh `check_archive_loss_after_replay.py` exercises all four stages.

This makes two retention horizons explicit. Historical receipt retention can record a prior checked conclusion without retaining all work inputs, but independently auditing that conclusion later needs the source rows again or a reliably available archived copy. The receipt here is NOT externally authenticated and cannot prove a real physical execution; a caller cannot elevate it to present owner authorization. Archival availability and capability revocation must be audited separately.

The bounded replay-retention question is resolved at this fixture: a digest-only reference has an availability obligation and historical mathematical verdicts do not automatically entail fresh replay. A successor should test dual-provider archival substitution when one archive is removed or revoked, distinguishing identical bytes from provenance/authority transfer and measuring only declared receipt fields. Analytic S,A,R,C,G mapping remains deferred.
