# Forced immediate exit targets

Fix a finite valid retained assembly. Allow only additions of electron packets by NE transfers; ask for packet reachability, not contact certification. In the old directed packet graph, consider the strongly connected components (SCCs) of packets that cannot reach an electron. This stranded subgraph is successor-closed among old packets. Every stranded packet reaches a terminal SCC.

Every cycle has constant origin since all edges have nonnegative origin increment. Reader and NN advances have positive increment. Electrons have no outgoing operations. The only remaining cyclic links are PN and inverse-PN. These pair packets across the same marked face, preserving aliases and origin and applying the involution `[3,1,2,0,4]` in either direction. Chart and packet interning identify the return with the original packet. Each SCC is therefore a singleton or a single P/N pair; it has at most one neutron. That neutron has at most one NE target.

A terminal stranded SCC cannot reach any old packet outside itself. To escape after adding only electrons, it must transfer directly to a new electron. This forces its unique NE target, or makes electron-only rescue impossible if none exists. Conversely, when all such targets exist and their union is jointly legal, adding them rescues every terminal SCC and hence every old packet. They are addable directly from retained readers; added electrons are already exits. Thus the distinct forced targets constitute the unique inclusion-minimal immediate rescue set. Extra unnecessary electrons can yield multiple nonminimal supersets. An assembly not already solved and admitting no one-electron rescue accordingly has at most one two-electron rescue set. This argument does not price face witnesses or cover a reader advance before either exit.
