# Original-chain ownership survives every tiny redex choice

Fresh `check_original_chain_all_tiny_schedules.py` follows every individually enabled COPY/Q1/Q2/ERASE redex choice for all 86 initial inputs with support length n<=2 and both query indices 0..n+1, quotienting by alpha-equivalent full port graphs. It verifies original B/N suffix ownership by COPY in all 6,400 states and across 13,431 transitions: COPY.p meets the first remaining original node, each original bit aux leads to the next original principal, and no query/eraser principal meets an original node. A deliberately constructed fully linear forest with E.p meeting a marked original B0 while COPY.p meets a different NIL fails this ownership check.

This strengthens the empirical invariant beyond one schedule and demonstrates why a typed original-row label matters: port degree/forest shape alone cannot identify which chain COPY is obliged to consume. It still is not an arbitrary-n induction. The required rule proof is finite: COPY--B0/B1/NIL removes exactly the original head and reconnects successor COPY.p to the old tail; no other admitted rule touches original nodes or creates nodes labelled original. Its premises depend on the constructor and on fresh copied nodes never inheriting original identity.

Next encode the original/copy distinction as a formal provenance type on agents and verify the three COPY and twelve non-COPY rule templates preserve ownership symbolically, including when two consumer principals wait at the copier auxiliaries. Then separate this bookkeeping type from observed real-world provenance: a net's internal label is not an owner grant.
