# The generalized Nevanlinna functional model supplies the conditional abstract boundary relation

## Scope

The interior positivity gate is not assumed. Start with the meromorphic reflected multiplier

\[
\Theta(z)=B(z)^{-1}S(z)
\]

of finite negative index \(\kappa\), together with its source-fixed endpoint residues.

The goal here is only to construct an abstract boundary realization of the already identified kernel. It does not prove that \(\kappa=0\), identify the realization with the physical Clifford operator, or prove endpoint leverage.

## Cayley transform

On the locus where \(1-\Theta(z)\neq0\), define

\[
M(z)
=i\frac{1+\Theta(z)}{1-\Theta(z)}.
\]

The inverse relation is

\[
\Theta(z)
=
\frac{M(z)-i}{M(z)+i}.
\]

The generalized Nevanlinna kernel is

\[
N_M(z,w)
=
\frac{M(z)-\overline{M(w)}}{z-\bar w}.
\]

A direct calculation gives a diagonal congruence between \(N_M\) and the reflected de Branges kernel

\[
K_\Theta(z,w)
=
\frac{1-\Theta(z)\overline{\Theta(w)}}{-i(z-\bar w)}.
\]

Explicitly,

\[
N_M(z,w)
=
\frac{2}
{(1-\Theta(z))(1-\overline{\Theta(w)})}
K_\Theta(z,w).
\]

Away from Cayley poles, the two kernels therefore have the same number of negative squares.

## Reproducing-kernel Pontryagin space

Let \(\mathcal P(M)\) be the reproducing-kernel Pontryagin space generated by the sections

\[
N_M(\cdot,w).
\]

Its negative index is \(\kappa\), after quotienting the kernel radical.

The Krein--Langer decomposition identifies its negative feature with the model space \(K_B\). If the odd endpoint line is retained separately, use the augmented carrier

\[
\mathcal P_{aug}(M)
=
\mathcal P(M)
\mathbin{[\dotplus]}
\mathscr H_{end}^{odd}.
\]

No positive completion is imposed.

## Maximal multiplication relation

Define the maximal relation \(T_{max}\) in \(\mathcal P(M)\) by declaring that

\[
\{f,g\}\in T_{max}
\]

when there exist scalars \(c,d\) such that

\[
g(z)-zf(z)
=
c+M(z)d.
\]

After the standard quotient by nonunique coefficient pairs, define

\[
\Gamma_0\{f,g\}=d,
\]

\[
\Gamma_1\{f,g\}=-c.
\]

If coefficient uniqueness fails at an exceptional constant direction, these maps are interpreted as a boundary relation rather than an ordinary boundary operator. This is the natural level of generality for a possibly degenerate generalized Nevanlinna function.

## Minimal symmetric relation

Define

\[
T_{min}
=
\ker\Gamma_0
\cap
\ker\Gamma_1.
\]

Equivalently, \(T_{min}\) is the closed multiplication relation obtained by imposing

\[
g(z)=zf(z).
\]

The functional-model Green identity is

\[
[g,f']-[f,g']
=
\Gamma_1\{f,g\}
\overline{\Gamma_0\{f',g'\}}
-
\Gamma_0\{f,g\}
\overline{\Gamma_1\{f',g'\}},
\]

with the Pontryagin inner product on \(\mathcal P(M)\).

Thus

\[
(\mathbb C,\Gamma_0,\Gamma_1)
\]

is an ordinary boundary triple when the coefficient maps are single-valued and surjective, and otherwise a generalized boundary relation.

## Weyl function

Let \(z\) lie in a regularity domain. A defect vector satisfying

\[
g=zf
\]

obeys

\[
(z-\zeta)f(\zeta)
=
c+M(\zeta)d.
\]

Choose

\[
c=-M(z)d.
\]

Then

\[
f(\zeta)
=
\frac{M(\zeta)-M(z)}{z-\zeta}d.
\]

Its boundary values are

\[
\Gamma_0\{f,zf\}=d,
\]

\[
\Gamma_1\{f,zf\}=M(z)d.
\]

Therefore the Weyl function of the model boundary relation is exactly

\[
M(z).
\]

Through the Cayley congruence, its Weyl kernel is exactly the reflected de Branges kernel already obtained from the contour calculation.

## Endpoint poles

Suppose \(M\) has a simple endpoint pole at \(z_\partial\):

\[
M(z)
=
\frac{\rho_\partial}{z_\partial-z}
+M_{reg}(z).
\]

The pole contributes a one-dimensional boundary residue feature with metric sign determined by \(\rho_\partial\) and the orientation convention.

For the paired endpoints, passing to even and odd parity diagonalizes the swap form. The even line is positive and the odd line is negative. Hence the augmented model starts with one endpoint negative square in addition to the interior index \(\kappa\).

This reproduces the previously derived endpoint signature without asserting cancellation.

## Source observation

Compose the completed generic boundary observation with the Cayley multiplier:

\[
\mathscr G_r
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Hol}(\mathfrak S)
\longrightarrow
\mathcal P_{aug}(M).
\]

On finite kernel packets this is the congruence

\[
K_\Theta
\longmapsto
N_M.
\]

The translated-Gaussian source detects the full interior negative subspace \(K_B\). The endpoint graph detects the separate odd residue line.

Thus the functional model retains, rather than conceals, every known negative direction.

## What the construction proves

The abstract model supplies:

1. a closed minimal symmetric multiplication relation;
2. a maximal adjoint relation;
3. boundary maps or a generalized boundary relation;
4. the Green identity;
5. Weyl function \(M\);
6. the reflected kernel through Cayley congruence;
7. the interior Krein--Langer index;
8. the endpoint parity residue block.

## What remains physical

The abstract model is canonical for the kernel, but it is not yet the external physical realization requested by the semilocal programme.

One still needs a source-authorized unitary or contractive comparison between:

\[
\mathcal P_{aug}(M)
\]

and the closed Heisenberg/Clifford bicomplex carrying the Green/Wronskian traces.

The existing signed contour identity determines the pullback forms on the analytic core. It does not prove equality of completed graph norms or provide a positive comparison.

## Successor issue

Convolution multiplication acts diagonally on the original reflected-kernel observations. The Cayley transform is nonlinear in \(\Theta\), so prime and conductor changes do not automatically induce bounded operators between the corresponding Pontryagin models.

Naturality therefore requires one of:

1. a bounded kernel-congruence operator between successive models;
2. a common ambient boundary relation containing all successor models;
3. retention of successor maps only on the source pullbacks.

The third option is already available. The first two remain stronger realization problems.

## Conditional positive reduction

If the interior arithmetic theorem gives

\[
K_B=\{0\},
\]

then \(\mathcal P(M)\) is Hilbert before endpoint augmentation. The only negative direction is the retained odd endpoint residue.

The final positive reduction is then exactly the leverage inequality

\[
b_r^*b_r
\preceq
A_{S,r}^*A_{S,r}.
\]

The boundary relation identifies the row \(b_r\); it does not prove the inequality.

## Disposition

The canonical kernel now has an abstract generalized boundary realization with the correct Weyl function, Green identity, interior negative index, and endpoint residue signature.

The remaining boundary-triple gate is no longer existence of an abstract model. It is identification of that model with the physical Clifford/Green carrier, followed by the odd endpoint leverage estimate after interior defect vanishing.
