# Clifford products measure unresolved prime-index planes, not edge-route composition

## Question

Does the Clifford product of blind prime-index generators predict the blind generator of a combined arithmetic cube?

## Claim boundary

For the probe-induced Hankel form, the Clifford product of radical vectors canonically records exterior volume inside the unresolved index subspace. It does not directly compose one-dimensional edge-route classes: its exterior degree is two, while the current arithmetic checker acts on one-chains. A route-composition claim requires a separately defined lift to higher cubical chains.

## Radical polynomial space

Let \(W_n\) be represented by polynomials with shell degrees one through \(n\). For \(m<n\) distinct settings \(T=\{t_1,\ldots,t_m\}\), define

\[
q_T(z)=z\prod_{r=1}^{m}(z-t_r).
\]

The radical of the probe-induced form is exactly

\[
R_{n,T}=q_T(z)\,k[z]_{\leq n-m-1}.
\]

Hence

\[
\dim R_{n,T}=n-m.
\]

For six shell directions and four total evaluations, the radical plane has basis

\[
a=q_T(z),
\qquad
b=zq_T(z).
\]

## Clifford product

Because \(a,b\in\operatorname{rad}Q_T\),

\[
Q_T(a,a)=Q_T(a,b)=Q_T(b,b)=0.
\]

Their Clifford product therefore reduces to their exterior product:

\[
c(a)c(b)=\varepsilon(a\wedge b),
\qquad
c(a)c(b)+c(b)c(a)=0.
\]

The bivector \(a\wedge b\) is nonzero and spans

\[
\Lambda^2R_{6,T}.
\]

It measures the oriented unresolved two-plane. It is not another vector in \(R_{6,T}\), and it is not an edge flow.

## Effect of another setting

Add a setting \(s\). Evaluation on the radical plane has rank one because

\[
b(s)=s\,a(s).
\]

The surviving radical line is generated by

\[
b-sa=(z-s)q_T(z)=q_{T\cup\{s\}}(z).
\]

Thus one setting removes one metric-null dimension by an exact polynomial multiplication law. The bivector \(a\wedge b\) records the plane that existed before restriction; it does not equal the surviving vector.

Adding a second independent setting \(u\) removes the remaining radical because

\[
q_{T\cup\{s\}}(u)\neq0.
\]

## Typing obstruction to route multiplication

The current probe complex is

\[
E=C_1\longrightarrow C_0=V.
\]

A blind route lies in a kernel inside \(C_1\). The external product of two one-chain classes belongs naturally to

\[
C_1\otimes C_1\longrightarrow C_2
\]

of a product cube. Likewise, the Clifford product of two radical vectors has exterior degree two. Mapping it back to \(C_1\) would require an additional contraction or readout map, none of which is presently source-derived.

Therefore the naive proposal that two blind edge routes Clifford-multiply into another blind edge route is ill-typed. The corrected prediction is:

> Clifford products organize higher-dimensional unresolved index volumes; cubical chain products should realize them in matching homological degree.

## Strongest falsification attempt

Compute the six-direction, four-evaluation Hankel form exactly. Verify that \(q_T\) and \(zq_T\) form its radical, their wedge is nonzero, their Clifford anticommutator and squares vanish, and adding \(s=2/3\) leaves exactly the line generated by \((z-s)q_T\). Verify that adding \(u=3/5\) makes the form nondegenerate. Explicitly reject equality between the bivector coefficient object and the surviving vector by degree and dimension.

## Disposition

The Clifford structure earns predictive content: it predicts the dimensions and exterior volumes of unresolved prime-index subspaces and the rank-one removal law under new settings. It does not yet provide multiplication of edge-route classes. The next admissible extension is a graded cubical chain model carrying exterior degree \(r\) to cubical degree \(r\).
