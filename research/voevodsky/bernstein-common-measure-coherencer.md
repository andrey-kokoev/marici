# Bernstein common-measure coherencer

## Question

Do the different finite-difference orders in the Bernstein matrix require separate nearby-saddle estimates, or are they moments of one common observer measure?

## Claim boundary

They are moments of one common base measure whenever the underlying frequency functional is positive. Rank-two positivity then becomes a variance identity. For the completed prime contribution the corresponding frequency functional is signed, so a relative form bound remains necessary.

## Bernstein matrix

Use the Bernstein-type basis

\[
e_j(y)=(1-y)^j.
\]

The transformed Hankel matrix has entries

\[
M_{ij}
=
\Delta_h^{i+j+1}F(t).
\]

If \(F\) has a positive frequency representation

\[
F(t)=\int_0^\infty e^{-tr}\,d\sigma(r),
\]

then

\[
M_{ij}
=
\int_0^\infty
e^{-tr}(1-e^{-hr})^{i+j+1}
\,d\sigma(r).
\]

Define

\[
d\mu_{t,h}(r)
=
e^{-tr}(1-e^{-hr})\,d\sigma(r)
\]

and

\[
z(r)=1-e^{-hr}.
\]

All entries are moments of this single measure:

\[
M_{ij}
=
\int z(r)^{i+j}\,d\mu_{t,h}(r).
\]

Thus the apparently different saddles for orders \(i+j+1\) are coordinate projections of one Gram object, not independent observers.

## Rank-two determinant

Let

\[
\mu_k=
\int z^k\,d\mu.
\]

Then

\[
M_2
=
\begin{pmatrix}
\mu_0&\mu_1\\
\mu_1&\mu_2
\end{pmatrix}
\]

and

\[
\det M_2
=
\mu_0\mu_2-
\mu_1^2
=
\mu_0^2
\operatorname{Var}_{\mu/\mu_0}(z).
\]

A positive common measure therefore controls the even mixed entry automatically by covariance. Separate diagonal sign estimates are unnecessary in the positive sector.

## Beta fixture

For the radial filter with Lebesgue measure \(dr\), the rank-two base weight is

\[
e^{-tr}(1-e^{-hr})\,dr.
\]

Under

\[
Z=e^{-hR},
\qquad
a=t/h,
\]

the normalized variable satisfies

\[
Z\sim\operatorname{Beta}(a,2).
\]

For \(Y=1-Z\),

\[
\operatorname{Var}(Y)
=
\frac{2a}{(a+2)^2(a+3)}.
\]

This gives an exact positive rank-two covariance margin for the model frequency measure.

## Prime obstruction

The regularized prime cosine functional is not a positive measure. It defines a signed perturbation \(d\nu_P\) of the archimedean base measure. Entrywise estimates at nearby saddles discard the common Gram structure.

The appropriate target is the relative polynomial inequality

\[
\left|
\int |p(z(r))|^2
 e^{-tr}(1-e^{-hr})
\,d\nu_P(r)
\right|
\leq
\theta
\int |p(z(r))|^2
\,d\mu_\Gamma(r)
\]

with one \(\theta<1\) uniform in polynomial degree and the admissible parameters.

At rank two, a weaker covariance perturbation bound could suffice, but it would not extend automatically to all ranks.

## Coherence interpretation

The higher coherencer is the common measure \(\mu_{t,h}\). Its moments simultaneously produce every pairwise Bernstein observer relation. Associativity of multiplication by \(z\) supplies all higher algebraic coherence identities.

There is no algebraic residue in the positive reference sector. The only residue is whether the signed prime functional is relatively bounded by the archimedean Gram norm.

## Disposition

The full matrix should not be analyzed as unrelated frequency saddles. It is one polynomial Gram problem. The next decisive object is a source-valid relative bound for the regularized prime functional on the weighted polynomial closure generated by \(z=1-e^{-hr}\). Without that bound, covariance coherence does not prove RH.
