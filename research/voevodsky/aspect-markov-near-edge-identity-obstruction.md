# Aspect–Markov near-edge identity obstruction

## Question

Does the exact equality between an Aspect route Gram matrix and a Markov Green covariance construct the first cross-sector edge?

## Claim boundary

The audit concerns a fixed finite mathematical matrix. It makes no optical detection or physical-identification claim.

## Exact coincidence

Aspect's nonorthonormal route frame uses \(S=\operatorname{diag}(2,1)\), giving

\[
S^TS=\operatorname{diag}(4,1).
\]

A two-site scalar metric-transition Markov object with \(M_0=4\), \(M_1=1\), and zero transfer covariance has the same Green matrix. In chosen coordinates, the identity comparison is invertible and has zero residual.

## Failed identity provenance

The optical basis labels are route labels; the Markov basis labels are site labels. No source-derived correspondence identifies them. Mapping both to abstract labels \(e_0,e_1\) is a chosen linear-algebra identification, not a derived cross-sector map.

Therefore the candidate is a near-edge, not an admitted edge. Equal matrices, positivity, and zero comparison residual do not construct common-object identity.

## Contract repair

The ten-field v1 edge contract was syntactically satisfied and therefore insufficient. Version 2 adds an eleventh field, `common_object_identity`, requiring:

- source-derived correspondence between retained labels;
- faithful quotient coordinates;
- coefficient and ordering compatibility;
- explicit rejection of identity inferred only from matrix invariants.

The near-edge is the regression fixture: it passes every coordinate test and fails identity provenance.

## Disposition

Aspect now supplies a credible second sector vertex mapping route frames to finite Green forms, but no Aspect–Markov edge is admitted. The first missing object is a source-derived route-to-site correspondence, not another matrix calculation.
