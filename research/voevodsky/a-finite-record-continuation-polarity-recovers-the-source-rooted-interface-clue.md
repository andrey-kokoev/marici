# A finite record/continuation polarity recovers the source-rooted interface clue

The prior results `research/voevodsky/sufficient-state-is-relative-to-permitted-continuations.md`, `research/nima/stable-carrier-data-is-a-record-control-formal-concept.md`, and `research/nima/interface-coherence-closes-as-a-source-rooted-proof-dag.md` suggest one architecture: declared records, compatible future controls, and source-rooted proof support. They are not interchangeable theorems; Nima's commutant is literal only on an operator-algebra carrier. Here is an exact finite CLASSICAL polarity on the two Farkas proof histories from the minimum-surplus counterexample.

Let H consist of the direct proof `(2,3);c=0` and staged proof `(1,2);c=1`, both of the same public inclusion. For an indistinguishability relation E on histories, let Compat(E) be the controls whose outputs are equal on every E-related pair. For a set C of controls, let Ind(C) identify histories giving equal outputs under all controls in C. Then `E subset Ind(C) iff C subset Compat(E)`: a contravariant Galois connection. Public inclusion and target-residual controls admit one coarse class; adding the path-bound proof-replay control splits it into two singleton classes. Fresh `check_record_continuation_polarity.py` exhaustively checks 128 finite adjunction instances, the two equivalence profiles, and a separate finite source-rooted support DAG for a comparison node. Cyclic and unsupported support are rejected.

This makes the relationship among the four open questions precise within one tiny model: current public equality is a RECORD quotient; the allowed continuation language determines whether it is sufficient; proof selection is a CONTROL with distinct path outputs; and an operative comparison must be supported by source-rooted dependencies rather than authorized by equal endpoints. It does NOT construct a proof-homotopy comparing the direct/staged certificates, prove their operative equivalence, authenticate actual history, or assign analytic S,A,R,C,G roles. The finite DAG shows how a claimed comparison would be justified, not that a missing semantic/proof cell exists.

The next research gate is to replace the toy output relation by typed operations (including failure and live capability), then ask if a source-derived normalization/rewriting constructor generates a comparison cell for the two actual Farkas proofs. Only after the cell exists can a source-rooted DAG justify it. This is a nonredundant branch beyond merely picking a different surplus policy.
