# The stable cone target supplies a canonical suspension seam, but cross-dimension identification remains a comparison theorem

## New opportunity from the stable target

The prior twisted 2-Segal system requires a seam between the terminal presentation at stage \(k\) and the initial presentation at stage \(k+1\).

In the original presentation category, no source-derived dimension-raising map was found.

After realization in the stable category of closed cone packages, a canonical successor operation exists: suspension.

For a two-term cone package

\[
\mathcal C
=
[X\to Y],
\]

define

\[
\Sigma\mathcal C
=
[Y\to X[1]],
\]

with the standard shifted differential and orientation convention. More invariantly, \(\Sigma\) is the shift functor of the stable target.

## Candidate seam

Let

\[
\mathfrak R_{4,k}
\]

be the realized terminal cone package and

\[
\mathfrak R_{1,k+1}
\]

the realized initial package at the next stage.

The seam can be retyped as a comparison

\[
\chi_k:
\Sigma\mathfrak R_{4,k}
\to
\mathfrak R_{1,k+1}.
\]

This is more structured than an arbitrary map from the fourth presentation to the next first presentation. Its source is fixed canonically by the stable realization.

## Acceptance conditions

The comparison \(\chi_k\) must be:

1. a chain equivalence on the closed graph domains;
2. reciprocal-duality compatible;
3. natural for every internal presentation edge;
4. compatible with the octahedral cone cells;
5. compatible with cutoff completion;
6. orientation-preserving on the determinant line.

If these hold, the four-periodic system becomes suspension-twisted:

\[
\tau^4
\simeq
\Sigma.
\]

This is the stable analogue of a paracyclic or twisted-cyclic closure law.

## Relation to the existing order-four chart rotation

The retained common graph already has an internal chart rotation satisfying

\[
\tau_k^4=I
\]

at fixed \(k\). That rotation changes presentation while retaining the same underlying datum.

The suspension seam performs a different operation: it promotes the completed terminal cone to the next coherence degree.

Therefore the two operations should remain separate:

- internal chart rotation: order four at fixed stage;
- stable successor: suspension between stages.

A helical operator combines them only after \(\chi_k\) is supplied.

## What suspension resolves

Suspension supplies:

- the target type of the seam;
- its degree and orientation shift;
- its action on kernels and cokernels;
- its compatibility with cone triangles;
- its determinant-line parity.

It replaces an untyped dimension-raising arrow by a sharply typed comparison theorem.

## What remains source-dependent

The identity

\[
\Sigma\mathfrak R_{4,k}
\simeq
\mathfrak R_{1,k+1}
\]

must still be proved from the source realization. Stable formalism cannot identify two independently defined analytic packages merely because their grades differ by one.

The practical test is now explicit: compare their graph domains, differentials, reciprocal actions, and determinant orientations.

## Regrading option

A cleaner indexing may separate:

- realization dimension \(k\);
- homological suspension degree \(h\);
- four-presentation index \(i\).

Then the carrier is written

\[
V_{i,k,h}.
\]

The internal rotation changes \(i\), suspension changes \(h\), and any genuine arithmetic dimension successor changes \(k\). This prevents the formal suspension seam from being confused with an unconstructed physical dimension-raising map.

## Disposition

The stable cone realization supplies the first canonical seam candidate: suspension. It converts the old missing arrow into a concrete comparison

\[
\Sigma\mathfrak R_{4,k}
\simeq
\mathfrak R_{1,k+1}.
\]

The next analytic task is this comparison theorem, with realization dimension and homological degree kept separate.
