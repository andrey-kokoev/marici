# Nonduplicate source syzygies have convex proof bridges but no canonical cell

Duplicate primitive rows were NOT essential to competing zero-surplus proofs. On the irredundant square source `[0,1]^2` with rows `-x<=0,x<=1,-y<=0,y<=1`, the original proof `(0,1,0,0;c=1)` of x<=2 has two positive-bound normal-kernel choices: the x-cycle d_x=(1,1,0,0) and independent y-cycle d_y=(0,0,1,1). They yield different zero-surplus certificates `(1,2,0,0;0)` and `(0,1,1,1;0)` for the SAME target. Each of the four source inequalities defines a genuine facet; there is no duplicate-row identity to erase.

Their signed difference is in the joint normal-and-bound kernel. Since both endpoints are valid Farkas certificates of the same target, every convex interpolation between their multiplier vectors is also a valid zero-surplus certificate. Fresh `check_nonduplicate_syzygy_interpolation.py` verifies five such square interpolations, plus 45 rational cases for the nonduplicate but redundant one-dimensional rows `-x<=0,x<=1,2x<=3`, where the two normal forms `(1,2,0;0)` and `(2/3,1,1/3;0)` are joined. Extrapolation beyond the verified nonnegative segment is refused.

Thus the attempted obstruction is NOT lack of a mathematical continuous proof-packet path: the Farkas certificate fiber is convex. The first missing obligation is to specify an actual typed, source-rooted comparison CELL and its composition/coherence, preserving the distinct retained row histories. Convexity alone does not select a canonical path, authorize one as a 2-cell of the original Farkas 1-category, or create higher path equality. A stronger successor should test whether affine interpolation is natural under a nontrivial Farkas postcomposition with surplus and where a path-replay control first distinguishes competing constructions. Analytic S,A,R,C,G mapping stays deferred.
