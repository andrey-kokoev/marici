# A triple with three common red neighbors has a pair with exactly three

In a legal 35-vertex graph, let A be three vertices with three common red neighbors B. By the global K3,4 exclusion, these six vertices induce K3,3 and no further vertex sees all of either side in red. Each pair in A already shares B, so by the two-vertex bound it has either no or one extra common red neighbor. Call the pair doubled in the latter case. **Not all three pairs in A can be doubled.** The statement applies equally to B. It does not exclude K3,3.

Suppose all three pairs of A are doubled. The cells adjacent to A but outside B form a 12-vertex set X: three doubles and three groups of three single cells, one group for each root in A. The remaining H has order 17, is blue to A, and hence is a (3,6) graph. There are seven catalogued types. X is blue to B and cannot contain a blue six. A double ij can be red-adjacent in X only to singles of the third group k. It has at least one such neighbor: otherwise it joins the five X-neighbors of k to form a blue six.

For roots i,j in A, their four common neighbors are B plus their double. Write k_ij for the number of red edges between their two private fours. The common blue neighborhood of the pair is a (3,7) 21-type of size 44+k_ij in **edges**. It consists of H plus the third root and its three single cells. The third root therefore has degree three in this 21-type, and its three neighbors are independent. Deleting its closed neighborhood recovers H together with the attachment supports of the three singles. The complete catalogue has no degree-three vertex in either the 51- or 52-edge layer, so every k_ij>=9.

Degree counting gives e(X)=e(H)-20. An edge of X between singles occurs once in the sum of the three k_ij; an edge from a double to a single occurs twice. There are at most nine of the latter. Thus sum k_ij<=e(H)-11<=31, giving k_ij<=13. Scan *all* degree-three marks in layers 53..57 of the 21-catalogue; transport their three supports to canonical H coordinates and close under Aut(H) before joining. The three marked types need not be distinct. Only 49,398 unordered panel triples meet the edge budget, with labelings on a common H retained.

Two checks rule these out. First, enumerate the allowable single-to-single edges (red requires disjoint H-supports) and single-to-double edges in X with the prescribed degrees and the three cross counts. The 1,805 triangle-free, blue-six-free remaining templates **all** already have a single and an H vertex sharing at least five red neighbors. No unknown opposite-side attachment can undo that. Second, a separate SAT encoding on at most 36 X edges rules out all 49,398 choices using just those degree, triangle, blue-six and single/H codegree conditions. No 35-vertex extension search timeout is involved.

Consequences: two quartet cells at any root sharing three labels cannot have a common red neighbor in the core (otherwise those cells and the root would violate this result). For any K3,3 write a,b for the numbers of doubled pairs on its two sides. The number of outside vertices seeing neither side in red is a+b-1 by 8-regularity and triangle-freeness. Thus, up to exchanging sides, the remaining count patterns are (0,1), (0,2), (1,1), (1,2), (2,2). None is asserted feasible.
