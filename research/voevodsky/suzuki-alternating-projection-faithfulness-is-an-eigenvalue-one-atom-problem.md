# Suzuki alternating-projection faithfulness is an eigenvalue-one atom problem

## Positive contraction behind the construction

Let

\[
P=P_{H_+},
\qquad
Q=KPK=P_{KH_+},
\]

and restrict to `H_+`. The symmetric alternating-projection operator is

\[
A=PQP|_{H_+}.
\]

It satisfies

\[
0\preceq A\preceq I.
\]

The Suzuki intersection is

\[
V=H_+\cap KH_+.
\]

## The intersection is exactly the eigenvalue-one space

For `f in H_+`,

\[
\langle Af,f\rangle
=\langle Qf,f\rangle
=\|Qf\|^2.
\]

Since `Q` is an orthogonal projection,

\[
\|f\|^2-\langle Af,f\rangle
=\|(I-Q)f\|^2.
\]

Therefore

\[
Af=f
\iff
Qf=f
\iff
f\in H_+\cap KH_+=V.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
V=\ker(I-A),
\qquad
P_V=1_{\{1\}}(A).
}
\]

The alternating-projection limit is the spectral projection onto the atom at the endpoint `lambda=1`:

\[
A^n\xrightarrow[s]{n\to\infty}1_{\{1\}}(A).
\]

## Source-vector survival criterion

For a source vector `v in H_+`, let `mu_v` be its spectral measure for `A`. Then

\[
\|A^nv\|^2
=
\int_{[0,1]}\lambda^{2n}\,d\mu_v(\lambda)
\longrightarrow
\mu_v(\{1\}).
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\|P_Vv\|^2=\mu_v(\{1\}).
}
\]

Faithfulness of the projected source is not merely concentration near one. It requires a genuine spectral atom at one.

## Approximate invariance is insufficient

For every `epsilon>0`, a unit vector can have spectral measure supported in

\[
[1-\epsilon,1)
\]

and satisfy

\[
\langle Av,v\rangle\ge1-\epsilon
\]

while

\[
P_Vv=0.
\]

Likewise every finite alternating iterate `A^n v` can have large norm even though its strong limit is zero. Therefore no finite-depth positivity or small-leakage estimate proves that the Suzuki intersection carries a nonzero source vector.

This is the exact spectral version of the completion-collapse warning encountered in the semilocal quotient route.

## Closed-angle criterion and why it is too strong

If the Friedrichs angle between `H_+` and `KH_+` is bounded away from zero off their intersection, then there is `q<1` such that

\[
\|A^n-P_V\|\le q^n.
\]

This gives norm convergence and makes the endpoint atom stably detectable. But proving such a gap uniformly for the completed-zeta symbol is much stronger than constructing `P_V`. In infinite-dimensional Hardy geometry, the two subspaces may have angle zero even when their intersection is trivial.

Thus an approximate source eigenvector sequence

\[
\|(I-A)v_n\|\to0
\]

does not imply `V != 0` without compactness or a closed-range estimate for `I-A`.

## Exact missing coercive estimate

A source-side theorem sufficient to control the complement would be

\[
\|(I-P_V)f\|^2
\le C\,\|(I-Q)f\|^2
\qquad(f\in H_+),
\]

which is equivalent to a positive lower bound for `I-A` on `V^perp`. Such an estimate separates the exact intersection from near-intersection states and yields stable alternating-projection convergence.

But it still does not prove source survival. One additionally needs

\[
\mu_{A_{src}f}(\{1\})>0
\]

for the relevant arithmetic vectors, or an identity placing them directly in `V`.

## Relation to zeros

Under Suzuki's RH-dependent model-space identification, `V` maps to the nontrivial de Branges/model space generated by critical-line zero data. Off the hypothesis, the boundary involution and the contraction `A` remain defined, but the endpoint spectral atom is uncontrolled. This explains Suzuki's remark that deciding whether `V(0)=0` is extremely difficult.

The problem has moved from positivity to threshold spectral theory:

\[
\text{Does }PQP\text{ have an eigenvalue exactly at }1,
\text{ and does the source image charge it?}
\]

## Disposition

Alternating projections provide an unconditional positive contraction, but faithfulness is precisely

\[
\boxed{
\text{positive spectral mass at the endpoint atom }\lambda=1.
}
\]

Finite powers, approximate fixed vectors, and spectra accumulating at one do not establish this. A valid continuation needs either a source identity producing an exact fixed vector or a compactness/coercivity theorem that promotes controlled approximate fixed vectors to an endpoint eigenvector.
