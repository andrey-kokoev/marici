# A redundant square row is a split proof retraction, not an isomorphism

Adjoin primitive row `x+y<=2` to the unit-square inequalities; it equals the SUM of `x<=1` and `y<=1` bounds and changes no feasible points. Old proof vectors embed with zero new multiplier. A nonnegative retraction sends new multiplier z into both old upper-row multipliers: `(a,x,b,y,z)->(a,x+z,b,y+z)`. It preserves target normal and bound, including the forward image of old P->Q comparison. Retraction after inclusion is identity on old proof matrices, but inclusion after retraction is NOT identity on new ones: the one-row proof `(0,0,0,0,1)` of x+y<=2 becomes `(0,1,0,1,0)`.

Their difference `(0,-1,0,-1,1)` is a signed zero-normal, zero-bound relation, not a nonnegative standalone proof. Fresh `check_redundant_square_row_retraction.py` verifies these equations after correcting a checker error that had tried to apply a nonnegative-only retraction to that signed relation; its linear extension is the correct operation there. Thus equality of the feasible square does not imply proof-presentation equivalence as an invertible map: new row-labelled histories exist, and any inverse claim must compare or quotient them explicitly.

This is a noninvertible SPLIT mathematical proof-data morphism, not an owner-authorized source migration or an analytic S,A,R,C,G functor.
