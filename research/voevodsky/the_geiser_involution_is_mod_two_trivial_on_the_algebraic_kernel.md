# The Geiser involution is mod-two trivial on the algebraic kernel

## Question

Can the canonical deck involution of a degree-two del Pezzo surface provide the nontrivial parity action needed by the symmetry selector?

## Integral Picard action

Let

\[
S=\operatorname{Bl}_{p_1,\ldots,p_7}\mathbb P^2
\]

with canonical class

\[
K_S=-3H+E_1+\cdots+E_7,
\qquad K_S^2=2.
\]

The Geiser involution \(g\), the deck transformation of the anticanonical double cover, fixes \(K_S\) and acts by minus the identity on

\[
K_S^\perp\cong E_7(-1).
\]

Equivalently, on the full Picard lattice,

\[
g(D)=(D\cdot K_S)K_S-D.
\]

This is an integral isometry and satisfies \(g^2=1\).

## Mod-two consequence

For every algebraic-kernel vector \(D\in K_S^\perp\),

\[
g(D)=-D\equiv D\pmod2.
\]

Therefore the Geiser action on

\[
K_S^\perp/2K_S^\perp
\]

is the identity. Any two-bit support plane contained in the primitive algebraic kernel, including the source-supported plane generated by \(e_6,v_{\rm alg}\), inherits trivial mod-two Geiser action.

The elliptic quotient does not repair this: the deck involution restricts as inversion on the genus-one double cover and hence acts by \(-1\) on \(H^1(D;\mathbb Z)\), again the identity modulo two.

## Relation to the quartic involution

The source quartic symmetry \(t\mapsto-t\) exchanges the two nodal points. It has not been identified with the Geiser deck coordinate on the compactified del Pezzo surface. If that identification were established, this calculation would prove its parity action trivial and eliminate it as a selector.

If \(t\mapsto-t\) is instead a distinct surface automorphism, its action on the seven-dimensional algebraic kernel must still be computed. Node exchange on the boundary curve alone does not imply a nontrivial mod-two action on the support plane.

## Consequence

The two most visible symmetries now both fail as demonstrated selectors:

- loop-coordinate site exchange is identity on the displayed support plane;
- the canonical Geiser involution is identity modulo two on the entire algebraic kernel.

A symmetry-only resolution now requires a distinct automorphism with a proved nontrivial mod-two action, not merely a nontrivial integral sign action.

## Disposition

The canonical degree-two del Pezzo deck involution cannot distinguish the three remaining parity classes modulo two. The unresolved task is to identify the actual surface lift of \(t\mapsto-t\); if it is Geiser, the automorphism-selector route closes.
