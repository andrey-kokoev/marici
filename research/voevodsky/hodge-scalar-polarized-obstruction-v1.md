## 1. Every scalar top curve is bielliptic

Retain the universal scalar classification from iteration21, not merely the subsequent explicit equations. The group of the top curve C=C17 is generated by commuting rho of order four and a scalar sheet lift sigma of order two. Put h=rho^2. After choosing the scalar sign, all eight branch inertias over E' are sigma, and

    q:C -> D=C/<sigma>, degree2,

Let P=Prym(C/C9) be the connected anti-h part of J(C), with canonical principal polarization Xi. Its inclusion i:P->J(C) satisfies

## 2. An elliptic Prym subcurve produces a degree-2e curve map

Suppose j:T->P is an elliptic subcurve and deg j^*Xi=e. The composite j_C=i j is an elliptic embedding in J(C) of Theta degree2e. Let f:J(C)->T be its polarization adjoint, using the canonical degree-one polarization on T. Then

    f j_C = [2e].

Compose f with an Abel-Jacobi map to get phi:C->T. The Jacobian universal property gives phi_*=f, and polarization adjunction gives phi^*=j_C. Norm/pullback therefore yields

    [deg phi] = phi_* phi^* = [2e],
    deg phi = 2e.

This is the curve-map degree, not the degree (2e)^2 of an elliptic multiplication isogeny. In particular e=2 gives a degree-four curve map, not two or sixteen.

Since h acts as minus identity on P and preserves Theta_C, the adjoint satisfies f h_J=-f. The nonzero pullback of a holomorphic one-form from T under phi is thus h-anti-invariant.

## 3. Independence for degrees two and 2e: no coprimality shortcut

Every form pulled back from D by q is h-invariant, since h descends to a translation of D. Hence phi cannot factor through q: that would make its nonzero differential both invariant and anti-invariant in characteristic zero.

Let B normalize the joint image of (q,phi):C->D times T. Its function field contains C(D), and the extension C(C)/C(D) has degree TWO. Thus the joint degree is either one or two. In degree two the joint function field equals C(D), and phi factors through q, already ruled out. Therefore the joint map is birational.

The maps have even, noncoprime degrees. Independence follows from the quadratic extension and h-signs, not from a false coprimality claim and not from non-isogeny of the elliptic curves. It remains valid even if D and T are isogenous or isomorphic.

## 4. Genus bound, including correspondence classes on the product

For a birational joint image Gamma in the product of elliptic curves with projection degrees m,n, write its numerical divisor class as the fibre-axis part plus gamma, orthogonal to both fibre classes. The product has trivial canonical class. The fibre-axis part has square2mn. Hodge index makes gamma^2<=0: it is orthogonal to the ample sum of the two fibre classes. Thus

    Gamma^2 <= 2mn,
    genus(normalization Gamma) <= p_a(Gamma)
        = 1+Gamma^2/2 <= 1+mn.

This includes possible Hom/correspondence terms when the elliptic curves are isogenous; it does not silently assume NS rank two or equality Gamma^2=2mn.

Here m=2, n=2e, so

    17 <= 1+4e, hence e>=4.

The finite controls give e=1,2,3 genus bounds5,9,13, each too small. At e=4 the bound is17 and supplies no contradiction. Explicit positive Hermitian product-divisor controls with off-diagonal terms also check that such terms lower, rather than increase, the arithmetic genus bound.

A polarized isomorphism P~(Y2,theta2) would pull back the actual degree-two elliptic axis of Y2, contradicting e>=4. Its degree-three axis would contradict the same bound independently. The degrees are bound to the actual degree2304 principal-model construction, not guessed from dimension or transported unchanged across an arbitrary isogeny.
