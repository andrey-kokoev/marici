# Stale lift replacement raises the finite-menu receipt minimum by 180

The three-provider finite menu now includes a REAL live-generation hostile. After computing the pre-advance candidate cover, the owner-checked `full_lift` provider is advanced through public `u<=3/4`. Its recorded original handle becomes stale and no longer supplies the full-domain lifting task. The `full_archive` provider remains live and full-domain; the restricted archive remains the ONLY source for the published refined answer and fine re-exposure. Thus the finite receipt minimum moves from `restricted_archive+full_lift = 3,658` to `restricted_archive+full_archive = 3,838`, a 180-byte difference in this frozen receipt metric. The newly advanced lift head remains valid at u=1/2 but cannot supply u=1; a valid replacement handle does not automatically retain the old domain.

`uv run --with sympy python research/voevodsky/checkers/check_fixed_menu_provider_bundles.py` passed the live hostile. A separate `verify_fixed_menu_provider_bundles.py` reconstructs all three finite valid subset covers from the frozen service eligibility table and separately recorded receipt accounts; it confirms the two minima and indispensability of the restricted archive. That verifier cannot reconstruct process-local handle liveness from serialized results: the live producer exercises it, and the packet replay treats it as an explicit premise, not as proven by a digest.

This is a complete finite candidate-menu enumeration for a SCALAR receipt-byte objective. It does not minimize shared code, heap, owner-verification time or historical grants. Both winning bundles have two independent bootstraps, but excluded/unknown costs can alter a total-cost ranking. A subsequent sensitivity analysis can quantify exactly how large an omitted differential account must be to reverse the 180-byte choice; it must not call this receipt minimum a universal resource optimum.
