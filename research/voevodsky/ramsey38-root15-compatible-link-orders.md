# Which two rows can root 15 move inside?

A filling induces cyclic orders before it induces any particular search result. Cone the required boundary faces to an auxiliary exterior point 0. This represents the outside of the shell, not another Ramsey vertex. If a PL ball realizes the requested supports, gluing on the cone of its boundary embeds the marked complex in an oriented 3-sphere. At each vertex its link graph must embed in a 2-sphere. Triangles from required tetrahedra must bound faces of that link. Around each shared edge the cyclic orders of its incident marked triangles at the two endpoints must be reverse when viewed with outward orientations. These restrictions survive adding mesh vertices: omit the extra faces in taking the induced orders.

The checker enumerates the planar rotations of each marked link graph with the required triangle faces. It then joins the finite choices subject to the reversed-order condition on every shared edge. A successful join is only a necessary test in general; it constructs no ball. In this instance it gives exactly the nine pairs already certified by actual shellings. Pairs missing {5,20,27} are rejected directly by the earlier alternating-chord proof. Thus the classification no longer assumes a fixed vertex budget or shellability for its negative direction.

For the previously difficult release {5,27}, only three vertices are needed to witness failure: exterior point 0 and labels 3,6. Each has planar orders, and any two of the three constraints can coexist. Choose the orientation at 0 such that its order about 3 is (1,6,17); simultaneous reversal handles the other case. At 3 the order about 0 must then be (1,17,6), forcing the order about 6 to be (0,10,1). Meanwhile at 0 the order about 6 is either (3,10,26,22) or (3,26,10,22). Reversing either order at 6 forces the order about 3 there to be (0,10,1) as well. The two views of edge 3–6 now agree rather than reverse. The displayed shell and the proposed interior cannot be oriented together.
