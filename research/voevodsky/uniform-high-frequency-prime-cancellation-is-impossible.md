# Uniform high-frequency prime cancellation is impossible for the translation symbol

The proposed improvement of the absolute prime-translation norm cannot hold
at operator-norm level.

For a fixed logarithmic window, the prime packet is finite. Up to normalization
and signs fixed by the explicit formula, its Fourier symbol has the form

\[
p_L(u)=2\sum_{n\le e^{2L}}c_n\cos(u\log n),
\qquad c_n=\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}>0.
\]

The frequencies are generated by the finite set of `log p` for primes
`p<=e^{2L}`. The orbit

\[
u\longmapsto (e^{iu\log p})_p
\]

lies in a compact torus and starts at the identity. Recurrence (equivalently,
Dirichlet simultaneous approximation) supplies arbitrarily large `u` for
which every generator is arbitrarily close to `1`. Every admitted prime power
phase is then also close to `1`. Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{u\ge U}p_L(u)=2\sum_{n\le e^{2L}}c_n
\quad\text{for every }U.}
\]

The equality is a supremum; exact simultaneous return is unnecessary.
Continuity gives positive-measure neighborhoods approaching the same value,
so the essential supremum is identical. Therefore the norm of the full-line
prime Fourier multiplier on every high-frequency tail equals its absolute
coefficient mass.

No large-sieve or average-cancellation estimate can improve this **uniform
operator norm**: phase-aligned high-frequency quasimodes always exist.

## Consequence for compact-window coercivity

A compressed interval operator can differ because translations meet the
boundary. But any claimed uniform cancellation theorem must explicitly spend
that boundary geometry. For a subpacket whose shifts preserve a common
interior region, oscillatory wave packets supported in that region reproduce
the recurrent Fourier phases and obstruct cancellation there as well.
Thus an argument using only high frequency, with no support loss or additional
source constraint, cannot beat absolute mass.

The log-elliptic estimate still proves eventual positivity for each fixed
`L`, but its enormous threshold is structural rather than merely a crude
triangle inequality. This terminates the proposed route of obtaining useful
global bounds from a uniform high-mode norm estimate for the prime packet.

## Surviving possibilities

Any coercive continuation must use one of the following nonuniform features:

1. boundary/support geometry that removes most simultaneously aligned
   translations;
2. restriction to the special Gaussian/heat-polynomial source family rather
   than every high-mode vector;
3. a coupled endpoint--gamma--prime identity that cancels aligned packets
   before taking norms;
4. an averaged estimate together with a theorem excluding concentrated
   quasimodes from a minimal negative witness.

Absent such a restriction, uniform high-frequency prime cancellation is
false.
