For the source interval `[0,1]` (rows `-x<=0`, `x<=1`), target `x<=2` and target reference shifted by d=1/4, two valid nonnegative Farkas rows prove the IDENTICAL source-to-target inclusion. Multiplier `(0,1)` uses source bound 1 and has surplus 1; multiplier `(1,2)` combines `-x<=0` and twice `x<=1`, obtains source bound 2 and has surplus 0. Both give target normal 1 and residual `2-1/4=7/4`. Thus surplus is not even fixed by the pair of source/target presentations and reference shift; it is tied to a chosen PROOF arrow. A scalar or vector 'residue' derived from semantic inclusion alone cannot equal Farkas surplus without choosing or quotienting the proof.
