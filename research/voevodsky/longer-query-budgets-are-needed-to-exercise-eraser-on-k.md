# Longer query budgets are needed to exercise ERASE--K

Fresh `check_progress_premise_rule_ledger.py` initially audited every transition of the 86 n<=2 inputs with query indices 0..n+1 and found only FOURTEEN of the fifteen rule pairs: ERASE--K was absent. The absence is explained by the boundary: a query running past the support consumed all its K budget before spawning the eraser, leaving NIL. Expanding indices through n+2 supplies a leftover K tail, and ERASE--K appears 419 times. The extended search traverses 11,319 alpha graph states and 23,641 transitions; all 15 rule pairs occur. At each encountered rewrite boundary, the before/after graph obeys root coverage, phase and finite-tail typing, and constructor-marked original-chain ownership.

This closes an important FINITE CASE-COVERAGE gap but is not a universal rule-template proof: some typed boundary port combinations might be absent from the tested constructors. To promote the conditional progress lemma to a theorem for arbitrary finite n, each rule must preserve the global root predicate and allowed typed port incidences for *symbolic* outside wires, not just these concrete states. Especially ERASE--K can leave more K before NIL; COPY--NIL drops the shared frontier and must attach both pending consumers to fresh NIL nodes.

Next develop symbolic boundary-context templates for COPY--NIL and ERASE--K first, then the other thirteen cases.
