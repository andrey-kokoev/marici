# Source-manifest widening preserves bracket syntax but breaks proof support

Hypothetically replace the primitive row `x<=1` in `[0,1]^2` by `x<=2`, giving `[0,2]x[0,1]`. The manifest digest changes. Among the four archived leaf certificates for the short pentagon path, x<=2 proof `a=(1,2,0,0)` and x<=1 proof `c=(0,1,0,0)` FAIL bound verification under the new source; y<=2 proof b and y<=1 proof d still verify. The old composite certificate `(1,3,1,3)` of `2x+2y<=6` also fails under the new rows. The target composite remains provable with DIFFERENT multipliers `(0,2,0,2)`, while x<=1 itself is false at new source point (2,0). Fresh `check_source_manifest_change.py` verifies every claim.

The formal short rotation sequence still consists of valid syntactic reassociations on tags a,b,c,d. But its old source-rooted mathematical proof is not valid relative to the NEW row manifest: the leaf-to-root support dependency was invalidated. Reusing the old thin endpoint arrow as a new source proof would conflate bracket syntax, semantic target validity and proof-record authority. A new proof of some targets cannot repair leaf c, which is semantically false on the widened source.

This is a mathematical source-edit audit, not an owner-admitted mutation or real live revocation. The next branch should formalize dependency-sensitive cache invalidation: retain purely syntactic tagged-path replay, mark exactly a/c and their composite proof DAG stale, preserve independent b/d evidence if their roots and source semantics remain valid, and refuse old-root thin evidence under a new source generation. Analytic S,A,R,C,G mapping stays deferred.
