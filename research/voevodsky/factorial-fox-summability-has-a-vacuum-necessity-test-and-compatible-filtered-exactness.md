# Factorial Fox summability has a vacuum necessity test and compatible filtered exactness

## Result

Nima's factorial source domain is sufficient for the summed Fox receiver. For the prescribed factorial jet graph weights, factorial path control is also necessary on its all-forgotten sector. Thus the earlier exponential-radius source is genuinely too large for that summed realization.

The source-only exponential Fox lifts remain valid after multiplying all corner norms by n!. Consequently every fixed finite filtered attachment and its strict exact sequence transfers to the factorial all-radius domain. This does not assert a minimal domain for general marked forcings or an unchanged fixed Banach topology.

## 1. The top jet is an exact forgotten-sector probe

For an all-forgotten path of length n, D_n selects every event. Its normal form retains the entire ordered sequence of seam edges, all seam letters are Omega, and all n+1 buffers are vacuum. Different paths give different ordered edge sequences. Thus there is no cancellation between distinct forgotten paths in this top jet.

With unit vacuum/Omega and the prescribed cut-l1 shape norm, for a forgotten coefficient vector x_c in an endpoint corner of length n,

    ||D_n(x_c)||_unweighted = ||x_c||_path.

The kth graph weight is (2 lambda s)^k k!. Therefore the full summed jet norm has the lower bound

    ||D(x)||_(s,b) >= sum_c n(c)! (2 lambda s)^(n(c)) ||x_c||_path

on the forgotten sector. The feature radius b is irrelevant because these terms contain no retained features.

For each desired factorial source radius R, choose s with 2 lambda s>=R A. Then q_R(x)<=||D(x)||_(s,b). Hence belonging to every graph-depth scale requires all-radius factorial summability of the forgotten coefficients. This is an exact source probe, not an estimate based on theta-tail conditioning.

The same argument applies to the forgotten-degree projection of a general source vector: retained paths occupy different total feature sectors in their top jets. It does not supply a lower bound on all retained-feature coefficients, whose feature norms can be small.

## 2. A source in the old domain whose full jet is not summable

Let v_n be the forgotten diamond relation followed by a forgotten suffix to total length n, with distinct terminal endpoints as n increases. It has two path coefficients of modulus one. Define

    x=sum_(n>=2) v_n/sqrt(n!).

For every fixed p and R, the series

    sum_(n>=2) 2(1+n)^p (R A)^n/sqrt(n!)

converges by the ratio test. Thus x belongs to the earlier fixed-polynomial, all-exponential-radius common-path ideal. Closedness of the record kernel keeps it in the completed relation ideal.

But its top-jet contribution at length n has weighted norm

    2(2 lambda s)^n sqrt(n!),

which does not even tend to zero. The full jet is not in any of the prescribed factorial depth-summed receiver spaces. Its finite jets still exist and remain faithful as a product-topology family.

This proves that the sufficient factorial strengthening is not merely optional bookkeeping. A claim that the old entire source already maps into this summed receiver would be false.

## 3. Exponential lifts survive factorial strengthening

All paths and factorizations in a fixed endpoint corner have the same total length n. Multiplying both sides of a source lift estimate by n! preserves it:

    ||L_r(z)||_tuple <= 32^n ||z||_path,

and the sharper two-factor estimate

    ||H(z)||_tuple <= 3(2^n-1)||z||_path

therefore require only the same fixed exponential radius losses in the factorial norms. No additional factorial loss is generated by these source lifts.

For each FIXED finite depth, the polynomial depth weight is also absorbed by radius enlargement. The simultaneous unweighted minimizing lifts in each finite corner still minimize every scalar-multiplied radius/factorial norm. Hence the intersection-surjectivity argument and Frechet strictness apply unchanged.

It follows that each finite filtered sequence and its nonzero attachment roof from the common-path construction has a strictly exact version on the compatible factorial domains. Source actions and the normalized suspension phases are unchanged. The lifts are not source-module splittings of the nonsplit extensions.

This fixed-depth transfer must not be confused with comparing every depth-dependent graph weight to one scalar corner weight uniformly in depth. Summation over all Fox orders is supplied by Nima's explicit full-jet estimate, not by that invalid shortcut.

## 4. The bounded inverse-limit criterion in the new domain

Use the factorial source seminorms q_R and their COMMON quotient seminorms q_(R,m) on I_fact/(I_fact intersect J_(m+1)). Endpoint stabilization and monotone convergence give the same reconstruction criterion:

    q_R(x)=sup_m q_(R,m)(x_m).

A compatible filtered family belongs to the factorial source exactly when this supremum is finite for every R. Give that bounded part of the inverse limit these supremum seminorms. The norm-minimizing corner lift argument provides the quotient identifications needed here.

Thus the full summed receiver and the filtered reconstruction now coexist on one explicit stronger domain. An arbitrary compatible inverse-limit family is still not admitted, and the product-topology inverse limit is still not the supremum-topology realization.

## 5. Current convergence remains channelwise

On this domain Nima's bound

    ||D(x)||_(s,b) <= 2 q_(2 lambda s b)(x)

places the full Fox family in the forcing-resolved receiver. The already bounded bulk, forcing, and incidence current maps pull back separately, with their existing type matching and spectral indices. Source approximation, jet-order truncation, and permitted normalizations remain controlled by the explicit stronger radii.

No new cross-degree Green pairing is introduced, no root state is duplicated, and no stable inverse for general retained features is inferred from the forgotten-sector necessity test.
