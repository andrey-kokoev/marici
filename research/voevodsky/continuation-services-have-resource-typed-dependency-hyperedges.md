# Continuation services have resource-typed dependency hyperedges

## Frozen service menu

Within the exact three-atom retired-domain fixture and the existing forced-zero-tail migration, define distinct requests `answer(F,Q)`, `lift(F,y)` and `reexpose(F)`; F is later admitted PUBLIC evidence. These are not interchangeable: an answer may require only the exact public relation, lifting needs an admitted source-point provider over the requested scope, and re-exposure requires the complete original fine predicate plus its source interpretation and later public constraints. Issuance of an old-compatible witness does not select the actual history. The service menu is fixed for this packet; arbitrary fresh fine queries and changing source models are outside scope.

A dependency is an AND-hyperedge: a service succeeds only if *all* premises of at least one verified provider bundle are present. For answers a bundle contains a checked public relation and query semantics. For lifts a bundle additionally includes a source-bound section, scope-coverage and source-admission proof, an active generation handle, and required interpretation code. For re-exposure a bundle includes an archive of the original fine predicate, its owning source binding, verification context and capability to conjoin F before reuse. An OR between independently certified providers is permitted only when a comparison proves same owning migration, exact source formula on overlapping scope, and matching task; numerical agreement at sampled points is inadequate.

Origin binding is immutable provenance of what relation was checked; access is a revocable live capability to use a provider. The nine-live-provider cone demonstrates the difference: four meshes have distinct digests but verified full-domain identical source formula, and a stale T0 handle fails after public advancement despite retained mathematical section equality. The simple retirement fixture similarly has answer-only, lift-enabled and archive-enabled states with strictly different advertised service sets. Detaching a live provider destroys a lift bundle without changing the public answer or the historical source formula. Replacing it with a certified compatible, live full-scope provider restores THAT scoped lift, not re-exposure.

## Resource accounting

Charge separately (1) canonical retained public/fine relation bytes, (2) section/column and proof payload bytes, (3) source graph, archive and authoritative history records, (4) shared checker/interpretation code and external admission service, (5) live provider generations and handles, (6) time and memory for initial verification, fresh queries, comparison and repair, (7) growth from redundant refinements, including authority and parsing history. None is inferred from another. The fixed-chart rebase case bounds consistent repair EVENT count by an integer shortest-path potential with fixed grid but allows global regeneration on a single update and unbounded redundant-history storage. Thus neither bounded live columns nor finite repair-event count bounds total work or total resources.

This dependency model predicts two discriminating controls already reported: stale handle removes one lift edge while a compatible live full-domain provider can substitute; and losing the fine archive forbids re-exposure even when a selected lift remains. It does not assert a minimal optimization algorithm. A formal candidate optimizer must accept exact service domains and resource weights, find provider bundles under these AND/OR constraints, and replay hostile deletion/substitution without pretending capability flags are authority.

The nine-provider cone gives an actual substitution control on its fixed migration; the answer/lift/archive distinction is established on another exact fixture. Those fixtures have DIFFERENT source bases and cannot be silently spliced into one experiment.
