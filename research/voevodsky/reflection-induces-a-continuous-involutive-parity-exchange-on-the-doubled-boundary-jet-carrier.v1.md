# Reflection induces a continuous involutive parity exchange on the doubled boundary-jet carrier

## Question

What is the reciprocal transport on the complete boundary-jet tower?

## Claim boundary

Let \(J_{\partial}\) be the projective boundary-jet carrier with coordinates \(a=(a_0,a_1,a_2,\ldots)\), where \(a_k=f^{(k)}(0)\) for a source-generated smooth boundary germ.

For reciprocal reflection \((Rf)(t)=f(-t)\), differentiation gives \((Rf)^{(k)}(0)=(-1)^kf^{(k)}(0)\). Define the jet-parity operator \(P_J:J_{\partial}\to J_{\partial}\) by \((P_Ja)_k=(-1)^ka_k\).

The identity \((-1)^{2k}=1\) gives \(P_J^2=I\). The defining projective seminorms control finite derivative coordinates, polynomial lag moments, compact parameter sets, and rapid source labels through absolute magnitudes. Coordinatewise multiplication by unit-modulus signs therefore preserves every finite jet-coordinate bound and defines a continuous involution on \(J_{\partial}\).

Let \(J_{\partial,\mathrm{rec}}=J_{\partial,+}\oplus J_{\partial,-}\). Define reciprocal jet transport by \(R_J(a_+,a_-)=(P_Ja_-,P_Ja_+)\). Then \(R_J^2=I\), and \(R_J\) continuously exchanges the two reciprocal boundary-jet sectors while applying the source-derived derivative parity.

The zeroth endpoint coordinate is even, the first derivative coordinate is odd, and successive jet coordinates alternate parity. This parity is forced by reflection of the underlying boundary germ and introduces no adjustable phase.

## Disposition

Step 1 is complete. The doubled boundary-jet carrier has a source-derived continuous reciprocal involution. The next calculation is transport of the jet shift and endpoint evaluation under this involution.
