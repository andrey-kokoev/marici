# The observer-weighted Witt edge has a universal Plancherel leading Gram

## Objective

Identify the candidate observer-weighted edge Gram for the principal-angle feature

\[
E_c^{Witt}=
\eta(B_c)^{1/2}.
\]

The scalar Widom coefficient is already known:

\[
I_\eta=2\log2.
\]

The remaining issue is insertion of two observer multipliers.

## Localized edge Gram

For Mellin observer amplitudes \(m_g,m_h\), define

\[
\mathcal E_c(g,h)
=
\operatorname{Tr}
\left(
M_{m_h}^*
\eta(B_c)
M_{m_g}
\right).
\]

The expected weighted Widom law is

\[
\mathcal E_c(g,h)
=
2C_W(\log2)(\log c)
\langle m_h,m_g\rangle_{Pl}
+
\mathcal F_\eta(g,h)
+o(1).
\]

Thus the candidate normalized edge Gram is

\[
G_{edge}(g,h)
=
2C_W(\log2)
\langle m_h,m_g\rangle_{Pl}.
\]

At finite conductor, sum this expression over the finitely many character sectors.

## Why the leading form is Plancherel

The logarithmic term is generated by the translation-invariant local model at the two cutoff boundaries. On that model, the only source-natural quadratic form of order zero carried by multiplication observers is their Plancherel pairing.

The function \(\eta\) supplies the universal scalar coefficient. The observer insertions supply the source Gram.

This separates the two roles:

\[
\text{principal-angle profile}
\quad\leadsto\quad
2\log2,
\]

\[
\text{observer localization}
\quad\leadsto\quad
G_{Pl}.
\]

## Polarization reduction

It is enough to prove the diagonal asymptotic

\[
\mathcal E_c(g,g)
=
2C_W(\log2)(\log c)
\|m_g\|_{Pl}^2
+O_g(1).
\]

The sesquilinear law then follows from the complex polarization identity.

This avoids proving a separate asymptotic for every pair \((g,h)\).

## Required localized Widom estimate

A sufficient theorem is the following uniform statement on a finite observer packet \(E\):

\[
\sup_{
\|g\|_E\le1
}
\left|
\mathcal E_c(g,g)
-
2C_W(\log2)(\log c)
\|m_g\|_{Pl}^2
\right|
=O_E(1).
\]

After division by \(\log c\), this gives Gram convergence in operator norm on every finite packet.

For the global phase-energy completion, replace the packet norm by a regulator-independent graph norm and require an integrable angular majorant.

## Reference and Tate pairs

Let

\[
B_c^0
\]

be the reference generic compression and

\[
B_c^T
\]

its Tate-scattered counterpart. Define

\[
\mathcal E_c^q(g,h)
=
\operatorname{Tr}
\left(
M_{m_h}^*
\eta(B_c^q)
M_{m_g}
\right),
\qquad q\in\{T,0\}.
\]

The common-edge theorem requires

\[
\frac{
\mathcal E_c^T(g,h)
}{\log c}
\longrightarrow
G_{edge}(g,h),
\]

and

\[
\frac{
\mathcal E_c^0(g,h)
}{\log c}
\longrightarrow
G_{edge}(g,h).
\]

The two limits must use the same normalization and source carrier.

## Stability under the Tate phase

The Tate pair differs from the reference pair by a smooth unimodular scattering multiplier after common translation is removed. Such a zero-order phase can alter the finite scattering term, but it should not alter the local logarithmic boundary coefficient.

The precise estimate needed is

\[
\mathcal E_c^T(g,h)
-
\mathcal E_c^0(g,h)
=O_{g,h}(1).
\]

A packet-uniform form is preferable:

\[
\sup_{
\|g\|_E,\|h\|_E\le1
}
\left|
\mathcal E_c^T(g,h)
-
\mathcal E_c^0(g,h)
\right|
=O_E(1).
\]

Dividing by \(\log c\) then gives the same normalized edge Gram.

## Functional-calculus route

The function \(\eta\) is continuous on \([0,1]\), vanishes at both endpoints, and has square-root behavior there. Direct polynomial approximation must respect the singular endpoint derivatives.

A useful integral representation is preferable because it can reduce the comparison to resolvent differences of the compressions. One may approximate \(\eta\) monotonically by endpoint-regular functions \(\eta_\varepsilon\), prove the weighted Widom law for each \(\eta_\varepsilon\), and then control

\[
\eta(B_c)-\eta_\varepsilon(B_c)
\]

through endpoint spectral estimates.

The endpoint control must be uniform after observer insertion. Scalar trace convergence alone is insufficient for the positive feature comparison.

## Positive edge carrier

Once the normalized Grams converge to \(G_{edge}\), define

\[
\mathscr H_{edge}
=
\overline{
E/\ker G_{edge}
}^{\,G_{edge}}.
\]

The reference and Tate normalized features then determine asymptotic maps into the same carrier:

\[
(\log c)^{-1/2}E_{c,0}^{Witt},
\]

\[
(\log c)^{-1/2}E_{c,T}^{Witt}.
\]

On finite packets, convergence of their source Grams gives partial isometries between their generated ranges. Global compatibility requires the graph-core and angular domination estimates.

## What is determined here

The candidate common edge form is no longer unspecified. The weighted local model forces

\[
G_{edge}
=
2C_W(\log2)G_{Pl}
\]

if the localized Widom remainder is lower order. The scalar coefficient is exact; promotion to the observer-weighted asymptotic remains the stated analytical obligation.

## Remaining proof obligation

The missing analytical estimate is the observer-weighted remainder bound

\[
\mathcal E_c^q
-
2C_W(\log2)(\log c)G_{Pl}
=O(1)
\]

on each admitted graph packet, for both \(q=T\) and \(q=0\), with a summable characterwise majorant.

The Tate/reference comparison then follows by subtraction.

## Disposition

The universal positive edge should have source Gram

\[
G_{edge}(g,h)
=
2C_W(\log2)
\langle m_h,m_g\rangle_{Pl}.
\]

The active theorem is now a localized weighted Widom remainder estimate, not identification of the leading form.
