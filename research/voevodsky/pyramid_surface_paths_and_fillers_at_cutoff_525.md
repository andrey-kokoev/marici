# Pyramid surface paths and fillers at cutoff 525

## Question

Can the first three path priorities be executed on the cutoff-525 arithmetic pyramid: enumerate typed surface routes, attach every source-derived filler, and separate missing fillers from genuine residual cycles?

## Claim boundary

The construction concerns the finite adjacent-prime route graph at cutoffs 522 and 525. Higher cells are admitted only when every subset vertex and every directed edge of the corresponding effective-divisor cube is present under the same cutoff. Homology is computed for this explicit finite cubical chain complex. It does not assert that every future pyramid view uses the same cutoff filtration.

## Typed surface paths

A typed directed edge is

\[
(N,i,Np_{i+1}/p_i;g),
\qquad
g=Np_{i+1},
\]

where \(p_i\mid N\) and \(g\leq L\). A cell

\[
[N;I],
\qquad I=\{i_1<\cdots<i_k\},
\]

is admitted when every subset of the shell moves in \(I\) is effective and all resulting edges have grade at most \(L\).

Each permutation \(\sigma\in S_k\) gives a typed surface path from the base to the top:

\[
N\to N\frac{p_{\sigma(1)+1}}{p_{\sigma(1)}}
\to\cdots\to
N\prod_{i\in I}\frac{p_{i+1}}{p_i}.
\]

The \(k!\) paths are connected by adjacent transpositions. Each transposition is witnessed by a square face, so the filler records why those surface paths are equivalent.

## Filler attachment

For every admitted cell, attach its cubical boundary

\[
\partial[N;I]
=
\sum_{r=1}^{k}(-1)^{r-1}
\left(
[Np_{i_r+1}/p_{i_r};I\setminus\{i_r\}]
-
[N;I\setminus\{i_r\}]
\right).
\]

At cutoff 525, the canonical cell

\[
[30;\{1,2,3\}]
\]

is admitted. It supplies six typed surface paths from 30 to 105, six square faces, and one three-dimensional filler relation among their oriented boundaries.

## Residual classification

Let \(C_k(L)\) be generated by admitted \(k\)-cells and let \(d_k\) be the cubical boundary matrix. Define

\[
H_k(L)=\ker d_k/\operatorname{im}d_{k+1}.
\]

A graph cycle in \(\ker d_1\) is:

- filled when it lies in \(\operatorname{im}d_2\);
- a genuine residual when its class in \(H_1\) is nonzero;
- part of a higher filler relation when its square-chain presentation lies in \(\ker d_2\), with membership in \(\operatorname{im}d_3\) deciding whether that relation is filled.

The predicted cutoff transition is:

- at 522, every edge cycle is a square boundary and there is no square-boundary relation;
- at 525, every edge cycle remains filled, while one new square-boundary relation appears and is exactly the boundary of the canonical three-cube;
- hence \(H_1\) and \(H_2\) both vanish at each cutoff despite the increase in one-skeletal cycle rank.

## Strongest falsification attempt

Enumerate cells directly from arithmetic edge admission, without inserting the known cube by hand. Construct all boundary matrices over exact rationals, verify \(d_{k-1}d_k=0\), and compute chain dimensions, boundary ranks, and Betti numbers. Enumerate every maximal surface route of the canonical cube and verify that adjacent route swaps correspond to admitted square faces. Any nonzero \(H_1\) or \(H_2\), absent face, or unmatched square relation falsifies the predicted filler classification.

## Computed census

At cutoff 522, the cell counts are

\[
(\dim C_0,\dim C_1,\dim C_2)=(165,148,21),
\]

with boundary ranks \((127,21)\), 190 typed maximal cell paths, and vanishing \(H_1,H_2\).

At cutoff 525, the cell counts are

\[
(\dim C_0,\dim C_1,\dim C_2,\dim C_3)
=(166,150,23,1),
\]

with boundary ranks \((128,22,1)\), 202 typed maximal cell paths, and vanishing \(H_1,H_2,H_3\). The canonical cube is the unique three-cell. Its six directed paths from 30 to 105 are enumerated separately in the result artifact.

## Disposition

The first three path priorities are realized at cutoff 525. Every surface cycle has a square filler; the unique square-boundary relation has the canonical cube filler; no genuine residual class remains in positive degree. The census is reusable as the first bounded pyramid surface-and-filler model.
