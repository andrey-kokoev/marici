# The left-Hardy placement correction is a nonlocal double-pole phase current

## Hardy kernels

Fix a Hardy orientation with off-diagonal Cauchy kernel

\[
\Pi(s,u)
=
\frac1{2\pi i}
\frac1{s-u-i0}.
\]

Let

\[
\sigma_1(s)
=e^{ias}
\gamma(s),
\qquad
\sigma_0(s)
=e^{ias},
\]

where `a=2L` in the cutoff normalization. Define

\[
Q_j
=M_{\sigma_j}
\Pi
M_{\sigma_j}^*,
\qquad
\Delta Q
=Q_1-Q_0.
\]

After cancellation of the common diagonal distribution, the regular off-diagonal kernel is

\[
\boxed{
\Delta q(u,t)
=
\frac1{2\pi i}
\frac{
e^{ia(u-t)}
(\gamma(u)
\overline{\gamma(t)}-1)
}{u-t}.
}
\]

The numerator vanishes at `u=t`, so this is a smooth divided difference after endpoint/index terms are separated.

## Composition with the left Hardy projection

The ordered kernel is

\[
(\Pi\Delta Q)(s,t)
=
\int_\mathbb R
\Pi(s,u)
\Delta q(u,t)du.
\]

Substituting the kernels gives

\[
\boxed{
(\Pi\Delta Q)(s,t)
=
-
\frac1{4\pi^2}
\int_\mathbb R
\frac{
e^{ia(u-t)}
(\gamma(u)
\overline{\gamma(t)}-1)
}{(s-u-i0)(u-t)}du,
}
\]

interpreted with the Hardy boundary prescription.

## Diagonal double pole

Set `t=s`. Formally,

\[
(s-u-i0)(u-s)
=
-
(u-s+i0)(u-s).
\]

Thus the regular diagonal is a finite-part double-pole integral:

\[
\boxed{
(\Pi\Delta Q)(s,s)
=
\frac1{4\pi^2}
\operatorname{FP}
\int_\mathbb R
\frac{
e^{ia(u-s)}
(\gamma(u)
\overline{\gamma(s)}-1)
}{(u-s)^2}du
+
\mathcal L_{diag}(s).
}
\]

Here `L_diag` denotes the local contribution generated by the `i0` prescription. It is proportional to the first derivative of the numerator at `u=s`, hence to

\[
ia
+
\partial_s
\log\gamma(s),
\]

with orientation-dependent constants.

The common `ia` term cancels between `sigma_1` and `sigma_0`; the gamma derivative remains.

## Ordered observer trace

For a scalar observer density `f`, the ordered trace is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Tr}
(M_f\Pi\Delta Q)
&=
\frac1{4\pi^2}
\int_\mathbb R
f(s)
\operatorname{FP}
\int_\mathbb R
\frac{
e^{ia(u-s)}
(\gamma(u)
\overline{\gamma(s)}-1)
}{(u-s)^2}duds\\
&\quad+
\int_\mathbb R
f(s)
\mathcal L_{diag}(s)ds\\
&\quad+
E_{index}(f).
\end{aligned}
}
\]

This is the direct analytic form of the left-Hardy placement problem.

## Local versus nonlocal parts

The uncompressed relative projection trace sees only the local diagonal anomaly

\[
\boxed{
\frac1{2\pi i}
\int
f(s)
\partial_s
\log\gamma(s)ds
+
E_{index}(f).
}
\]

The left-compressed ordered trace also contains the nonlocal current

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal N_{a,\gamma}(f)
=
\frac1{4\pi^2}
\iint
f(s)
\frac{
e^{ia(u-s)}
(\gamma(u)
\overline{\gamma(s)}-1)
}{(u-s)^2}duds,
}
\]

with finite-part prescription.

Therefore the placement correction is not determined pointwise by `partial log gamma`.

## Near-diagonal expansion

Let `r=u-s`. Since `gamma` is unimodular,

\[
\gamma(s+r)
\overline{\gamma(s)}
=
1
+r
\frac{\gamma'(s)}{\gamma(s)}
+O(r^2).
\]

Hence

\[
e^{iar}
(\gamma(s+r)
\overline{\gamma(s)}-1)
=
r
\frac{\gamma'(s)}{\gamma(s)}
+O(r^2).
\]

The apparent double pole reduces to a principal-value simple pole plus a finite diagonal term. The Hardy `i0` prescription converts part of that simple pole into the local logarithmic derivative anomaly.

The remaining principal-value integral depends on phase values away from the diagonal.

## Symmetric real part

The Hermitian eight-leg readout corresponds to

\[
\frac12
(\Pi\Delta Q
+
\Delta Q\Pi).
\]

Its diagonal trace is the real part of the ordered current. Pairing the integrand with its `(s,u)` transpose yields a real kernel involving

\[
\operatorname{Re}
\left[
e^{ia(u-s)}
(\gamma(u)
\overline{\gamma(s)}-1)
\right]
\]

divided by `(u-s)^2`, together with the local anomaly.

The anti-Hermitian readout similarly selects the imaginary antisymmetric part.

## Constant phase sanity check

If

\[
\gamma(s)
=e^{i\theta_0}
\]

is constant, then

\[
\gamma(u)
\overline{\gamma(s)}-1
=0.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\Delta Q=0,
\qquad
\mathcal N_{a,\gamma}=0,
\qquad
\mathcal L_{diag}=0.
}
\]

The placement current vanishes, as required.

## Linear phase sanity check

If

\[
\gamma(s)
=e^{ibs},
\]

then Tate and reference projections are two differently translated Hardy half-lines. The ordered trace reduces to overlap/mismatch lengths of nested projections.

The double-pole integral becomes explicitly computable from

\[
e^{iar}
(e^{ibr}-1).
\]

This model calibrates the coefficient and sign of `L_diag`. Any proposed general formula must reproduce the piecewise-linear interval-overlap answer.

## Trace-class localization

For Schwartz `f` obtained from two observer legs, the finite-part current is absolutely controlled after cancellation near the diagonal and rapid decay in the center variable. The corresponding operator is trace class under the two-sided localization theorem.

Thus the displayed current is regulator-independent in the iterated outer-cutoff limit. The problem is its evaluation, not its existence.

## Large-cutoff oscillation

The factor

\[
e^{ia(u-s)}
\]

oscillates rapidly as `a=2L->infinity`. Away from the diagonal, integration by parts suggests decay. Near the diagonal, the singular scaling retains the local gamma derivative.

This suggests the target asymptotic

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal N_{a,\gamma}(f)
\longrightarrow
c_{Hardy}
\frac1{2\pi i}
\int
f\partial_s
\log\gammads
}
\]

for a universal coefficient `c_Hardy` determined by the linear-phase model and the `i0` prescription.

The required coefficient is the one that makes the compressed ordered trace agree with Connes's local distribution.

## Required estimate

Split the `r=u-s` integral with a smooth cutoff:

\[
1
=
\eta(Lr)
+
(1-\eta(Lr)).
\]

Then prove:

1. the near-diagonal rescaling `rho=Lr` converges to the local derivative term;
2. the far part tends to zero by integration by parts;
3. errors are dominated by Schwartz observer seminorms and polynomial gamma derivatives;
4. characterwise constants are summable on the conductor-filtered core.

This is a concrete stationary/nonstationary phase problem.

## Connection with orbit volume

Connes's physical proof expresses the finite part through the defect

\[
-\log|u|_S.
\]

The double-pole Hardy current is its spectral-side placement representation. Proving equality should amount to Fourier transforming the principal-value local distribution while retaining the left Hardy polarization.

This is stronger than the ordinary local Tate identity because it includes the Hardy overlap geometry.

## Disposition

\[
\boxed{

with the Hardy finite-part prescription and endpoint index retained. The next calculation is the near/far diagonal asymptotic calibrated by an additional linear phase.
