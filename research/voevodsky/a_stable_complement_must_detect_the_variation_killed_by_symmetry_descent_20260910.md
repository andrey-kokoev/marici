# A stable complement must detect the variation killed by symmetry descent

## Question

How does the complementary-observer lower-frame theorem interact with a symmetry descent \(\pi:G\to H\)?

## Claim boundary

If the analytic observer factors equivariantly through \(G\to H\), it annihilates every variation generated by \(\ker\pi\). Any stable complementary observer must therefore be bounded below on that variation subspace. If the complement also factors through the same descent, stable reconstruction is impossible unless the kernel action was already trivial. This theorem does not claim that kernel variation is the only analytically invisible subspace.

## Problem

Let \(G,H\) be groups with a surjection

\[
\pi:G\to H,
\qquad
K=\ker\pi.
\]

Let \(X,Y\) be Hilbert spaces with unitary representations \(\rho_X\) of \(G\) and \(\rho_Y\) of \(H\). Suppose

\[
A:X\to Y
\]

is bounded and descends equivariantly:

\[
A\rho_X(g)=\rho_Y(\pi(g))A.
\]

Let \(D:X\to Z\) be a second bounded observer.

## Bold conjecture

A complementary observer may factor through the same quotient symmetry as \(A\) and still restore stable reconstruction of a source on which \(K\) acts nontrivially.

## Named rivals

1. The complement must detect the kernel-generated variation annihilated by \(A\).
2. A line-valued lift of the quotient action is enough even if its observation norm vanishes on that variation.
3. Kernel detection is unnecessary when \(A\) is coordinatewise injective.

## Kernel-variation subspace

Define

\[
X_K=
\overline{\operatorname{span}}
\{(\rho_X(k)-I)x:k\in K,\ x\in X\}.
\]

For \(k\in K\),

\[
A\rho_X(k)
=
\rho_Y(e)A
=A.
\]

Therefore

\[
A(\rho_X(k)-I)=0,
\]

and hence

\[
X_K\subseteq\ker A.
\]

This is the exact information loss caused by passing to the quotient action, though \(\ker A\) may be larger.

## Theorem: stable complements detect descended variation

Suppose the row observer

\[
T=\binom AD
\]

has lower bound \(\delta>0\). Then

\[
\|Dv\|\ge\delta\|v\|
\qquad(v\in X_K).
\]

### Proof

For \(v\in X_K\), continuity gives \(Av=0\). The joint lower bound becomes

\[
\delta^2\|v\|^2
\le
\|Av\|^2+\|Dv\|^2
=
\|Dv\|^2.
\]

Thus \(D|_{X_K}\) is bounded below by the full joint margin.

## No-common-descent corollary

Assume additionally that \(D\) is invariant under \(K\):

\[
D\rho_X(k)=D
\qquad(k\in K).
\]

Then \(D\) annihilates \(X_K\). If \((A,D)^\top\) is bounded below, the theorem forces

\[
X_K=0.
\]

Hence, on any source with nonzero kernel variation, two observers that both factor through the same symmetry quotient cannot jointly reconstruct stably.

The complement must retain some representation data discarded by \(\pi\).

## Orthogonal form for compact groups

If \(K\) is compact, averaging defines the orthogonal projection

\[
P_Kx=\int_K\rho_X(k)x\,dk
\]

onto the invariant subspace \(X^K\). Then

\[
X_K=(X^K)^\perp.
\]

The theorem says that \(D\) must be bounded below on the nontrivial \(K\)-isotypic sector. The analytic descended channel may act only through the invariant quotient, but the complementary channel must retain the omitted isotypic information.

## Finite cyclic example

Let \(G=C_4\), \(H=C_2\), and let

\[
\pi([m]_4)=[m]_2.
\]

Suppose \(F=\rho_X([1]_4)\). The kernel is generated by \(F^2\). A channel descending through \(C_2\) identifies vectors along the variation

\[
(F^2-I)X.
\]

Any stable complement must be bounded below on the closure of this subspace. A second channel invariant under \(F^2\) cannot do so unless \(F^2=I\) already on the source representation.

## Green-boundary qualification

The radial comparison has a related but direction-sensitive form:

\[
C_uF^2=W_uC_u.
\]

Here \(F^2\) is not killed; it descends to the nontrivial radial involution \(W_u\). The information omitted by the radial carrier is the distinction between quarter-turn lifts above the same radial parity action, not the half-turn itself.

Thus one must first identify the actual kernel of the representation descent. If the map from presentation symmetries to radial symmetries sends \(F\) to no radial operator but sends \(F^2\) to \(W_u\), it is not a group homomorphism \(C_4\to C_2\) with \(F\mapsto W_u\); that homomorphism would send \(F^2\) to the identity. The correct structure is a partially defined comparison: the subgroup \(\langle F^2\rangle\) is represented radially, while the full quarter-turn action remains on a larger presentation carrier.

This corrects an overcompressed quotient description. The established sewing square is subgroup restriction, not quotient descent.

## Strongest falsification attempt

The principal example itself falsifies a naive formulation that treated \(F\mapsto W_u\) as a quotient homomorphism while also requiring \(F^2\mapsto W_u\). Those assignments are incompatible with group multiplication.

The revised claim survives: whenever an actual homomorphism \(\pi:G\to H\) defines the descent, the complement must detect \(X_K\). The radial example instead instantiates restriction from \(C_4\) to its half-turn subgroup, followed by an isomorphism with the radial reciprocal \(C_2\).

## Constructor-role consequence

A `presentation_lift` records how the larger \(C_4\) action sits above the radial subsystem. A `comparison_cell` identifies the restricted half-turn action with \(W_u\). These roles are not interchangeable:

- the lift handles extension from the subgroup to the full presentation action;
- the comparison handles equivalence on the subgroup already represented.

Calling both “symmetry descent” erases the variance needed to state the sewing law.

## Disposition

The symmetry theorem is proved for genuine quotient descent: stable complements must detect kernel-generated variation. The radial worked example forces a correction to the conceptual spine: its current map is subgroup restriction plus comparison, not a quotient homomorphism from the full Fourier cycle. The programme must henceforth distinguish quotient descent, subgroup restriction, and presentation lift as separate constructors.
