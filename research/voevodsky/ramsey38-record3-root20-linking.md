# A boundary quadrilateral excludes the root-20 repair

Record 3, root 20, release 22. Keep all supports intact, with all other short rows on the boundary sphere. This is a budget-independent obstruction for a PL 3-ball. Label 20 is the graph root, not one of the four quadrilateral vertices.

1. The rim `1—3—13—12—1` is a simple cycle of boundary edges (rows 15, 2, 10, 5). The disjoint paths `1—21—13` (rows 23, 4) and `3—19—12` (rows 16, 8) connect alternating points on it. These two paths must be on opposite sides of the rim on the sphere. In particular 21 and 19 are on opposite sides.
2. The rim also bounds a disk consisting of triangles `{1,3,13}` and `{1,12,13}`. Both are faces of the observed tetrahedron in row 14. Its vertices are disjoint from edge `21—19` of released row 22 `{19,21,24}`. The disk and edge must therefore be disjoint as simplices of the proposed complex.
3. To see why this is impossible even if the disk touches the shell, double the ball to a 3-sphere. Close the edge by joining 21 and 19 through the outside ball. The closed loop has odd linking number (mod 2) with the rim: push the original edge slightly inward away from the rim and the exterior arc slightly outward; the loop crosses one of the two boundary caps exactly once, near its endpoint in that cap. But the disk in (2) has zero intersection with the loop: its triangles miss the interior edge and lie in the original ball, away from the exterior arc. The two spanning surfaces of the same rim cannot give different mod-2 linking numbers. Thus no such ball exists, even with additional mesh vertices. This argument needs neither a preselected boundary triangulation nor a shelling order.
