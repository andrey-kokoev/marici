# No fixed finite recurrence source factor

## Question

Can the all-rank remainder cone arise from a fixed finite-dimensional transfer operator, terminating J-fraction, or bounded-order recurrence?

## Claim boundary

Such finite mechanisms are ruled out conditional on the exact grouped general-zero expansion having infinitely many distinct nonzero poles. Infinite-dimensional source factors remain possible.

## Finite exponential theorem

Suppose

\[
a_n=\sum_{j=1}^m w_jy_j^n
\]

with distinct bases \(y_j\) and nonzero weights \(w_j\). Then

\[
A(z)=\sum_{n\geq0}a_nz^n
=
\sum_{j=1}^m\frac{w_j}{1-y_jz}.
\]

After grouping equal bases, this rational function has exactly \(m\) poles. Its denominator is

\[
\prod_{j=1}^m(1-y_jz).
\]

Consequently \((a_n)\) satisfies the constant-coefficient recurrence whose characteristic polynomial is

\[
\prod_{j=1}^m(X-y_j).
\]

The recurrence order cannot be smaller: the leading \(m\)-by-\(m\) Hankel matrix factors through a square Vandermonde matrix and is nonsingular.

Thus the following data are equivalent in the finite distinct-base case:

- number of exposed poles;
- minimal recurrence order;
- minimal finite state-space dimension;
- generic Hankel rank.

## Source obstruction

The grouped general-zero expansion has infinitely many distinct spectral rates \(\lambda_j\). At a generic mesh,

\[
y_j=e^{-h\lambda_j}
\]

remain distinct. If every grouped residue is nonzero, the moment transform has infinitely many exposed poles.

It therefore cannot be:

- rational;
- generated by a fixed finite-order recurrence;
- realized by a finite-dimensional transfer operator;
- represented by a terminating J-fraction;
- captured by an eventually stabilized finite Jacobi matrix.

Any finite truncation can approximate the transform but cannot equal it at all ranks.

## PNT split does not evade the obstruction

The reported decomposition into a PNT continuum and arithmetic fluctuation does not diagonalize rank-two positivity. At the harder sample, both diagonal determinant pieces are negative and the cross term supplies the small positive total. At the earlier sample, the fluctuation determinant is also negative.

Thus PNT subtraction explains endpoint-scale asymptotics but does not reduce the cone to a finite positive background plus a harmless finite correction. Archimedean--arithmetic fluctuation coupling remains essential.

These numerical signs are uncertified; they reject only that observed mechanism, not the exact PNT decomposition as an identity.

## Falsification result

The proposed risky consequence “a fixed source recurrence” is falsified by source pole cardinality, conditional on the exact infinite expansion and nonzero grouped residues.

This is stronger than observing that fitted recurrence coefficients drift with rank. It proves that no bounded order can stabilize exactly.

## Surviving constructors

A viable all-rank source factor must be one of:

- an infinite-dimensional positive operator;
- a nonterminating J-fraction with proved convergence and coefficient signs;
- an integral operator or continuous-frame factor;
- an inductive system of finite factors with nonstabilizing rank and proved coherent limit.

It must preserve the infinite pole set rather than hide it behind a finite realization.

## Remaining source premise

To make the no-go unconditional within the project, attach the exact grouped complex-zero transform and prove:

1. infinitely many distinct grouped \(\lambda_j\);
2. generic-mesh separation of their sampled bases;
3. nonzero grouped residues;
4. no cancellation by completed nonzero terms.

## Disposition

The broad mixed Gram target remains unfalsified, but its finite-dimensional and fixed-recurrence subclasses are eliminated. Search should now be restricted to genuinely infinite-dimensional source constructions. Finite Jacobi reconstructions remain diagnostics, not candidate exact proofs.
