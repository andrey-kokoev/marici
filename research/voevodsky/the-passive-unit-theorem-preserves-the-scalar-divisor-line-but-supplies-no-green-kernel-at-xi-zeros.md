# The passive unit theorem preserves the scalar divisor line but supplies no Green kernel at Xi zeros

On each strict same-sign passive chart,

\[
D_{\rm coupled}=D_{\rm Euler}^{\rm gr}D_{\rm ret},
\qquad D_{\rm ret}\in\mathcal O^\times.
\]

Therefore the coupled determinant line and bare graded Euler line have the same
local divisor after choosing the corresponding direct or inverse orientation.
This is a determinant-line statement only.

The paired Green block represented by `I-K` remains invertible throughout the
chart, including at seam parameters where the external scalar Xi section may
vanish.  Consequently an Xi zero does not produce a kernel vector of the
same-sign paired history--arithmetic block.  In the direct Sonin orientation,
Xi zeros appear as poles of the inverse scalar line; reversing the line turns
them into zeros but still does not create an operator kernel.

Hence the unit theorem cannot be promoted to an independent conservative
spectral criterion.  It proves that passive boundary dressing preserves an
already supplied scalar divisor, while the divisor itself resides in the
external graded determinant line.

A kernel-bearing realization would require a different source-derived block in
which the graded divisor line is internalized as an actual Fredholm defect, or
a chain map from the Xi Koszul residue to a Green complex.  The current strict
same-sign block deliberately excludes such a defect by coercivity.
