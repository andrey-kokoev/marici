# Lossless source-retention review of the vacuum-acquisition chain

## Rule and scope

A transition may produce a nonfaithful view, but that view may not replace authoritative incoming data unless the retained output admits reconstruction of those data. Invisible information is not thereby zero in the source.

This review concerns the documented marked-path/observer chain leading to vacuum acquisition. It is not a repository-wide audit or evidence that any physical acquisition has occurred. Existing quotient theorems remain valid; the new rule changes which objects may serve as authoritative retained states.

For known symbolic sources, reconstruction means recovering the actual source element, with its labels, actions and filtration. For unknown physical sources, finite observations do not magically supply hidden values: preserve the observations and their compatible-source ambiguity rather than asserting a reconstructed source.

## 1. Marked paths -> terminal recorder: the first lossy map

The marked-path source S retains ordered events, endpoints and retained/forgotten marks. The terminal recorder rho:S->B has kernel I. Consequently rho is not a lossless replacement for S. The forgotten diamond is the earliest explicit example: a nonzero source maps to zero.

The source literature already provides an appropriate repair. In `../nima/universal-form-fox-lifts-control-the-completed-ideal-power-topologies.md`, a fixed ordered linear section sigma satisfies rho sigma=id, and the Fox telescoping identity gives

    t nabla(x)=x-sigma rho(x).

Thus retaining

    (rho(x), x-sigma rho(x))

permits exact reconstruction by addition. Equivalently the recorded Fox remainder supplies that residual. The section is NOT asserted multiplicative or source-bimodule equivariant; this is not a canonical splitting of every later extension.

Verdict: terminal recording is a legitimate view. Terminal-only authoritative replacement violates the rule. Studying I separately is legitimate provided the terminal component and source provenance are not implicitly discarded.

## 2. Ideal filtration -> finite quotients and graded layers

The chain uses F_m=J_1/J_(m+1) and G_r=J_r/J_(r+1). These intentionally forget deeper layers. Passing to associated graded objects also omits extension data between layers.

Verdict: valid analytical views, not lossless source replacements. Retain the parent filtered source and quotient maps. Neither an abstract list of kernels nor a list of graded dimensions reconstructs an element or its attachment data. A finite truncation must retain its actual tail somewhere if it is to satisfy the rule.

An inverse system does not automatically solve this: source separation and reconstruction in the declared completion still require proof.

## 3. Fox records, balancing and analytical realization

The ordered Fox records retain seams, endpoints and buffers. The owning all-depth theorem proves coherent reassociation and prescribed balancing. These are claims about the represented records, not an automatic claim that each fixed derivative reconstructs S.

Indeed lower derivatives annihilate sufficiently deep ideal powers. Every finite-depth record is therefore only a view of the complete source.

The analytical-shape audit verifies agreement with the terminal expansion on the declared 270 cubic columns. Formal potential keys are not independent analytical measurement channels. That identification is legitimate in its specified carrier; it is not a full-source inverse theorem.

Verdict: keep the marked source or a proved reconstructive record. Reassociation coherence, equality on a chosen packet and bounded analytical transport must not be promoted to global faithfulness. Full-jet reconstruction on completed sources remains a separate gate unless explicitly established.

## 4. Scalar detectors -> source-action-saturated observers

The detectors ell_r are selected scalar evaluations. Saturation retains every contextual functional ell_r(a x b), restoring the required source action. It does not restore arbitrary invisible source directions.

The owning construction, `../nima/source-action-saturation-builds-an-all-depth-residual-observer-tower.md`, explicitly says Obs_m is onto but need not be injective and makes no general reconstruction claim.

The observer is therefore a quotient view of the source. Its inverse-tower restriction maps are also potentially lossy when used as state updates. Adding observations is information enrichment; projecting back to an earlier stage is not enrichment.

Verdict: saturation repairs equivariance, not faithfulness. The observer tower must remain attached to the source carrier rather than replace it without a residual.

## 5. Calibration and private-row corrections

The original cubic detector was replaced or supplemented by private-coordinate, sixteen-private, 270-private and matched-optimal tests. Agreement on the cubic layer does not imply agreement on the whole filtered source. The corrected construction appropriately retains lower-filtration discrepancies.

Verdict: an invertible frame change with its inverse is acceptable. Replacing an old source functional merely because a new one agrees on 270 cubic products is not a lossless transition. Retain discrepancies, context actions and normalization metadata; numerical norm optimization is not a reconstruction theorem.

## 6. Vanishing observed attachment classes

The saturated observer can detect a nonzero witness while sending an adjacent extension class to zero. The documented nullhomotopy establishes vanishing of the IMAGE class in a specified target category. It does not annihilate the source extension.

The filtered/unfiltered distinction is equally substantive: forgetting the filtration can make a nonzero filtered class trivial unfiltered.

Verdict: retain the source extension, its filtration, the comparison map and the nullhomotopy. “Zero after this observation” is permitted; “the source attachment has vanished” is not. Preserving source elements alone would also be insufficient if the filtration or source action were silently dropped.

## 7. Finite implementation: 449 readings -> one scalar

The finite receiver consumes 449 joint readings and returns a weighted scalar with error information. The interface does not prescribe caller archival. The scalar sum is not an injective encoding of its input vector.

Verdict: the scalar is a valid derived output. Discarding the input readings violates the rule unless a reconstructive residual is retained. Finite-filter approximation also retains an error statement, not an exact reconstruction of an unknown continuous signal. Preserve the actual acquisition evidence and its uncertainty; do not call an error enclosure a recovered exact source.

## 8. The vacuum blind direction

The documented source k=a_0 b_0 c_0 is nonzero, consists of eight forgotten paths, and is invisible to every specified old saturated detector. The source pair x and x+epsilon k remains distinct, even within the same admissibility budgets.

The owning note `../nima/a-vacuum-channel-distinguishes-sources-the-residual-observer-tower-cannot-see.md` already makes this distinction correctly. It is precisely the source-retention principle in action.

Verdict: k never vanished from the source. It vanished only from the selected view. If only the old observer value survives, later recalibration cannot recover its coefficient. If the exact symbolic source is retained, its vacuum value can be computed; for an unknown physical source, the new coordinate requires independent acquisition or justification.

## 9. Vacuum acquisition and retention-dependent increments

Adjoining the vacuum row refines the observer equivalence relation. It does not create a previously nonexistent source component.

For the stated protocols the restriction kernels have dimensions seven for combined-scalar retention and six for raw-reading retention. Their inherited filtrations are (7,5,1,0) and (6,5,1,0). The difference concerns what was already visible in the OLD observer, not a difference in the underlying source.

Verdict: legitimate enrichment. Calling its kernel the entire newly existing source information would be wrong. Nonsplitting also prevents assuming a canonical old-state-plus-new-coordinate decomposition.

## 10. Scalar plus row 76: task-sufficient, not lossless

The minimal policy reproduces the six-state vacuum increment. It was explicitly certified only for that comparison. The subsequent calculation exhibits a 15-dimensional source submodule of K=ker(E_raw->E_minimal); the full K is not yet classified.

Verdict: valid view, but NOT a lossless replacement for the raw observer. Keep the raw readings or a genuinely reconstructive residual. An abstract 15-dimensional module, a witness list, or the statement that K exists does not retain the actual discarded values.

The filtered pullback theorem says K lifts canonically through vacuum acquisition. It does not say K is zero or disposable, nor that the compression extension itself splits.

## Review conclusion

The earliest explicitly noninjective transition is rho, not the final receiver sum. Much of the source mathematics already recognizes and studies its residual. The repeated interpretive hazard is later treating an observer quotient, an image class, or a task-sufficient compression as the complete retained state.

The chain should henceforth distinguish:

- authoritative source/acquisition data, preserved recoverably;
- derived observer views, with their kernels and scopes;
- uncertainty or unmeasured source ambiguity;
- structure that must survive alongside values: labels, action, filtration and extension maps.

No existing quotient needs to be abolished. What must be prohibited is an unmarked promotion of a quotient view to an authoritative replacement.

The next implementation gate is therefore a retention ledger for each actual handoff: incoming carrier, output view, noninjectivity witness or inverse, retained source/residual reference, reconstruction procedure, structural metadata and uncertainty contract. Until such a ledger exists, the analytical comparison certificates should not be labelled lossless-transition certificates.
