# A conditional progress lemma for rooted typed copier/query nets

Fresh `check_rooted_nonterminal_progress.py` checks the directed root/phase/tail predicates on all 6,400 alpha states and 13,431 individual-redex transitions for 86 inputs n<=2. Every no-redex state is exactly two BOOL--OUT pairs (86 terminal classes across the inputs); 232 states have every query/eraser principal waiting at COPY auxiliary but COPY remains enabled. These are bounded checks, not a confluence result.

**Conditional progress lemma (finite, fully wired port graphs).** Assume: (1) each connected component has an OUT or ERASE node; each OUT_i meets a one-port BOOL or channel-i Q; (2) any COPY.p meets B0/B1/NIL.p; (3) each Q/E principal either meets a corresponding typed B/K/N principal admitting its phase rule, or waits on COPY.a/b; (4) only the stated typed active pairs occur. Then a graph with no active pair consists solely of two BOOL--OUT pairs. Proof: a COPY would itself give a redex. With no COPY, no Q/E can wait, so any Q or E would give a redex. The remaining OUTs must meet BOOL. Each BOOL and OUT is unary and closed as a two-agent component. Root coverage forbids any other component; hence exactly two pairs. This is a graph-theoretic implication under the stated conditions, independent of n. It does NOT establish that every reachable arbitrary-n graph satisfies those premises; induction/preservation of the component grammar remains open. It also says nothing about unique normal forms or correct boolean values.

Next write an explicit constructor and 15-rule preservation ledger for these four progress premises, testing changes at every rewrite boundary. In particular, root coverage preservation must account for detached erasers and COPY's disappearance on NIL; phase cases must account for Q_S turning a budget tail back into Q_B support state.
