# Raw lex proof choice fails row-order naturality

The two zero-surplus proofs B=(1,2,0,1) and C=(0,1,1,2) of the same weak row `x+y<=3` expose a sharper policy failure. In the original primitive-row order, raw lexicographic choice picks C. Apply a positive row-presentation isomorphism that reorders old rows as (y-low,y-high,x-low,x-high), with positive scales (2,3,1/2,4). In NEW coordinate order, raw lexicographic choice picks the transported B instead of the transported C. Fresh `check_square_lex_policy_naturality.py` checks the exact inequality, plus 81 fixed-order positive scaling controls where raw lex DOES remain stable.

A different, explicitly SOURCE-ROOTED key does commute with this presentation change: sort by retained primitive row identity and compare the original normalized row contributions (new multiplier times its positive row scale). It still picks the transported C. This rescue depends on the row-identity bijection and source scales being retained, not just the feasible square or raw multiplier tuple. It does NOT imply composition-functorial choice across different proof factorizations; the earlier min-surplus and sparse-selector counterexamples remain.

This closes the fixed square row-isomorphism policy audit: exact presentation transport forms a groupoid, redundant-row extension is a split nonisomorphism, surplus transport composes for frozen derivations, and unrooted coordinate-lex selection fails even under an isomorphism.
