# Derivative plus boundary observation is stable on a finite interval but not on the half-line

## Question

Does the Green-boundary observer architecture transfer to a non-arithmetic first-order system, and which geometric hypothesis controls stability?

## Claim boundary

For the Sobolev graph domain on a finite interval, derivative observation plus one endpoint trace is bounded below. On the half-line, the same constructor fails because normalized broad packets can have vanishing derivative and zero endpoint trace. The distinction is a Poincare-type confinement property, not boundary sewing or Real coherence.

## Problem

Let

\[
\mathcal E_I=H^1(I)
\]

with graph norm

\[
\|f\|_{H^1(I)}^2
=
\|f\|_{L^2(I)}^2+
\|f'\|_{L^2(I)}^2.
\]

Consider the observer

\[
Q_If=(f',f(0))
\]

with target

\[
L^2(I)\oplus\mathbb C.
\]

This combines a bulk derivative with one boundary trace.

## Bold conjecture

The same derivative-plus-endpoint observer is bounded below on every connected one-dimensional radial domain.

## Named rivals

1. Finite interval length supplies a Poincare estimate.
2. The half-line admits broad translated or dilated packets invisible to derivative and endpoint observation.
3. Boundary trace alone is sufficient because it fixes the integration constant.
4. Green and Real compatibility determine the lower margin.

## Finite-interval theorem

Let

\[
I=(0,L),
\qquad 0<L<\infty.
\]

For every \(f\in H^1(0,L)\),

\[
f(r)=f(0)+\int_0^r f'(s)\,ds.
\]

By Cauchy--Schwarz,

\[
|f(r)|^2
\le
2|f(0)|^2+2r\|f'\|_{L^2(0,L)}^2.
\]

Integrating in \(r\) gives

\[
\|f\|_{L^2(0,L)}^2
\le
2L|f(0)|^2+L^2\|f'\|_{L^2(0,L)}^2.
\]

Therefore

\[
\|f\|_{H^1(0,L)}^2
\le
2L|f(0)|^2+(L^2+1)\|f'\|_{L^2(0,L)}^2.
\]

Writing

\[
M_L=\max\{2L,L^2+1\},
\]

we obtain

\[
\|Q_If\|^2
=
\|f'\|^2+|f(0)|^2
\ge
M_L^{-1}\|f\|_{H^1}^2.
\]

Thus \(Q_I\) is bounded below. The endpoint trace repairs the one-dimensional kernel of the derivative, while the derivative supplies the cofinite bulk margin.

## Constructor-role factorization

On the finite interval:

- `bulk_derivative_observer`: \(f\mapsto f'\), with kernel the constant functions;
- `boundary_finite_defect_repair`: \(f\mapsto f(0)\), injective on that kernel;
- `stable_joint_observer`: \(Q_I\).

This is exactly the essential-margin plus finite-defect-repair architecture.

Boundary trace alone is not sufficient: its kernel

\[
\{f\in H^1(0,L):f(0)=0\}
\]

is infinite-dimensional. Rival 3 fails.

## Half-line counterexample

Now let

\[
I=(0,\infty).
\]

Choose a nonzero smooth compactly supported function

\[
\phi\in C_c^\infty(1,2)
\]

with \(\|\phi\|_{L^2}=1\). Define the broad packets

\[
f_n(r)=n^{-1/2}\phi(r/n).
\]

Then each \(f_n\) is supported in \((n,2n)\), so

\[
f_n(0)=0.
\]

A change of variables gives

\[
\|f_n\|_{L^2(0,\infty)}=1
\]

and

\[
\|f_n'\|_{L^2(0,\infty)}
=
\frac1n\|\phi'\|_{L^2}.
\]

Hence

\[
\|f_n\|_{H^1}\to1
\]

while

\[
\|Q_If_n\|
=
\|f_n'\|
\to0.
\]

The half-line observer is not bounded below. This rejects the bold conjecture.

The variable \(r\) is a radial coordinate; no temporal interpretation is used.

## Nature of the obstruction

The half-line defect is not an exact kernel: every nonzero \(L^2\) constant function is excluded. It is an essential approximate kernel generated by arbitrarily broad packets far from the endpoint. A finite-dimensional boundary trace cannot detect it.

In Calkin terms, the derivative-plus-endpoint Gramian has zero in its essential spectrum on the half-line. The endpoint trace changes only finite-rank data.

## Doubled Green system

For

\[
\mathbb D=
\operatorname{diag}(\partial_r,-\partial_r)
\]

apply the construction to both orientations. On a finite interval, endpoint traces at one chosen end plus the two derivative channels control the doubled graph norm. Reciprocal exchange permutes the two copies and Green/Real comparisons preserve the estimate.

On the half-line, take the broad packet in either reciprocal parity eigenspace:

\[
(f_n,\pm uf_n).
\]

Both endpoint traces vanish and the derivative norm tends to zero. Thus reciprocal doubling, maximal-isotropic wall sewing, and the twisted Real structure do not remove the essential approximate kernel.

## Symmetry consequence

The canonical fold and phase gauges are graph-unitary, so they transport the finite-interval estimate and the half-line counterexample without changing constants. Symmetry coherence preserves both stability and instability; it does not decide which one occurs.

## Why finite interval works

The operative hypothesis is the finite-interval Poincare estimate with endpoint anchoring. It bounds the bulk \(L^2\) norm by derivative energy plus one trace. The half-line lacks a corresponding uniform estimate because support scale and radial location are unbounded.

No inference from finite interval to half-line is valid without an additional weight, confining potential, bulk mass term, or other source-derived coercive structure.

## Possible repairs, typed

A half-line repair could use:

- a weighted bulk observer bounded below on broad packets;
- a confining graph norm;
- an admitted mass term;
- an infinite family of localized observers with a frame bound.

Each changes the observer or source topology. Adding more copies of the endpoint trace does not.

## Strongest falsification attempt

The derivative has no nonzero constant kernel in \(L^2(0,\infty)\), so one might expect injectivity to imply stability. The broad packets show why this fails: injectivity excludes exact zero modes but not an essential approximate kernel. This is the same distinction exposed by the row-operator and Calkin theorems.

## Disposition

The programme transfers beyond the arithmetic example. On a finite interval, derivative observation supplies the essential margin and the boundary trace repairs a finite kernel. On the half-line, broad packets produce essential instability despite exact Green sewing and Real symmetry. The next frontier is to characterize weights or localized observer frames that restore the missing half-line margin.
