# Surviving bit and budget tails have strict recursive successors

Fresh `check_recursive_tail_endpoints.py` inspects four fully wired small inputs under all individual redex choices: 219 alpha-states and 452 transitions. The original generous endpoint grammar can be STRENGTHENED: every surviving K.a connects only to another K.p or NIL.p, and every surviving B0/B1.a connects only to B0/B1.p, NIL.p, or COPY.a/b. In these states NO surviving B/K auxiliary attaches directly to a query or eraser; their reduction rules eliminate the bit/budget head and reconnect its successor to the query/eraser. Traversing each surviving chain terminates at NIL or, for bit chains, COPY.a/b; loops are rejected by the validator. These observations give a simple finite recursive tail syntax: Budget ::= NIL | K(Budget); BitTail ::= NIL | B0(BitTail) | B1(BitTail) | B0/B1(...COPY auxiliary frontier), with COPY auxiliary as a possibly shared continuation rather than a closed node.

This remains a necessary local grammar, not a complete global grammar: the frontier belongs to one shared copier, output paths and detached eraser tails must still be constrained jointly; disconnected but locally typed stuck graphs can pass. The finite experiment is not an arbitrary-n induction.

Next validate global coverage: every surviving bit/budget tail root must be reached from exactly one Q/E principal, Q auxiliary or COPY frontier (respecting the original support suffix); both output roots must be Q or BOOL.
