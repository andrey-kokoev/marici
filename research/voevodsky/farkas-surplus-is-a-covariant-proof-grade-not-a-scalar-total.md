# Farkas surplus is a covariant proof grade, not a scalar total

On a fixed one-coordinate chart choose source residual normals A=(-1,1) and r=(0,1). The first Farkas arrow uses nontrivial mixing M with rows `(1,0),(0,1),(1,1)`, reference shift d=1/4 and positive surplus c=(0,1/2,1/3). The second arrow uses N rows `(1,0,0),(0,1,1)`, shift e=-1/8 and k=(1/5,2/5). Exact rational verification gives final normals (-1,1), total reference shift 1/8 and composite surplus `N c+k=(1/5,37/30)`. The direct residual formula and staged formula agree coordinatewise, not just as feasible-set inclusion. Fresh `check_farkas_surplus_transport.py` passes these equations and rejects negative surplus, negative multiplier, wrong residual/reference transport and a same-total-but-differently-distributed surplus.

The invariant is typed: surplus resides in the TARGET ROW space and transports covariantly by the NEXT Farkas multiplier N. A single number `sum(c)` is not enough because N weights/merges rows asymmetrically. This extends the two redundant-row labels to a genuine nontrivial mixture and chart shift, but it does NOT construct an analytic residue jet, source history authority or a row-presentation invariant graded cofiber. Passing one composite square also does not show arbitrary proof equivalences respect a quotient.
