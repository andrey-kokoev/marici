**Question:** Which results from this window extend 4338, and which use a different model?

**Answer:** There are three different state spaces: retained P/N/E histories, paired edge fillings, and Ramsey graphs. We obtained exact results inside each. We did not derive the Ramsey thresholds by identifying their state counts.

### Continuations after one P→N entry

For neutron stays and crossings followed by one attempted N→E exit, immediate readings are blocked, pending, or certified. Closing the implemented geometric summaries under the two moves gave 276 summaries per aligned seed. Refinement by future exit behavior gave **seven classes**, separated already by the three probes “now / after stay / after cross.” The three present readings have fibers of sizes 4, 1, 2, respectively. Three common crossings reset all seven classes to the blocked sink **for neutron-only continuation**. This is not 4338's eight-state wiring action. Allowing one return to P with one proton reread splits each of the seven classes in the tested prefixes through length eight: **at least 14** joint classes, not a proof of exactly 14. An unwitnessed P–N face and an unwitnessed N–N face could each be repaired by a later legal path in explicit examples. ### Paired edges: exact ideal, different carrier

On edges of length **n = l+k**, use a common column index (reverse the opposite edge's own numbering). Each column must contain at least one colored cell, and there are exactly n colored cells in total. Hence each column contains exactly one. If x_i records red, the opposite blue indicator is 1−x_i. The ideal over the reals is generated by x_i²−x_i and Σx_i−l. Its dimension is binomial(n,l): choosing the blue indices is not another independent choice. For l=3 and k=7,8,9,10 the counts are **120, 165, 220, 286**. The ledger's ten pairs of five labels are not these 120 fillings.

Order is an *extra* constraint. Requiring one change along the open (3,7) edge leaves two reversed fillings. Given this promise, one end probe identifies the filling. Testing the promise from the count ideal takes four freely chosen column probes, or six under the alternating-end rule. Inferring that both a rise and a fall exist, locating both, and directly reading both gaps are different tasks: on rejected fillings their worst alternating depths were 6, 9, and 10. [Free-probe checker](../checkers/edge37_order_probe_depth.py) · [signal-resolution checker](../checkers/edge37_signal_resolution.py).

The proposed square boundary has an elementary meaning: l red columns make l+1 gaps on a line. If no gap has l blues, at most (l+1)(l−1) blues fit. So an l-run is first forced at **k=l²**. On a circle there are only l gaps: the threshold is **l(l−1)+1**. A marked start is not a cut unless comparisons across it are forbidden. At (3,9) a blue triple on the line is guaranteed without probing; naming one takes seven free probes, or eleven alternating probes. Naming one of the four fixed disjoint triples takes nine free probes. [Location search](../checkers/edge_forced_triple_probe.py).

Do not equate a run with a clique. Likewise the marked path `7R—G—7B—G—3R—G—3B` has length 23 by its written-position convention; unchanged it gives 25, 27, 29 for the next three targets. And the 12 directed rotation classes of the (3,7) circle are *alternative fillings*, not twelve vertices to add. The twelve extra vertices in **4338** are already accounted for by two copies of five labels and two poles.

### Enumeration-free track: proof checkpoint, not a finished theorem

The [scalar induction](../checkers/ramsey_affine_degree_bound.py) uses `n e(G) = Σ_v [d(v)² + e(G−N[v])]` in triangle-free graphs. No graph enumeration; upper bounds 24, 30, 37, 45 respectively. The window also gave direct lower-witness arguments for 22 (two rings of 11) and 35 (five layers of seven using shifts {1,2,4}). These do not close the upper bounds.

Key proof sketches to resume (conversation derivations, not independently formalized):

1. At order 18 with no independent six-set, local equality forces 5-regularity. Each 12-vertex nonneighbor graph has 20 edges, split by internal endpoint degrees into 4 edges of type (4,4), 8 of (4,3), 8 of (3,3). Each root then lies on 4+16+32=52 pentagons. Five does not divide 18·52. Hence R(3,6)≤18 without a catalogue.
2. At order 23 with no independent seven-set, choose an edge-maximal counterexample. Degrees are 5 or 6. At a degree-five root with q low neighbors, its 17 nonneighbors use 25−q cross edges. At least 9+q of them touch only one port; at most two can use each port alone. Thus q≤1. The 17-vertex edge lower bound 38 forces at most four low vertices. The four-low branch forces two disjoint low edges; the two ends of either edge give incompatible matching requirements for a singleton port of the other edge. The two **nonadjacent** low branch forces 17 residual vertices into singleton groups of sizes 1,2,2,2,2 and eight double attachments. Equality forces the second low vertex to have exactly two singleton neighbors, while the one-member group and the required pair matchings force at least three.
3. **Remaining:** two **adjacent** degree-five vertices, or a **6-regular** graph. In the regular case codegree four is excluded by a 25-versus-26 edge budget; counting four-cycles shows an odd number of pairs of codegree three. Such a pair leaves 12 vertices with 20–22 edges. At 20, four internal degree-four vertices form a square and the eight degree-three vertices form an eight-cycle with forced square labels. The attachment contradiction is missing. Do not report R(3,7)≤23 as obtained on this track.
