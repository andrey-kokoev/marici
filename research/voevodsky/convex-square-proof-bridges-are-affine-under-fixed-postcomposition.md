# Convex square proof bridges are affine under fixed postcomposition

On the irredundant unit-square source, let X and Y be the two distinct zero-surplus proofs of x<=T obtained from x- and y-cycles. Let I_t=(1-t)X+tY be their convex packet interpolation. A genuine Farkas postcomposition combines the intermediate row x<=T with the independently proved primitive row y<=1 using nonnegative coefficients k and ell and adds post-surplus delta. Its source proof is `P(m,c)=(k m+ell m_y, k c+delta)` for target `(k,ell)·(x,y)<=kT+ell+delta`. Since the same fixed second proof and the same delta occur on both sides, `P(I_t)=I_t(P(X),P(Y))` EXACTLY as packets. With delta>0, normalizing after postcomposition using the interpolated kernel `d_t=(1-t)d_x+t d_y` gives the interpolation of the separately normalized postproofs.

Fresh `check_square_affine_bridge_postcomposition.py` verifies 120 rational two-input squares, including 80 with positive added slack. This is stronger than the earlier single-target weakening tests and shows the affine bridge has a natural packet-level action under a fixed positive Farkas combination. The normalizer witness must also interpolate; blindly choosing d_x on the mixed route would not match both endpoints. Proof replay can still distinguish which source cycles and which postcomposition path generated a packet. The original category has no admitted interpolation 2-cell or naturality 3-cell; equal vectors are not those missing cells.
