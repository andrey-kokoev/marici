# A clean universal category for the eight-lattice four-chart suspension system

## Purpose

The tuple of indices is not itself a simplex. A complete formal model must specify objects, arrows, higher cells, the chart rotation, and the exactness relations imposed on analytic realizations.

This note gives such a model without identifying realization dimension, regulator rung, or homological suspension.

## The tetrahedral lattice

Let

\[
L=\operatorname{esd}_7(\Delta^3)
\]

be the seventh edgewise subdivision of the tetrahedral simplicial set. Its vertices include the barycentric lattice points

\[
\alpha=(\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3,\alpha_4),
\qquad
\alpha_a\geq0,
\qquad
\sum_a\alpha_a=7.
\]

The eight points on the conductor from corner \(i\) to corner \(j\) are

\[
C_{ij,m}=(7-m)e_i+me_j,
\qquad 0\leq m\leq7.
\]

Adjacency, triangles, and tetrahedra are not inferred from the coordinate tuple alone. They are the simplices of \(L\).

Let

\[
\int L
\]

be the simplex category of \(L\). Its objects are all lattice simplices, and its arrows are generated by face and degeneracy maps. This removes the earlier ambiguity about lattice incidence.

## External gradings

Let \(K\) and \(R\) be the categories indexing realization dimension and convolution/regulator rung.

Until source-derived transition maps are given, take both to be discrete:

\[
K=\mathbb N_{\mathrm{disc}},
\qquad
R=\mathbb N_{\mathrm{disc}}.
\]

Consequently the formalism makes no unproved assertion about transport in \(k\) or \(r\).

## Four-chart suspension helix

Define the helix category \(H_4\) with objects

\[
(h,i),
\qquad
h\in\mathbb Z,
\quad
1\leq i\leq4,
\]

and a generating successor \(q\) given by

\[
q(h,i)=
\begin{cases}
(h,i+1),&i<4,\\
(h+1,1),&i=4.
\end{cases}
\]

Define suspension translation by

\[
\Sigma(h,i)=(h+1,i).
\]

Then, strictly on objects and arrows,

\[
q^4=\Sigma,
\qquad
q\Sigma=\Sigma q.
\]

The index \(i\) is a phase framing for the helix. It is not a lattice coordinate and not a conductor midpoint.

## Rotation of the tetrahedron

Let \(\rho:L\to L\) be the order-four simplicial automorphism induced by cyclic permutation of the four tetrahedral coordinates. Fixing a direction convention, take

\[
\rho(\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3,\alpha_4)
=
(\alpha_4,\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3).
\]

It satisfies

\[
\rho^4=1.
\]

The helix successor acts simultaneously by \(q\) on \(H_4\) and by \(\rho\) on \(L\).

## Total indexing category

The complete source is the semidirect-product category

\[
\mathcal I
=
K\times R\times
\bigl(H_4\ltimes_\rho\int L\bigr).
\]

An object is

\[
(k,r,h,i,\sigma),
\]

where \(\sigma\) is a lattice simplex. For a lattice vertex, write \(\sigma=\alpha\).

Morphisms are generated by:

1. face and degeneracy maps in \(\int L\);
2. the helix successor
   \[
   q:(k,r,h,i,\sigma)
   \longrightarrow
   (k,r,q(h,i),\rho\sigma);
   \]
3. suspension translation;
4. any later admitted arrows in \(K\) or \(R\).

The defining mixed relation is

\[
q\,u=\rho(u)\,q
\]

for every lattice incidence map \(u\). It implies

\[
q^4=\Sigma
\]

because \(\rho^4=1\).

## Exactness completion

The ordinary indexing category records incidence but not quotient laws. Let

\[
\mathcal U
\]

be the free stable infinity-category generated by \(\mathcal I\), modulo the following relations:

1. every declared zero vertex is a zero object;
2. every oriented lattice edge is represented by a morphism;
3. every designated triangular quotient cell is a cofiber triangle;
4. every tetrahedral cell is the corresponding octahedral compatibility;
5. the helix relation is identified with stable suspension;
6. reciprocal reversal carries each generator to its declared adjoint mate.

Equivalently, \(\mathcal U\) is the stable localization of the spectral presheaf category on \(\mathcal I\) at these exactness maps.

This definition guarantees formal existence. It does not guarantee a nonzero analytic realization.

## Duality

Let \(\omega:L^{op}\to L\) reverse the tetrahedral orientation and let

\[
\mathbb D:\mathcal U^{op}\to\mathcal U
\]

be generated by reciprocal adjoint reversal. It acts schematically by

\[
\mathbb D(k,r,h,i,\sigma)
=
(k,r,-h,5-i,\omega\sigma),
\]

with indices normalized through the helix relation.

The required stable sign law is

\[
\mathbb D\Sigma
\simeq
\Sigma^{-1}\mathbb D.
\]

The precise determinant sign belongs to the orientation enhancement of \(\mathcal U\), not to its object set.

## Universal analytic representation

For any stable analytic infinity-category \(\mathcal C_{\rm cl}\) of admitted closed cone packages, an analytic realization is an exact functor

\[
F:\mathcal U\to\mathcal C_{\rm cl}
\]

together with a duality comparison

\[
F\mathbb D
\simeq
\mathbb D_{\rm cl}F^{op}.
\]

This is universal in the precise sense that specifying such an exact functor is equivalent to specifying closed analytic data on all generators satisfying the declared cofiber, octahedral, helix, and reciprocal relations.

## Waldhausen and 2-Segal image

Applying the Waldhausen construction gives

\[
S_\bullet(\mathcal U)
\longrightarrow
S_\bullet(\mathcal C_{\rm cl}).
\]

The source is the universal 2-Segal object for the declared quotient cells. Every analytic model is its exact image. Degree two records cofiber triangles, degree three records octahedra, and higher degrees record coherent iterated quotienting.

## Consistency status

This categorical presentation is internally consistent as a free stable construction provided the set of declared relations is small. A zero realization always exists, so the relations cannot be formally contradictory.

The substantive nontriviality question is separate: construct an exact functor \(F\) whose generator objects and conductor maps are the intended nonzero closed analytic packages.

Thus there are now two distinct assertions:

1. **formal consistency:** supplied by the free stable category \(\mathcal U\);
2. **analytic realizability and nontriviality:** still requires graph-domain, completion, and source-comparison theorems.
