# Möbius channel symmetry does not imply Weil positivity

## Typing distinction

Two unrelated two-dimensional spaces occur in the current discussion:

1. the local channel fiber generated by the two coherence directions;
2. a rank-two Gram packet generated by two translated Gaussian tests.

Both admit an exchange operation, but equality of dimensions and similar matrices do not identify them. A comparison would require a source-derived intertwiner preserving the relevant forms.

## Symmetry hostile

On the channel fiber, signed exchange is

\[
J=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&-1\\
-1&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Consider the symmetric form

\[
G=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&2\\
2&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

It is exactly invariant under channel transport:

\[
J^TGJ=G.
\]

Nevertheless, the fixed channel vector \((1,-1)\) has negative value. Reversing the cross sign gives another \(J\)-invariant form whose Möbius eigenline is negative instead.

Thus involutive orientation-reversing monodromy permits indefiniteness in either eigenchannel.

## Consequence

The Möbius theorem determines:

- the topology of channel transport;
- the splitting into fixed and sign-reversing eigenlines;
- the obstruction to global channel labels.

It does not determine:

- the Weil Hermitian form;
- the sign of any Gaussian Gram minor;
- positivity of the completed spectral distribution.

Even rank-two Weil positivity cannot be inferred from the topology. It requires a form-preserving map from the arithmetic kernel into a positive carrier, which is precisely the missing RH-bearing construction.

## Disposition

The route from Möbius nonorientability directly to RH positivity is closed. The geometric model remains useful for typing reciprocal transport and preventing global endpoint labels, but it supplies no positivity inequality.

Further progress must occur in the analytic arithmetic kernel itself, not by strengthening the channel topology.
