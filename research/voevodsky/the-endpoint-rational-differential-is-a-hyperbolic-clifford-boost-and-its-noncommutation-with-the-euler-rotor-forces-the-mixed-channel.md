# The endpoint rational differential is a hyperbolic Clifford boost and its noncommutation with the Euler rotor forces the mixed channel

## Indefinite Clifford plane

Choose Clifford generators with

\[
\gamma_1^2=\gamma_2^2=+I,
\qquad
\gamma_3^2=-I,
\]

and pairwise anticommutation. Then

\[
B_{12}=\gamma_1\gamma_2,
\qquad B_{12}^2=-I,
\]

is an elliptic rotation bivector, while

\[
B_{13}=\gamma_1\gamma_3,
\qquad B_{13}^2=+I,
\]

is a hyperbolic boost bivector. This is the minimal Clifford signature capable
of carrying both the unimodular Euler phase and the one-negative-direction
endpoint metric.

## Endpoint boost

Let

\[
p_{end}(z)=z^2+\frac14.
\]

On the open strip with a branch cut ending at the boundary zeros
\(z=\pm i/2\), define

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_{end}(z)
=
\exp\left(
\varepsilon_{end}B_{13}\log p_{end}(z)
\right).}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_{end}^{-1}\partial_z\mathcal R_{end}
=
\varepsilon_{end}B_{13}
\frac{2z}{z^2+1/4}.}
\]

Thus the completed endpoint rational differential is exactly the connection of
a hyperbolic Clifford boost. Its two boundary singularities are the completed
endpoint evaluations, and its single negative Clifford direction is the odd
endpoint parity.

The boost is Krein-unitary in the corresponding indefinite metric; it is not
an ordinary Hilbert-unitary rotation. This matches the endpoint swap form.

## Combination with the Euler--gamma rotor

The local scattering phase supplies

\[
\mathcal R_{loc,S}
=
\exp\left(\frac12B_{12}\varphi_S\right),
\]

whose connection is \(B_{12}V_{loc,S}\). Form the ordered completed transport

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_{comp,S}(z)
=
\mathcal R_{loc,S}(z)
\mathcal R_{end}(z).}
\]

Its logarithmic connection is

\[
\mathcal R_{comp,S}^{-1}d\mathcal R_{comp,S}
=
\operatorname{Ad}_{\mathcal R_{end}^{-1}}
(\mathcal R_{loc,S}^{-1}d\mathcal R_{loc,S})
+
\mathcal R_{end}^{-1}d\mathcal R_{end}.
\]

The two terms cannot be treated as orthogonal scalar summands because their
bivectors do not commute. Direct Clifford multiplication gives

\[
\boxed{
[B_{12},B_{13}]=-2B_{23},
\qquad B_{23}=\gamma_2\gamma_3.}
\]

Therefore ordered composition necessarily generates the third bivector
\(B_{23}\). This is the missing mixed gamma--prime/endpoint Green channel.
It is forced by Clifford closure rather than appended as an arbitrary coupling.

## Three-channel closure

The bivectors

\[
B_{12},\quad B_{13},\quad B_{23}
\]

close under commutators as the rank-one indefinite spin algebra. Hence the
completed local system is a Spin-type connection with:

- elliptic component: Euler--gamma phase current;
- hyperbolic component: endpoint residue boost;
- mixed component: Green coupling generated by their noncommutation.

This supplies a source-derived signed endpoint attachment to the previously
constructed local rotor.

## Prime and refinement compatibility

Prime succession changes only the coefficient of \(B_{12}\). The endpoint
boost is unchanged, while the induced \(B_{23}\) mixed component changes by the
adjoint action of the same fixed boost. Thus prime increments remain coherent
inside one three-bivector algebra rather than creating independent endpoint
copies.

The complete polyphase frame acts in the grade coordinate and commutes with the
finite Clifford action, so residue-class completion is retained.

## Positive projection

Krein-unitarity of \(\mathcal R_{comp,S}\) proves preservation of the signed
completed metric. It does not imply that its compression to the physical
Hilbert observation is contractive. That final statement is the positive
rung-four/Weil gate.

The advance is that the mixed channel is no longer an unspecified block
\(B_S\): at signed source level it is the forced \(B_{23}\) component of the
ordered elliptic--hyperbolic Clifford connection. The remaining task is to
identify its physical compression and prove that compression is positive.
