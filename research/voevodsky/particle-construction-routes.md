# Three construction routes and a conditional fourth

## Question

Operator intuition: proton, neutron and electron may be three composite “angles” of one construction, with a fourth completing a meta-tetrahedron. The operator then proposed recovering the procedures from a common barycenter to the three known endpoints, so the missing procedure could constrain the fourth.

The task here is to reconstruct those sequences from retained generators, without naming a new particle. Active obligations: **forward realization** and **route/comparison compatibility**.

## What common starting record can actually be recovered?

The three current constructions share an **oriented, labelled regular tetrahedral geometry**. The proton and electron routes use it directly. The belt route uses two opposed copies of a selected tetrahedral base.

A candidate central record must therefore retain the tetrahedral frame and labels as well as its center. The coordinate O=(0,0,0) alone is insufficient: multiple belt gaps give the same center and different constructions.

The retained sources use different scale and placement conventions. The checker verifies regular squared edge lengths 8 for the source proton coordinates and 3 for the displayed seed. These are similar tetrahedra, not literally identical coordinate arrays. Their original ABCD orders have opposite orientation: determinants −16 and +3/2 in their respective scaled frames. Comparing them by an orientation-preserving similarity requires exchanging the displayed C and D labels. That comparison is recorded explicitly; neither source is silently relabelled.

This recovers a common **geometric seed**, not yet one complete source state with three established view maps. In particular, the current proton and belt returns select one complex mode each, whereas the electron return retains two complex components.

## The first candidate fourth sequence

Two changes are visible in these routes:

- replace the tetrahedral face construction by the signed, paired belt construction;
- replace the vector-mode return by the spinorial face-projection return.

Combining those changes supplies a concrete candidate:

```text
common tetrahedral seed
→ opposed copies and stitched belt
→ retain the neutron route's face signs
→ apply the electron route's spinor projection rule
→ normalize the resulting frame
→ a fourth coherent return
```

This is a proposed procedural completion, not a proof that the four endpoints satisfy a meta-tetrahedral coherence law.

## Risky test: retain the actual neutron signs

Conjecture: applying the face-count normalization from the electron example to the six signed belt faces closes the fourth return without further work.

Rivals are: the sign pattern requires a different normalization; a successful construction accidentally discarded the neutron signs; or common geometry was mistaken for a common complete state.

Let n_i be the six outward belt normals and chi_i its recorded mode signs. The electron rule, which takes the opposite spinorial polarization, gives

\[
P_i=\frac{I-\chi_i n_i\cdot\sigma}{2}.
\]

For the actual gap and signs,

\[
S=\sum_iP_i
=3\left(I-\frac5{\sqrt{74}}\sigma_y\right).
\]

Thus the naive division by sqrt(3) fails. Two input spin states have total output weights 1−5/sqrt(74) and 1+5/sqrt(74), rather than one. The resulting return even has trace two, but is **not idempotent**.

This rejects normalization by face count alone; it does not reject the combined route.

## Recomputing the frame normalization

The frame has positive eigenvalues 3(1±5/sqrt(74)), so its positive inverse square root exists. Define

\[
J_i=P_iS^{-1/2},\qquad
N_{ij}=P_iS^{-1}P_j.
\]

Then J†J=I and N=JJ†. The exact inverse is

\[
S^{-1}=\frac{74I+5\sqrt{74}\,\sigma_y}{147}.
\]

The checker computes the full return over Q(sqrt(3),sqrt(74),i) and verifies that it is Hermitian, idempotent and of rank two. With L=diag(P_i), G=N and return N, the declared support is:

| Stage | Transfers |
|---|---:|
| L | 24 |
| G | 144 |
| N | 144 |
| Total | **312** |

There are twelve raw complex coordinates and two coherent components. The count identifies this particular protocol; it is not a particle identity or a minimum-over-all-constructions claim.

As a control, discarding the neutron signs gives a different valid return with 264 transfers. Thus preserving the actual third route matters: the unsigned belt is not an interchangeable substitute.

## Disposition

We now have three source-traced partial sequences and a **constructible fourth candidate**: the spinorial return on the signed belt, with its recomputed frame normalization. Its exact coefficients are retained.

What remains missing for the proposed meta-tetrahedron is the common *full* starting record, the typed comparisons between the original three protocols, and a completion law that selects this combined route rather than merely allowing it. The geometric center and the number of endpoints do not supply those comparisons.

The fourth endpoint remains unnamed. No existing viewer or earlier source construction was changed.
