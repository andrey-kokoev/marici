# The completed generic convolution-degree boundary observation is a projective strip graph

## Objective

Construct the completed generic degree-\(r\) boundary observation before computing its Krein--Langer defect.

The construction must retain:

1. the phase-energy real-boundary norm;
2. holomorphic observation in the open critical strip;
3. the completed endpoint traces;
4. convolution-degree naturality;
5. source labels and radicals.

A single unweighted \(L^2\) completion cannot do this because off-real evaluation is not continuous there.

## Open strip

Let

\[
\mathfrak S
=
\{z\in\mathbb C:|\operatorname{Im}z|<1/2\}.
\]

For \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\), define the closed inner strip

\[
\mathfrak S_\varepsilon
=
\{z:|\operatorname{Im}z|\le1/2-\varepsilon\}.
\]

Let \(H^2(\mathfrak S_\varepsilon)\) denote the two-boundary Hardy space with norm

\[
\|m\|_{H^2_\varepsilon}^2
=
\sup_{|y|\le1/2-\varepsilon}
\int_{\mathbb R}
|m(t+iy)|^2
\frac{dt}{2\pi}.
\]

Point evaluation at every point of the interior of \(\mathfrak S_\varepsilon\) is bounded on this Hilbert space.

## Degree-r analytic core

Let \(E_r^{an}\) be the degree-\(r\) convolution source generated by finite-character Bruhat--Schwartz observers whose Mellin transforms extend holomorphically to \(\mathfrak S\) and possess the declared endpoint traces.

For \(p\in E_r^{an}\), write

\[
m_p=\mathcal M_rp.
\]

The Mellin map is injective after quotienting the ordinary source null space.

## Phase-energy graph norm

The regular real-boundary completion is

\[
\mathscr E_S
=
L^2(e_Sdt),
\qquad
 e_S=1+\kappa_{loc,S}.
\]

At aperture \(\varepsilon\), define the analytic graph Hilbert space

\[
\mathscr G_{r,\varepsilon}
=
\overline{E_r^{an}/\mathcal N_{r,\varepsilon}}
^{\|\cdot\|_{r,\varepsilon}},
\]

where

\[
\|p\|_{r,\varepsilon}^2
=
\|m_p\|_{\mathscr E_S}^2
+
\|m_p\|_{H^2_\varepsilon}^2
+
\|\Gamma_{end,r}p\|_{\mathscr H_{end}}^2.
\]

The null space is

\[
\mathcal N_{r,\varepsilon}
=
\{p:
\|p\|_{r,\varepsilon}=0
\}.
\]

The endpoint term uses the already constructed second-order endpoint graph trace. It is not inferred from the inner-strip Hardy norm.

## Projective completion

If \(0<\varepsilon'<\varepsilon\), then \(\mathfrak S_{\varepsilon'}\) is wider and its graph norm is stronger. Restriction gives a continuous map

\[
R_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon'}:
\mathscr G_{r,\varepsilon'}
\to
\mathscr G_{r,\varepsilon}.
\]

Define the completed generic degree-\(r\) source as the projective graph

\[
\mathscr G_r
=
\varprojlim_{
\varepsilon\downarrow0
}
\mathscr G_{r,\varepsilon}.
\]

This is a complete locally convex source carrying all inner-strip evaluations and the independent completed endpoint bundle.

It is preferable to forcing one Hilbert norm that treats all approaches to the endpoint lines uniformly.

## Holomorphic boundary observation

Let

\[
\operatorname{Hol}(\mathfrak S)
\]

carry the compact-open topology. Define

\[
\mathcal O_r^{hol}:
\mathscr G_r
\to
\operatorname{Hol}(\mathfrak S)
\]

by

\[
\mathcal O_r^{hol}(p)=m_p.
\]

This map is continuous. On every compact subset of the strip, choose an aperture containing it and apply the Hardy point-evaluation bound.

The endpoint observation is the separate continuous map

\[
\beta_r:
\mathscr G_r
\to
\mathscr H_{end}.
\]

The complete boundary observation is

\[
\mathcal O_r
=
(\mathcal O_r^{hol},\beta_r).
\]

## Finite kernel observations

For a finite point packet

\[
Z=(z_1,\ldots,z_N)
\subset\mathfrak S,
\]

define

\[
\mathcal O_{r,Z}p
=
\begin{pmatrix}
m_p(z_1)\\
\vdots\\
m_p(z_N)
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

This is a bounded map on every \(\mathscr G_{r,\varepsilon}\) whose inner strip contains \(Z\), and a continuous map on \(\mathscr G_r\).

Pairing with the finite de Branges Gram matrix gives the signed boundary form

\[
q_{r,Z}(p,q)
=
\mathcal O_{r,Z}(q)^*
G_{K_S,Z}
\mathcal O_{r,Z}(p).
\]

No positivity of \(G_{K_S,Z}\) is assumed.

## Endpoint observation

The completed endpoint coordinates are

\[
\beta_r(p)
=
\begin{pmatrix}
m_p(i/2)\\
m_p(-i/2)
\end{pmatrix}
\]

on the analytic core, interpreted through the continuous endpoint graph extension after completion.

Their signed metric is the swap form

\[
J_{end}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The open-strip observation and endpoint trace are retained as separate target coordinates because endpoint evaluation is not continuous in a fixed inner-strip norm as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\).

## Admissible convolution multipliers

For \(a\in E_s^{an}\), convolution by \(a\) acts on Mellin amplitudes by multiplication with \(m_a\).

To extend this action to \(\mathscr G_r\), require that for every aperture

\[
m_a\in H^\infty(\mathfrak S_\varepsilon)
\]

and that multiplication by \(m_a\) is bounded on the phase-energy and endpoint graph components.

Call such \(a\) an admitted degree multiplier.

For an admitted \(a\), convolution extends continuously:

\[
L_a^{(r)}:
\mathscr G_r
\to
\mathscr G_{r+s}.
\]

## Holomorphic naturality

The completed observation satisfies

\[
\mathcal O_{r+s}^{hol}L_a^{(r)}
=
M_{m_a}\mathcal O_r^{hol}.
\]

For a finite packet \(Z\), this becomes

\[
\mathcal O_{r+s,Z}L_a^{(r)}
=D_{a,Z}\mathcal O_{r,Z},
\]

where

\[
D_{a,Z}
=
\operatorname{diag}
(m_a(z_1),\ldots,m_a(z_N)).
\]

At the completed endpoints,

\[
\beta_{r+s}L_a^{(r)}
=D_a^\partial\beta_r,
\]

where

\[
D_a^\partial
=
\operatorname{diag}
(m_a(i/2),m_a(-i/2)).
\]

These equations hold first on the analytic core and then on the projective completion by continuity.

## Composition

For admitted multipliers \(a\in E_s^{an}\) and \(b\in E_t^{an}\), one has

\[
L_b^{(r+s)}L_a^{(r)}
=L_{b*a}^{(r)}.
\]

The observation mates compose strictly:

\[
D_{b,Z}D_{a,Z}
=D_{b*a,Z},
\]

and

\[
D_b^\partial D_a^\partial
=D_{b*a}^\partial.
\]

Thus convolution-degree naturality survives completion.

## Radical

The completed observation radical is

\[
\operatorname{Rad}\mathcal O_r
=
\ker\mathcal O_r^{hol}
\cap
\ker\beta_r.
\]

Because a holomorphic function vanishing throughout the strip is zero, Mellin injectivity gives a trivial radical after the source null quotient:

\[
\operatorname{Rad}\mathcal O_r=0.
\]

This retains source faithfulness before any positive or negative kernel compression.

## Relation to the phase-energy boundary

Restriction to the real line gives the continuous map

\[
\mathscr G_r
\to
\mathscr E_S.
\]

The bounded normalized Tate multiplier

\[
\mathcal A_S=M_{w_S/e_S}
\]

therefore acts on the regular boundary coordinate of every degree cell.

The holomorphic observation adds divisor and endpoint detection without changing the already constructed phase-energy minimal boundary.

## What is constructed

For every convolution degree \(r\), the following are now defined:

1. a complete projective graph source \(\mathscr G_r\);
2. a continuous holomorphic strip observation;
3. a continuous completed endpoint observation;
4. bounded finite-point kernel observations;
5. continuous degree successors for admitted multipliers;
6. strict composition and naturality laws;
7. a trivial observation radical.

## Remaining estimates

The construction is complete as a projective graph. Stronger Hilbert-category claims require additional estimates:

1. one aperture-independent Hilbert norm controlling approach to both endpoint lines;
2. boundedness of every desired convolution successor, rather than the admitted multiplier subclass;
3. angular summability uniformly over apertures;
4. compatibility with infinite Blaschke sampling in one Hilbert target.

These belong to the next defect-space computation and infinite-completion stages.

## Disposition

The completed generic degree-\(r\) boundary observation is

\[
\mathcal O_r:
\mathscr G_r
\to
\operatorname{Hol}(\mathfrak S)
\oplus
\mathscr H_{end}.
\]

It is continuous, source faithful, multiplicative under admitted convolution successors, and compatible with every finite de Branges/Krein--Langer observation packet.
