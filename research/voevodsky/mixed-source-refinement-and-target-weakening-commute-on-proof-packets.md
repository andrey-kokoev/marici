# Mixed source refinement and target weakening commute on proof packets

On S_L=[0,L] with target x<=T, let F_LK refine the source to K<=L by adding `(L-K)b` to proof surplus; let W_TU weaken the target to U>=T by adding `U-T`. The two increments add independently, hence `W_TU F_LK(p) = F_LK W_TU(p)` **strictly on raw Farkas proof packets**, whenever the retained primitive bounds and domain types are valid. Re-normalizing at the final (K,U) yields the unique proof `(U/K-1,U/K;0)` along both routes. Normalizing at an intermediate stop does not generally preserve strict packet equality, but re-normalizing at the tip joins the routes.

Fresh exact-rational `check_mixed_farkas_comparison_square.py` checks 70 mixed squares on `0<K<=L<=T<=U`; 35 intermediate packet equalities fail despite strict raw transport commutation and common normalized tips. The checker rejects omitted primitive source rows. This distinguishes three separate claims: (i) strict commutation of two additive raw transport formulas in this restricted 1D family; (ii) lax coherence of intermediate normalizers; (iii) still absent proof-history path equality, even though the raw square agrees as packets. The free-grammar 4-cell obstruction remains in force when fine replay of rewrite steps is permitted.

The scoped endpoint law now covers a nontrivial two-axis calculus of nested source refinement and target weakening, but not arbitrary source widening or analytic S,A,R,C,G roles. The next gate should test a genuine non-additive operation (for example a source-row rescaling or a general affine target map) and determine whether the normalizer can be transported naturally with correct bounds; simply adding more additive squares would not address the source-rooted comparison problem.
