# Zero-newest-row choice fails the two-extension diamond

Starting with old proof p=(0,1,0,1) of x+y<=2, add two exact duplicate inequalities z and w, each `x+y<=2` but with DISTINCT row IDs. Candidate sets after one extension are {p,z} and {p,w}; after both they are {p,z,w}. A relative selector minimizing newest-row multiplier, then lex in prior coordinate order, picks p at either intermediate stage. At the final common presentation, z-first makes w newest and selects z, whereas w-first makes z newest and selects w. The final candidate set contains both earlier p choices, so this is NOT a failure of inclusion closure. The first version of `check_two_extension_relative_selector.py` incorrectly predicted p in both final routes; the failed run exposed the tie at zero newest multiplier. The corrected fresh run checks the diamond explicitly.

Both final choices prove exactly the same normal and bound. A BASE-ANCHORED priority minimizing total multiplier on all non-base rows picks p on this finite set, regardless of order. That repair presupposes a durable base-root inclusion and excludes newly preferred proof histories; no general composition-functorial selector follows. Without the row-introduction and ancestry maps, the phrase 'fresh row' has no invariant meaning under opposite extension orders.
