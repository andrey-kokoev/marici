## Discriminating relations

1. If a quadratic readout factors as q(v)=norm(BPv)^2 with positive definite pairing, ker(P) is invisible. The converse fails if B erases additional directions. This does not equate pairwise arity with quadratic degree.
2. Darkness does not imply empty realization: the existing state (0,1) is dark under first-coordinate projection, even when realization is the identity on all of Q^2.
3. A linear witness reader F descends through s only if ker(s) is contained in ker(F). A surjective s(x,y)=x and F(x,y)=y demonstrate that failure of witness recovery is compatible with every target having a representative.
4. Let mu:A->M and rho:M->R. Compressed filler existence implies actual filler existence for every admitted m precisely when im(mu) is a union of rho-fibers. Proof: both statements say that rho(m)=rho(mu(a)) implies m belongs to im(mu). This condition concerns admitted m; absent configurations are outside M and have no such test.
5. The common-source conjecture requires the erasure mechanisms to inhabit one independently constructed Hodge diagram. Separate existential examples cannot be conjoined without that common witness.
6. An independently defined class-level O killing every algebraic class but not alpha would refute Hodge. This elementary implication does not establish any of its premises.

The fourth relation localizes the recalled empty/nonpresent intuition more sharply: compression can turn a genuinely empty fiber into an apparently populated readout fiber. It need not erase domain membership to do so. This is distinct from a nonzero source mode with zero intensity.
