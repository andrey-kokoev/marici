# Transverse coherence planes have a shared determinant line

## Question

When does the common incidence of the forward and backward coherence planes supply the real rank-one line required by the orientation-torsor construction?

## Construction

Let the local carrier tangent space be a real three-dimensional vector space \(E\). Let the two plane tangents be kernels of conormals

\[
\alpha_+,
\qquad
\alpha_-
\]

in \(E^*\).

Their shared tangent space is

\[
L=
\ker\alpha_+
\cap
\ker\alpha_-.
\]

If the conormals are independent, their joint map has rank two. Rank-nullity gives

\[
\dim L=3-2=1.
\]

The determinant line of the shared incidence is therefore

\[
\det L=L.
\]

Its orientation type is the required \(C_2\)-torsor.

## Exact model

For the right-angle embedding, take the planes \(z=0\) and \(y=0\). Their conormals are independent, and their common kernel is generated by

\[
(1,0,0).
\]

The checker verifies conormal rank two and shared-intersection rank one exactly.

## Transversality hostile

If the plane conormals are proportional, the two planes coincide. Their joint conormal rank is one, so their intersection has rank two rather than one. No distinguished shared line follows.

Thus plane incidence alone is insufficient. The required condition is transverse incidence inside a rank-three real carrier.

## Disposition

Within the geometric thickening, the previously assumed line is now derived:

1. rank-three carrier tangent;
2. two independent plane conormals;
3. rank-one shared kernel;
4. orientation \(C_2\)-torsor;
5. parity and reversal action;
6. invariant right-angle metric up to scale.

The 2-Segal object alone does not supply a real tangent carrier or conormals. The geometric enrichment must include those objects before the line can be constructed.
