# Restoring one vertex lowers a shadow-cover budget by at least one

Let H=G−D and v∈D. A class (b,T) contains supports that contain b and miss the independent set T⊂H; its capacity is k=11−|T|. Covers may have nonnegative weights, provided every legal support receives weight ≥1. An integral cover is the special case of unit weights. Let B(D) be the minimum weighted (respectively integral) budget. Then B(D−{v})≤B(D)−1, whenever B(D) is finite. Thus B(D)−|D| is nondecreasing under further deletion.

Proof. The original support A=N(v)∩H is legal. Restore v. In every class containing A, replace T by T∪{v}. This set is independent because A missed T. A legal support S on H+v projects to a legal support on H. If that projection lies in this class then b∈S and b∈A; hence v∉S by independence of S. Therefore S still misses the enlarged set, so its coverage is preserved. If the resulting capacity is zero the class can be discarded: no legal support misses an independent eleven-set. Other classes stay unchanged. The budget falls by the weight of the classes covering A, which is at least one. This proof requires neither saturation nor an optimal assignment of supports to classes.

Consequences on the stored witness. Both known integral budget-six failures force integral budget ≥8 for each of their 46 immediate seven-deletion descendants (91 distinct cores). At their common descendant {10,14,37,39,41,43,47}, the fractional minimum is nevertheless exactly seven: rational upper certificate plus the seven restored originals as lower bound. The fractional gap from the deletion count is zero, not inherited from the integral gap. No assertion is made about the fractional budget on the other 90 descendants. Conversely one tight fractional cover certifies tightness for every subset of its deletion set by restriction.
