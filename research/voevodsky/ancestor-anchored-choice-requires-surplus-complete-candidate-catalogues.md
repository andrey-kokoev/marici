# Ancestor-anchored choice requires surplus-complete candidate catalogues

For weak new row `x+y<=3`, the frozen old upper-row packet p=(0,1,0,1;c0) certifies bound 2, not the exact bound 3 of the new row. To compare packets AT THAT EXACT BOUND, lift p by surplus c=1. The new one-row packet q=(0,0,0,0,1;c0) and lifted old packet prove the same normal and bound. A base-anchored minimum new-row mass chooses lifted p if included in the candidate set. If a restricted catalogue lists only zero-surplus q, the selector returns q instead: this is CATALOGUE coverage, not evidence of mathematical nonexistence of an ancestor proof.

Indeed old rows even admit a zero-surplus packet B=(1,2,0,1;c0), obtained by consuming the lift's surplus through an x-cycle. Fresh `check_ancestor_surplus_catalogue.py` checks all three exact proofs and distinguishes the missing-candidate case. Thus neither 'old source cannot certify exact bound' nor 'zero-surplus is unique to the new row' is justified here. To formulate a robust relative selector, the catalogue must declare surplus closure, row-root identity, candidate enumeration and proof-history provenance; otherwise a choice may change simply because the generator omitted a valid old packet.

Next test whether bounded finite candidate enumeration can have a certified coverage envelope (rational multiplier/denominator caps, surplus cap, source root set), with explicit out-of-envelope refusal rather than misreporting semantic absence. Do not treat this catalogue specification as source issuer authority or analytic correspondence.
