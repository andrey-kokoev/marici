# Sparsest Farkas proof selection breaks two-input composition

The affine packet interchange of the prior leaf does NOT extend to a nonlinear proof-sensitive continuation. On the irredundant square, x<=2 has two zero-surplus proofs X0=(1,2,0,0) of support 2 and X1=(0,1,1,1) of support 3; y<=2 has Y0=(0,0,1,2) of support 2 and Y1=(1,1,0,1) of support 3. A deterministic candidate-set policy chooses least support, breaking ties lexicographically. Choosing LOCALLY selects X0,Y0, whose composed proof of x+y<=4 is (1,2,1,2), support 4. Directly selecting among the four composed candidate pairs selects X1+Y0=(0,1,2,3), support 3. Fresh exact-rational `check_nonlinear_sparsity_selector.py` verifies all four proof packets, the target normal/bound and the policy choices.

This is a frozen FINITE candidate-set counterexample to functorial sparse selection, not a claim that X1+Y0 is globally sparsest among every Farkas certificate. Farkas composition and convex interpolation are affine, but the support-count selector is nonlinear and can exploit cancellation of a multiplier when differently supported proof choices are combined. Semantic target equality supplies no path equivalence or permission to replace the recorded staged proof.

The interface boundary is sharp: a certificate-output contract may expose either proof with its actual chosen policy and row identities; claiming one canonical proof independent of factorization would be false under this policy. A next test should determine whether a stable support-face quotient or set-valued proof relation can preserve both candidates across composition without erasing fine proof replay, and identify the first typed obstruction to a single-valued selector.
