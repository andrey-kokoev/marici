# Recover the repair row from the axis of D

Assume the width-ten square and pentagon model with the prescribed disjoint-row links. Rows A, B, D are distinct. Each is legal alone; each pair passes with its link blue. Their all-blue triple has a blue seven, and the link A–B may turn red. Suppose A contains movable sites x0, y0 and outside site a. Write x1,y1 for the respective partners. No legal row contains the fixed site f (its port, all four square ports and a missed blue outside pair would give seven). Every legal row contains a movable site, since three outside sites contain a red edge.

First B cannot use x1 and y1 together. Then the only old square ports common-blue to A and B are the two cross corners. To supply four vertices for the triple witness one needs both: the four uncovered sites are f and three outside sites, not all mutually blue; taking one square port excludes f, and the three outside sites still cannot all be used. D must meet both cross corners. It cannot use f or both members of a movable pair. It therefore repeats the two movable sites of A or of B. Two distinct rows sharing both movable sites already fail as a pair: their two ports, the three square ports they both meet, and a missed blue outside pair form seven.

Thus B has one movable site. Name the axes so that it is x1. Write S for its outside pair, and T for the two outside sites left over after removing a and S. The only sites jointly missed by A and B are f, y1 and T. Their only common blue square port is the corner f,x1,y0. That port and f are red to one another. Consequently any four old vertices blue to all three row ports and to each other must include y1 and both members of T. These three sites are mutually blue and D misses all three.

**Axis test.** D also misses y0. If D contained y0, both square ports through y0 would be blue to A and D. Both ports miss y1 and both outside sites T. They would join the blue triple {y1} ∪ T to form a blue five common to A and D. Since A and D share y0, their mutual link must be blue. This gives a forbidden seven in the pair alone. Thus D uses neither y site. It uses exactly one x site, because every legal row needs a movable site and x0,x1 are consecutive. The axis of D determines B's movable site: take the member of that axis not used by A.

**Outside test.** D has two outside sites, at least one different from a. On deleting a the pentagon becomes a four-site path. Both S and T are blue pairs, hence they are the unique partition of that path into blue pairs (alternate sites). D misses T, so its outside site different from a identifies S. This recovers the entire row B from A, D and the pentagon, up to no choices. The constructed candidate still needs the legality and obstruction tests; uniqueness is not existence.

Together with the two-square-port case this rules out a minimum repair with shared endpoint A: two necessary links A–B and A–C require the two obstructing triples ABD and ACD. If A uses two movable sites the axis and outside tests give B=C. If A uses one, the two square ports missing A and the three sites of A form a blue five common to B,C. A seven on B,C plus that five either already fails as a pair or needs a repair not incident to A. In neither case do the two proposed links suffice.
