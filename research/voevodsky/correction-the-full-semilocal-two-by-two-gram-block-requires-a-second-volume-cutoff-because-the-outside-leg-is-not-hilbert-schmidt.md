# Correction: the full semilocal two-by-two Gram block requires a second volume cutoff because the outside leg is not Hilbert--Schmidt

## Claim being audited

The sewing remainder was formally written as

\[
\mathcal E_{\Lambda,S}(g)
=
\langle
Q_\Lambda P_\Lambda A,
Q_\Lambda(I-P_\Lambda)A
\rangle_{HS},
\qquad
A=U_S(g).
\]

This identity is valid only if both displayed legs are Hilbert--Schmidt, or if a more general trace pairing is independently justified.

The inside leg is localized by `P_Lambda`. The outside leg is not.

## Why the outside leg is generally not Hilbert--Schmidt

In logarithmic coordinates, the semilocal scaling representation is translation-type. For compactly supported `g`, the operator `A=U_S(g)` is a convolution/smoothing operator but remains translation invariant along the noncompact scaling direction.

The Fourier cutoff `Q_Lambda` has infinite-dimensional range. The operator

\[
Q_\Lambda(I-P_\Lambda)A
\]

still sees an outside region of infinite measure. Smoothing in the difference variable does not make a translation-invariant operator Hilbert--Schmidt over an infinite center-of-mass direction.

In the elementary real-line model, if `A` has convolution kernel `a(x-y)`, then the squared Hilbert--Schmidt norm of an outside restriction contains an integral of the form

\[
\int_{|y|>L}
\int_\mathbb R
|k_\Lambda(x,y)|^2dxdy.
\]

Translation covariance makes the inner mass asymptotically independent of `y`; the outer integral diverges. Thus

\[
\boxed{
Q_\Lambda(I-P_\Lambda)A
\notin
\mathcal L^2
}
\]

generically.

## Consequence for the previous polarization

The expression

\[
\operatorname{Tr}
\left(
(QPA)^*Q(I-P)A
\right)
\]

cannot automatically be interpreted as a Hilbert--Schmidt inner product. One factor may be Hilbert--Schmidt while the other is merely bounded, in which case their product need not be trace class.

Connes's theorem proves trace-classness/asymptotic traceability for the **combined product-cutoff expression** in its stated order. It does not imply that every factor obtained by inserting `I=P+(I-P)` has an independent trace.

Therefore the four-entry Gram completion is formal until an additional regulator is supplied.

## Minimal second cutoff

Choose an outer physical cutoff

\[
P_R,
\qquad
R>\Lambda,
\]

and define the finite annular outside channel

\[
C_{\Lambda,R,S}
=
Q_\Lambda(P_R-P_\Lambda)A.
\]

Keep

\[
B_{\Lambda,S}
=
Q_\Lambda P_\Lambda A.
\]

Under the usual local smoothing assumptions, both are Hilbert--Schmidt because their physical input supports have finite measure.

The regulated Gram matrix

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal G_{\Lambda,R,S}(g)
=
\begin{pmatrix}
\|B_\Lambda\|_{HS}^2&
\langle B_\Lambda,C_{\Lambda,R}\rangle_{HS}\\
\langle C_{\Lambda,R},B_\Lambda
angle_{HS}&
\|C_{\Lambda,R}\|_{HS}^2
\end{pmatrix}
\succeq0
}
\]

is now mathematically legitimate.

## Regulated product trace

The truncated product-cutoff trace decomposes exactly as

\[
\operatorname{Tr}
\left(
P_\Lambda Q_\Lambda P_RAA^*
\right)
=
\|B_\Lambda\|_{HS}^2
+
\langle B_\Lambda,C_{\Lambda,R}
angle_{HS},
\]

provided cyclicity applies to these regulated trace-class products.

The original theorem is recovered only after sending

\[
R\to\infty
\]

in the combined expression. The individual entries need not possess limits.

## Divergence of the outside square

Typically,

\[
\|C_{\Lambda,R,S}
\|_{HS}^2
\]

grows with the logarithmic volume of the annulus as `R -> infinity`. This divergence is absent from Connes's original trace because the theorem never inserts the outside-square term.

Consequently the full positive square

\[
\|B_\Lambda+C_{\Lambda,R}
\|_{HS}^2
\]

contains a new volume divergence not fixed by the subtraction

\[
2h(1)\log\Lambda.
\]

One cannot obtain a positive completion merely by adding the missing Gram entries and then taking `R -> infinity` termwise.

## Correlated two-parameter finite part

A valid completion would require a prescribed relation

\[
R=R(\Lambda)
\]

and a common counterterm matrix

\[
\mathcal C_{\Lambda,R}(g)
\]

such that

\[
\operatorname*{FP}_{\Lambda,R\to\infty}
\left(
\mathcal G_{\Lambda,R,S}(g)
-
\mathcal C_{\Lambda,R}(g)
\right)
\]

exists and remains positive.

Subtracting independent scalar divergences from each matrix entry does not preserve matrix positivity. The counterterm must itself be a positive Gram matrix or arise from quotienting a common null/asymptotic subspace.

## Natural quotient interpretation

The divergent outside direction is generated by translation volume. A promising formulation is to regard the regulated features modulo the common asymptotic translation mode. If

\[
B_{\Lambda}=B_{div,\Lambda}+B_{fin,\Lambda},
\]

\[
C_{\Lambda,R}=C_{div,\Lambda,R}+C_{fin,\Lambda,R},
\]

and the divergent pieces lie in one common feature line or closed subspace, then taking the Hilbert quotient by that subspace preserves positivity and can implement the finite part geometrically.

This would be preferable to subtracting `2h(1) log Lambda` numerically after taking the trace.

The source candidate for this common asymptotic subspace is the range/radical of the semilocal Eisenstein map `E` emphasized by Connes--Consani--Moscovici.

## Relation to the radical

The 2023 paper states that the radical of the Weil quadratic form is generated by the range of the map `E` and that conditioning by this radical is implemented through the semilocal prolate/Sonin decomposition.

This suggests the correct positive completion:

1. form the regulated positive Gram matrix;
2. identify its common volume-divergent feature with `ran E`;
3. quotient or condition by `overline{ran E}` before removing cutoffs;
4. compute the induced finite trace on the quotient.

This is an operator quotient, not a termwise finite-part subtraction.

## Revised next theorem

One needs to prove an asymptotic feature decomposition

\[
\boxed{
Q_\Lambda P_RU_S(g)
=
\sqrt{2\log\Lambda}\Xi_S(g)
+
F_{\Lambda,R,S}(g),
}
\]

where:

- `Xi_S(g)` lies in the source radical/asymptotic subspace;
- its squared norm is `h(1)=||g||^2`;
- the decomposition is orthogonal before taking the limit;
- `F_Lambda,R,S` converges in the quotient;
- the construction is compatible under adjoining places.

Then the divergent term

\[
2h(1)\log\Lambda
\]

would be removed by a Hilbert quotient while retaining positivity.

## Disposition

The unregulated full `2x2` Gram matrix is not yet defined because the outside leg is generally not Hilbert--Schmidt. A second volume cutoff makes it positive but introduces an additional divergence.

The next viable construction is not a four-entry termwise finite part. It is a positive regulated Gram space followed by quotienting the common asymptotic/radical feature before cutoff removal.
