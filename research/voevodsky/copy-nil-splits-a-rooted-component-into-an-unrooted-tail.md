# COPY--NIL splits a rooted component into an unrooted tail

The proposed root-coverage plus local typed-tail conditions are NOT preserved on all well-wired graphs. Fresh `check_copy_nil_root_preservation_counterexample.py` constructs a port-linear graph with COPY.p--original NIL, COPY.a--B0.a and B0.p--copied NIL, COPY.b--ERASE.p; two separate BOOL--OUT pairs provide the outputs. Before rewriting, each connected component has OUT or ERASE root; the strict surviving B.a successor and typed principal conditions hold. After COPY--NIL replaces each output by fresh NIL, the B0--NIL--NIL branch detaches WITHOUT a root; its nodes are stuck. The graph is not claimed reachable from the intended constructor. This refutes closure of the *coarse invariant*, not progress of the intended net.

A stronger necessary condition is **per-frontier branch anchoring**: removing COPY and its original-source suffix must leave EACH auxiliary-side component with at least one correctly oriented Q/OUT or ERASE root, not merely one root somewhere in the whole COPY component. A side with B.a pointing into COPY must be anchored via the B principal predecessor chain toward an output/eraser; pairing B.p with an unrelated NIL is inadmissible. The finite boundary audit (`check_nil_copy_and_erase_k_boundaries.py`) saw 19 COPY--NIL boundary-role pairs and 22 ERASE--K steps on 313 tiny reachable classes, but does not establish branch anchoring universally.

Next encode branch anchoring by cutting COPY.a/b and inspecting each consumer-side component separately on every reachable state, then test preservation for COPY--B and COPY--NIL, ERASE--K, and all remaining rules. The previously stated conditional no-redex lemma still holds AS AN IMPLICATION when its hypotheses hold; this counterexample shows why those hypotheses cannot yet be claimed invariant.
