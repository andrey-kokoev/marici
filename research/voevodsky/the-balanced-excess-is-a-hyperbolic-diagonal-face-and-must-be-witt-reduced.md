# The balanced excess is a hyperbolic diagonal face and must be Witt reduced

## Absolute fold at one tetrahedron

Fix one lattice tetrahedron \(\alpha\). After removing its shared physical face, let its two polarized residual features be

\[
R_\alpha^T:E_\alpha\to K_\alpha^T,
\qquad
R_\alpha^0:E_\alpha\to K_\alpha^0.
\]

The folded target is

\[
K_\alpha^{fold}=K_\alpha^T\oplus K_\alpha^0.
\]

It carries the Hilbert metric and the polarity involution

\[
J_\alpha=I\oplus(-I).
\]

The folded feature is

\[
R_\alpha=R_\alpha^T\oplus R_\alpha^0.
\]

Its ordinary Gram is the positive residual form

\[
r_\alpha
=(R_\alpha^T)^*R_\alpha^T
+(R_\alpha^0)^*R_\alpha^0.
\]

Its oriented readout is

\[
D_\alpha
=R_\alpha^*J_\alpha R_\alpha.
\]

## Balanced excess

Suppose both residuals contain the same positive feature \(K_\alpha^{1/2}\). Then, after target isometries, they have the form

\[
R_\alpha^T
=(D_\alpha)_+^{1/2}\oplus K_\alpha^{1/2},
\]

\[
R_\alpha^0
=(D_\alpha)_-^{1/2}\oplus K_\alpha^{1/2}.
\]

The common component enters the fold diagonally:

\[
u\longmapsto
\bigl(K_\alpha^{1/2}u,K_\alpha^{1/2}u\bigr).
\]

The diagonal subspace is neutral for the polarity form. For every \(z\),

\[
\langle(z,z),J_\alpha(z,z)\rangle=0.
\]

It contributes nothing to \(D_\alpha\), but contributes twice its Hilbert Gram to \(r_\alpha\).

Therefore balanced excess is not an unexplained analytic error. It is a hyperbolic diagonal face of the folded polarized tetrahedron.

## Witt reduction

Let \(N_\alpha\) be the closed diagonal feature subspace generated by all common residual rows:

\[
N_\alpha
=
\overline{\operatorname{span}}
\left\{
(K^{1/2}u,K^{1/2}u)
\right\}.
\]

This subspace is totally isotropic. The polarity-preserving reduction is the Witt quotient

\[
K_\alpha^{red}
=N_\alpha^{\perp_J}/N_\alpha.
\]

After this reduction, the common positive row disappears from both polarities. The surviving source Grams are the minimal Jordan legs when \(N_\alpha\) is the maximal common hyperbolic summand:

\[
G_{\alpha,red}^T=(D_\alpha)_+,
\qquad
G_{\alpha,red}^0=(D_\alpha)_-.
\]

The ordinary Gram of the reduced absolute fold is then

\[
r_\alpha^{red}=|D_\alpha|.
\]

## Relation to lattice faces

The first common face \(B_\alpha\) is removed before forming the residual fold. A second common component can remain inside the two residual polarities. In the full lattice, such a component should appear as a face shared by the two residual sub-tetrahedra.

Therefore the complete positive lattice should not stop after one subtraction. It should normalize the polarized cell by quotienting every face that occurs identically in opposite polarities.

This is the positive analogue of passing from an unnormalized simplicial object to its normalized complex. Degenerate common faces are retained by the unnormalized absolute sum but vanish in the normalized polarized cell.

## Role of the 210 tetrahedra

A common row may not be visible from one tetrahedron in isolation. The surrounding tetrahedra determine whether that row is genuinely a boundary feature or a duplicated face transported through another route.

Because all tetrahedral routes are analytically available, the full 210-cell system supplies the incidence data needed to identify these duplicate polarized faces consistently.

The required global construction is therefore:

1. assemble the polarized face-incidence diagram over all 210 tetrahedra;
2. identify opposite-polarity copies of each common face by strict cell commutativity;
3. form their hyperbolic diagonal subspaces;
4. take the compatible Witt reductions;
5. only then form the positive absolute residual forms.

## Revised finite candidate

The unreduced form \(r_\alpha\) is not yet the correct Mosco candidate. The correct object is the Gram of the normalized or Witt-reduced fold:

\[
q_\alpha^{min}
=\operatorname{Gram}(R_\alpha^{red}).
\]

When maximal common-face reduction is exact, this satisfies

\[
q_\alpha^{min}=|D_\alpha|.
\]

At the boundary, the desired limit is the positive form of \(|\mathcal A_S|\).

## New exact gate

The key finite-cell question is now combinatorial and feature-theoretic.

Does the 210-tetrahedron incidence diagram identify a maximal compatible hyperbolic diagonal subfeature in every absolute fold?

If yes, finite minimalization is internal to the lattice. The remaining analytic work concerns convergence of the reduced forms.

If no, the lattice supplies only a nonminimal Krein realization, and a separate analytic minimalization remains necessary.

## Disposition

The balanced term \(K_\alpha\) is the Gram of a diagonal neutral face in the folded polarity carrier. The appropriate operation is not an estimate forcing that term to decay. It is compatible Witt reduction of the duplicated face.

The next task is to construct this reduction from the full polarized face-incidence diagram and test whether it is maximal and successor-natural.
