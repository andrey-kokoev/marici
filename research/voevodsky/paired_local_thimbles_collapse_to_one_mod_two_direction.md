# Paired local thimbles collapse to one mod-two direction

## Question

Can the two exchanged Picard–Lefschetz nodes determine both coordinates of the ambient cusp extension class using only their exchange symmetry and local thimbles?

## Claim boundary

This analyzes the integral module generated by the two local thimbles. It does not identify their image in the degree-two del Pezzo Picard lattice.

## Paired thimble module

Let \(\Theta_+\) and \(\Theta_-\) be the two primitive local thimbles at the roots of

\[
xt^2+y=0.
\]

The source involution \(t\mapsto-t\) exchanges them. Define the invariant and anti-invariant combinations

\[
c=\Theta_++\Theta_-,
\qquad
d=\Theta_+-\Theta_-.
\]

Over \(\mathbb Z\), the change from \((\Theta_+,\Theta_-)\) to \((c,d)\) has determinant \(-2\). Thus the invariant/anti-invariant splitting is not an integral direct-sum basis; it has index two.

Modulo two,

\[
c\equiv d\equiv\Theta_++\Theta_-
\pmod2.
\]

Therefore the two symmetry channels collapse to one class in the mod-two thimble module.

## Consequence for the cusp extension

The unresolved ambient class lies in

\[
\mathcal A_{--}/2\mathcal A_{--}
\cong(\mathbb Z/2)^2.
\]

Any construction that uses only the unordered pair of nodes, their local thimbles, and the exchange involution can produce at most one mod-two direction in this plane. It cannot determine two independent coordinates.

This explains why the paired-node geometry yields width two but does not select the full extension class. A second independent datum must break or refine the local exchange-only description.

## Required refinement

To compute both bits one needs at least one of:

- an ordered global marking of \(\Theta_+\) and \(\Theta_-\) in the Picard lattice;
- two separately normalized intersections with duals to \(e_6\) and \(v_{\rm alg}\);
- a global path system from a fixed base fiber distinguishing the two thimbles before reduction modulo two.

Local node exchange supplies none of these.

## Relation to conductor walls

The conductor has two separately labelled wall parities. Mapping the single mod-two class of the unordered thimble pair to both wall characters would identify them diagonally and lose the independent \((\mathbb Z/2)^2\) structure. Therefore site labels cannot be recovered from the local involution alone.

## Disposition

The paired local thimbles determine one index-two phenomenon but at most one mod-two direction. The second cusp-extension bit—and hence any labelled comparison to both conductor walls—requires a global ordered Picard marking.
