# A red K3,3 leaves at most one vertex blue to all six

This is a necessary condition on any legal 35-vertex endpoint, not an existence result or an exclusion of K3,3. Following the [(2,2) result](ramsey35-three-by-three-two-two.md), the asymmetric count pattern (1,2) is also impossible. Thus the two sides together have at most two doubled pairs. Since the number of neutral vertices is a+b-1, the only remaining unordered count patterns are (0,1), (0,2), (1,1).

In (1,2) the private sets X and Y have orders 14 and 13. The same two neutral vertices Z must be identified in the legal (3,6) graphs X+Z of order 16 and Y+Z of order 15. The three blue fives of X meet in a single double, and the others are disjoint; in Y their intersection graph is a two-edge path. The former side requires one degree-five star and the latter two degree-four stars. To see this, take the unique meeting pair of roots on the first side. Its private fours have k cross edges, with 7<=k<=13 as before. Its 21-vertex common blue neighborhood has 44+k red edges. Inside that predecessor the third root has precisely five neighbors; removing its closed neighborhood gives exactly Y+Z. Therefore there is no need to presume that the available catalogue of 15-types is complete: every usable 15-type and the required five attachments must arise by this deletion. The analogous degree-four deletions on the other side must both recover the chosen 16-type. An edge-budget check in this direction is e(Y+Z)=4+e(X), and in reverse e(X+Z)=12+e(Y).

The side enumeration, allowing partial-graph rejections only by established common-neighbor bounds, yielded 14,806 marked 16-side configurations. The 21-catalogue signatures requested by these yield 7,427 induced 15-types (before checking whether they can carry the requisite fives). Both directions were tested on the same base types, respecting automorphisms. On identifying the two Z vertices the edge between them must agree and each vertex must have total red degree eight. Of 2,219,585 such identifications after reciprocal catalogue checks, 14,945 admitted all three panels separately. The five single-only X vertices in the first panel cross eight single-only Y vertices in the two other panels. Their forty red/blue decisions must agree in both directions. In 14,560 candidates a single bit was already forced oppositely; in the other 385 there was no single-bit conflict but no shared coloring of a 5-by-4 block. Independent block reconstruction verified every empty intersection. No conflict budget or timeout is used as a verdict.

At a root, two quartets sharing three labels have two doubled pairs on the root/cell side. Hence **none** of the three shared label pairs can occur in another core cell, or that K3,3 would have at least three doubled pairs. Closing the (0,2) case would now exclude this kind of quartet overlap altogether. It would not by itself settle whether any red K3,3 exists.
