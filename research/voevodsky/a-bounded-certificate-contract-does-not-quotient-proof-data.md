# A bounded certificate contract does not quotient proof data

The responsible resolution of the open higher-cell choice is a **bounded certificate-output contract**, not a forced identification of Farkas proof paths. The caller supplies exact primitive row identities and bounds, a valid Farkas proof, and a named positive-bound kernel witness. The response retains the multiplier vector, zero surplus, chosen witness and public target. Public-answer projection can agree across two responses while the certificate records remain different. No equality of the two certificates is asserted without a separate typed redistribution cell; no replay equivalence of the paths producing them is asserted at all.

Fresh `check_endpoint_contract_boundary.py` constructs both zero-surplus results for the separately rooted three-row source. They report the same public normal/bound but different multipliers and witness IDs. The contract refuses requests for proof replay, actual-history identification, analytic role assignment or source widening; it also refuses a missing primitive root or a witness not rooted in the fixed source. These are scope refusals, not proofs that the disallowed operations can never be defined by a richer interface. A request for the certificate itself is permitted and sees the distinction, so calling this a quotient of certificates would be incorrect: only PUBLIC ANSWERS have the coarse equality.

This freezes one usable CLASSICAL interface without pretending to solve the missing mixed 3-cell or either free-path 4-cell. It also preserves the continuation-relative record/control lesson: extending permitted controls to proof-history replay changes the sufficiency question and demands new independently sourced constructors. The analytic S,A,R,C,G correspondence remains deferred until its own typed role/edge/base mapping exists. A successor research leaf should test a genuine nonduplicate multi-row example where redistribution is not available, and report the first failed proof-comparison obligation rather than weaken the certificate contract silently.
