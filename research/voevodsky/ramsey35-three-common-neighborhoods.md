# A red K3,4 cannot occur in a legal 35-vertex graph

Suppose three vertices have four shared red neighbors. All three are blue to each other by triangle-freeness. By the [two-vertex bound](ramsey35-common-neighbor-bound.md), those four are the *only* common red neighbors of any pair of the three. Each of the three vertices therefore has four private red neighbors, giving three disjoint blue fours. Denote their union by P. All of P is blue to the common four, so P is a 12-vertex (3,5) graph. Its red degrees are at most four (its neighborhoods are blue) and at least three (its nonneighbors are a (3,4) graph of order at most eight).

For any pair of the three original vertices, let k count the red edges between their private fours. Their common blue neighborhood H has 21 vertices. It is (3,7)-legal and has 44+k red edges: the four shared neighbors send 24 edges to H, and the eight private neighbors of the pair send 56-2k, leaving 2e(H)=168-80+2k. The edge floor 51 gives k>=7 for every pair. Now H contains the third original vertex at degree four. Its four neighbors in H have degrees 8-d_P (they lose exactly their P-neighbors lying in the two opposite private sets). Those degrees are four or five, and at most two are five: the sum of d_P over this group is the sum of two cross counts, at least fourteen. None of the four 51-edge catalogue graphs has such a marked vertex. Hence every k>=8. Since e(P)<=24, all three k must equal eight and all twelve P-degrees equal four. H must consequently be one of the 70 graphs with 52 edges and have a degree-four vertex with four degree-four neighbors. None does. There is no singleton or unsaturation assumption. At any root, three labels may occur together on **at most two core cells** (the root is already one common red neighbor), and a quartet mask cannot be repeated (the two copies and the root would see the same four). The known witness actually has maximum triple codegree two; exclusion of K3,3 is **not** proved.

## A separate singleton obstruction

Five mutually blue singleton cells are impossible at any root. If their five labels are T, no other cell may have mask S contained in T: it would form a blue nine with the singleton cells of S and the unused newcomers. The other 21 cells must therefore use the three outside labels; those labels carry exactly 21 incidences, so no cell uses two of them. The seven cells on one outside label are consequently all blue to the two other outside newcomers, another blue nine. Hence the singleton-cell graph is (3,5)-legal. In particular no label in the pair graph can have degree five when all eight singletons occur, since every pair mask forces a red edge between its singleton endpoints. The known witness attains four blue singletons. [Pair-graph screening](results/ramsey_singleton_five_gate.json).
