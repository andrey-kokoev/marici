# A symmetric normal deformation realizes the forced equal-order ray

## Question

Does a concrete normal deformation of the total-energy parameter map realize the equal-order triple-divisor approach required by the semisimple conductor limit?

## Claim boundary

This constructs a relative-parameter deformation. It does not derive the deformation coordinate or its orientation from the conductor source, and it does not construct the nearby-cycle functor.

## Arrangement identity

The three vanishing factors satisfy the exact relation

\[
E=X_1+X_2=(X_1-Y)+(X_2+Y)=B+C.
\]

Consequently, if \(B\) and \(C\) vanish simply along a normal coordinate and their leading coefficients do not cancel, then \(E\) also vanishes simply. Equal order is generic among transverse approaches to this triple intersection; failure occurs only on the tangent direction where the leading terms of \(B\) and \(C\) cancel.

## Symmetric deformation

Let \(Y_0\ne0\) denote the specialized common Kummer parameter, with the prior comparison giving \(Y_0=\pm2xy\). Introduce a deformation coordinate \(q\) and set

\[
Y=Y_0,
\qquad
X_1=Y_0+q,
\qquad
X_2=-Y_0+q.
\]

Then

\[
B=X_1-Y=q,
\qquad
C=X_2+Y=q,
\qquad
E=X_1+X_2=2q.
\]

All three factors have vanishing order one. The nonvanishing complementary factors are

\[
A=X_1+Y=2Y_0+q,
\qquad
D=X_2-Y=-2Y_0+q,
\]

which remain nonzero near \(q=0\) when \(Y_0\ne0\).

Therefore this deformation approaches only the intended triple divisor and realizes

\[
(m_B,m_C,m_E)=(1,1,1).
\]

The constant coefficient two in \(E=2q\) does not change \(d\log E\)'s normal residue.

## Symmetry

Exchanging the two sites together with

\[
X_1\leftrightarrow X_2,
\qquad
Y_0\mapsto -Y_0
\]

preserves \(q\) and interchanges \(B\) with \(C\). Thus the deformation treats the two active endpoint factors symmetrically. Reversing \(q\) changes normal orientation but not vanishing order.

## Consequence

The previously selected equal-order ray is nonempty and has an explicit symmetric representative. The residue along this representative is exactly the audited \(R_{\rm eq}\), with one flat line and one index-two eigenlattice defect.

This does not yet authorize identifying \(q\) with a conductor parameter. The conductor total-energy locus supplies \(Y_0\), but no source map currently supplies the normal displacement \(q\), its loop orientation, or a contour whose monodromy realizes the residue.

## Disposition

Existence of an equal-order, site-symmetric relative normal deformation is constructed. The remaining first missing object is conductor-side normal and contour authority for \(q\), followed by a nearby-cycle comparison.
