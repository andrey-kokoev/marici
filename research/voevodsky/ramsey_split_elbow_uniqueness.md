# The split elbow at width ten is unique up to reflection

In the three-corner path chart, write C={f,x,y}, D={f,X,y}, E={f,x,Y}. Each lowercase/uppercase pair differs by one site, and X and Y are nonadjacent because the fourth square corner is legal. Consider the rare repair shape: each of CD and CE, together with both new ports, admits a blue seven, the elbow cannot take a repair link, and the repairs go from D to S and E to T. The two new supports overlap (otherwise one repair suffices). All single attachments are legal.

C meets S, but D is disjoint from S: thus x belongs to S. Similarly y belongs to T. The three independent sites witnessing CD must include Y: if not they miss E too, and the three parent ports plus S and those three sites already violate single legality. Similarly the CE triple includes X. In particular neither new support contains X or Y. Neither contains f. Thus S={x}∪K and T={y}∪L, where K,L are two-element sets outside the five-site parent union U. They overlap. Let W be the sites outside U∪S∪T. The two witness triples lie in W∪{Y} and W∪{X} respectively. If K≠L, W has just two sites; both triples must use the same independent pair from W. That pair together with X,Y forms four independent sites missed by C,S,T. This is an unrepairable seven, since each pair among C,S,T overlaps. Hence K=L and W has three sites.

W cannot be independent: all three parents plus S already avoid it. Each witness triple needs an independent pair from W. The two triples cannot use the same pair (the unrepairable seven again). Consequently W contains exactly one adjacent pair, say w,w+1, and one isolated site b; the only two independent pairs use b and either w or w+1. Each of X,Y admits a pair and together they admit neither. They must separately block opposite members of the adjacent pair. The only outside positions that can do this are w−1 and w+2. The original swap partners x,y must be adjacent to X,Y but outside W. Thus up to orientation there is a consecutive six-site block x,X,w,w+1,Y,y.

Any site immediately outside this block is adjacent to x or y. It cannot belong to K because S and T are separated triples, and cannot be f because C is separated. The only remaining possibility is b. There is just one b, so the block touches a path endpoint. Reflect to place it at sites 1–6; b=7. The common new pair K must use two nonadjacent sites among 8,9,10, hence K={8,10} and f=9. This recovers C=169, D=269, E=159, S={1,8,10}, T={6,8,10}. The positive check and its two necessary links are in [the repair census](results/ramsey_two_repair_shapes.json). No matching or target count was needed to select the arrangement. Scope: necessity for the stated split-elbow shape, not for all two-link repairs.
