# Five-leaf rotation graph exposes pentagon overlap obligations

A fresh free binary-tree construction on five ordered, uniquely labelled Farkas proof leaves has 14 parenthesizations and 21 directed elementary rotations `((a,b),c)->(a,(b,c))`. Its underlying finite graph contains three simple four-cycles and six simple five-cycles. `check_five_leaf_associahedron.py` enumerates all parenthesizations, edges and these short cycles; it does NOT infer fillers from their existence. The four-leaf short/two-step and long/three-step pentagon routes remain distinct words of rotations although all bracketings have the same summed packet image.

This locates the first obstruction precisely: in the FREE rotation path presentation there is **no pentagon 3-cell generator** comparing its parallel 2-cell paths. Adjoining one is a new typed, independently sourced choice, not a consequence of Farkas vector addition or of primitive row roots for the LEAVES. At five leaves, pentagon and disjoint-rotation square overlaps create additional 4-dimensional path-coherence obligations if those fillers are adjoined. Counting graph cycles checks boundaries, not higher path coherence; no five-leaf 4-cell has been constructed.

A possible enriched specification would explicitly adjoin rotation 2-cells, pentagon and independent-rotation interchange 3-cells, and higher overlap 4-cells, each with exact source-rooted support. Without an independent constructor, that is a proposed presentation rather than an admitted record/control comparison or evidence of actual execution equality. The bounded tagged-candidate interface remains valid because it retains exact rotation histories and declines their identification. Analytic S,A,R,C,G roles remain deferred.
