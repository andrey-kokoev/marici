# Source-bound refinement preserves normal endpoints laxly; widening can destroy a proof

Extend the fixed source interval to `S_L=[0,L]`, L>0. A proof `(a,b;c)` of `x<=T` obeys `b-a=1` and `L*b+c=T`. Its source-rooted normalizer is `N_L(a,b;c)=(a+c/L,b+c/L;0)`, with unique normal certificate `(T/L-1,T/L;0)` when T>=L. Refining to `S_K`, 0<K<=L, transports the same multipliers and adjusts surplus by `(L-K)b`: `F_LK(a,b;c)=(a,b;c+(L-K)b)`. Exact algebra gives `N_K F_LK = N_K F_LK N_L`, but generally `F_LK N_L != N_K F_LK`. Thus source refinement is also LAX at proof-packet level, while normalized endpoints agree.

Fresh `check_source_bound_change.py` checks 20 rational refinement squares, 10 strict failures, and the concrete `(1,2;1)` proof of x<=3 on `[0,1]` transported to `[0,1/2]` as `(1,2;2)` and normalized to `(5,6;0)`. A widening hostile is decisive: the valid proof `(0,1;0)` of x<=1 on `[0,1]` transports to negative surplus on `[0,2]`; indeed x<=1 is false on that widened source. The old normal form is also invalid under an unadjusted source bound. A source-change control must carry and check the bound, not merely reuse the row normal or public target.

The endpoint comparison interface thus extends from target weakenings to **typed source refinements** in this 1D family. It does not extend to arbitrary source widening, identify proof histories, or solve the missing interchange 4-cell. A next check should test mixed source refinement and target weakening squares with domain conditions, keeping the bound-support DAG and fine replay controls separate.
