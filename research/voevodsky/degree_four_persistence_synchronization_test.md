# Degree-four persistence synchronization test

## Question

Does exact-grade positive-dimensional persistence synchronization survive in multiplicative degree four, where four-cells first enter within the grade-20000 window?

## Claim boundary

The test covers distinct-shell cells in multiplicative degree four through grade 20000 over two prime fields. It does not test repeated-shell cells, higher multiplicative degree, integral torsion, or an unbounded filtration.

## Bold conjecture

Every positive-dimensional persistence birth is paired with a cell one degree higher at the same grade. Consequently there are no positive-length or right-censored bars in homological degrees one through four.

## Rivals

1. Four-factor interactions produce the first positive-length one- or two-bar.
2. Lower-dimensional synchronization survives, but a three-class waits for a later four-cell.
3. A top-dimensional four-cycle appears without a possible five-cell filler.
4. Pairings differ across fields.

## Test

Enumerate all vertices and all distinct-shell cells of dimensions one through four whose base has multiplicative degree four and whose birth grade is at most 20000. Reduce the filtration-compatible boundary matrix over two prime fields, ordering equal-grade cells by increasing dimension. Classify every positive-dimensional zero column by homological degree and by zero-length, positive-length, or right-censored lifetime. Verify every boundary composition.

## Falsifier

Any positive-length or right-censored positive-dimensional bar falsifies synchronization on the bounded sector. Field disagreement or nonzero \(d^2\) is an execution defect.

## Computed result

Through grade 20000 the sector contains 1394 vertices, 1452 edges, 388 squares, 54 cubes, and 2 four-cells. All 336 reduced one-class births, 52 two-class births, and 2 three-class births pair with a cell one degree higher at the same grade. No four-class is born. There is no positive-length or right-censored bar in any positive degree.

Both fields give identical persistence data and every boundary composition vanishes.

## Disposition

Synchronization survives the bounded degree-four test, including the first three-class births and four-cell fillers. The evidence now covers multiplicative degrees two, three, and four, but remains finite-cutoff evidence. A structural proof or a deliberately altered filtration is needed to determine whether synchronization is forced by the grade formula.
