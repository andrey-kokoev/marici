# Merging two histories forces linear common-section complexity

## Representation model

Count the number of affine formulas in a finite continuous piecewise-affine source lifting section, over a fixed compact public polygon. Cells may be chosen freely. Count history-selection bits separately. Shared source code, history constraints, proofs of section admission and query-region lookup are not included in this piece count.

For each n>=4 we construct TWO source histories, each admitting a single globally affine section, whose common sections require at least ceil(n/3) affine formulas. A common section with n-2 pieces exists. This gives matching order Theta(n), not an exact optimum or a lower bound for arbitrary programs.

## Construction

Let t_i=i/n for i=1,...,n, public vertices v_i=(t_i,t_i^2), and heights z_i=t_i^3. The v_i are all extreme vertices of their convex polygon Q: strict convexity of the parabola exposes each, with the endpoint chord closing the polygon.

Let P be the convex hull of the lifted points (v_i,z_i). Its vertical fiber over y in Q is an interval [f(y),g(y)]. Here f is a finite convex piecewise-affine lower envelope and g is a finite concave piecewise-affine upper envelope, with 0<=f<=g<=1. At each public vertex, f(v_i)=g(v_i)=z_i: an extreme public point has only its own vertex in any representing convex combination.

Define histories on the same public domain:

    A: f(y)<=h<=1,
    B: 0<=h<=g(y).

These are jointly polyhedral histories. A admits the constant section h=1; B admits h=0. Their common relation is P, and every common section is forced through all n lifted vertices.

One explicit representation uses supporting planes through triples. At parameters a,b,c, the plane over (p,q) is

    h=-(ab+ac+bc)p+(a+b+c)q+abc.

Keep planes lying below all lifted vertices as lower bounds and those above all vertices as upper bounds. Together with y in Q and 0<=h<=1, these give the vertical hull. The exact controls enumerate those planes and verify all vertex conditions.

## Lower bound for EVERY finite continuous piecewise-affine section

No four lifted vertices are coplanar. Indeed, the residual between t^3 and its quadratic interpolant at a,b,c is (t-a)(t-b)(t-c), which is nonzero at a fourth distinct parameter. Equivalently the four-point determinant is a nonzero Vandermonde determinant.

Each affine formula for h therefore agrees with the forced heights at at most three public vertices. Even if cells are not closed, finite piecewise affinity and continuity imply that at each public vertex at least one formula agrees there: approach through cells, choose a recurring formula and pass to the limit. Consequently K formulas can accommodate at most 3K forced vertices, so K>=ceil(n/3).

This also bounds affine formulas for a source-vector section: h is an affine readout of the source vector, hence each affine source formula induces an affine h formula. Arbitrary choices of triangulation, cells or source witnesses cannot evade the forced values.

## Matching-order upper bound

Triangulate Q by a fan from its first vertex. There are n-2 triangles. Interpolate the lifted vertices on each triangle. Convexity of P proves admission, and common vertex data proves face agreement. This gives a continuous common section with at most n-2 formulas.

## Owning source embedding

Use m=4, center (50,51,52,53), delta=128^-4 and slopes 128^-j. Encode

    U=Ucenter+delta*p, V=Vcenter+delta*q,
    t0=50+delta*h, t1=51.

The remaining two moment equations determine t2,t3 affinely. All eight corners of (p,q,h) in [0,1]^3 lie strictly inside the source atom caps, so convexity admits the entire parameter cube. Restrict the public domain to Q and impose the displayed history inequalities. These are rational linear evidence in the original moments and raw atom audit coordinates.

Thus both separate sections are globally affine actual source lifts, not merely formal local selectors. The common source sections inherit the lower bound above. The affine coordinate rescaling is used for exact feasibility only; no approximation, conditioning or coefficient-bit claim is inferred from it.

## The tradeoff and its limits

Keeping two history states costs one history-selection bit and permits one affine section for each state. Merging them removes that bit but forces Theta(n) affine formulas in any continuous piecewise-affine common section. The semantic minimum number of states is one, yet it is not the minimum of every reasonable section-representation cost.

This is not a total-byte compression theorem. In particular:

- The family and histories contain n-dependent data, even if generated by a small rule.
- Admission proofs for the separate constant sections may still depend on those constraints.
- An explicit table of K formulas costs Omega(K) records, but a generating program need not.
- A more expressive representation, such as an optimization program, is outside the lower bound.
- No lower bound on certificate size or query time follows here.

These exclusions are substantive. The result proves a sharp ORDER separation in one declared representation model, not that forgetting one bit universally consumes linear information.
