# Labelled set-valued Farkas composition retains nonfunctorial candidates

Instead of applying the sparse selector early, retain both proof candidates for x<=2 and both for y<=2 on the irredundant square. Their coefficient-one Farkas composite is a labelled Cartesian product of FOUR derivations. Two distinct labels, `(x0,y0)` and `(x1,y1)`, happen to produce the SAME proof matrix `(1,2,1,2)`. Consequently the bare SET of packet vectors has only THREE elements: it cannot replay which of those two distinct pair histories occurred. Keeping tagged candidate pairs preserves all four and allows the global sparse selector to see `(x1,y0)` rather than being trapped by local selection.

Fresh `check_set_valued_farkas_composition.py` verifies the four-versus-three distinction and extends by a third fixed primitive proof. The left- and right-parenthesized three-input tagged families have four matching packet tips and a canonical leaf-preserving bijection `((x,y),z) <-> (x,(y,z))`, but the label trees themselves differ. Packet sets compose associatively under vector addition; tagged syntax needs the EXPLICIT associator. It is a mathematical relabelling, not proof that the two actual execution histories are the same.

Thus a set-valued certificate relation is a viable bounded repair for the nonlinear selector if it retains tags and defers selection until the appropriate global boundary. A plain packet set already loses a fine continuation. The next gate is a controlled branching check: does this tagged associator remain coherent over four inputs (pentagon) while refusing a fabricated root or execution-history equality? Even an affirmative finite coherence test would only specify a candidate formal presentation, not source-derived operational or analytic authority.
