# A lexicographic rank decreases across the copier-query net rules

For the intended finite input class (one original B0/B1/NIL support chain, COPY and two unary-index queries), let M=(number of live ORIGINAL support B/N nodes, number of live NONORIGINAL B/N/K nodes), ordered lexicographically. A COPY--B/N rewrite consumes exactly one original node; it may generate fresh copy nodes, but the primary coordinate falls. Each Q_B/Q_S/Q_R or ERASE rewrite consumes at least one nonoriginal B/N/K node and creates none, while leaving the primary coordinate unchanged. Consequently M strictly decreases on every listed rewrite, independently of family scheduling. Fresh `check_copy_query_termination_measure.py` instruments the fully wired engine: all 2,430 query-first runs through support length 5 strictly decrease M at EACH step, with correct final outputs (maximum 32 rewrites).

Together with linear principal-port active pairs and boundary-fresh rewrites, this suggests termination and commuting diamonds on THIS restricted net family. It is not a machine-checked universal rule-table proof: the audit covers operational rules and tested inputs, and a formal global confluence conclusion still needs a precise state-space closure theorem and critical-pair analysis. The rank will not automatically extend to future add(i)/union rules, which may create new support nodes.

Next formalize the exact rule signature and inductive invariant for reachable typed port graphs; prove all enabled principal-port redexes disjoint, every rewrite stays in the invariant, and M decreases. Only then invoke a termination-plus-local-confluence result for unique normal forms, scoped to this destructive two-query system.
