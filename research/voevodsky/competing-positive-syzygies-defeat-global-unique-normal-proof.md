# Competing positive syzygies defeat a global unique normal proof

Retain THREE primitive source-row identities `-x<=0`, `x<=1` (upper-A), `x<=1` (upper-B). The two upper rows denote the same set but are distinct provenance roots. The row normals are (-1,1,1) and bounds (0,1,1). Both nonnegative kernel directions d_A=(1,1,0) and d_B=(1,0,1) have positive bound contribution 1. Starting from the valid certificate `(0,1,0;c=1)` of x<=2, surplus consumption with d_A yields `(1,2,0;c=0)`; with d_B it yields `(1,1,1;c=0)`. They prove the same inclusion, with the same zero surplus, but are different proof records. Fresh exact-rational `check_competing_source_syzygies.py` passes nine bounded cases; an initial overdraw hostile assumed the third step was invalid after two, corrected to the actual third-step boundary before the passing run.

The difference of the two normalized multiplier vectors is `(0,-1,1)`, a SIGNED zero-bound kernel direction. A redistribution step from `(1,2,0)` to `(1,1,1)` preserves normal and bound and stays nonnegative; indiscriminate further moves can violate nonnegativity. This is a candidate *proof-level* comparison with both upper-row roots retained, not equality of row-labelled histories or a declared 2-cell. Collapsing the duplicate upper rows would change the source presentation and erase a fine replay distinction.

Thus the earlier claim of a unique zero-surplus normal certificate is strictly limited to the fixed two-row interval presentation (and its explicitly transported isomorphisms). Positive-bound syzygy existence alone does not yield canonical proof policy in general. The next gate is to define a typed redistribution cell with exact source-row support and test whether its comparison is stable under composition, without confusing zero surplus with unique proof or with operational/analytic authority.
