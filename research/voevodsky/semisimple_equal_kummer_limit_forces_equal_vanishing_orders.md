# A semisimple equal-Kummer limit forces equal vanishing orders

## Question

Is the equal-order transverse specialization an arbitrary favorable choice, or is it forced by comparison with the coincident two-wall conductor residue?

## Claim boundary

This derives a necessary condition on any proposed normal deformation. It does not construct or authorize that deformation geometrically.

## General normal residue

Write the positive vanishing orders at the triple divisor as

\[
p=m_B,
\qquad q=m_C,
\qquad e=m_E.
\]

The normal residue has characteristic polynomial

\[
\lambda(\lambda-2e)(\lambda-p-q).
\]

The coincident conductor Kummer factors require the two nonzero eigenvalues to agree. Hence

\[
2e=p+q.
\]

This spectral condition alone permits unequal \(p,q\).

## Semisimplicity test

Impose \(e=(p+q)/2\) and subtract the repeated eigenvalue \(p+q\). The nonzero two-by-two minors of

\[
R-(p+q)I
\]

are, up to sign,

\[
\frac{(p-q)(p+q)}2.
\]

For positive vanishing orders, \(p+q\ne0\). The repeated nonzero eigenspace has dimension two exactly when

\[
p=q.
\]

Combining this with \(2e=p+q\) gives

\[
p=q=e.
\]

Thus equal vanishing orders are forced, up to a common positive scale, by the two requirements inherited from the coincident conductor connection:

1. equal nonzero Kummer eigenvalues;
2. semisimple diagonalizability of their two-dimensional eigenspace.

## Ramification scale

If

\[
p=q=e=n,
\]

then

\[
R(n,n,n)=nR_{\rm eq}.
\]

The eigenvectors and their index-two eigenlattice are unchanged. The common scale \(n\) records normal ramification. A primitive unramified normal coordinate corresponds to \(n=1\); choosing it still requires geometric authority.

## Consequence

Unequal-order deformations are not rivals with the same target behavior:

- if \(2e\ne p+q\), the two nonzero eigenvalues differ;
- if \(2e=p+q\) but \(p\ne q\), the repeated eigenvalue is not semisimple;
- only \(p=q=e\) yields the required semisimple equal-Kummer residue.

This turns equal order from an unexplained numerical convenience into a necessary acceptance condition for any source-derived normal deformation.

## Disposition

The target conductor structure uniquely selects the equal-order ray in the positive vanishing-order cone, modulo common ramification. Existence and orientation of a geometric normal map on that ray remain unproved.
