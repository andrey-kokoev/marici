# Categorical residual doctrine and constraint levels

## Question

How should the five residual modalities and their implications at different constraint levels be represented categorically?

## Claim boundary

This packet defines the categorical architecture and its admissible arrows. It does not prove the arithmetic generator relations, differentiated tail bounds, completed positivity, or RH.

## Why the modalities are not a five-object chain

Presentation residuals, observer invisibility, pre-descent anomalies, cutoff errors, and substantive positive residuals have different codomains and different required dispositions. There is no canonical sequence

\[
\mathsf{Presentation}\to\mathsf{Anomaly}\to
\mathsf{Approximation}\to\mathsf{Positive}\to\mathsf{Invisible}.
\]

Such a chain would falsely permit positive signal to be annihilated or cutoff error to be reclassified as gauge.

Instead let \(\mathcal C\) be the category of constraint stages. It is generated by:

- presentation quotient \(q\);
- local-to-global descent \(d\);
- arithmetic completion \(a\);
- observer evaluation \(o\);
- packet restriction \(r\).

Relations in \(\mathcal C\) are the source-derived commuting cells among these generators.

Define a residual doctrine

\[
\mathbf{Res}:\mathcal C^{\mathrm{op}}\longrightarrow\mathbf{Cat}.
\]

For each stage \(c\), the fiber \(\mathbf{Res}(c)\) contains the residual objects meaningful at that stage. A stage arrow \(u:c\to c'\) induces reindexing

\[
u^*:\mathbf{Res}(c')\longrightarrow\mathbf{Res}(c).
\]

The total residual category is the Grothendieck construction

\[
\int_{c\in\mathcal C}\mathbf{Res}(c).
\]

An object is a typed pair \((c,E)\). A morphism \((c,E)\to(c',E')\) is a stage arrow \(u:c\to c'\) together with a comparison

\[
E\longrightarrow u^*E'.
\]

Thus every residual arrow records both where the residual lives and how it is transported.

## Five subfibrations

| Fiber | Typical object | Internal arrows | Required disposition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Presentation | \(G\hookrightarrow X\) | maps of inclusions | quotient by \(G\) |
| Invisible | \(L=\ker O/G\) | induced maps of quotient kernels | prove \(L=0\) |
| Pre-descent anomaly | transition cochain \(\Omega\) | coefficient transport and coboundary maps | construct compatible trivializer |
| Approximation | \(T_N=K-K_N\) | cutoff comparison and tail estimates | prove \(T_N\to0\) in the declared topology |
| Substantive | \(R=A^*A-B^*B\) | positive maps and packet restrictions | preserve and prove \(R\geq0\) |

The disposition is part of the type. A record pairing a modality with the wrong action is malformed.

## Admissible cross-fiber constructors

Cross-fiber arrows are not automatic; each requires a witness.

### Quotient descent

If \(G\subseteq\ker O\), observation descends through

\[
X\longrightarrow X/G\longrightarrow Y.
\]

This induces the invisible object \(\ker O/G\). Without the inclusion witness, the constructor is undefined.

### Observed curvature

Before global descent, two parallel local routes may have curvature \(\kappa\). If every observer kills it, then

\[
\kappa\longmapsto[\kappa]\in\ker O/G.
\]

Joint faithfulness proves only \([\kappa]=0\); literal \(\kappa=0\) additionally requires removal of presentation representatives.

Once compatible global \(K_i\) exist, \(\Omega_f=K_j-f_*K_i\) is a formal coboundary. The active condition is then ordinary equality of parallel global routes, not a claimed nontrivial anomaly class.

### Completion-to-observation comparison

For packet \(I\), cutoff \(N\), and derivative order \(k\), the comparison cell is

\[
T_{I,N,k}=O_{I,k}(K)-O_{I,k}(K_N),
\qquad
\|T_{I,N,k}\|\leq r(I)\epsilon_{N,k}.
\]

This is the mixed Beck--Chevalley cell comparing completion followed by observation with finite observation followed by cutoff inclusion. It need not vanish at finite \(N\); it must converge with a typed bound.

### Positive restriction

A global substantive section restricts contravariantly:

\[
R_J\longmapsto R_I,
\qquad I\subseteq J.
\]

Order reflection is the converse implication

\[
R_I\geq0\ \text{for every finite }I
\Longrightarrow R\geq0.
\]

It requires density, continuity, and completeness. It is not supplied by functorial restriction alone.

### Nullspace comparison

A map

\[
\ker R\longrightarrow G
\]

is admissible only after proving every zero-energy direction is presentation. Otherwise \(\ker R/G\) is a genuine invisible sector.

## Constraint cube

The architecture is a product of independent constraints rather than a linear ladder.

| Coordinate | Condition | Licensed conclusion | Independent residual |
|---|---|---|---|
| Typing | objects, maps, actions, and topologies exist | expressions are defined | malformed-map defect |
| Quotient | all maps preserve \(G\) | descent to \(X/G\) | presentation residual |
| Coherence | generator relations and parallel paths agree | functor on the presented category | route curvature |
| Completion | \(T_{I,N,k}\to0\) | finite arithmetic formulas define completed observations | approximation residual |
| Positivity | every \(R_I\) lies in its cone | positive-definite kernel and abstract RKHS | substantive residual |
| Faithfulness | \(\ker(\prod_IO_I)/G=0\) | observed equality implies quotient equality | invisible residual |
| Source identity | completed object factors through the arithmetic derivation chain | arithmetic interpretation | missing-source residual |

A theorem occupies a vertex of this constraint cube. Promotion along one coordinate requires its own arrow; movement along another coordinate cannot substitute for it.

## Principal commuting squares

### Quotient–observation square

\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
X&\longrightarrow&Y\\
\downarrow q&&\Vert\\
X/G&\longrightarrow&Y
\end{array}
\]

The square exists exactly when \(G\subseteq\ker O\).

### Completion–observation square

\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
K_N&\longrightarrow&G_{I,N}\\
\downarrow&&\downarrow\\
K&\longrightarrow&G_I
\end{array}
\]

Its finite failure to commute is the bounded tail residual \(T_{I,N,k}\).

### Coherence–positivity square

\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
R_J&\longrightarrow&\operatorname{Pos}(V_J)\\
\downarrow&&\downarrow\\
R_I&\longrightarrow&\operatorname{Pos}(V_I)
\end{array}
\]

Naturality and cone preservation are separate obligations. Commutativity without cone admission gives coherent signed data.

## Implications by constraint level

### Local level

Typed generator maps and residuals localize defects. No global path claim follows.

### Relation level

Vanishing fundamental directed periods gives path independence on the generated category. It says nothing about analytic completion or positivity.

### Completion level

Tail bounds extend identities from finite cutoffs to completed observations. Derivative cells require order-dependent bounds because powers of \(\log n\) enter under differentiation.

### Order level

Cone admission of every completed packet yields positive definiteness. It does not establish that the observers are faithful or that the kernel is arithmetically sourced.

### Conservative-observer level

Zero invisible quotient kernel promotes observed equality to equality on the intended quotient. It does not imply positivity.

### Apex level

The final object consists of:

\[
(\text{global route coherence},
\text{vanishing cutoff error},
\text{natural positive section }R,
\text{zero invisible quotient kernel},
\text{source-derived identification}).
\]

This is an intersection of constraints, not a demand that the substantive residual vanish.

## Derived operational consequences

1. Infinite verification reduces to generators and relations only after the presentation theorem is proved.
2. Rational dense observers suffice for semidefinite conclusions under continuity, but not for uniform strict margins near collisions.
3. Arithmetic cutoff and observer packet are distinct indices; valid certificates use schedules \(N=N(r,k,\Delta,D,W)\).
4. A failed tail bound cannot be repaired by adding observers.
5. An invisible kernel cannot be repaired by proving positivity.
6. An abstract RKHS reconstructed from assumed positivity cannot provide the missing source-derived positive map.
7. Endpoint and gamma terms may trivialize pre-descent prime anomalies while leaving the positive Weil residual nonzero.

## Disposition

The categorical picture is an indexed residual doctrine over a constraint category, with five typed subfibrations and witness-dependent cross-fiber constructors. Its principal use is failure localization: each residual has one admissible disposition, and no transport across the diagram can silently change that disposition.
