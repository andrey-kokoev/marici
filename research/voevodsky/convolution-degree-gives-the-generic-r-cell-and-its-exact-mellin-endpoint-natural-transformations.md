# Convolution degree gives the generic r-cell and its exact Mellin--endpoint natural transformations

## Graded observer source

Let \(E_1\) be the admitted observer test space. Retain convolution ancestry before identifying functions that happen to have the same scalar presentation.

Define the degree-\(r\) source as the completed image

\[
E_r
=
\overline{
\operatorname{span}
\left\{
g_1*\cdots*g_r:
g_j\in E_1
\right\}
}.
\]

The completion is taken in the declared common graph topology. Degree zero is the unit source:

\[
E_0=\mathbb C1.
\]

Convolution gives graded multiplication maps

\[
\mu_{r,s}:
E_r\widehat\otimes E_s
\longrightarrow
E_{r+s}.
\]

They satisfy strict associativity on the source core:

\[
\mu_{r+s,t}(
\mu_{r,s}\otimes I)
=
\mu_{r,s+t}(
I\otimes\mu_{s,t}).
\]

The involution preserves degree and reverses order:

\[
(f*g)^*=g^**f^*.
\]

## Mellin realization

Let

\[
\mathcal M_r:E_r\to\mathscr M_r
\]

be the angular Mellin transform. For \(p\in E_r\), write

\[
m_p=\mathcal M_rp.
\]

The convolution theorem gives

\[
m_{p*q}(z)
=m_p(z)m_q(z).
\]

Thus the Mellin realization is a graded algebra map.

## Degree successor generated by an observer

Fix \(a\in E_s\). Define the source successor

\[
L_a^{(r)}:E_r\to E_{r+s}
\]

by

\[
L_a^{(r)}p=a*p.
\]

On the Mellin side,

\[
\mathcal M_{r+s}L_a^{(r)}
=
M_{m_a}\mathcal M_r.
\]

This is the basic analytical natural transformation between two generic cells of degrees \(r\) and \(r+s\).

For \(a\in E_1\), it gives the elementary degree successor

\[
r\longmapsto r+1.
\]

## Composition law

For \(a\in E_s\) and \(b\in E_t\), associativity gives

\[
L_b^{(r+s)}L_a^{(r)}
=L_{b*a}^{(r)}.
\]

On Mellin amplitudes, this is

\[
M_{m_b}M_{m_a}
=M_{m_bm_a}.
\]

Hence all multi-step degree transitions are generated by multiplication operators and compose strictly.

## Endpoint transformation

Define the completed endpoint map

\[
\beta_r:E_r\to\mathbb C^2
\]

by

\[
\beta_r(p)
=
\begin{pmatrix}
m_p(i/2)\\
m_p(-i/2)
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

For \(a\in E_s\), define

\[
D_a^\partial
=
\begin{pmatrix}
m_a(i/2)&0\\
0&m_a(-i/2)
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Then

\[
\beta_{r+s}L_a^{(r)}
=D_a^\partial\beta_r.
\]

This is the exact endpoint naturality square for convolution degree.

For two arbitrary source elements, endpoint multiplication is componentwise:

\[
\beta_{r+s}(p*q)
=
\beta_r(p)\odot\beta_s(q).
\]

## Endpoint polarity

The endpoint Krein metric is

\[
J_{end}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The degree successor is generally not an isometry for this fixed matrix. Its transported metric obeys

\[
(D_a^\partial)^*
J_{end}
D_a^\partial
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&
\overline{m_a(i/2)}m_a(-i/2)\\
\overline{m_a(-i/2)}m_a(i/2)&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Therefore endpoint naturality is exact as a metric-bundle transformation, with the metric transported by the endpoint values of the convolution factor.

On a reflection-compatible source class satisfying

\[
m_a(-i/2)
=\overline{m_a(i/2)},
\]

the transport reduces to the source-prescribed reciprocal parity law.

## Even and odd endpoint rows

Apply the Hadamard transform

\[
H_2
=
\frac1{\sqrt2}
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1\\
1&-1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The endpoint coordinates become

\[
\beta_r^{even}
=
\frac{
m_p(i/2)+m_p(-i/2)
}{\sqrt2},
\]

\[
\beta_r^{odd}
=
\frac{
m_p(i/2)-m_p(-i/2)
}{\sqrt2}.
\]

In this frame, the endpoint metric has one positive and one negative line.

The transformed degree-successor matrix is

\[
\widetilde D_a^\partial
=H_2D_a^\partial H_2.
\]

Unless the two endpoint values of \(a\) agree, convolution degree mixes even and odd endpoint parity. This mixing must be retained in the generic cell.

## Regular phase-energy transformation

On each degree source, define the Mellin phase-energy embedding

\[
\Gamma_rp
=
\left(
m_p,
D_S^{loc}M_{m_p}
\right).
\]

For the degree successor generated by \(a\),

\[
\Gamma_{r+s}L_a^{(r)}p
=
\left(
M_{m_a}m_p,
D_S^{loc}M_{m_a m_p}
\right).
\]

The first row is exactly multiplicative. The second row obeys the operator product decomposition

\[
D_S^{loc}M_{m_a m_p}
=
(D_S^{loc}M_{m_a})M_{m_p}
\]

when the symbols are interpreted on the common multiplication domain.

Boundedness of this successor is controlled by the multiplier norm of \(m_a\) on the phase-energy space. It is not automatic for every completed observer.

## Logarithmic connection law

Where \(m_p\) and \(m_q\) are nonzero, define their logarithmic connection currents

\[
\omega_p
=
\frac1i\partial_t\log m_p.
\]

The product law gives

\[
\omega_{p*q}
=
\omega_p+
\omega_q.
\]

Thus convolution ancestry is multiplicative at feature level and additive at connection level.

Zeros require divisor or endpoint channels and must not be removed by choosing a logarithm branch globally.

## de Branges observation

Let

\[
K_S(z,w)
=
\frac{
1-\Theta_S(z)
\overline{\Theta_S(w)}
}{
2\pi i(\bar w-z)
}
\]

be the signed de Branges/Krein--Langer kernel.

For a finite test family \(Z=(z_1,\ldots,z_N)\), define the degree-\(r\) evaluation map

\[
\mathcal O_{r,Z}p
=
\begin{pmatrix}
m_p(z_1)\\
\vdots\\
m_p(z_N)
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The induced signed kernel form is

\[
q_{r,Z}(p,q)
=
\mathcal O_{r,Z}(q)^*
G_{K_S,Z}
\mathcal O_{r,Z}(p),
\]

where

\[
G_{K_S,Z}
=
(K_S(z_i,z_j))_{i,j}.
\]

Under convolution by \(a\), define

\[
D_{a,Z}
=
\operatorname{diag}
\left(
m_a(z_1),\ldots,m_a(z_N)
\right).
\]

Then

\[
\mathcal O_{r+s,Z}L_a^{(r)}
=D_{a,Z}\mathcal O_{r,Z}.
\]

Consequently

\[
q_{r+s,Z}(a*p,a*q)
=
\mathcal O_{r,Z}(q)^*
D_{a,Z}^*
G_{K_S,Z}
D_{a,Z}
\mathcal O_{r,Z}(p).
\]

This is the exact finite kernel naturality law.

## Positive and defect features

Under a Krein--Langer factorization

\[
\Theta_S=B_S^{-1}S_S,
\]

the finite kernel Gram decomposes as a positive Schur Gram minus a positive Blaschke-defect Gram after diagonal congruence.

Denote the corresponding source features by

\[
A_{S,r,Z},
\qquad
A_{B,r,Z}.
\]

The degree successor acts on both through the same diagonal observation multiplier \(D_{a,Z}\). Therefore the desired contraction comparison is naturally indexed in \(r\):

\[
A_{B,r,Z}
=C_{r,Z}A_{S,r,Z}.
\]

Successor coherence is the commutation condition

\[
C_{r+s,Z}
D_{a,Z}^{S}
=
D_{a,Z}^{B}
C_{r,Z}
\]

on the generated feature ranges.

This equation is meaningful only after the positive and defect Kolmogorov carriers are fixed independently of the desired contraction.

## Convolution square cell

For \(p\in E_r\), the positive test object is

\[
p^**p
\in E_{2r}.
\]

Its Mellin amplitude is

\[
m_{p^**p}(t)
=
|m_p(t)|^2
\]

on the unitary axis.

Thus every finite-rank Gram test is represented by one degree-doubled convolution-square cell. Matrix rank is carried by the ancestry of \(p\), not by a new positivity matrix at each rank.

## Disposition

The generic degree-\(r\) cell is the Mellin realization of \(E_r\). Every transition generated by convolution with \(a\in E_s\) is multiplication by \(m_a\).

This yields exact naturality formulas for:

1. Mellin observations;
2. endpoint evaluation and parity;
3. finite de Branges kernel observations;
4. positive and Blaschke-defect feature comparisons;
5. convolution-square degree doubling.

The remaining positivity theorem is now typed as a graded, successor-compatible contraction between the Schur and defect feature systems.
