# Klein-four symmetry can leave a unique nonzero parity only conditionally

## Question

What can the quartic Klein-four symmetry do on the two-bit cusp parity space before its geometric Picard action is known?

## Linear representations

Let

\[
K=(\mathbb Z/2)^2.
\]

Its automorphism group is

\[
\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb F_2)\cong S_3.
\]

The quartic involutions \(r,s\) commute and generate a Klein four group. Exhaustive enumeration of commuting involutions in \(\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb F_2)\) shows:

1. their image has order at most two;
2. at least one of \(r,s,rs\) acts trivially on \(K\);
3. every such representation fixes a nonzero vector;
4. a nontrivial image fixes exactly one nonzero vector.

Thus the quartic Klein four cannot act freely or faithfully on the three nonzero parity classes.

## Torsion of the four extension presentations

For

\[
L_{a,b}
=
\frac{\mathbb Z\langle e_6,v_{\rm alg},m\rangle}
{\langle2m-ae_6-bv_{\rm alg}\rangle},
\]

the torsion order is

\[
\gcd(2,a,b).
\]

Hence \(L_{0,0}\) has one \(\mathbb Z/2\) torsion summand, while the three nonzero parity presentations are torsion-free.

Current source work explicitly retains all four possibilities, so torsion-freeness of the relevant ambient complement group has not been established as an admissible selector.

## Conditional selection theorem

If two new facts were proved:

- the ambient integral group containing \(m\) is torsion-free;
- the induced quartic symmetry action on \(K\) is nontrivial;

then \((0,0)\) would be excluded and the unique nonzero fixed vector of that involution would be the only symmetry-invariant parity candidate.

This would determine the parity without computing both intersections separately. Neither premise follows from the present local cusp or rational de Rham data.

## Consequence for the automorphism programme

Automorphism obstruction alone cannot eliminate every parity class: every possible two-dimensional mod-two representation has a nonzero invariant. The decisive next computation is the actual Picard action matrix of one quartic involution, paired with an integral torsion audit of the ambient complement.

## Disposition

The symmetry route is sharpened from a generic obstruction to a conditional selector. It can determine a unique nonzero parity precisely when ambient torsion-freeness and a nontrivial induced involution are both established. Until then, symmetry proves noncanonical ordering but not a parity value.
