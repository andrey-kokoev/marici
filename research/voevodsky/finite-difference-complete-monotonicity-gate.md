# Finite-difference complete-monotonicity gate

## Question

Can the remaining all-derivative positivity condition be replaced by a bounded, enumerable hierarchy that avoids differentiating the completed prime sum?

## Claim boundary

Finite differences are shown to be equivalent to complete monotonicity under the stated smoothness assumptions and to avoid derivative-generated logarithmic powers on spectral atoms. Positivity of the explicit arithmetic function remains open.

## Difference hierarchy

For \(h>0\), define

\[
D_{k,h}H(t)
=
\sum_{j=0}^k
(-1)^j\binom{k}{j}H(t+jh).
\]

This is \((-1)^k\) times the ordinary forward difference of order \(k\).

If

\[
H(t)=\int_0^\infty e^{-t\lambda}\,d\nu(\lambda)
\]

with \(\nu\geq0\), then

\[
D_{k,h}H(t)
=
\int_0^\infty
e^{-t\lambda}
\left(1-e^{-h\lambda}\right)^k
\,d\nu(\lambda)
\geq0.
\]

Conversely, if \(H\) is sufficiently smooth and every finite-difference inequality holds, then

\[
\lim_{h\downarrow0}
\frac{D_{k,h}H(t)}{h^k}
=
(-1)^kH^{(k)}(t)
\geq0.
\]

Thus the difference hierarchy is equivalent to complete monotonicity once the regularity of \(H\) is sourced.

## Arithmetic advantage

Differentiating a prime term introduces powers of its spectral parameter, which appear arithmetically as powers of \(\log n\). The finite-difference operator instead inserts

\[
\left(1-e^{-h\lambda}\right)^k,
\]

which lies between zero and one for \(\lambda,h>0\).

Therefore the difference hierarchy can reuse undifferentiated Gaussian prime-tail domination at the spectral-atom level. It avoids the derivative-order growth \((\log n)^k\).

This does not itself prove the signed endpoint--gamma--prime sum positive: the positive-measure representation cannot be assumed in the forward RH argument. The explicit coupled finite differences must still be estimated directly.

## Exact hostile

The signed atomic function

\[
H_-(t)=1-\frac12e^{-\pi^2t}
\]

is positive for every \(t>0\), but

\[
D_{1,h}H_-(t)
=
-\frac12e^{-\pi^2t}
\left(1-e^{-\pi^2h}\right)
<0.
\]

Hence the first finite difference detects the negative spectral atom that scalar heat positivity misses.

## Categorical meaning

The derivative-jet category can be replaced by an equivalent difference category generated by translations

\[
t\longmapsto t+h
\]

and alternating cubical boundary operators. The order-\(k\) difference is the evaluation on a \(k\)-cube in the positive heat-scale direction.

This makes complete monotonicity a cubical cone condition: every finite heat-scale cube has nonnegative alternating boundary value.

Its indices are:

- base scale \(t\);
- step \(h\);
- cubical degree \(k\).

Arithmetic cutoff \(N\) remains an independent source index.

## Countable reduction

If \(H\) is continuous, it is enough to test rational positive \(t,h\) and every natural \(k\). Density extends the inequalities to all positive real \(t,h\). Smoothness then recovers derivative complete monotonicity.

This gives one enumerable scalar observer family rather than all real Gram packets.

## Updated proof bracket

The remaining arithmetic target can be stated as

\[
D_{k,h}H_{\mathrm{explicit}}(t)
\geq0
\]

for every natural \(k\) and rational positive \(t,h\), after endpoint, gamma, and prime completion have been coupled.

If established with the required regularity and source comparison, Bernstein reconstruction gives the positive measure, the entire kernel, and every Gram PSD condition.

## Disposition

The all-derivative gate has a bounded finite-difference replacement. This removes one technical source of derivative-tail growth and reveals complete monotonicity as positivity on every heat-scale cube. The unresolved content is direct positivity of those coupled arithmetic differences; assuming a positive representing measure would be circular.
