# The seven-transition dependency poset has 28, not 5040, admissible maximal coherence chambers

## Goal

Replace the provisional `7!` coherence count by the number of orderings that are actually well-typed. Factorial growth applies only when all seven transitions are independent. The semilocal construction has mandatory dependencies.

## Eight conductor states and seven transition types

Use the following seven transition labels:

| Label | Transition |
|---|---|
| `A` | semilocal amplification / adjoining the conductor places |
| `J` | canonical--dual or primal--contragredient presentation |
| `P` | convolution-square polarization |
| `C` | physical/Fourier cutoff pair and Halmos colligation |
| `T` | semilocal trace and volume finite-part observation |
| `E` | endpoint--gamma completion in the same polarization convention |
| `F` | positive apex filler identifying the completed trace route with a Gram route |

Together with the initial source object, these give eight conductor states.

The labels describe operations, not a forced linear chain. Different admissible orders are the presentations whose coherence must be filled.

## Mandatory dependency relations

The first-pass typing constraints are

\[
A<J,
\qquad
A<C,
\]

because the local dual/contra presentation and semilocal cutoff pair require the semilocal carrier.

The trace observation requires the paired presentation, cutoffs, and polarization:

\[
J<T,
\qquad
C<T,
\qquad
P<T.
\]

Endpoint completion must use the same polarized source convention:

\[
P<E.
\]

Finally, the apex filler compares the completed endpoint route with the trace route:

\[
T<F,
\qquad
E<F.
\]

Thus the dependency poset is generated by

\[
\boxed{
A<J,
A<C,
J<T,
C<T,
P<T,
P<E,
T<F,
E<F.
}
\]

## Why some operations remain incomparable

The following pairs should not be ordered in the poset, because their two orders are exactly coherence squares to be proved:

- `A` and `P`: polarize before or after semilocal amplification;
- `J` and `C`: form the dual presentation before or after introducing the cutoff geometry;
- `E` and `A`: construct the source endpoint block before or after place amplification;
- `E` and `T` after their prerequisites: endpoint and local trace routes meet only at the filler.

Imposing an order on these pairs would erase rather than prove the relevant coherence.

## Exact linear-extension count

An exhaustive enumeration of the `7!=5040` permutations retaining only those satisfying the eight dependency relations gives

\[
\boxed{
\#\operatorname{LinearExtensions}(\mathsf P)=28.
}
\]

The reproducible counter is

- `temp/count_coherence_poset.py`

and prints all 28 admissible maximal orderings.

Therefore one coherence plane has 28 maximal chambers under this first-pass dependency model, not 5040.

## Provisional polarity count

If there are:

- eight conductor states in each polarity;
- two coherence planes in each polarity;
- 28 maximal chambers per plane;
- one shared polarity-fixed inner filler with 28 maximal chambers;

then the corresponding maximal-component count is

\[
\boxed{
2(8+2\cdot28)+28
=156.
}
\]

If the inner filler must be separately oriented in both polarities, the count becomes

\[
\boxed{
2(8+2\cdot28+28)
=184.
}
\]

These are counts of conductor states plus **maximal coherence chambers**. They are not counts of all simplices in the resulting complex.

## Why `7^3`, `6^3`, and `7!` differ

- `7!` counts all orderings of seven independent operations;
- `7^3` counts points/cells in a three-axis cubical subdivision with seven levels;
- `6^3` counts elementary cubes between seven vertices on each axis;
- `28` counts admissible total orders of this specific dependency poset.

The semilocal coherence geometry should be the order complex of the dependency poset, or a quotient/refinement of it, rather than a full permutohedron unless all dependencies are deliberately forgotten.

## The two polarity posets

Let

\[
\mathsf P^+,
\qquad
\mathsf P^-
\]

be copies exchanged by the involution

\[
J_{pol}:
E\leftrightarrow E^\#,

P\widehat P
\leftrightarrow
\widehat P P,
\]

and canonical transform `leftrightarrow` dual transform. The dependency relation is invariant under this involution, so both polarities have the same 28-chamber order complex.

A shared inner filler is justified only if the involution acts on it with fixed central axis and exchanges its two boundary coherence planes.

## What still needs refinement

The count 28 depends on the chosen seven labels and eight edges. It will change if, for example:

1. gamma completion and endpoint completion are separate transitions;
2. volume renormalization and trace observation are separated;
3. the Halmos dilation and physical cutoff are separate;
4. positivity is a property of the filler rather than a separate transition;
5. an additional dependency between `A` and `E` is source-required.

Therefore 28 is a reproducible first typed count, not yet a topological invariant.

## Next combinatorial construction

To count all inner simplices rather than only maximal chambers, construct the order complex or the relevant Boardman--Vogt/permutohedral resolution of this poset and compute its `f`-vector:

\[
(f_0,f_1,\ldots,f_6).
\]

Shared faces must be counted once. Multiplying 28 by a chamber size would overcount all common boundaries.

## Disposition

The first explicit dependency model reduces the maximal ordering count from

\[
7!=5040
\]

to

\[
\boxed{28}.
\]

Under a shared polarity-neutral inner filler, the corresponding provisional outer count is 156 rather than 25216. The mathematical task is now to validate the seven transition labels and dependency edges against the semilocal source before computing the full simplicial `f`-vector.
