# The faithful joint-graph energy cannot descend through a noninjective scalar codiagonal

## Faithful carrier

Let the source-generated joint graph be

\[
\mathcal G
=
\{(x,Ax,Cx):x\in U\}
\]

with positive energy

\[
\mathcal E(x)
=
\|x\|_U^2
+
\|Ax\|^2
+
\|Cx\|^2,
\]

possibly augmented by the already constructed bounded skew linking block. Because the source coordinate is retained, the positive quadratic part has zero radical:

\[
\mathcal E(x)=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x=0.
\]

## Physical codiagonal

Let the proposed scalar or lower-dimensional output be

\[
D(x,Ax,Cx)
=
Ax+TCx,
\]

with the final scalar observation applied afterward if needed.

For the positive energy to descend to the quotient by \(D\), it must be constant on every fiber. Equivalently, every fiber direction must belong to the radical of the polarized energy:

\[
\ker D
\subseteq
\operatorname{rad}\mathcal E.
\]

Since the faithful energy has zero radical, descent requires

\[
\ker D=\{0\}.
\]

Thus positive descent through the codiagonal is possible only when the codiagonal is injective on the complete retained source carrier.

## Finite-packet consequence

On an \(n\)-dimensional source packet, a scalar codiagonal has rank at most one. Therefore

\[
\dim\ker D
\ge
n-1.
\]

For every packet with \(n>1\), the kernel is nonzero. Pick \(0\ne h\in\ker D\). Then

\[
D(h,Ah,Ch)=0
\]

but

\[
\mathcal E(h)>0.
\]

Hence the zero output fiber already contains states of different positive energy. No positive form on the scalar output can pull back to the faithful joint-graph energy.

The same argument applies to any output of dimension smaller than the source packet unless the admissible physical state space has first been compressed injectively.

## Meaning for the top coherencer

The top coherencer cannot simply identify the faithful joint graph with its scalar Clark/Xi codiagonal. Such a map necessarily forgets positive source directions.

A valid higher object must retain enough output data to make the source map injective, for example:

1. the complete primal--dual flag;
2. the two-port Weyl matrix together with source labels;
3. the oriented determinant and kernel lines;
4. an explicit quotient whose kernel has first been proved null for the relevant signed form.

Only after such a relative object is formed may Xi appear as one distinguished minor or scalar shadow.

## Chamber interpretation

The incidence-chamber condition

\[
\ell(Tv)
e0
\]

protects one distinguished minor. It does not make the full codiagonal injective and therefore does not authorize lossless descent of the complete positive energy.

This separates two statements:

- chamber preservation prevents the physical scalar coordinate from vanishing;
- faithful graph retention preserves the full positive storage.

Both are needed, but they perform different functions.

## 4-simplex consequence

The top 4-cell must live in a category of relative or flagged realizations, not scalar outputs. Its scalar Xi value is obtained only after the coherence cell has been formed and the distinguished chamber has been selected.

Therefore the candidate vertices must retain the same source/observer flag data on every shared face. A tetrahedral facet scalarized prematurely cannot be a literal face of the same analytic 4-simplex.

## Disposition

The codiagonal descent test is decided abstractly: the faithful positive joint-graph energy cannot descend through any noninjective scalar codiagonal. The positive apex must remain graph- or flag-valued, with Xi retained as a distinguished minor rather than used as the carrier itself.
