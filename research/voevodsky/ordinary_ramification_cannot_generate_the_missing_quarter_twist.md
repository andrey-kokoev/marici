# Ordinary ramification cannot generate the missing quarter twist

## Question

Can a finite ramified covering convert one of the source-listed integer FRW exponents into the quarter exponent required by the Kummer sign monodromy?

## Claim boundary

This concerns ordinary pullback of a logarithmic connection. Root stacks or independently supplied rank-one local systems are different objects and are not ruled out.

## Pullback law

Let a logarithmic connection have local residue \(r\) at \(q=0\). Under a ramified coordinate

\[
q=s^n,
\qquad n\in\mathbb Z_{>0},
\]

one has

\[
d\log q=n\,d\log s.
\]

Therefore pullback multiplies the residue:

\[
r\longmapsto nr.
\]

It never divides the residue by the ramification index.

## Application to the relative residue

The equal-order relative residue has nonzero eigenvalue \(2\varepsilon\). For the source-listed values

\[
\varepsilon\in\{0,-1,-2,-3\},
\]

and every positive ramification index \(n\), the pulled-back exponent is

\[
2n\varepsilon\in\mathbb Z.
\]

Hence its monodromy remains

\[
\exp(2\pi i\,2n\varepsilon)=1.
\]

No ordinary finite ramified pullback produces the required sign \(-1\).

## What would be required

A quarter exponent could arise only from additional fractional data, such as:

- a source-derived rank-one local system with monodromy \(-1\) on the relevant normal loop;
- a stacky or orbifold character whose descent records a fourth-root weight;
- an integrand whose local exponent is independently shown to shift the FRW exponent by one quarter.

These are added geometric structures, not consequences of replacing the normal coordinate by a ramified cover.

## Consequence for contour authority

Changing the contour orientation replaces monodromy by its inverse. Both \(+1\) and \(-1\) are involutions, so orientation cannot turn the trivial monodromy of integer \(\varepsilon\) into the Kummer sign. The missing datum is fractional local-system authority, not merely contour orientation.

## Disposition

The ordinary-cover branch is falsified. The remaining admissible branch requires an independently source-derived fractional local system or stacky descent compatible with the two wall-labelled integral pairings.
