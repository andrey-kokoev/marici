# Glue that cannot be erased by an identity-stage simplification

## Existing ingredients, new declared contract

The previous identity-stage glue tests changed arrow counts but preserved both observed return maps. This experiment retains an additional global observable: the state permutation obtained by traversing the existing six-panel belt.

Inputs are the verified belt around vertex A and the two coefficient presentations already found by the **linear**, orientation-repaired S4 action. They are the original 1836-arrow state and its exact complex conjugate. The transposition (AB) exchanges them and squares to identity.

Choose transport in the subgroup C2 generated by (AB). Every belt edge carries either identity or this checked state swap. Every panel must have identity transport around its boundary. This is an explicit information-structure contract, with no charge or dynamical interpretation. The source geometry does not choose the nontrivial class for us.

## Nontrivial global glue

Put swap labels on the inner and outer circumference edges of one panel, and identity labels elsewhere. Every panel still has even swap parity, so all six local compatibility conditions hold. But a circuit around either boundary loop has odd parity:

```text
one circuit:   original presentation -> conjugate presentation
two circuits:  original presentation -> original presentation
```

These statements are checked on the full exact coefficient inventories, not on names alone. They do not identify the states as proton/neutron, particle/antiparticle or spin states.

## Exhaustive invariant calculation

The belt has 12 vertices, 18 edges and 6 panels. Over F2, its six independent panel-flatness conditions leave 12 independent edge-label choices. Vertexwise relabelling of the two sheets has rank 11; a simultaneous relabelling at every vertex acts trivially on edge labels.

Consequently:

```text
flat assignments:        2^12 = 4096
gauge orbit size:        2^11 = 2048
inequivalent C2 classes: 2
```

The checker exhausts all 2^18 raw edge assignments, finds exactly 4096 flat ones, and verifies that the 2048 gauge changes form exactly the zero-holonomy class. No local sheet relabelling removes the nontrivial loop permutation. This is the concrete H^1(annulus; F2) = F2 distinction for the declared transport group.

It classifies this C2 restriction, not every possible S4-valued transport or every conceivable glue invariant.

## Same cost, different retained structure

Lift each base vertex to two sheet-labelled ports. Each of the 18 unoriented base edges has two lifted edges, and both directions are explicitly retained: 72 unit-coefficient arrows in either case.

| Property | Trivial glue | Nontrivial glue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| All six panel transports | Identity | Identity |
| One circuit of the belt | Identity | Sheet exchange |
| Connected components of the lifted complex | 2 | 1 |
| Boundary loops of the lifted complex | 4 | 2 |
| Directed unit-coefficient arrows | 72 | 72 |

Thus an arrow count alone misses the distinction. The nontrivial two-sheeted cover is still an orientable annulus; the word twist refers to sheet monodromy, not a Möbius strip in physical space.

These are witness-fibre transport arrows. They have not been silently added to the previous 1908/1944 coefficient-network costs: an actual typed connection to the electron interior and the chosen nuclear/interface location still needs its maps.

## What contraction may and may not do

This does **not** forbid compression. A coarse representation can retain a single loop with the swap label, preserving the invariant. It cannot replace that labelled loop by identity while claiming to retain the same global transport interface.

The distinction therefore survives witness-preserving simplification but not forgetting the observable. If one changes the comparison convention to the previous parity-semilinear action, the unmarked coefficient-state distinction disappears; that is a different interface policy, not a proof that this linear-action witness was identity.

A raw graph loop is not enough: the stored transport action, its source/target coefficient states and the global readout are essential. Conversely, the construction does not show that 72 is a reconstruction minimum. It supplies an invariant that a valid smaller realization must preserve.

## Tests and disposition

All 21 checks passed: source witnesses, exact group action, matrix ranks, exhaustive flat-assignment census, all gauge changes, one- and two-circuit transport, lifted-complex connectivity/boundaries, equal arrow counts, valid coarse-loop compression, false invariant erasure, a locally nonflat seam mutation and serialization/replay.

This gives a concrete answer to the missing glue invariant: **retained loop-state permutation**. It does not yet select the nontrivial class as a neutron or derive an H-1/H-2 distinction. The next substantive requirement is to specify which realization obligation forces one class rather than merely allowing either, and how it attaches to the electron complex without forgetting this witness.
