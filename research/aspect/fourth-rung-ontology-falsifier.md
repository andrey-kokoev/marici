# Fourth rung: falsify the hostile language

## Question

Can a tester compiler establish that its mutation carrier contains every
scientifically relevant way the apparatus could counterfeit the claim?

## Result

No finite carrier can certify absolute closure if the hostile grammar admits a
fresh-symbol constructor. For any finite language `L`, the term `fresh(L)` is
well formed and absent from `L`. The claim that rung three closed its frozen
sixteen-dimensional carrier remains exact; the stronger claim that it closed
all possible hostiles is false.

The executable attack supplies eight ontology transformations: nonlinear
degree lift, cross-coordinate composition, probe-dependent adaptation, port
creation, support refinement, arity lift, singular support, and a fresh
predicate. All eight have zero signature in the old language. Enlarging the
language gives each a distinct coordinate and closes that frozen challenge.
Applying the fresh constructor again then produces an explicit successor
outside the enlarged finite language.

## Inverted architecture

The fourth rung changes the output of the tower. It does not produce a final
list of hostiles. It produces an extension policy:

`new hostile term -> provenance and admissibility check -> separating intervention obligation -> enlarged carrier`.

Completeness is therefore indexed by a declared language and challenge set.
The stable invariant is not a terminal carrier but the rule by which a carrier
must enlarge when a legitimate new distinction appears.

## Claim boundary

The diagonal result is syntactic and exact. It does not show that every fresh
term is physically realizable. Physical admission requires a constructor or
apparatus model for that term. Conversely, declaring unknown terms impossible
is not a test and is rejected by the rung-four meta-gate.

Rung five now audits that admission decision. See
`fifth-rung-admissibility-governance.md`.
