# Constructor permutohedron and Bianchi closure

Pairwise `UV/VU` fringes detect constructor curvature. The next rung asks whether those curvatures fit together into a coherent higher network.

Take three registered local constructors `U`, `V`, and `W`. Their six orderings form the vertices of a hexagonal permutohedron:

```text
UVW → UWV → WUV → WVU → VWU → VUW → UVW
```

Each adjacent edge swaps one neighboring pair. Full process tomography reconstructs the relative transport from one word implementation to the next. The ordered product of the six transported edges must be the identity when the implementations belong to one associative, context-independent compiler algebra.

This is a discrete Bianchi closure test. Pairwise curvature may be nonzero while the higher boundary still closes exactly.

## Why scalar fringes are insufficient

A scalar fringe is one matrix element of a relative transport. Products of projected matrix elements do not equal the corresponding matrix element of the transport product. Consequently, multiplying six scalar fringes can report an apparent defect even when the full operator boundary is exactly closed.

The experiment therefore requires informationally complete ancilla probes and reconstruction of every two-dimensional edge transport. This is the new equipment rung above the pairwise curvature interferometer.
