# Uniform noise law for the pro-instrument tower

Raw detector variance is not the invariant quantity. Each level first removes
its preregistered common gain. The adjacent-level restriction then pushes the
high-level covariance through the same first-jet projector used by the value
map. The natural residual uses the complete paired-route covariance, including
cross covariance.

The candidate uniform law needs finite all-level suprema for independent
noise, common-mode noise, sorter noise, and adjacent common-gain ratios.
Clipping cannot enlarge calibrated error because its activity projector has
entries zero or one. Under those source-derived bounds, every restriction has
one common covariance envelope.

This exposes a hard asymmetry between explanation and measurement. Any finite
prefix can be copied exactly by a hostile variance law that starts growing at
the next untested level. Therefore finite experiments can falsify a proposed
uniform law but cannot establish its all-level quantifier. Uniformity requires
a source-derived envelope. Data test that envelope; they do not manufacture it.

The checker validates a bounded synthetic family and constructs an explicit
finite-prefix camouflage hostile. It does not certify physical uniformity.
