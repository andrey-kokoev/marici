# Where `3+2+1` fails and `2(2+1)+1` works

## Question

Which optical distinction requires two locally completed witness systems plus
one outer mate, rather than one `3+2+1` tower?

## Exact obstruction

Order the paired outcomes as `00, 01, 10, 11`. Relational coherence changes
the joint law along the vector

\[
k=(1,-1,-1,1).
\]

Both marginal maps annihilate this vector. Consequently the flat law and the
relational law at visibility `3/5` have identical local marginals:

\[
p_A=p_B=(1/2,1/2).
\]

Their parity differs: zero for the flat law and `3/5` for the relational law.

A single `3+2+1` tower fails when its final mate is a local totalization. Once
the record has been replaced by either local marginal, no later function of
that marginal can reconstruct the parity coordinate. The outer relation has
been quotiented away.

## Working factorization

The correct architecture is

```text
wing A: forward/backward witnesses + local mate
                                                \
                                                 outer relational mate
                                                /
wing B: forward/backward witnesses + local mate
```

This is `2(2+1)+1` when input, output, and control are treated as typing data
rather than counted as an additional dynamical layer.

Each local `2+1` completion may close its own calibration, conditioning, and
no-click accounting, but it must retain the immutable pair-instance key and
its record map. The outer `+1` acts on the paired record before the marginal
quotient. It evaluates the parity character that both local marginal maps kill.

## Where the distinction matters

`3+2+1` is sufficient for one-system questions whose target observable
descends through one totalization: conditioned versus unconditional fringes,
detector completion, or one local uncomputation.

It fails for:

- Bell correlations with two independently chosen controls;
- coherence visible only in joint parity;
- correlated phase channels with zero local first moments;
- any comparison whose witness lies in the common kernel of both marginal
  maps.

`2(2+1)+1` works because it has two independently closed local interfaces and
an outer comparison constructor that acts before their shared relational
record is forgotten.

## Necessary qualification

Two completed marginals are not enough. If either `2+1` tower discards the
pair-instance key, the outer `+1` receives only two flat distributions and the new
architecture degenerates to the original failure.

## Disposition

The architectures serve different shapes of question. `3+2+1` completes one
fibered experiment. `2(2+1)+1` completes two local experiments while preserving
a joint fiber for a final relational mate.
