# Common forcing generates a finite-valuation odd current

## Affine Green cell

Put `a=1/2+z` and `b=1/2-z`.  For constant common endpoint forcing `f`,
the reciprocal tail carrier evolves across an interval of length `ell` by

`T_out=D_ell T_in-r_f(ell)`,

where

`D_ell=diag(exp(-a ell),exp(-b ell))`

and

`r_f(ell)=f((1-exp(-a ell))/a,(1-exp(-b ell))/b)^T`.

The associated three-port affine matrix is

`Dhat_ell=[[D_ell,-r_f(ell)],[0,1]]`.

It composes exactly:

`Dhat_eta Dhat_ell=Dhat_(ell+eta)`.

Thus the forcing reservoir is not an optional correction.  It is the extra
coordinate that makes interval concatenation a representation.

## The hidden odd current

The injection is common to both reciprocal channels, but the two channels
decay at different rates.  Its odd boundary displacement is

`J_f(ell,z)=-[1,-1]r_f(ell)`

`=f((1-exp(-b ell))/b-(1-exp(-a ell))/a)`.

Near a zero-length interval,

`J_f(ell,z)=f z ell^2+O(ell^3)`.

It vanishes at the reciprocal seam `z=0`, yet is generically nonzero at every
finite valuation length.  After vacuum normalization it is order `ell`, so it
disappears in the archimedean infinitesimal response while remaining visible
at finite primes.

This is the first concrete reason the homogeneous valuation response cannot
close the braid cube by itself: it passes every infinitesimal comparison but
deletes a finite-place odd current generated by the shared source.

## Optical consequence

Inject an equal coherent displacement into two modes with decay constants
`1/2+z` and `1/2-z`.  A balanced difference detector is dark at injection and
then brightens quadratically with propagation length.  Measuring only the
homogeneous transfer matrix predicts the wrong dark-port output.  Measuring
the affine displacement recovers it and tests the missing Green coherencer.
