# Typed coefficient proliferation DPC

## Question

Can apparently competing coefficients arise because one underlying construction is viewed through forward realization, backward mate, composite coherence, and readout roles?

## Claim boundary

This packet establishes a mechanism in an exact one-dimensional linear model. It does not yet prove that any specific historical Marici coefficient discrepancy has this cause.

## Typed coefficient roles

Let \(A:C\to D\) be a linear realization map with coordinate coefficient \(a\). Give the one-dimensional spaces weighted pairings

\[
\langle x,y\rangle_C=g_Cxy,
\qquad
\langle u,v\rangle_D=g_Duv.
\]

The backward adjoint has coefficient

\[
a^\dagger=\frac{g_D}{g_C}a,
\]

because

\[
\langle Ax,y\rangle_D
=
\langle x,A^\dagger y\rangle_C.
\]

For another forward map \(B:D\to E\) with coefficient \(b\), the composite coefficient is \(ba\). A readout functional \(q_D:D\to\mathbb Q\) with coefficient \(q\) pulls to \(C\) with coefficient \(qa\).

Thus the displayed numbers

\[
a,
\qquad
a^\dagger,
\qquad
ba,
\qquad
qa
\]

belong to different hom-spaces or module actions. Their inequality is not a contradiction.

## Gauge behavior

Under basis changes \(e'_C=s_Ce_C\) and \(e'_D=s_De_D\), the forward coordinate becomes

\[
a'=\frac{s_C}{s_D}a,
\]

and the metric weights become \(g'_C=s_C^2g_C\), \(g'_D=s_D^2g_D\). The mate coefficient transforms compatibly. Coordinate values change, while the defining pairing identity remains invariant.

## DPC cycle

### Governing conjecture

Repeated coefficient proliferation can be the visible shadow of a collapsed type distinction: forward maps, backward mates, coherencers, and readout actions were projected into one scalar coordinate. The mechanism is hard to vary because each coefficient is forced by a different universal equation.

### Rivals

1. Every difference is removable gauge.
2. The roles share one literal scalar, and all deviations are defects.
3. Every difference represents an independent physical parameter.
4. Canonical mate, composition, and module maps relate distinct typed coefficients; only residuals left by those maps are defects or independent parameters.

### Risky consequences

One exact model must produce four unequal coefficients while satisfying every canonical relation. Basis changes must alter their displayed values without altering the mate pairing. Forcing the forward coefficient into the mate or readout role must produce a nonzero residual fixed in advance.

### Falsification attempt

The checker uses \(a=2\), \(g_C=3\), \(g_D=5\), \(b=3\), and \(q=7\). It derives coefficients \(2\), \(10/3\), \(6\), and \(14\), verifies the pairing, composition, and readout equations exactly, and repeats the mate test under nontrivial rational basis changes. Deliberate role substitution produces predicted nonzero residuals.

### Residual

This proves possibility and structural necessity in the fixture, not historical attribution. Each past discrepancy still requires source typing, canonical comparison maps, and an audit excluding convention errors and fitted corrections.

### Disposition

Rivals 1, 2, and 3 are rejected as universal explanations. Rival 4 is provisionally retained. The new structure can explain coefficient proliferation, but application to Marici history remains an open audit.

## Explanation

The coefficients looked like alternatives because their types were suppressed. Restoring the equipment and record-module roles turns several apparent numerical choices into images of one construction under different canonical operations. Equality is replaced by typed compatibility.

## Disposition

Compare coefficients only after transporting them into the same typed hom-space or module. A residual has meaning only after that comparison is constructed.
