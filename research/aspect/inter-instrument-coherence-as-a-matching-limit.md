# Inter-instrument coherence as a matching limit

## Question

What structure makes several instruments attached to one system jointly coherent rather than merely co-present?

## Claim boundary

This packet defines mathematical compatibility of occurrence-indexed attachment data. It does not infer physical interaction, simultaneity, causal order, or unique reconstruction from compatibility.

## Occurrences and incidence

For an attachment \(D\) over \(S\), let \(I_D\) be its category of instrument occurrences. Repeated instruments remain distinct objects of \(I_D\).

Let \(B_D\) be the boundary-incidence category generated by:

- local instrument contexts;
- shared interfaces and overlap contexts;
- incidence arrows from local contexts to their exposed boundaries;
- any higher intersection contexts required by the source attachment geometry.

The functor from occurrences into local contexts must preserve occurrence labels. Passing to instrument types before constructing \(B_D\) can collapse distinct interfaces and is not admissible.

## Constraint presheaf and matching object

Let

\[
F_D:B_D^{op}\to\mathbf{Set}
\]

assign admissible local configurations to every context and restriction maps to every incidence arrow. The joint matching object is

\[
M_D=\varprojlim_{b\in B_D}F_D(b).
\]

Equivalently, for finite incidence data, \(M_D\) is the equalizer inside the product of local configuration sets cut out by all shared-boundary equations.

A family of local instrument configurations is coherent exactly when it determines a point of \(M_D\). Co-presence supplies a product point only; it does not prove the equalizer equations.

## Attachment coherence certificate

The coherence component \(\kappa_D\) of an attachment is a typed cone into \(F_D\), or equivalently a witness

\[
\kappa_D\in M_D
\]

when one configuration is selected. For a family or state space \(X_D\), coherence is a map

\[
\kappa_D:X_D\to M_D.
\]

This separates three claims:

1. local admissibility: every component lies in its local configuration set;
2. compatibility: all restrictions agree in the matching limit;
3. realizability: a source-defined global configuration maps to that compatible family.

The first does not imply the second, and the second does not imply the third without a specified global-to-matching map.

## Continuation interfaces

For an ordered transformer interface \(e:i\to j\), declare a continuation-state object \(Q_e\) and typed maps from the output boundary of occurrence \(i\) and the input boundary of occurrence \(j\). The continuation equation is equality of their images in \(Q_e\). It is one incidence equation in the limit, not an interpretation of composition order as physical time.

A genuinely joint interaction that is not reducible to boundary equality requires an additional interaction cell in \(B_D\) and a corresponding constraint object. Pairwise interface checks cannot manufacture that cell.

## Transport under attachment rewrites

Suppose a decorated boundary rewrite gives an equivalence

\[
q:B_D\simeq B_{D'}
\]

and a natural isomorphism

\[
F_D\cong q^*F_{D'}.
\]

Then the universal property of limits induces an isomorphism

\[
M_D\cong M_{D'}.
\]

Thus coherence transports only when both incidence and constraints transport. A bare permutation of instrument labels is insufficient. This reuses the previously established matching-object and decorated-boundary transport criteria rather than replacing them with a finite fixture.

## Finite diagnostic

Three bit-valued occurrences \(A,B,C\) have shared boundaries \(AB,BC,CA\), each requiring equality. Their local product has eight points; the full matching limit has two. The tuple \((0,0,1)\) is locally admissible and passes the truncated diagram containing only \(AB\), but fails the full incidence diagram. A cyclic occurrence rewrite preserves all three constraints and induces a bijection of matching points.

## Disposition

Joint instrument coherence is membership in the limit of an occurrence-indexed constraint presheaf. Continuation equations and higher interaction cells must occur explicitly in the incidence diagram. Coherence transports through decorated boundary equivalence plus natural constraint transport, not through type-level relabeling alone.
