# Optical first-jet naturality square

Let (M(L)) be the present response, (K(L)=M'(L)), and define

[
R=K_{22}-LK_{11}-M_{11}.
]

The source packet also satisfies (M_{22}=LM_{11}). Consider a detector-chart
transition that applies one common nonzero gain (g(L)) to the complete
authorized response packet:

[
widetilde M=gM,qquad widetilde K=g'M+gK.
]

Then

[
widetilde R
=gR+g'(M_{22}-LM_{11})
=gR.
]

Thus the zero locus of the first-jet residual descends through arbitrary
offset-dependent common gain. The (g') transport term is essential; omitting
it is the detector analogue of omitting retained-state transport from a Schur
first jet.

The square does not close under independently fitted channel gains. For
constant gains (a) and (b), the residual acquires

[
(b-a)(M_{11}+LK_{11}),
]

which is generically nonzero. Independent channel normalization is therefore a
hostile, not a gauge freedom. The admitted chart law is: preserve the
symmetric/antisymmetric labels and transform the complete two-channel packet
by one common gain, including its derivative transport.
