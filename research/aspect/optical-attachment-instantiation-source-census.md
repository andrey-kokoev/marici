# Optical attachment instantiation: source census

## Question

Can the attachment existence and coherence gates presently be instantiated against independently identifiable optical sources in the Aspect corpus?

## Claim boundary

This packet reports a bounded repository census. It makes no claim that the optical constructions are false, and it does not treat internally authored packets or graph communications as independent scientific sources.

## Admission criterion

A sourced instantiation requires, for each optical interface:

1. an independently identifiable source artifact;
2. a locator such as DOI, arXiv identifier, ISBN, stable publication URL, or admitted captured-source reference;
3. the source-defined system, interface, modes, loss channels, and readout;
4. a typed map from those definitions into the attachment objects and morphisms;
5. a discriminating prediction separating at least two semantic rivals.

An internal instrument registry records proposed constructions but does not supply external source authority by itself.

## Census

A fresh bounded search of `research/aspect/` found 408 immediate entries and 1,710 tree entries. It found 123 broad textual occurrences of terms such as `source`, `Optica`, URLs, and optical headings, but a narrower search found zero occurrences matching DOI URLs, arXiv URLs, ISBN markers, or DOI-number syntax.

This is evidence only about machine-identifiable citation markers in the current corpus. It does not prove that no packet paraphrases a published result, but such a paraphrase cannot satisfy the source-identity gate without a durable locator and captured definitions.

## DPC cycle

### Governing conjecture

The existing Aspect corpus already contains enough independently identifiable optical source material to instantiate deletion, mode mismatch, loss, and staged/direct comparison gates. The mechanism would be a source locator plus a typed derivation from source apparatus to attachment semantics.

### Rivals

1. Internal instrument packets are sufficient source authority without independent locators.
2. Familiar textbook optical facts may be treated as sourced implicitly.
3. The corpus contains machine-identifiable sources, but the bounded citation search failed to detect their syntax.

### Risky consequences

The governing conjecture predicts at least one recoverable source locator and enough source-defined interface data to construct a typed attachment comparison. Rival 1 predicts that provenance is irrelevant; rival 2 predicts that community familiarity substitutes for source identity; rival 3 predicts a concrete missed locator recoverable by a broader admitted source inventory.

### Falsification attempt

The repository search tested broad source vocabulary and then DOI, arXiv, ISBN, and DOI-number patterns over the complete reported Aspect tree fingerprint. The narrow search returned zero matches. The internal instrument registry was not promoted because it is an authored coordination artifact rather than independent publication evidence.

### Residual

No external bibliographic or source-capture capability was available in this turn. The search cannot exclude unconventional citations lacking the tested markers, and it does not inspect sources outside the repository.

### Disposition

The governing conjecture is not retained under the stated mechanical criterion. Rivals 1 and 2 are rejected by the source-identity gate. Rival 3 remains open but requires a broader admitted source inventory or externally supplied artifacts. Optical instantiation is deferred without physical promotion.

## Disposition

The categorical gates remain mathematical results. Their application to sourced optical interfaces is deferred until independently identifiable source artifacts are admitted. No deletion, mismatch, loss, or comparison claim is promoted as source-validated by this census.
