# The boundary first jet creates the first seam-zero lattice

## Zero-free accumulated carrier

The common-forcing odd displacement over a cutoff interval `L` is

`J(L,z)=2f integral_0^L exp(-u/2)sinh(zu) du`.

For positive `f`, it is sign-definite on the real strip.  On `z=i tau`, its
imaginary quadrature is strictly positive for every `tau>0`.  Neither the
finite affine carrier nor its positive prime aggregation can create a zero.

## Cutoff differentiation

Differentiate with respect to the moving boundary:

`Q(L,z)=partial_L J(L,z)=2f exp(-L/2)sinh(zL)`.

This is the endpoint current.  On the critical seam,

`Q(L,i tau)=2if exp(-L/2)sin(tau L)`.

It has the exact simple-zero lattice

`tau_n=n pi/L`, for positive integers `n`.

The alternating sign is not inserted by a signed source.  It is generated by
the boundary first jet of a positive cumulative transport.

## Relation to the determinant frame

A static triangular affine lift cannot expose its forcing reservoir through
an ordinary determinant.  Adding a return incidence is necessary but not
sufficient: the determinant-frame increment depends on the complete Schur
first jet, including derivatives of the return row, retained transport,
outgoing reservoir, and boundary block.

The calculation here identifies the minimal zero-bearing constituent of that
jet.  The derivative of the outgoing reservoir is the endpoint vector

`partial_L r_f(L)=f D_L (1,1)^T`.

Its odd codiagonal is `-Q(L,z)`.  Hence the source-derived moving-boundary
operation already supplies an oscillatory return signal; no arbitrary complex
gain is needed to manufacture its phase.  What remains is to embed this row
and column into the full Schur first jet and test whether the other terms
cancel, shift, or preserve its seam zeros.

## Prime-resolved optical prediction

For a prime-labelled segment, set `L=log p`.  Modulate the segment length and
read the odd displacement synchronously.  The derivative quadrature must have
dark fringes at

`tau_n=n pi/log p`.

Changing from prime `p` to prime `q` rescales the entire fringe lattice by
`log p/log q`.  The cumulative dark port remains bright at those same
frequencies.  Simultaneous measurement of the cumulative and derivative ports
therefore distinguishes affine transport from its boundary first jet.
