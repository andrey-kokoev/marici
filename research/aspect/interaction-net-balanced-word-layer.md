# Balanced-word layer for operation-sensitive optics

Kitaev's balanced-word theorem supplies the operation-sensitive layer that the
additive context tower lacks.

Two reversible projective constructor words can be compared without choosing
primitive phases exactly when their signed occurrence vectors agree. Their
relative word then lies in the commutator subgroup. Additive ports see the
shared occurrence vector; a coherent balanced interferometer can see the
ordered residue.

The smallest example is U V versus V U. Both branches use one U and one V, so
independent rephasing cancels. Their relative word is a commutator. This is
precisely the kind of parallel-operation distinction that endpoint records
erase.

The combined instrument now has two logically different layers:

1. Quotient-aware additive mode banks measure object, resource, birth, and
   death sectors.
2. Balanced word interferometers compare operations inside one additive
   sector.

This does not yet authorize an interaction-net experiment. The primitive O and
K moves have not been shown reversible or supplied with projective coherent
lifts. Nor has a coherent branch-control constructor been supplied. A
balanced algebraic pair is therefore a candidate optical comparison, not a
physical apparatus certificate.

The next source question is exact: which admitted interaction-net operations
have reversible coherent lifts, and which balanced word pairs remain distinct
after the exact relation quotient?
