# Optical conjugate first-jet identity

Take the source response transferred from the symmetric and antisymmetric
spectral packets at calibrated offset (L):

[
M(L)=operatorname{diag}(2f(L),,2Lf(L)).
]

A controlled spectral shear changes (L) while preserving the packet labels.
Its source-derived first jet is

[
K(L)=M'(L)=operatorname{diag}(2f'(L),,2f(L)+2Lf'(L)).
]

Therefore the unknown envelope derivative cancels from

[
K_{22}-L K_{11}=M_{11}.
]

The off-diagonal first jets are zero. These three equalities are the
preregistered optical prediction:

- (K_{12}=0);
- (K_{21}=0);
- (K_{22}-LK_{11}-M_{11}=0).

This predicts a relation among present and successor responses. It does not
predict the zero location, the envelope (f), or an absolute detector scale.
The identity is invariant under one common linear gain applied to the full
authorized response packet. Independent gains, post-hoc covariance fitting,
packet relabelling, and common-mode drift are separate null models rather than
allowed gauge transformations.

The physical experiment remains unexecuted. Its uncertainty threshold must be
frozen from calibration data before the controlled shear is applied.
