# Current constructors do not determine ontology or nomological equivalence

## Two theories can agree on every admitted record and disagree about reality

Take two preparation histories, `plus` and `minus`. The only currently admitted
constructor is a `Z` log that returns the same record for both.

Define two finite theories:

- coarse theory: both histories instantiate one state, and no distinguishing
  constructor is physically possible;
- fine theory: the histories instantiate two states, and an `X` constructor is
  physically possible that distinguishes them.

Restricted to the currently admitted `Z` constructor, both theories predict the
same complete observation table. Yet they disagree about ontic identity,
nomological equivalence, state-space cardinality, and the outcome of the proposed
`X` intervention.

Therefore present operational equivalence does not entail either ontic identity
or equivalence under all physically possible constructors.

## The circularity is avoided by joint conjecture, not operational bootstrapping

States, laws, and possible constructors must be specified together as one
explanatory theory. Experiments then constrain that package through risky new
interventions. Constructor analysis contributes by exposing which intervention
would distinguish rival packages; it cannot infer the maximal possibility set
from a restricted record without theoretical premises.

This gives a disciplined hierarchy:

1. Data establish equality of records on tested constructors.
2. Calibrated apparatus establishes resource-relative equivalence within a
   declared accessible set.
3. A dynamical theory conjectures the larger nomological constructor set and
   state space.
4. Novel interventions test consequences on previously unprobed contexts.
5. Ontic identification is warranted only within the surviving theory, not
   read directly from operational equivalence.

The anchor is thus not a privileged measurement. It is a hard-to-vary joint
theory whose state distinctions, transformation laws, conservation rules, and
counterfactual possibilities support independently testable consequences.

## Why a failed intervention is not yet an impossibility proof

If the proposed `X` constructor fails in the laboratory, that may reflect a
fundamental prohibition, insufficient control, decoherence, calibration failure,
or a wrong implementation. Claiming impossibility requires an explanatory law
that covers those alternatives and survives attempts to route around them.

This is the correct Deutsch-style asymmetry: possibility can be witnessed by one
working constructor, while impossibility requires a theory of why every allowed
implementation fails.

## Optical realization

A computational-basis detector gives identical statistics for `|+>` and `|->`.
A coherent analyzer predicts opposite `X` outcomes. The successful analyzer
falsifies the coarse operational quotient. But its design was licensed by the
quantum theory of phase and interference; the `Z` records alone did not derive
that constructor.

## Claim boundary

The checker proves finite observational underdetermination for one restricted
constructor table. It does not show that all scientific theories are globally
underdetermined or equally explanatory.
