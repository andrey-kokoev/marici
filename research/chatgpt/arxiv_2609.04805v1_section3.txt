3.1. The Modular Class and Shifted Graded Traces
Recall that the modular class of a Q-manifold is the obstruction to the existence of a Q-invariant Berezin volume, see [6, 7] for a review. A Q-manifold is said to be unimodular if the modular class vanishes, which by definition means that we can find Berezin volumes such that . Note that although the modular class requires a choice of Berezin volume to define, it is independent of this choice and has a cohomological understanding. For applications of modular classes of Q-manifolds in the theory of Lie algebroids, the reader may consult Grabowski [15, 16]. We remark that within the BV-formalism, the vanishing of the modular class implies that there is no ‘one-loop anomaly’ in the corresponding semiclassical quantum theory, see [6, Remark 4.7] and/or [30, Section 6.1], for example.

3.2. Derived Cyclic Cohomology of Q-manifolds
In this subsection, we apply cyclic cohomology to Q-manifolds via the derived associative algebra. Recall that cyclic cohomology was developed by Connes [10] and acts as a generalisation of de Rham cohomology to associative algebras. For a very accessible introduction, the reader may consult Connes [11, Chapter 3], Khalkhali [21], or Weibel [47, Chapter 9]. Kastler [20, Chapter 3] developed Hochschild and cyclic cohomology for -graded associative algebras. Thus, by employing the shifted grading to the derived product, we may freely employ Kastler’s constructions and results with minimal amendment.

The complication in doing so is that the underlying vector space is an LF-space, i.e., a strict inductive limit of Fréchet spaces, rather than a Fréchet space itself (see [41, Chapter 13]). While it remains a nuclear space, working with completed projective tensor products of LF-spaces is significantly more complicated than the Fréchet case. Thus, to avoid complications with defining -cochains (), we will not insist on compactly supported functions in the definition, and in practice will work with compact supermanifolds.

We expect, in general, calculating the derived cyclic cohomology and explicitly presenting cyclic -cocycles for Q-manifolds without extra structure to be very difficult. It is important to note that the derived associative algebra is non-unital and so many of the standard methods of calculating cyclic cohomology, for example Connes’ SBI sequence, will not directly apply to derived cyclic cohomology. We will comment on this a little further in our concluding remarks. In Subsection 3.3 we explore the derived cyclic cohomology of the odd line extending Example 3.4. We will present derived cyclic -cocycles for regular foliations via their foliation Lie algebroid in Subsection 3.6. Further examples are left open for future work.
