# Branch A: tangential duality and the common scalar pairing of the two excess traces

## Result and scope

There is an explicit coefficient-level derived tangential comparison

\[
\vartheta_g:T\longrightarrow\mathcal D_{R/A}[2]
\]

which factors through the actual relative-interval quotient. In the ordered frames below,

\[
\vartheta_g\nu_E=\vartheta_g\nu_R=-\beta\,\mathbf u,
\qquad
\mathbf u\in\operatorname{Hom}_{D(R)}(A[2],\mathcal D_{R/A}[2])\cong A.
\]

Here \(\mathbf u\) is the positive six-variable Koszul evaluation. The comparison and its two essential dualizing components are polynomial; only its unit normalization requires \(\beta^{-1}\).

More generally, the entire ordinary coefficient-linear tangential mapping module is

\[
\mathscr H=\operatorname{Hom}_{D(R)}(T,\mathcal D_{R/A}[2])
\cong
A^3/\langle(x,\beta x,0),(v,0,\beta v)\rangle.
\]

For its coordinates \((a,b,c)\), evaluation on the two recorded traces is

\[
[\vartheta]\longmapsto(-\beta a+b+c)(1,1).
\]

Thus every such scalar-valued duality comparison kills the difference \([\nu_E]-[\nu_R]\). That difference remains nonzero in the trace mapping group. This is loss under a subsequent scalar readout, not failure of duality to preserve nonzero maps.

The result constructs an ordinary derived comparison in the existing coefficient category. It does not identify it with the physical reciprocal/tangential Pochhammer--Cousin operation or with the physical conductor--Morse class. In particular, the relative map has zero restrictions to both complete edge-endpoint packets; it is not an assertion of positive endpoint-normalized values on them.

## 1. Coefficients, lines, and target complexes

Retain

\[
A=\mathbb Z[\beta,x,X_{14},v],\qquad x=X_{03},\quad v=X_{25},\quad z=X_{35},
\]

\[
S=A[X_{02},X_{04},X_{24},X_{13},X_{15},X_{35}],
\]

\[
R=S/(X_iX_j:i\in\{02,04,24\},\ j\in\{13,15,35\}),\qquad A=R/I.
\]

The two descriptions of \(A\) agree: \(I\) is the full six-short-variable conductor ideal. This is the existing normalization ring, extended by the regulator and long-coordinate base. No mixed-sheet coordinate is inverted.

Use the ordered ambient volume

\[
\Omega_6=dX_{02}\wedge dX_{04}\wedge dX_{24}\wedge
 dX_{13}\wedge dX_{15}\wedge dX_{35}.
\]

All displayed scalar matrices are in the inherited ordered native-pair, interval, and polarity frames. Their abstract versions retain the corresponding dual coefficient lines. The ordered volume is used both in the ambient dualizing model and in the conductor-unit evaluation; there is no independent sign adjustment at the end.

The ten-state trace target is

\[
T=(P_E\otimes K_R(z))\otimes\mathfrak o_{02,35}[2],
\]

where

\[
dg=xp_{03}+vp_{25},\qquad dh_{03}=\beta xp_{03},\qquad dh_{25}=\beta vp_{25},
\]

\[
dk=z,\qquad \xi=h_{03}+h_{25}-\beta g,\qquad d\xi=0.
\]

After accounting for the displayed degree-two shift, \(p_{03},p_{25}\) are in homological degree two; \(g,h_{03},h_{25},p_{03}k,p_{25}k\) are in degree three; \(gk,h_{03}k,h_{25}k\) are in degree four.

The complete endpoint subcomplex is generated by the eight states on the two endpoints. Its quotient is

\[
q:T\longrightarrow\mathcal O=K_R(z)[3],
\qquad q(g)=e_0,\quad q(gk)=e_1,\quad de_1=-ze_0.
\]

The connecting map is retained:

\[
\kappa(e_0)=xp_{03}+vp_{25},\qquad
\kappa(e_1)=(xp_{03}+vp_{25})k.
\]

The closed inclusion determined by the whole interval cycle is

\[
j_\xi:\mathcal O\longrightarrow T,
\qquad j_\xi(e_0)=\xi,\quad j_\xi(e_1)=\xi k,
\qquad qj_\xi=-\beta\,\mathrm{id}.
\]

This inclusion retains both \(h_{03}\) and \(h_{25}\). None of the computations removes the companion endpoint before its support quotient is formed.

## 2. Retain the nonsplit relative dualizing object

Let

\[
\mathcal D_{R/A}=R\operatorname{Hom}_S(R,S\Omega_6)[6].
\]

Its previously constructed normalization triangle is

\[
B[3]\longrightarrow\mathcal D_{R/A}\longrightarrow A[1]
\longrightarrow B[4],
\qquad
B=\Omega^3_{R_-/A}\oplus\Omega^3_{R_+/A},
\]

with the polarity and branch determinant lines understood in the fixed ordered frames. The final arrow is the nonzero dual of the normalization difference. It is not replaced by a split direct sum.

The present calculation reconstructs the entire ambient free resolution \(P_R\) of \(R\), with ranks

\[
(1,9,18,15,6,1).
\]

For resolution degree \(n\ge1\), its basis consists of ordered pairs

\[
(I,J),\qquad \varnothing\ne I\subseteq\{02,04,24\},\quad
\varnothing\ne J\subseteq\{13,15,35\},\quad |I|+|J|=n+1.
\]

In degree one, \(d(i,j)=X_iX_j\). Above degree one, apply the truncated Koszul differential to \(I\), and to \(J\) with the extra sign \((-1)^{|I|-1}\). Both the original and dual differentials square to zero coefficient by coefficient.

Exactness is an arbitrary-polynomial statement: tensor the truncated regular-sequence resolutions of the two ideals in disjoint variables, identify their tensor with their product, and prepend \(S\). The ambient free modules are \(S\)-free, not \(R\)-free models of the dualizing object.

Let

\[
p_*=(02\wedge04\wedge24)\otimes(13\wedge15\wedge35),
\]

\[
p_{\widehat{35}}=(02\wedge04\wedge24)\otimes(13\wedge15).
\]

Their resolution degrees are five and four, and the specified differential contains

\[
dp_*=z\,p_{\widehat{35}}+\sum_{i\ne35}\epsilon_iX_i p_{\widehat i}.
\]

The plus sign on the displayed term is fixed by the ordered blocks.

## 3. The relative tangential comparison has two essential entries

The occurrence target has the 100-state ambient resolution

\[
P_R\otimes_S\mathcal O_S,
\]

with the resolution placed first in the tensor product. Finite adjunction identifies an \(R\)-derived map \(\mathcal O\to\mathcal D_{R/A}[2]\) with a map from this resolution to \(S\Omega_6[8]\).

Define that ambient functional by

\[
\vartheta_{\mathcal O}(p_*\otimes e_0)=\Omega_6,
\qquad
\vartheta_{\mathcal O}(p_{\widehat{35}}\otimes e_1)=-\Omega_6,
\]

and zero on all other states. It is a degree-zero chain map. The only possible top differential test is the degree-nine state \(p_*\otimes e_1\): its two contributing terms evaluate to \(z-z=0\).

Deleting the second entry gives the defect \(z\Omega_6\). This is why reading the conductor cohomology generator alone does not provide a chain representative into the complete dualizing complex.

The corresponding map on the whole interval is

\[
\vartheta_g=\vartheta_{\mathcal O}q:T\longrightarrow\mathcal D_{R/A}[2].
\]

It has the same two ambient entries, with \(e_0,e_1\) replaced by \(g,gk\), in the 500-state resolution \(P_R\otimes T_S\). It is zero on all 400 resolution-state copies of the complete endpoint packets.

The finite-adjunction tensor interchange is also checked explicitly. With \(\widehat p_*\) and \(\widehat p_{\widehat{35}}\) denoting the corresponding ambient dual basis states, currying uses the sign \((-1)^{|t||p|}\). Thus the displayed functional corresponds to

\[
g\longmapsto-\widehat p_*,\qquad
 gk\longmapsto-\widehat p_{\widehat{35}}.
\]

The target differential sends \(\widehat p_{\widehat{35}}\) to \(-z\widehat p_*\). Its chain equation therefore agrees with \(d(gk)=-zg\) modulo the retained endpoint terms. The sign of the conductor quotient is not adjusted independently of this interchange.

Replacing \(g,gk\) by \(h_{03},h_{03}k\), or by \(h_{25},h_{25}k\), constructs two more maps \(\vartheta_{03},\vartheta_{25}\). These two do not vanish on the corresponding endpoint packets.

## 4. Compute all tangential classes, not only this representative

Shift the dualizing triangle by two:

\[
B[5]\longrightarrow\mathcal D_{R/A}[2]\longrightarrow A[3]\longrightarrow B[6].
\]

The perfect source \(T\) has terms only in homological degrees two through four. Hence

\[
\operatorname{Hom}_{D(R)}(T,B[5])=0,
\qquad
\operatorname{Hom}_{D(R)}(T,B[6])=0.
\]

It follows that composition with the conductor truncation is an isomorphism

\[
\operatorname{Hom}_{D(R)}(T,\mathcal D_{R/A}[2])
\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{D(R)}(T,A[3]).
\]

This uses the triangle, not a splitting of \(\mathcal D_{R/A}\). It is a statement about morphism classes; no claim of contractibility of the whole mapping space is made.

Modulo the conductor, \(z=0\). A degree-zero map to \(A[3]\) has five potential coefficients on

\[
(g,h_{03},h_{25},p_{03}k,p_{25}k).
\]

Its chain equation on \(h_{03}k,h_{25}k\) forces the last two coefficients to zero, since \(\beta x\) and \(\beta v\) are nonzero-divisors in the base \(A\). The first three coefficients are unrestricted. A homotopy with values on \(p_{03},p_{25}\) changes them by

\[
(x,\beta x,0),\qquad(v,0,\beta v).
\]

Consequently the three displayed tangential maps generate the entire mapping module:

\[
\mathscr H\cong
A^3/\langle x(1,\beta,0),v(1,0,\beta)\rangle.
\]

The simultaneous global sign from the finite-adjunction interchange identifies the displayed basis with the negative conductor-truncation basis; it leaves this presentation unchanged.

The checker also supplies direct ambient homotopies for both relations. For the first, its only nonzero values are \(-1\) on \(p_*\otimes p_{03}\) and on \(p_{\widehat{35}}\otimes p_{03}k\). Its differential is

\[
x(\vartheta_g+\beta\vartheta_{03}).
\]

The second is the identical construction on the \(25\) endpoint and has differential \(v(\vartheta_g+\beta\vartheta_{25})\).

The restrictions to the two endpoint packets give the exact sequence

\[
0\longrightarrow A\vartheta_g\longrightarrow\mathscr H
\longrightarrow A/(\beta x)\oplus A/(\beta v)\longrightarrow0.
\]

This identifies the relative class without fitting its value on the traces. The relative group is \(A\vartheta_g\); its later normalization at nonzero \(\beta\) uses the existing interval factor.

## 5. Evaluate both trace maps on free ambient resolutions

Let \(K_S(\mathbf X)[2]\) be the 64-state ordered Koszul resolution of the conductor source. Its top generator is the negative triple followed by the positive triple, in the order of \(\Omega_6\).

The relative maps \(\epsilon_E,\epsilon_R:A[2]\to\mathcal O\) have the known low-degree formulas

\[
\epsilon_E(e_i)=X_i e_0\quad(i=13,15,35),
\]

\[
\epsilon_R(e_i)=X_i e_0\quad(i=13,15),\qquad \epsilon_R(e_{35})=0,
\]

\[
\epsilon_R(c_{i,35})=X_i e_1\quad(i=13,15).
\]

The first comparison from the ambient Koszul source to the existing conductor resolution sends a mixed exterior pair \((i,j)\), with \(i\) negative and \(j\) positive, to \(m_{i,j}-m_{j,i}\). Same-sheet pairs map to their Koszul relation generator. Exactness of the source resolution extends this comparison to higher degrees; the two displayed trace targets only use the specified low-degree columns. This fixes which trace classes are being evaluated.

The checker lifts these maps to \(P_R\otimes\mathcal O_S\), matching their original augmentations. The full lifts have respectively 52 and 46 nonzero entries. All 64 source chain equations hold over the ambient polynomial ring \(S\), where mixed monomials must NOT be set to zero.

On the source top exterior generator, the two values are

\[
\widetilde\epsilon_E(e_{02,04,24,13,15,35})=p_*\otimes e_0,
\]

\[
\widetilde\epsilon_R(e_{02,04,24,13,15,35})=-p_{\widehat{35}}\otimes e_1.
\]

Therefore

\[
\vartheta_{\mathcal O}\widetilde\epsilon_E
=\vartheta_{\mathcal O}\widetilde\epsilon_R
=\mathbf u,
\]

where \(\mathbf u\) is the ordered six-variable Koszul evaluation. The first trace is detected by the first dualizing entry; the second is detected by the second entry. Removing that correction would change one scalar value and violate the chain equation.

The full traces are obtained from the actual closed interval cycle:

\[
\nu_E=j_\xi\epsilon_E,\qquad\nu_R=j_\xi\epsilon_R.
\]

Their ambient lifts have 156 and 138 nonzero entries. Both endpoint-normal contributions remain in these maps. Direct evaluation gives

\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 &\nu_E&\nu_R\\\hline
\vartheta_g&-\beta&-\beta\\
\vartheta_{03}&1&1\\
\vartheta_{25}&1&1.
\end{array}
\]

Every entry is the coefficient of \(\mathbf u\), not an unrestricted residue obtained by dividing out conductor factors. All composite maps agree literally on the full ambient source resolution, not only in cohomology.

## 6. Universal scalar blindness to the conormal difference

For any class represented by \((a,b,c)\),

\[
\vartheta\nu_E=\vartheta\nu_R=(-\beta a+b+c)\mathbf u.
\]

The row \((-\beta,1,1)\) annihilates both presentation relations, so this is a well-defined calculation on all tangential morphism classes.

The scalar coordinate splits off integrally. With

\[
\sigma=\vartheta_{03},\qquad
\kappa_1=\vartheta_g+\beta\vartheta_{03},\qquad
\kappa_2=\vartheta_{25}-\vartheta_{03},
\]

one obtains

\[
\mathscr H\cong A\sigma\oplus
\frac{A\kappa_1\oplus A\kappa_2}
 {\langle x\kappa_1,\ v\kappa_1+\beta v\kappa_2\rangle}.
\]

Only \(\sigma\) contributes to scalar evaluation of the two traces. At invertible \(\beta\), the scalar-zero part becomes \(A[\beta^{-1}]/(x)\oplus A[\beta^{-1}]/(v)\); neither long coordinate is inverted in stating that result.

On the relative submodule, the scalar functional is \(-\beta\). Thus

\[
(-\beta)^{-1}\vartheta_g\nu_E
=(-\beta)^{-1}\vartheta_g\nu_R=\mathbf u
\]

at fixed nonzero regulator. The raw comparison remains defined at \(\beta=0\), where its relative scalar evaluation is zero.

The previous checked trace group contains the nonzero primitive difference \([\nu_E]-[\nu_R]\). Its finite normalization readout is a primitive \(X_{35}\)-conormal-dual class in the adjacent grade. The scalar calculation here proves

\[
\vartheta(\nu_E-\nu_R)=0
\]

for EVERY ordinary \(R\)-linear derived \(\vartheta:T\to\mathcal D_{R/A}[2]\), not just the constructed relative one. No other choice in this mapping module can restore that lost coordinate.

This does not contradict biduality. Dualizing \(\nu_E-\nu_R\) gives a nonzero morphism \(\mathbb D_R(T)\to A[-2]\). The calculation says that its induced scalar evaluation on the tested degree-two cohomology elements is zero. The nonzero relation-level morphism is not determined by that cohomology readout.

## 7. Endpoint and physical scope

The 500-state ambient target resolution contains both 200-state endpoint resolutions. The relative quotient, both connecting components, and the \(\xi\)-inclusion are checked before evaluation. The relative tangential map is identically zero on those endpoint complexes. Thus its compatibility with their support triangle is explicit, but it must not be called a positive endpoint-normalized trace.

The two nonrelative generators \(\vartheta_{03},\vartheta_{25}\) have nonzero endpoint restrictions, respectively in \(A/(\beta x)\) and \(A/(\beta v)\). Their scalar values alone do not choose the physical boundary convention.

The construction uses no Laurent localization in short coordinates. It does not pretend that the tangential Laurent ring of Entry 97 is a nonzero base change of the glued normalization ring. The regulator family is the already stipulated coefficient model; no geometric purity theorem at \(\beta=0\) is inferred.

The coefficient-level arrow to the full relative dualizing complex is now constructed. A scalar such arrow necessarily loses the conormal/source-relation distinction between the two traces. A physical comparison that is required to retain that distinction must keep the two-grade normalization output or the complete dualized relation-level map. Neither the common scalar \(+1\) nor the existence of the relative arrow identifies the physical \(\Delta_J\).
