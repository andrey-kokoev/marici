# Rigidity away from a spare switch

Assume the reflected ten-visit path from midpoint A to its adjacent midpoint D in the folded prism and the literal gates on its lifted return. Delete the outward path (both fixed poles included after their incident gate edges are prescribed). Check return visit 4 or 7 against the swap. Orient the argument from the checked end, reversing and interchanging endpoint names if needed. The first three gate-to-checkpoint edges are forced by girth eight.

Each folded four-corner rim lifts to one eight-cycle: a split into two four-cycles would contradict girth. Every rim used by the outward path therefore has had at least one vertex deleted and contains no cycle. At each visited midpoint only its return copy is available. A and D cannot serve as internal crossings because their available copies are already the prescribed gates. After deleting the poles, the fixed spine connects B and C by a five-edge path through their four fixed attachments; it has no alternative outlet if either midpoint is forbidden.

**One interior switch V (B or C).** Forbid both copies of the spare U. The fixed spine is now a dead end, and a simple return can change rims only at the one available copy of V. It must cross: staying on one rim would require eight distinct corner visits there, but the outward path already used corners on each lifted eight-cycle. The known short side fixes the rim of departure (or, in reverse, arrival), since the switch in the intended route occurs at visit 5 or 6. With exactly one crossing and known V, both rim runs now have prescribed lifted endpoints. The rim graphs are forests, so neither admits two different paths between those endpoints. The return is exactly the reflected one.

**Both interior switches.** Normalize A–B–C–D; the checked short side fixes the first switch B at visit 4 (C in reverse). Six edges remain to D. The fixed-spine route from B to C takes five, leaving only one to D, impossible between distinct midpoints. Skipping C also fails: the B-to-D rim route would require four corner edges from b to d. Those columns are opposite in the square: in the lifted eight-cycle a simple four-edge segment ends back in the *same* column, not the opposite one. Thus it must use C. Each leg B–C and C–D takes at least three edges, so both must take exactly three. The incoming rim at B is already prescribed, its previous corner cannot be reused, and the intervening corner-to-corner links are forced by incidence. This leaves only the intended return. This proof uses the moving prism plus its fixed spine, not a census of corridors.

Scope: any alternative passing a checkpoint must therefore visit a spare switch. This alone does not say that every such visit must use its fixed neighbor: a return might try to traverse the spare switch as a normal rim crossing instead.
