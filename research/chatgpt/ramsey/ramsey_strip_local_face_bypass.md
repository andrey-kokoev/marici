# Local spare-switch test (distinct corners, no turn-word table)

In the folded prism with adjacent endpoint midpoints A,D, let U be an unused interior midpoint. A ten-visit route using a single interior switch V has U,V=B,C in some order: they are adjacent columns. A four-edge bypass through the fixed neighbor of U requires both rim corners u0,u1 on the intended return. If one is missing, the bypass has no attachment on that rim.

Conversely suppose both occur. From u0 to v0 on the first rim, the path between the adjacent columns must use one edge, not three: a three-edge arc would occupy the entire four-corner rim run, but the run also starts at column A, distinct from both. Similarly v1 to u1 must use one edge because the final run ends at column D. (Exchange the two rim labels if necessary.) Thus the return necessarily contains u0-v0-V-v1-u1. Add the two folded edges u1-U-u0 to obtain one lateral hexagon. Its lift cannot close in six steps, since the cage has girth eight. Hence the two lift copies of U adjacent to the ends of the four-edge arc are distinct. Both attach to the same fixed neighbor U*, giving a new arc u0-U-U*-U'-u1 of exactly four edges. Its interior is disjoint from both paths because U was unused and a reflected strip avoids fixed interior positions. The two lifted arcs form an octagon. This proves the local iff for **this detour mechanism**, without checking any return-path candidates.

The three replaced visits are centered at V. The two rim runs contain seven corners (ten visits minus A,V,D), split 3+4 or 4+3, so V occurs at visit 5 or 6. A switch at 5 changes visits 4–6 (checking 7 misses the detour); a switch at 6 changes visits 5–7 (checking 4 misses it). With both B,C used there is no spare switch. This argument constructs and places the ambiguity; necessity against *unrelated* alternative corridors still uses the separate five-shape certificate.
