# Why eight checks suffice: working conjecture net

Iteration 1. Domain: strict 16-site hosts (no red triangle, no blue six) with aligned side exchange. Pairwise extension checks are exact. Exclude joint red triangles.

- C0: Every residual blue seven has a transversal detector (some blue seven using one slot of each new pair). Verified by the earlier strict census.
- C1: No residual blue seven uses a doubled pair. Stronger than C0; possible explanation was failure of doubled slots to find enough old common blue neighbors.
- C2: No blue subset of the six new slots can both double a pair and meet the other two pairs. Stronger than C1. Exhaustive mask test: among all 597 eligible assignments over the 680 aligned catalogue hosts, zero such subsets, even before checking old neighbors. Thus old common-neighbor scarcity at this final stage is not needed. C2 => C1 => C0 given pairwise legality.
- Next discriminator: doubling an internally blue pair while selecting a slot in another requires their red block to be empty (by side exchange). Is an internally blue pair always incident to a nonempty block towards at least one other pair, or do two empty blocks occur but the remaining pair of occupied slots is forced red? Test block inventory; retain distinction between finite classification and a structural implication from strict-host hypotheses.

Evidence: `ramsey_doubled_capacity.mjs`, `ramsey_doubled_capacity.json`; run `node research/chatgpt/ramsey/ramsey_reserved_blind.mjs --doubled-capacity` from the root.

Iteration 2. E0 (false): the center type alone cannot have two distinct legal empty-block partners. Of 56,990 internally blue type occurrences across aligned hosts, 3,220 have at least two, taken separately. E1 (finite verified): once all three pairwise restrictions must be legal, an internally blue center has at most one empty incident block. Zero two-empty centers across all 15,469 assignments, before testing the three-way red triangle or blue seven. The 597 joint red-triangle-free assignments contain 118 one-empty center occurrences, ruling out the stronger no-empty claim. E1 => C2 because both center slots must be blue-adjacent to a chosen slot in each other pair, which by exchange requires both incident blocks empty. Next: for each open wedge with two empty arms, classify why each of the four closing masks (empty/straight/crossed/full) fails the outer two-pair test; distinguish common versus alternative certificates and include repeated partner types. Iteration 3. E2 (stronger than E1; census verified): every two-arm wedge has an outer slot pair u,v for which N_old(u) and N_old(v) intersect and their union misses an old independent five. Thus red uv makes a red triangle and blue uv a blue seven, independent of how the other entries of the closing block are set. 59,343 wedges including 15,053 repeated-type cases, all with this unary conflict. Empty always fails blue-only; full always fails red-only; matching masks exhibit both mechanisms. The universal closing obstruction needs no sign-product argument. Next discriminator: fix the center and form, for each eligible outer slot, its set of old red neighbors and the old independent fives it misses. Does legality of the two empty arms give a common red neighbor and common missed five by a bound on these sets (rather than inspecting each wedge)? Separate host strictness from center compatibility with controls. Checker/result `ramsey_arm_closing_audit.mjs/.json`, driver `--arm-closing`.

Iteration 6. E5 (false): two signatures suffice at an exceptional center. General lower bound: when neither signature is universal, take a partner missing each; the pair cannot share either.
