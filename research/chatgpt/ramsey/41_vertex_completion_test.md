# A centered 41-state completion and an exact obstruction to its square readout

Date: 2026-11-09.

## Result and scope

This investigation tests the proposed route of constructing a completed object on 41 vertices and obtaining a Ramsey-avoiding graph by puncturing it at a structurally distinguished center. It does not repair Exoo's 39-vertex graph and does not assume that a hypothetical 40-vertex witness is 9-regular.

A concrete algebraic carrier has 41 states. Its local square readouts give a family of 3^40 distinct labeled graphs. Every member has a triangle-free, 8-regular 40-vertex puncture. An exact finite branching certificate proves that every such puncture has an independent set of ten vertices. Consequently no completed member has a Ramsey-admissible single-vertex puncture, at its center or anywhere else.

This excludes one proposed construction family, not arbitrary 41-vertex completions and not arbitrary 40-vertex Ramsey graphs. No proof of R(3,10)=41 was obtained. No novelty priority is asserted for the finite-family exclusion.

The choices of the field, dimension, projective quotient, alternating form and square readout are explicit hypotheses, not conclusions already derived from the original R/B/G source. In particular, this is a candidate translation of that source, not a claim to have recovered its unique native interpretation.

## 1. What a successful 41-vertex completion must accomplish

For a red/blue coloring C of K_41, let F(C) be the family consisting of all red triangles and all blue ten-cliques. Define

    P(C) = intersection of all sets in F(C).

The published upper bound R(3,10)<=41 ensures that F(C) is nonempty. A vertex c belongs to P(C) if and only if C-c contains neither a red triangle nor a blue ten-clique. This follows directly by separating forbidden configurations containing c from those avoiding c.

Thus a completed object must localize every forbidden configuration at its admissible puncture. Merely having 41 vertices, or being a closed simplicial object, does not establish this property.

If a color-preserving automorphism sends c to another vertex, that other vertex is also in P(C). Every forbidden configuration must contain the entire automorphism orbit of c. Therefore such an orbit has at most ten vertices, and at most three when a red triangle exists. A vertex-transitive coloring on 41 vertices cannot have an admissible puncture: its forbidden configurations have fewer than 41 vertices.

The relevant symmetry requirement is therefore a distinguished center, not transitivity of the entire completed coloring. This statement concerns color-preserving graph symmetries; it does not identify the earlier r/s path directions with Ramsey edge colors.

## 2. Generate the centered carrier before any Ramsey graph

Let V=F_3^4. Identify x with -x and retain the zero orbit:

    C_states = V / (x ~ -x).

There is one zero orbit and (3^4-1)/2=40 nonzero orbits, so this carrier has 41 states. Its distinguished point c is [0]. The other states are the projective points of PG(3,3).

The quotient is a substantive choice. If opposite orientations must remain distinct Ramsey vertices, V has 81 states, not 41. Signed representatives or construction histories can be retained in a separate record, but that does not prove that the original problem licenses identifying their visible vertex states. Point reflection acts trivially on the quotient; this is not the literal model of twenty distinct antipodal pairs.

There is a local, representative-independent unordered operation:

    ([x],[y]) -> { [x+y], [x-y] }.

Changing either representative's sign exchanges the two outputs or changes their signs, so the unordered output is well defined. For independent x,y the four directions [x],[y],[x+y],[x-y] constitute their projective line. A single-valued addition on these quotient states is not being asserted.

Restrict the four-point line cells using the alternating form

    omega(x,y)=x1*y3+x2*y4-x3*y1-x4*y2 mod 3.

A line is admitted when omega(x,y)=0 for its generating independent pair. This gives 40 four-point lines; every direction lies on four of them. Each pair of distinct points lies on at most one admitted line. The construction and these counts are regenerated by the independent verifier.

This is the symplectic generalized-quadrangle incidence geometry W(3,3). Use of generalized quadrangles to build triangle-free Ramsey-related graphs is established mathematics; the method itself is not a new discovery here.

## 3. A complete family of local square readouts

For each admitted line L with four points, choose one of its three perfect matchings to omit. Color its other four pairs red. They form a cycle C4. Color all pairs not on an admitted line blue.

Let H be the resulting red graph on the 40 directions. The 40 local choices are completely independent inputs to the search: no cyclic pattern, regularity condition, specific orientation or matching symmetry is imposed. There are exactly 3^40 different labeled H because each line's six pairs occur on no other line.

For every choice, each vertex has two red neighbors on each of its four incident lines. Hence

    |V(H)|=40, degree_H(v)=8, |E(H)|=160.

Regularity is a consequence of this particular local square rule, not a theorem that all Ramsey witnesses are regular.

### Triangle-freeness follows from the geometry

Every triangle in the collinearity graph lies entirely on one admitted line. One proof is linear algebra: three pairwise omega-orthogonal independent vectors would span a three-dimensional totally isotropic subspace of the nondegenerate four-dimensional symplectic space, whose maximal isotropic dimension is two. Therefore three mutually collinear projective points lie on one line. The verifier also checks the assertion directly on all triples.

The selected C4 on a line contains no triangle. Thus H has no red triangle, for every global choice.

Now define the 41-vertex coloring C by joining the distinguished origin c to every direction in red. Equivalently, form the simplicial cone c*H, viewing H as a one-dimensional simplicial complex. This gives 41 vertices, 200 red edges and 160 triangular faces, all incident to c.

The cone is a simplicial complex, not a closed triangulated manifold. Nothing in this test promotes it to one. Its purpose is to provide an actual centered completion and test whether its puncture meets the Ramsey conditions.

A blue ten-clique in C is exactly an independent ten-set of H. Therefore c is an admissible puncture exactly when alpha(H)<=9.

## 4. The exact exclusion

The supplied certificate proves:

    Every choice of one C4 on each of the 40 admitted lines
    leaves an independent ten-set in H.

This covers all

    3^40 = 12,157,665,459,056,928,801

labeled choices without individually listing every graph.

### The local obstruction clauses

Fix a ten-element vertex set I. For a pair of its vertices on an admitted line, avoiding their red edge requires a specific omitted matching on that line. If I demands incompatible matchings on one line, it can never be independent and supplies no clause. Otherwise, collect the required choices into a partial assignment A_I.

Then I is independent precisely when all requirements in A_I hold. An avoiding graph must violate at least one of these requirements. This is a finite-domain clause, derived directly from the actual vertex incidences, not a separately chosen coherence axiom.

The final certificate retains 3,448 such ten-set clauses. Every clause has its actual ten vertices attached. The independent verifier reconstructs every collinear pair of these vertices and checks that the clause gives exactly the matchings that would make them independent.

### The branching certificate

Each line variable has the three matching choices. A clause forbids one simultaneous assignment on its participating lines. Propagation has only two rules:

- If all but one required choices are fixed, delete the last required choice from that line's domain.
- If all required choices are fixed, report a contradiction.

Otherwise branch on one unresolved line, covering every remaining choice. The retained tree has 27,903 nodes and 14,278 contradiction leaves. Its root has all three choices available at all forty lines. The verifier checks every permitted child, every propagation and every leaf, and ensures that every recorded node is reached exactly once.

The final verifier uses only Python's standard library and integer arithmetic. Z3 was used to discover a useful clause family, but no Z3 answer is needed to validate the delivered exclusion. The independent C++ search and Python verifier implement different propagation schedules.

A bounded local search also found a square-choice graph with 1,480 independent ten-sets and 32 independent eleven-sets. That graph is a rejected example, not a witness or a claimed optimum.

## 5. The completed objects fail at every possible puncture

The result is stronger than failure at the origin in this family.

Let I be an independent ten-set of H. Since H is 8-regular, exactly 80 edges have one endpoint in I. There are no edges inside I, so 80 of H's 160 edges remain in H-I. Choose one such edge uv.

Then C contains disjoint forbidden configurations:

    red triangle {c,u,v},
    blue ten-clique I.

Deleting any single vertex leaves at least one of them intact. Thus P(C) is empty for every completed member of the square family.

For the displayed rejected example, with point labels given in `symplectic_geometry.json`, the certificates are:

    red triangle: 40, 1, 6;
    blue ten-clique: 0, 2, 4, 5, 9, 15, 24, 25, 27, 30.

The origin has label 40. The verifier checks disjointness and every required edge color against the supplied 41-vertex adjacency masks.

## 6. Removing the square restriction

A maximal triangle-free graph on four labeled vertices is either one of the three C4s or one of the four stars K_(1,3). Every triangle-free subgraph of a K4 extends to one of these seven graphs.

Because every triangle of the symplectic collinearity graph is contained in one line, linewise extensions cannot introduce a cross-line triangle. Thus seven local patterns per line suffice to cover every maximal triangle-free subgraph of this 40-vertex host. Any Ramsey-avoiding subgraph could be extended to such a maximal one, since adding edges cannot increase its independence number.

This expanded model need not be regular. Its degree-at-most-nine constraint is necessary, not an extra conjecture: ten red neighbors in a triangle-free graph would be an independent ten-set.

A bounded search of this enlarged model produced one checked graph with 156 edges, degree distribution

    6^1 7^9 8^27 9^3,

and 7,170 independent ten-sets. It uses four star lines and 36 square lines. It is not a Ramsey witness.

The next solver iteration exceeded the process budget. The expanded seven-pattern family has NOT been excluded or solved. The receipt explicitly records an incomplete search. The square-family branching proof does not cover it.

## 7. Meaning for the original conjecture

The 41-state viewpoint can generate a genuinely distinguished center and local incidence structure before any 39-vertex graph is supplied. It does not by itself give the required avoidance theorem.

For this tested readout, all local square choices are compatible with the incidence geometry, all degrees match, and triangle-freeness is automatic. Nonetheless the global independent-set requirement fails for every choice. This is an actual Ramsey local-to-global obstruction within a specified compositional family.

The failure cannot be repaired merely by choosing different squares, imposing a more elaborate symmetry on the same square choices, or choosing a different puncture. A successful construction in this geometry must leave the square-only family. The star-enriched family is one precise relaxation; changing the host geometry is another. No claim is made that either will work.

More fundamentally, the original source still needs to justify its vertex interpretation and local incidence law. The numerical identity 1+(3^4-1)/2=41 is a consequence of chosen parameters, not a derivation of the Ramsey value.

## Reproduction

From this directory:

    python verify_geometry.py

This checks the geometry, all obstruction clauses, the entire branching certificate and the displayed 41-vertex example. It does not need a SAT package, a network connection or a floating-point optimizer.

To regenerate the branching tree:

    c++ -O3 -std=c++17 ternary_refute.cpp -o ternary_refute
    ./ternary_refute geometry_core.txt 120 geometry_refutation.tree
    python verify_geometry.py

A timed-out tree generation does not produce a refutation. The positive completed tree is already included.

Discovery scripts are optional. `z3mini.py` accesses an installed Z3 shared library through its documented C API. `geometry_sat.py` performs counterexample-guided square-family search; `extract_geometry_core.py` extracts a discovery core; `geometry7_sat.py` tests the strictly larger family. None is required to check the final theorem.
