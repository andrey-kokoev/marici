# Why a common cyclic frame forces cross-layer completion

Use the verified ten-cycle layer model, indices modulo 10:
C_i--C_(i+1), C_i--M_i--D_i, M_i--M_(i+5), D_i--D_(i+3).
The reflected row exchange can be indexed as i -> -i in each layer. It exchanges the two alternating matchings on C. Rotation rho: i -> i+2 has order five, preserves incidence type, and preserves each alternating matching on C.

## Sufficiency
Match every M_i to D_i. This residual matching is preserved by both rho and reflection. Therefore adding either alternating matching on C gives a pair with a common order-five subgroup.

## Necessity
Suppose both full matchings have the same order-five symmetry subgroup. The subgroup preserves their symmetric difference C as a set. Its restriction to the ten-cycle is faithful: an automorphism fixing C pointwise fixes each unique external neighbor M_i and then each D_i. An order-five subgroup of the automorphism group of a ten-cycle must be the rotation subgroup generated by a two-step rotation. The unique-spoke extension therefore makes it exactly <rho> on the entire cage.

Now classify rho-invariant residual matchings without a full matching census. Middle-layer edges M_i--M_(i+5) form one orbit of five. If one is used, all are used, and the outer ten-cycle must supply one of its two alternating matchings. Reflection exchanges those two outer matchings, so neither residual filling is row-invariant.

If no middle-layer edge is used, every M_i must be matched to D_i: its only available residual neighbor besides its middle-layer partner. This is the cross-layer matching. Hence it is the only residual matching preserved by both the row reflection and a common order-five frame.

This proves the equivalence conditional only on the three-layer model, not on enumeration of the 288 perfect matchings or the choice of literal poles.
