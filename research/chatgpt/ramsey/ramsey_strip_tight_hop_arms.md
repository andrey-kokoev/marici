# Tight spine hop: which shortest arms survive?

Orient from the gate adjacent to the spare U. Call that gate A, the far gate D, and the used switch V; in cyclic column order they are A,U,V,D. Reversal only exchanges 4 and 7. The tight arm lengths 3,2,4 put U,U*,U' at visits 4,5,6. Thus the hop cannot pass check 4 (which expects a corner). To pass 7, the far arm must agree with the intended return from visit 7 through D, by uniqueness of the three-edge path. In particular visit 7 is a u corner. The intended V cannot be at 6, since its next corner would be v, not u. Hence V is at 5. The intended near rim run is a,v-opposite intermediate,v: either a-u-v or a-d-v. The intended far run starts v-u and continues to D. The same bit at visit 7 fixes the far copy U', the fixed hop, and the near copy U.

Compare the two possible intended near runs. For a-u-v, the four-edge switch detour around the U,V face is available: its near arm stays on the intended near rim, and the fixed hop cancels the lateral hexagon's sheet change. For a-d-v, the path around the other side of the near square reverses the arriving copy of v for a fixed gate. Equivalently, to match the same far suffix, it needs the *opposite gate copy* from a-u-v. An ordinary shortest near arm cannot accommodate this. For fixed U copy its two possible A-to-U arms (one per rim) lead to opposite A copies, because together they project around the A,U lateral hexagon. The arm on the far rim is unavailable: from far-side u at visit 7 the forced far suffix goes u-a-d (not u-v-d, which repeats the v corner just used by the intended path). The outward path occupies the opposite lifts of this u-a edge. A far-rim near arm would use that opposite edge, since the hop interchanges U copies, so it collides. Only the near-rim arm survives. Therefore the near turn a-u-v is necessary as well as sufficient. With orientation reversed the same proof applies to check 4 instead. This closes the geometric exclusion of all checkpoint-preserving impostors without relying on the five-case midpoint census.
