# Directional path coherence, finite capacity, and Ramsey obstructions

**Consolidated research note — 2026-11-09**  
**Status:** Exact finite constructions, several proved elementary structural results and independently checked algebraic certificates; the proposed universal Ramsey generator remains conjectural.  
**Scope:** The development from paired simplex trees and directional words through cyclic coherence, simultaneous extension witnesses, rank-16 compatibility, and thirteen-point Steiner systems. The subsequently clarified *closed-circle-under-gluing* requirement is recorded explicitly as an unresolved gate.

## 1. Central research question

Can the Ramsey threshold \(R(l,k)\) be characterized as the first cardinality at which a system of endpoint-typed words, generated by two directional operations \(r,s\), cannot be extended while preserving its *admitted, witnessed coherence*?

The proposed explanation is stronger than a reformulation of forbidden monochromatic cliques. It must:

1. Generate the path system and its valid gluings from one fixed local operation grammar.
2. Retain distinct traversal words and distinct coherence histories, even when their endpoint transformations agree.
3. Produce—not insert post hoc—the admissibility criterion for a comparison witness.
4. Enforce a global **closed-circle condition** on completed glued traversals, independently of permissible gluing order, in a precisely specified sense.
5. Derive the Ramsey thresholds as the limits of *jointly realizable* extension, not as failures of a particular greedy construction.

At present, item 4 was **not enforced in the earlier path and Steiner experiments**, and item 3 is the principal unsolved bridge from source operations to Ramsey constraints.

## 2. Objects that must not be conflated

Let \(\Sigma\) be the alphabet of endpoint code letters. A directed primitive \(e\) has declared source and target letters \(\operatorname{src}(e),\operatorname{tgt}(e)\in\Sigma\). A word \(p=e_1\cdots e_j\) is a **typed path** only if

\[
\operatorname{tgt}(e_i)=\operatorname{src}(e_{i+1})\quad(1\le i<j).
\]

For complete words \(p,q\), the composite \(pq\) is admitted at the typing level only if \(\operatorname{tgt}(p)=\operatorname{src}(q)\). It is **closed** if \(\operatorname{src}(p)=\operatorname{tgt}(p)\).

Distinguish:

- **Path occurrence:** the entire ordered, endpoint-typed history. `rs` and `sr` are different words even if they evaluate to the same transformation.
- **Endpoint transformation:** the action of a word on a position or code state. Equality of its final positions does not equate the words.
- **Coherence witness:** a separate object \(N:p\Rightarrow q\) for parallel paths, when *admitted*. It is not a quotient identifying \(p\) and \(q\).
- **Higher witness:** an object between histories of coherence witnesses, e.g. \(NN:(N_{qr}\circ N_{pq})\Rightarrow N_{pr}\). It also retains both histories.
- **Ramsey graph:** a separate finite readout of pairwise relations. A graph edge is not intrinsically an \(r\)- or \(s\)-step unless a source-derived translation has been established.
- **Incidence carrier:** a block or higher simplex organizing possible relations. Its existence need not mean that it supports a closed path using the primitive operations.

Earlier four-leg signatures such as `RRBB` or `RBGG` specify only immediate surroundings; they do **not** determine the paths entering, leaving, and returning through a node. The global path environment is essential. In particular, a literal complete graph on six nodes has degree five, not four. A four-leg, six-node network must not be called \(K_6\).

### 2.1 The newly identified closure requirement

Previous experiments checked endpoint composability and some selected return cycles, but **not** the requested invariant: completed path systems must form closed circles regardless of admissible gluing order.

There are two non-equivalent interpretations that must be kept explicit until the intended one is fixed:

- **Cyclic-assembly requirement:** a declared complete gluing \(p_1\cdots p_t\) is admitted only when all consecutive endpoint letters match *including the last-to-first junction*. This is a necessary typing condition but, taken alone, does not restrict growth: one may simply decline nonclosing gluings.
- **Universal coherent-completion requirement:** every gluing in a specified permitted family must admit a closing continuation or closing comparison, and different gluing orders must have admitted coherence witnesses relating the completed circles. This is substantive and could restrict extensions. It must specify which gluings and continuations are demanded; otherwise arbitrary new return paths make it vacuous.

A still stronger interpretation—*every intermediate prefix must itself be a closed path*—would exclude ordinary primitive traversals and is not assumed here.

For a fixed list of codeword pieces, the stronger requirement might be formalized by a family \(\mathcal G\) of admissible gluing orders, closed traversal objects \(C_g\) for every \(g\in\mathcal G\), and admitted witnesses \(\kappa_{g,h}:C_g\Rightarrow C_h\) compatible with further gluing. None of the prior enumeration scripts checked this whole package.

## 3. Starting cases and the extension obstruction

The standard critical graphs (largest avoiding instances) have respectively 5 and 8 vertices at \(R(3,3)=6\) and \(R(3,4)=9\): the five-cycle \(C_5\) and, among the 8-vertex examples, the symmetric Wagner graph \(W_8\).

The initial two opposing three-member trees are a **proposed path model**, not automatically identical to the six-point Ramsey threshold. Adding a new node by cutting two connections produces four exposed ends. Their symbols alone do not determine whether the splice is legal: the preexisting paths, constraints and possible return traversals must be preserved. A locally balanced node can be globally incompatible.

An extension failure of one graph is also not a Ramsey threshold. For example, \(K_{3,3}\) is triangle-free and has independence number three, so it is admissible for the \(R(3,4)\) avoidance conditions on six vertices, but cannot be extended by one vertex with all old edges fixed. Admissible eight-vertex graphs nevertheless exist.

## 4. Cyclic directional systems: what rotation and inversion actually force

For \(2m\) positions, the original model is

\[
r(i)=i+1\pmod{2m},\qquad s(i)=i+m\pmod{2m}=r^m(i).
\]

The transformation group is \(C_{2m}\). Central inversion \(s\) has order two and is **not independent** of \(r\). The three labels \(r,s,rs\) denote distinguishable directional actions, but not three algebraically independent generators.

The endpoint displacement of a word containing \(a\) letters \(r\) and \(b\) letters \(s\) is

\[
\delta(p)=a+mb\pmod{2m}.
\]

A word closes at every starting position exactly when \(\delta(p)=0\). Because the action is abelian, **reordering the same collection of letters preserves endpoint closure**. This is order-independent endpoint closure only; it does not produce a coherence witness between the reordered words.

In particular, the \(m+1\) orderings of \(m\) copies of \(r\) and one \(s\) are all closed. The first basic comparison can be recorded as

\[
N_i:r^m\Rightarrow s
\]

from \(i\) to its antipode, and requires a separately admitted return witness

\[
U_i:s_i s_{i+m}\Rightarrow e_i.
\]

The first comparison boundary has \(m+1\) sides. The closed return uses the two traversals and an actual \(U_i\) witness; the group equality \(s^2=e\) alone does not identify their free histories.

| Positions \(2m\) | Rotation edges (undirected) | Antipodal edges | Basic comparison length | N boundary occurrences | U occurrences under full rotation closure |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 8 | 8 | 4 | 5 | 8 | 8 |
| 16 | 16 | 8 | 9 | 16 | 16 |
| 32 | 32 | 16 | 17 | 32 | 32 |
| 64 | 64 | 32 | 33 | 64 | 64 |
| 128 | 128 | 64 | 65 | 128 | 128 |

The underlying undirected graph is \(C_{2m}(1,m)\), with \(3m\) edges, triangle-free for the tested sizes \(2m\ge6\). Its independence number grows too quickly to represent the larger known Ramsey-critical graphs. Symmetry closure by itself does not impose the Ramsey threshold.

### 4.1 Corrected higher-boundary count

The early \(3,7,15,31,63\) sequence counted independent relations after adjoining only \(m\) return witnesses \(U_i\) for \(2m\) positions. That selection is **not closed under rotation**: rotating one \(U_i\) produces its antipodal counterpart, retaining a distinct history.

With all \(2m\) directed \(U\)-occurrences and all \(2m\) proposed \(N\)-occurrences, the 2-cell boundary matrix has rank \(2m+1\), with \(4m\) proposed 2-cells. Its formal kernel therefore has rank

\[
4m-(2m+1)=2m-1.
\]

For eight positions the corrected number is **7**, not 3. This is a count of independent **closed 2-chains** in the stipulated cell complex, not proof that NN fillings exist. The earlier candidate tetrahedron accounted for only three classes and was not a complete rotation-closed construction.

The overlap \(sss\) has two distinct reduction histories, \((ss)s\) and \(s(ss)\), giving a genuine local **NN question**. Constructing its witness requires an admitted comparison operation; it does not follow merely from the equality of their evaluated endpoints.

## 5. The matrix path-counting experiments

The \(4\times4\) table \(r^is^j\) has 16 word occurrences but only 8 distinct \(C_8\) transformations. Treating its positions as vertices produces a \(3\times3\) array of internal squares; treating its entries as cells produces a \(5\times5\) enclosing vertex grid. These are different incidence constructions, **not** counts of distinct traversal words.

For monotone \(r,s\) paths from one corner of an \(n\times n\) grid to the other, the exact count is \(\binom{2n-2}{n-1}\). For a \(3\times(k-1)\) grid, it is \(\binom{k}{2}\). Thus \(k=8,9\) give 28 and 36, but \(k=7\) gives 21 instead of \(R(3,7)=23\); the same rule also fails other parameters. Treating \(rs\) as an independent diagonal step changes these to Delannoy counts and does not recover the Ramsey sequence.

On the actual six-arrow Marici source, the reflection-pair counts of words of lengths 2, 3, 4, 5 were 5, 8, 13, 21. The first three coincide numerically with critical graph orders \(5,8,13\), while 21 differs from the \(R(3,6)\) critical order 17. These are counted words, not a proof of a bijection to graph vertices.

## 6. Source-relative coherence is not history equality

Marici's original seed has endpoint letters \(A,B,C,D\) and six occurrences

`AB, BC, CA, AD, DB, BA`.

In that system, \(AB\) and \(AD\cdot DB\) are parallel from \(A\) to \(B\), but their retained words are distinct. Likewise \(BA\) and \(BC\cdot CA\) are parallel from \(B\) to \(A\). The free source has no face automatically identifying these alternatives. The existing Agda obstruction proves that certain proposed source-path equalities are impossible in the specified free realization.

Distinct closed examples are `AB·BC·CA` and `AD·DB·BA` (both return to A). Mere endpoint matching also allows open words such as `AB·BC`; endpoint compatibility is not cyclic closure.

Existing Marici constructions do provide **conditional** effect-comparison squares and, in a separate source-comparison interface, native retained comparisons between certain attachment orders. The latter requires a chosen comparison mode, whole-carrier source compatibility and a continuation convention; it does not supply every desired N or NN filler. This must not be interpreted as an impossibility theorem for all potential source extensions.

## 7. Finite capacity from simultaneous, environment-sensitive witnesses

This section concerns a **verified translation into ordinary Ramsey graph constraints**, not a derivation of those constraints from the native \(r,s\) words.

Let \(G\) be a triangle-free graph with \(\alpha(G)<k\). To add a new vertex \(x\), let \(S\subseteq V(G)\) be its proposed graph-neighborhood. The extension remains admissible if and only if

\[
S\text{ is independent in }G,\qquad
S\cap T\ne\varnothing\text{ for every independent }(k-1)\text{-set }T.
\]

The first requirement prevents a new triangle. The second prevents a new independent \(k\)-set. The independent set \(S\), together with selected intersections, serves as a **joint realization witness** for the extension's local requirements. All those intersections must be realized by *one and the same* \(S\), in the original graph environment.

This explains how individual requirements can have witnesses while their complete family is inconsistent. A formal higher cell with no joint realization would not repair the incompatibility. It also explains why a single attempted insertion can fail before the universal Ramsey threshold.

### 7.1 Irreducible witness incompatibility

For a fixed triangle-free graph \(G\) of independence number \(a\), let a *requirement* be a maximum independent \(a\)-set \(T\). For a family \(\mathcal F\) of requirements, define the witness fiber

\[
\mathcal W_G(\mathcal F)=
\{S\subseteq V(G): S\text{ independent and }S\cap T\ne\varnothing\ \forall T\in\mathcal F\}.
\]

A family is irreducibly incompatible when \(\mathcal W_G(\mathcal F)=\varnothing\), but every proper subfamily has a nonempty witness fiber. Let \(h(G)\) be its largest such family size (zero if none exist) and \(H(a)\) the maximum over triangle-free environments of independence number \(a\).

The proposed general conjecture is

\[
\boxed{H(a)=2^a.}
\]

A construction with \(a\) disjoint edges gives the lower bound \(H(a)\ge2^a\): the \(2^a\) choices of one endpoint on each edge form a minimally incompatible family. The deletion-witness set-pairs argument supplies the general upper bound \(H(a)\le\binom{2a}{a}\). Exact finite enumeration establishes

| \(a\) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(H(a)\) | 2 | 4 | 8 | 16 |

A further classification in the project proves the bound for the class \(\alpha(G)+\nu(G)=|V(G)|\), including bipartite graphs. In that class, the incompatible cases are disjoint balanced complete bipartite components and attain \(2^c\) when there are \(c\) such components; the sharp \(2^a\) case is \(a\) disjoint edges. The general \(H(a)\) conjecture remains open in this programme and its novelty relative to the literature has not been established.

### 7.2 The thirteen-point nonbinary environment

Let

\[
G_{13}=\operatorname{Cay}\bigl(\mathbb Z_{13},\{\pm1,\pm5\}\bigr).
\]

It is triangle-free, \(\alpha(G_{13})=4\), and has 39 maximal independent sets, all of size four. Its smallest irreducible incompatibility has five requirements: every four of these have a joint witness, but all five do not. Its largest irreducible incompatibility has thirteen requirements, with an explicit deletion witness for each one. Consequently, the five-way subfamily realizes an induced boundary \(\partial\Delta^4\) in the *witness-existence complex*, and the thirteen-way one realizes \(\partial\Delta^{12}\). These are missing compatibility simplices, **not** automatically nontrivial higher homotopy classes or existing NN morphisms.

The finite audit enumerated 1,029 nonempty triangle-free graphs with independence number at most four (up to isomorphism) and checked the required incompatibility bounds. It does not establish the all-\(a\) theorem.

## 8. Exact rank-sixteen compatibility dictionary

For \(G_{13}\), index a \(39\times39\) matrix by its maximum independent sets \(T,S\), requiring

\[
M_{T,S}=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad T\cap S\ne\varnothing.
\]

The separate exact certificate establishes

\[
\min_{\mathbb R}\operatorname{rank}M=16=2^4.
\]

The lower bound comes from a forced nonzero-diagonal triangular 16-submatrix of the support pattern; the upper bound from a symmetric, translation-equivariant exact algebraic factorization \(M=UBU^{\mathsf T}\), \(U\in\mathbb R^{39\times16}\). The certificate verifies all 1,521 entries over an explicitly described degree-12 real algebraic number field and checks the Fourier block ranks \(2,1,1,1,2,1,1,1,1,2,1,1,1\).

This is a **compatibility representation**, not a quotient of 39 retained paths into 16 paths, and not proof that any two endpoints determine a unique traversal. It tensor-composes over disjoint graph copies, giving rank \(16^t=2^{4t}\) certificates and irreducible-obstruction upper bounds for that infinite family. It has not been derived from the native path-operation or closed-circle rule.

## 9. Steiner-like two-point completion at thirteen positions

A cyclic projective-plane construction uses

\[
D=\{0,1,3,9\}\subset\mathbb Z_{13}.
\]

The twelve nonzero ordered differences between its four points represent every nonzero residue modulo 13 exactly once. Consequently, the 13 translates \(D+i\) form the lines of a Steiner system \(S(2,4,13)\): **after choosing that block system**, two distinct positions lie on exactly one four-point line.

But important distinctions survive:

1. The 715 four-subsets include 52 valid difference sets, forming **four labeled cyclic block systems**, all of the same abstract projective-plane type.
2. To obtain a reflection on 13 cyclic positions, the experiment changed the meaning of \(s\) from central inversion to \(s(i)=-i\). With \(r(i)=i+1\), this gives the **nonabelian** dihedral group \(D_{13}\), not the original cyclic \(C_{2m}\) system.
3. Reflection of one 13-block family produces a distinct second family. Their union has 26 blocks, so each unordered pair belongs to **two** possible lines. The 52 difference sets form two such reflection-stable 26-block systems.
4. The graph automorphism \(i\mapsto5i\) exchanges those two reflection-stable systems. The unlabelled Ramsey graph cannot select one while preserving that symmetry.
5. None of the 39 independent four-sets of \(G_{13}\) is a line of these cyclic projective-plane systems. Every such line contains exactly two edges of \(G_{13}\).

Thus **unique two-point block completion** is conditional on choosing an incidence system; it is not unique traversal, nor yet a source-derived Ramsey witness.

### 9.1 New closed-circle discriminator (added after the user correction)

The Steiner analysis did **not** require the four-point lines to be traversable as closed circles using the original elementary directional steps.

I checked all 26 blocks in the rotation/reflection closure of \(D\). The test asks whether some ordering \((a,b,c,d)\) of the four distinct block points yields a closed traversal

\[
a\to b\to c\to d\to a
\]

using only \(r:x\mapsto x+1\) and \(s:x\mapsto-x\), keeping every step within the chosen four-point block. I also allowed reverse rotation \(r^{-1}\) as a stronger control.

| Allowed elementary steps | Blocks admitting such a simple closed four-point traversal | Blocks checked |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(r,s\) | **0** | 26 |
| \(r,r^{-1},s\) | **0** | 26 |

This is an **exact negative result** for this specific minimal circle interpretation. It does **not** exclude closed longer paths that leave and return to the carrier, higher witnessed traversals, or a different derived direction. It does show that Steiner pair incidence and primitive circular traversability are separate conditions.

A second test exposes order dependence when reflection is used on an odd cycle:

\[
rsrs=e\quad\text{but}\quad rrss=r^2\ne e\qquad\text{in }D_{13}.
\]

The words have the same multiset of letters, but one closes and the other does not. By contrast, in the original even-position \(C_{2m}\) system, an already closing collection of \(r,s\) letters remains endpoint-closed under every reordering, since \(r\) and \(s\) commute.

**Interpretation:** The newly asserted closure-under-gluing requirement could discriminate between the original central-inversion construction and the later odd-position mirror-reflection construction. The earlier 13-point Steiner construction is *not yet a candidate satisfying all of the requested path axioms*.

## 10. What has been shown, and what has not

**Established within the specified models and supplied exact audits:**

- Typed concatenation, selected closed words, and free-history separation in the Marici seed; some comparisons require additional source-selected witnesses.
- The cyclic even-position \(r,s\) closure relation and its induced polygon boundaries; corrected full-rotation closed-chain counts.
- Exact graph extension criterion via a single independent neighborhood \(S\) meeting every maximum independent requirement.
- Irreducible incompatibility bounds \(H(a)=2^a\) for \(a\le4\) by finite computation, plus the special matching-class theorem.
- The thirteen-point graph's five-way and thirteen-way incompatibility examples.
- The minimum real compatibility-matrix rank 16 for that graph, with an exact coefficient certificate.
- Cyclic Steiner difference-set generation, four cyclic systems, two reflection-stable systems, mismatch with Ramsey-independent sets, and the new zero-of-26 primitive four-circle test.

**Not established:**

- A universal admitted N/NN witness generator built solely from \(r,s\) and typed path composition.
- A mathematically fixed, nonvacuous definition of “closed circle regardless of how glued” covering the user's intended complete path systems.
- Selection of a unique two-point **path** rather than a selected incidence block or a family of coherently compared paths.
- A derivation of a Ramsey graph's pairwise relation readout from the typed directional histories, or a proof of \(R(l,k)\) from this source model.
- The all-\(a\) conjecture \(H(a)=2^a\), and any universal connection of its \(2^a\) to Golay/Steiner block uniqueness.

## 11. Highest-value next experiment

**Make closed-circle gluing an executable source rule and retest the smallest two cases before adding symmetry dimensions.**

Freeze the primitive alphabet, endpoint typing, allowed operations and admitted closure *without* using the known Ramsey numbers to select them. For each input collection of path pieces:

1. Enumerate the genuinely admissible gluings, checking every junction's ending and starting code letters.
2. Require the specified completed gluings to close; record any generated return continuation rather than assuming one.
3. Preserve the full histories of different closing orders and generate the corresponding N comparison **questions**.
4. Distinguish comparison questions from **admitted comparison witnesses** and demand compositional compatibility of their NN histories.
5. Reject nonclosing or mutually incompatible gluing families for source-internal reasons, without introducing a fresh unconstrained witness to erase the obstruction.
6. Test separately whether the source-generated admission criterion agrees with the exact independent-neighborhood extension criterion, first on the 5-cycle and 8-position Wagner construction, then on the thirteen-position nonbinary example.

**Minimal falsifiers:** a primitive block required to be a circle with no admissible cyclic path (the Steiner four-line test already supplies such a case); two permitted glue orders with incompatible closure and no NN witness; an unrestricted ability to adjoin arbitrary returns; or an early dead-end incorrectly declared to be a universal Ramsey threshold.

**Desired theorem:** a preservation/obstruction statement relating typed, gluing-invariant closed path systems and the existence of a *jointly realizable* extension. Only after such a bridge is established should \(R(3,7)=23\), \(R(3,8)=28\), and \(R(3,9)=36\) be treated as numerical targets for the same unchanged generator.
