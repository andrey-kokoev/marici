# When does the folded frame select ten interior visits?

The literal row-preserving poles (0,29) admit closed reflected strips with 4, 6, 8, 10 and 12 interior visits. Restricting `ramsey_strip_length_control.json` to these endpoints gives respectively (closed, failed) = (2,0), (2,0), (4,4), (6,4), (2,0). Thus closure, the poles, and the swap do not fix ten. As a stronger counterexample, both 0–16–13–24–3–25–11–22–7–29 and 0–16–13–24–3–25–11–22–2–21–5–27–4–29 close, with the same first seven steps.

The folded moving graph has six positions of each incidence type. An interior route between a fixed left pole and a fixed right pole visits equal numbers of both types. With 2k visits, the unused moving positions therefore have k subtracted on each side: (6-k,6-k). This is distinct from the fixed positions outside the moving graph. Requiring one last *moving* incidence to be tested (one unused position on each side) implies k=5, independent of the mark definitions. Conversely ten visits leave exactly one candidate moving link: the two remaining positions are either incident or not. Twelve visits exhaust both sides and leave no such test. Eight leave two positions on each side. This is a stopping premise, not a deduction from the raw four-letter rows or from the size of the constructed Ramsey witness.
