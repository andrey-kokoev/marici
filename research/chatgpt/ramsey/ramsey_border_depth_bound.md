# Signed extension depth

Assume k >= 3 and two legal bases of the same order. Fix a bijection of their legal one-column patterns preserving signed extensions of at most k-1 columns, including repeated patterns. For an arbitrary legal extension of the first base, replace each column by its image, leaving every sign between new vertices unchanged. A violation cannot lie entirely in the old base, since that base is legal, or entirely among new vertices, since their induced matrix did not change. A crossing positive triangle uses at most two new vertices; a crossing negative k-set uses at most k-1. Restricting to those new vertices contradicts preservation at depth k-1. The inverse bijection gives the converse. Thus depth k-1 preserves every finite extension, not merely maximum-order eligibility.

For (3,6), the minimum universal depth for the **binary decision** of eligibility for order 17 is four, by finite catalogue checks. Depth three fails at order 10 (`ramsey_36_try_three.py 10`). For sufficiency, `ramsey_36_try_four.py` checks all nonempty orders through 10, using invariants of signed pair compatibility and counts of valid all-negative triples and quartets incident to each column, counting multiplicities. Distinct hashes certify distinct profiles; equal hashes must not be used to certify equality. No mixed eligibility group remains. Prior full depth-three checks cover orders 11 and 12 (`ramsey_36_try_three.py`). From order 13 on, reaching 17 itself takes at most four columns. The empty base is unique. This argument does not establish that four columns preserve every extension type, nor that the threshold for general k is k-2.
