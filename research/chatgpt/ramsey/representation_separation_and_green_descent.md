## 1. Fixed source, retained histories

The original source vertices are A,B,C,D with occurrences:

- `AB : A→B`, `BC : B→C`, `CA : C→A`;
- `AD : A→D`, `DB : D→B`, `BA : B→A`.

The point-reflection graph involution is A↔B and C↔D, exchanging AB↔BA, BC↔AD, and CA↔DB. The direct and indirect A→B histories are `p=AB` and `q=AD·DB`. They have equal endpoint types but distinct source words. Likewise the B→A histories are `s=BA` and `t=BC·CA`.

## 2. A free-history representation separates all distinct source paths

Let `C` be the free category on the directed graph of the six occurrences. For every vertex `v`, take the source-native effect carrier

    X(v) = Hom_C(A,v) = Path A v.

For an occurrence `e : v→w`, define its action by postcomposition:

    act(e)(h) = h · e.

This is exactly the `History` and `advance` pattern of `SeedFirstReturnActions.agda`; it is not an invented effect policy. For every pair of A-originating paths `p,q : A→v`:

    run(p)(id_A) = p,
    run(q)(id_A) = q.

Therefore the action distinguishes `p` and `q` whenever the retained source words differ. In particular, a purported comparison polymorphic in *all* `Effects X act` would imply `AB = AD·DB`, contradicting the existing path-distinction theorem. This is a direct source-level **no-universal-G theorem** for the unconstrained effect interface. It does not exclude selective effect models that identify the two outputs while retaining the history channel.

For strictly invertible effects, replace the free category with its free groupoid completion and use the representable set `Hom(A,v)`. Postcomposition by primitive arrows is then bijective and still separates unequal reduced groupoid words. Merely requiring reversibility does not invalidate the separation argument.

## 3. R/B face relations and the quotient geometry

Now *conditionally* attach two faces with boundaries

    R = AB·BC·CA,
    B = AD·DB·BA.

Let `X_RB` be the resulting connected 2-complex. Its fundamental group is `Z`, generated by

    z = AB·BA.

One way to see this is to contract a spanning tree in the six-edge graph, obtaining three free loop generators, then eliminate two using the two distinct face relations. The surviving generator can be chosen as `z`.

Reflection acts freely on this cell structure. The quotient `Y_RB = X_RB / reflection` has two vertices, three edge orbits `a,b,c`, and a single triangular face orbit with relation

    a b c = 1.

Contracting the edge `b`, then eliminating `c`, gives

    pi1(Y_RB) ≅ <a | > ≅ Z.

The double-cover inclusion on fundamental groups is `2Z ↪ Z`, and the return `z` is the lift of `a²`. The quotient green cell would impose `a²=1`, changing the quotient fundamental group to `C2`. This rederives the nonsplit extension and its possible green completion from the source incidence.

## 4. Classification of strictly reversible observable effects

A Set-valued *flat local system* on the connected quotient `Y_RB` is, up to natural isomorphism, a set `S` together with an action of `pi1(Y_RB) ≅ Z`. After choosing a frame at the basepoint, the entire action is a single permutation

    U ∈ Sym(S)

representing `a`. Changing the frame conjugates U. Thus isomorphism classes of finite n-state local systems correspond to conjugacy classes of permutations in `S_n`, and framed systems correspond to actual permutations.

Pulled back to the reflected two-sheeted R/B complex, the return `z` has effect `U²`. A strict green filling can be interpreted exactly when

    U² = identity.

Equivalently, the local system's monodromy representation factors through the quotient `Z → C2`:

    Z --phi--> Sym(S)
     \         ^
      v        |
      C2 ------

The dashed factorization exists if and only if the squared monodromy vanishes. **It is not entailed by reflection on the base space.**

For non-discrete higher-valued effects, this criterion becomes a *homotopy-coherent factorization* problem: one needs a comparison `U² ~ id` and its continuation/higher coherence. A strict permutation countermodel already refutes any purported universal filler under the weaker source assumptions.

## 5. Explicit finite separating model

Take the effect set `S = {0,1,2}` and the 3-cycle `U=(0 1 2)`. Use the same set at all four vertices, with these invertible actions:

    AB=BA=U;
    AD=BC=id;
    DB=CA=U^{-1}.

Every pair of reflected edge occurrences has the same action in the displayed fiber frames. The red and blue loop actions are both identities:

    U · id · U^{-1} = id,
    id · U^{-1} · U = id.

But both green boundary actions, and the return `AB·BA`, equal `U² ≠ id`. All primitive operators are permutation matrices, hence unitary; moreover, all operators commute. The return remains nontrivial under every admissible continuation in this model because the effect group is the abelian group `C3`.

This refutes green derivability from the combined conditions **R/B closure + reversible primitive effects + unitary effects + strict equality of reflection-paired edge actions + commutativity + continuation invariance**. It does not refute a stronger, independently sourced rule requiring the reflected reverse to act as the inverse.

The **minimality within this framed reflection-equivariant permutation class** is exact: every element of `Sym(1)` and `Sym(2)` squares to the identity. `Sym(3)` has two three-cycles whose squares are nontrivial.

## 7. A separation theorem for all finite reflected local systems

Let `k≠0`. There exists a finite reflection-equivariant, invertible, R/B-closed effect interpretation detecting `z^k`.

**Proof.** Choose an odd prime `p` with `p > 2|k|`; let `U` be a p-cycle and use the construction in §5 with p states. Then `z^k` acts by `U^{2k} ≠ id`. Since `k` was arbitrary, the intersection of kernels of all such finite-state interpretations on the loop group `<z> ≅ Z` is trivial.

Consequently no nonzero power of the return is observationally forced to vanish by the stated conditions. The source cannot select a finite return period by adding only reflection, reversibility, R/B closure, and unrestricted finite local-system semantics.

## 8. Interpretation for the research programme

The observation question is not whether an effect family *can* make `z` trivial; many do. The question is whether native operating law independently restricts admitted effects to a family that **forces monodromy to factor through `C2`**, or else constructs a typed higher comparison in place of that factorization.

A requirement literally equivalent to `U²=id` would rename green closure, not derive it. A viable selection principle must be stated and justified without mentioning G and must reject the 3-state permutation control for an independently demonstrable reason.

The next source audit should locate an actual native **effect-admission policy**: which effect carriers, edge operations, observational tests, and reversal transport are mandatory rather than merely allowed as parameters. `SeedFirstReturnActions.Effects` permits arbitrary functions; `SeedGeometricResponseInterpretation.Interpret` explicitly accepts equivalences as external inputs. Neither interface, by itself, selects the involutive subfamily.

**Strongest established conclusion:** Under the inspected source signatures and the stated R/B/reflection/reversibility assumptions, a universal green filler is impossible. **Unresolved:** whether some independently justified additional native operation or admission rule narrows the family, and whether such a rule extends to higher coherent branching, not merely strict return cancellation.
