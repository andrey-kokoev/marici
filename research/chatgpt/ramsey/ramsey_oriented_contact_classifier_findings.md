# Oriented-contact completion law — verified finite scope

Carrier: Cayley graph of `F_2^6` generated by `{1,2,4,8,16,32,63}`. B means nonadjacency; admissibility is A-triangle-free and B-clique number at most 22. Three birth channels operate on six distinct lower-half squares. All three square-pair edits are performed before selecting any newborn subset.

## Complete three-birth classifier

**Pair missing-face witness (11+10).** For selected newborns `i,j`, select one low-bit vertex in each of their four contact squares, avoiding the active birth ports. In the other four squares select one or two low-bit vertices, respecting local square A-edges and A-edges between neighboring square cells. Seek exactly eleven selected lower vertices (hence three double-occupied squares) touching exactly nine of the sixteen antipodal quotient classes. If the untouched quotient classes contain the entire Clebsch five-neighbor set of some quotient vertex q, choose both upper representatives of those five classes. Eleven lower plus ten upper vertices form a 21-node common B-link; the selected newborn pair gives B23.

**Triple missing-face witness (8+12).** If no pair is missing, the B-triple fails if and only if the oriented contact equations `p_[a_i]=0`, `p_[b_i]=1` are consistent on the four antipodal square classes `[c]={c,c xor7}`. Their solution generates eight lower vertices in four antipodal pairs, together with twelve upper parity vertices; the three newborns give B23.

The contact-only pair witness and triple parity, combined, classify all 3,360 oriented arrangements. The two constructions are independently verified as B23; **completeness within this finite family** is independently checked against exact common-link capacities on all 56 symmetry orbits, not proved by a general structural necessity lemma.

## Four-birth stress test: pair witness changes type

In the four-birth arrangement `((0,1),(2,3),(4,6),(5,7))`, the B-pair of newborns 0 and 2 is obstructed by a different canonical witness: ten lower vertices forming five antipodal pairs, plus eleven upper parity vertices. This gives an exact B23 with 10+11 old witness rather than 11+10. `ramsey_four_birth_alternate_pair_certificate.py` verifies the witness.

The 11+10 three-birth pair template alone does not extend unchanged to four births. A full higher-birth classifier must include antipodal templates and possibly additional upper quotient deficiency patterns. No global universality is claimed.
