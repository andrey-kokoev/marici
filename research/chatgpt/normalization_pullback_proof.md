# Native exceptional packet on the Rees normalization–conductor square

## Result and scope

This is a derived coefficient base-change computation for the supplied native
245-state logarithmic blowdown and its homotopy-equivalent 225-state retained
model. It uses both alternating normalization sheets and the common conductor.
All sixteen endpoint states and all seven native Q states are retained.

Only the six **short** normal graphs are imposed. The three long normal
parameters, including `u03`, remain independent of the long occurrence
coordinates. In particular, `u03` is not identified with `X03`. The calculation
is unlocalized in occurrence, normal, and Rees parameters. Localization at
monodromy units `1+u` is allowed. No inverse-normal Čech term is base changed
through a vanishing occurrence coordinate.

The old exceptional generator and its two named primitives become zero as
chains. The full complex does not become zero. The normalization–conductor
sequence produces a different class in homological degree one, with exact
annihilator generated by the six short occurrence coordinates, `u03`, `t04`,
and `t35`. This class remains nonzero in the complete native complex. Its
conductor comparison has a two-parameter Koszul resolution, with unit Ext²
class after retaining the determinant line.

This is not yet an identification with the physical normalized residue. The
new supported class is in the kernel of ordinary blowdown. A physical Gysin
comparison must specify what happens to it, rather than identifying it with
an unrestricted coefficient unit.

## 1. Rings and source maps

Use diagonal labels, not the cyclic short-variable indices:

\[
O=\{13,15,35\},\qquad E=\{02,04,24\},\qquad L=\{03,14,25\}.
\]

In words: `O` and `E` label the positive and negative alternating sheet
coordinates; `L` labels the long diagonals.

Let `R=Z[X_d,u_d]`, optionally localized at every `1+u_d`, be the previous
native coefficient ring. Adjoin `t_s` for all six short diagonals and define

\[
\mathcal B=
\mathbb Z[X_d,u_\ell,t_s:\ d\in O\cup E\cup L,\ \ell\in L,\ s\in O\cup E]
/(X_eX_o:e\in E,o\in O).
\]

The coefficient map is

\[
u_s\longmapsto t_sX_s\quad(s\in O\cup E),
\qquad u_\ell\longmapsto u_\ell\quad(\ell\in L).
\]

All six `t_s` are retained on both sheets, even
when their corresponding occurrence coordinate is zero. This retains the
normal-line information of the common ambient Rees family.

Define

\[
\mathcal B_+=\mathcal B/(X_e:e\in E),\quad
\mathcal B_-=\mathcal B/(X_o:o\in O),\quad
\mathcal C=\mathcal B/(X_s:s\in O\cup E).
\]

There is an exact coefficient sequence

\[
0\longrightarrow\mathcal B\longrightarrow
\mathcal B_+\oplus\mathcal B_-
\xrightarrow{\epsilon_+-\epsilon_-}\mathcal C\longrightarrow0.
\]

This is the source's alternating normalization–conductor sequence, extended
by the common Rees and long-normal coefficient variables. Localizing at the
compatible monodromy units preserves this sequence.

The native and retained complexes are finite free. Their ordinary tensor
products with these rings therefore compute their derived pullbacks. An
arbitrary ordinary tensor product of their homology modules would not.

## 2. The old two-extension and its inclusion behave differently

Write

\[
f=u_{03}+u_{13}+u_{03}u_{13},\qquad
\mu=X_{03}X_{13}u_{02}u_{13}u_{14}u_{15}u_{24}u_{25}.
\]

After the six graph substitutions, before the sheet quotient,

\[
\mu'=X_{03}u_{14}u_{25}\,
 t_{02}t_{13}t_{15}t_{24}\,
 X_{02}X_{13}^{2}X_{15}X_{24}.
\]

Hence `mu'=0` in the glued alternating ring. Its orders in the ambient ideals
of the positive sheet, negative sheet, and conductor are respectively
`2`, `3`, and `5`. It is not a first conductor normal symbol.

The remaining equation is

\[
f_{\mathcal B}=u_{03}+(1+u_{03})t_{13}X_{13}.
\]

On the positive sheet it has this same form; on the negative sheet and the
conductor it is `u03`. It is a nonzerodivisor on all four rings. One proof
uses the monodromy unit `1+t13 X13`: eliminating `u03` identifies each quotient
with a localization of the corresponding polynomial sheet or fiber-product
ring. Alternatively, inject the glued ring into its two sheets and use
nonzerodivisibility on each sheet.

The full Koszul resolution therefore pulls back as

\[
K_R(f,\mu)\otimes_R\mathcal B=K_{\mathcal B}(f_{\mathcal B},0).
\]

Its homology has one copy of `B/(f_B)` in degree zero and one in degree one.
The degree-one copy carries the retained excess basis `e_mu`. Dualizing the
finite free complex retains a unit in degree two. It does **not** identify
that unit with ordinary `Ext²_B(B/(f_B),B)`, which vanishes because `f_B` is a
single regular equation.

The old three-generator truncation had an actual chain map to the native
complex, with basis images `g`, `W_f`, and `W_mu`. That map has a different
base-change behavior.

Put `c=04`, `d=35`, `s=t04`, `t=t35`. The new polynomial syzygy is

\[
g_*=-st\,r_0+t\,r_c+s\,r_d,
\qquad
Z_*=-st\,z_0+t\,z_c+s\,z_d.
\]

Before the alternating quotient,

\[
g=-X_cX_dg_*,\qquad Z=-X_cX_dZ_*.
\]

Since `Xc Xd=0` on the alternating ring, both old chains vanish literally.
Every coefficient of the old complete top cycle `Omega` contains the product
of all six short occurrence coordinates after the graph substitution. Thus
`Omega`, the native top cycle, and both full native primitives also vanish.
The checker verifies these statements in the original 245-state basis.

Consequently the nonzero dual unit of the abstract derived Koszul pullback
cannot be claimed to be the image of the old native generator: that native
chain map has become zero. New homology of the base-changed native complex
must be calculated separately.

## 3. Complete exceptional complex on each sheet

The retained ten-state packet `N` has bases

\[
N_1=\langle p_c,p_d\rangle,\quad
N_2=\langle r_0,r_c,r_d,y_c,y_d\rangle,\quad
N_3=\langle z_0,z_c,z_d\rangle.
\]

After the graph substitution its differential is

\[
\begin{aligned}
d r_0&=-X_cp_c-X_dp_d,&
d r_c&=-sX_cp_c,&d r_d&=-tX_dp_d,\\
d y_c&=-fp_c,&d y_d&=-fp_d,\\
d z_0&=fr_0-X_cy_c-X_dy_d,\\
d z_c&=fr_c-sX_cy_c,&d z_d&=fr_d-tX_dy_d.
\end{aligned}
\]

`f` means its pulled-back value. The factorization by `K(f)` remains valid;
`f` is regular. Write

\[
A_+=\mathcal B_+/(f_+),\quad A_-=\mathcal B_-/(u_{03}),\quad
A_0=\mathcal C/(u_{03}).
\]

On the positive sheet `Xc=0`; on the negative sheet `Xd=0`. Explicit unit
basis changes give

\[
H_2(N_+)=A_+\langle r_c,\ r_d-tr_0\rangle,
\qquad
H_2(N_-)=A_-\langle r_c-sr_0,\ r_d\rangle,
\]

\[
H_2(N_0)=A_0\langle r_0,r_c,r_d\rangle.
\]

All three third homology groups vanish. The lower terms are retained:

\[
H_1(N_+)=A_+\langle p_c\rangle\oplus A_+/(X_d)\langle p_d\rangle,
\]

\[
H_1(N_-)=A_-/(X_c)\langle p_c\rangle\oplus A_-\langle p_d\rangle,
\qquad H_1(N_0)=A_0^2.
\]

For example, on the positive sheet replacing `r_d` by `r_d-t r_0` and
`z_d` by `z_d-t z_0` splits off a copy of `K(f)`; the remaining nontrivial
link column is multiplication by `Xd`. Both `f` and `Xd` form a regular
sequence there. The negative-sheet proof is identical with `c` and `d`
interchanged. No division by a coefficient is used.

## 4. Actual conductor comparison and its cokernel

Tensor the normalization exact sequence with the free complex `N`. The
result is an exact sequence of complexes, and hence a long exact homology
sequence. The difference map on degree-two sheet homology, after restricting
coefficients to the conductor, is

\[
M=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&-t&s&0\\
1&0&-1&0\\
0&1&0&-1
\end{pmatrix}:A_0^4\longrightarrow A_0^3.
\]

The columns are, in order, the two positive-sheet cycle restrictions and the
negatives of the two negative-sheet cycle restrictions. Coefficient
restriction from either sheet onto `A0` is surjective, so this matrix computes
the actual image in conductor homology, not just a constant-coefficient test.

Two integral unit eliminations reduce `M` to an identity block and the row

\[
(s,-t):A_0^2\longrightarrow A_0.
\]

The reduction is explicitly exported. Therefore

\[
\operatorname{coker}M=A_0/(s,t),\qquad
\ker M=A_0\langle(t,s,t,s)\rangle.
\]

The regular pair `(s,t)` also gives an exact free resolution

\[
0\longrightarrow A_0
\xrightarrow{(t,s,t,s)^T}A_0^4
\xrightarrow{M}A_0^3
\longrightarrow A_0/(s,t)\longrightarrow0.
\]

With these ordered bases, dualizing this resolution gives

\[
\operatorname{Ext}_{A_0}^2(A_0/(s,t),A_0)\cong A_0/(s,t),
\]

and its Yoneda two-extension is the unit. The invariant formulation retains
the dual determinant of the conormal lines of the two Rees divisors. The
chosen ordering fixes the displayed algebraic sign. No identification with
an independently normalized physical Gysin orientation is inferred.

This is a two-extension derived from the complete sheet/conductor
comparison. It is different from the previous two-extension on `R/(f,mu)`.

## 5. A new native conductor class and its exact annihilator

The long exact sequence embeds the cokernel just computed into degree-one
homology of the glued exceptional complex:

\[
A_0/(s,t)\hookrightarrow H_1(N_{\mathcal B}).
\]

A representative of its unit is

\[
\gamma=X_cp_c,\qquad [\gamma]=[-X_dp_d].
\]

Indeed, lift the conductor cycle `r0` to `(r0,0)` on the two sheets. Its
boundary is `(-Xd p_d,0)`, the image of the displayed global chain. The
second representative differs from the first by `-d r0`.

The annihilating homotopies are explicit:

\[
d(-r_c)=s\gamma,\qquad
d(-t r_0+r_d)=t\gamma,\qquad
d(-X_cy_c)=u_{03}\gamma.
\]

For an odd short `Xo`, multiplication by `Xo` kills the representative
`Xc p_c` directly. For an even short `Xe`, the primitive is `-Xe r0`;
its other boundary term vanishes by the alternating mixed-product relations.
Thus all six short occurrence coordinates annihilate the class.

The two displayed Rees primitives satisfy

\[
t(-r_c)-s(-tr_0+r_d)=-g_*.
\]

The syzygy `g_*` has `dZ_*=f g_*`. These are relations in the complete
retained complex, not fitted scalar conditions.

The class is zero on each individual sheet and on the conductor after
ordinary restriction. Its nonzero value is the comparison between those
sheetwise trivializations. This is why dropping the conductor comparison
would miss it.

## 6. Nonvanishing in the full native source

The exceptional attaching map into the old target still has its five columns,
all divisible by `X13`. To prove that `gamma` is not killed by these columns,
apply a further legitimate polynomial specialization:

- retain `x=X04` and `y=X35`, with `xy=0`;
- set the other four short occurrence coordinates to zero;
- set `u03=t04=t35=0`;
- keep all remaining spectator coefficients and their monodromy units.

The entire attaching map becomes zero because `X13=0`. In the exceptional
degree-two differential, the only nonzero column entering `p_c,p_d` is
`r0 -> -x p_c-y p_d`. The coefficient functional

\[
\ell(Ap_c+Bp_d)=[x]A-[y]B
\]

annihilates every polynomial multiple of that boundary column. Here `[x]`
means the coefficient of `x` with zero `y`-degree, retaining spectator
coefficients; `[y]` is defined analogously. It evaluates `gamma=x p_c` to one.
It is a coefficient-linear detector for this specialization, not an asserted
ring-linear trace on the original complex.

The same argument with arbitrary surviving spectator coefficients proves
that the embedded copy of `A0/(s,t)` remains injective after passage from
`N_B` to the full 225-state retained complex and hence to the 245-state native
complex. In particular,

\[
\operatorname{Ann}_{\mathcal B}[\gamma]
=(X_s:s\in O\cup E)+(u_{03},t_{04},t_{35}).
\]

The coefficient ring of this cyclic submodule has no integer torsion. Its
torsion is in the displayed geometric/Rees parameters.

The checker also exports the original native representative. It has four
polynomial entries (five monomials):

\[
\begin{aligned}
\gamma_{\rm native}=X_{04}\bigl(&-u_{03}[\{04,E\},\varnothing]
+[\{03,04,E\},\{03\}]\\
&+(1+u_{03})[\{04,13,E\},\{13\}]
-[\{04,13,E\},\{E\}]\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]

It is closed on all 245 coordinates. The retraction maps it to `gamma` in
`N_B`, and the ordinary blowdown maps it to zero. No endpoint state is deleted
to establish these identities.

## 7. The Q attachment is retained independently

For the original primitive seven-state quotient cycle `theta`, the retained
attachment still is

\[
\delta\widetilde\theta=X_{03}X_{13}u_{14}u_{25}r_0.
\]

It is nonzero on the positive sheet, zero on the negative sheet, and zero on
the conductor. It is not itself a cycle: its differential is cancelled by
the attachment of the existing lower boundary `beta=d theta_tilde`.
The checker verifies

\[
d_N\delta\widetilde\theta+\delta\beta=0
\]

on all five coefficient rings (ambient Rees, both sheets, conductor, glued
alternating ring). The new conductor class is not substituted for this
Q-attachment.

## 8. Consequence for the physical comparison

The preceding ordinary trace calculation over independent normal coefficients
cannot simply be specialized by substituting into its homology generator.
That generator maps to zero, while the derived pullback has additional sheet
cycles and a nonzero conductor-gluing class.

A supported class annihilated by `t04` and `t35` cannot map to the unrestricted
unit in a coefficient line on which either parameter is a nonzerodivisor.
A shifted supported duality operation can have a unit value: the actual
conductor comparison above has precisely the two-parameter Koszul
orientation. What remains to be constructed is its comparison with the
physical normal-line/endpoint frame. The scalar value `1` in the Ext²
coordinate is not that comparison.

The diagrams include incompatible spectator diagonals `04` and `35` only
through conductor descent. They do not put both inverse normals on a
previously nonexistent common marked face.

## Reproduction

Run `python check_normalization_pullback.py` in this directory. It imports
and replays the upstream matrix/retraction checker, then verifies 5,840
additional exact assertions. The upstream checker verifies 6,706 assertions.
The new script writes all five base-changed retained differentials, the
complete native/retained comparison matrices, the native conductor cycle,
and a certificate with source hashes.

The module classifications and exact annihilator use the displayed
regular-sequence, unit-elimination, exact-sequence, and detector arguments.
Finite coefficient controls are not substituted for those proofs.
