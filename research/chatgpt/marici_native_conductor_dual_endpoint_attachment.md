## 1. Result and scope

The actual normalization-conductor source has now been dualized as a complete polynomial complex, rather than replaced by one of its residue lines. Its dual has branch cohomology in degree three and conductor cohomology in degree five. The two degrees are joined by a nonzero, source-defined extension. They do not split.

This identifies the native attachment which any source-to-spatial comparison must preserve. On the minus normalization sheet, the previous four-row occurrence-Gysin map on `(X2,X4)` is only a codimension-two factor of that attachment. The missing factor is the **X0 conductor Gysin map**, forced independently both by the source conductor ideal and by the actual minus endpoint occurrence label. The plus attachment is the corresponding three-variable map on `(X1,X3,X5)`. Both are components of the dual of the same normalization difference, not separately chosen residue normalizations.

There is a decisive comparison test. The preceding five-variable occurrence packet cannot map to the native dual with primitive conductor coefficient one in the required frame. Its complete homogeneous Hom differential is injective, with 28 unit pivots. Even the native conductor cohomology line itself has no derived section into the source dual: the corresponding complete Hom differential has 49 unit pivots. Thus equality of a degree-five cohomology value is not enough; the nonzero attachment must be transported as part of the whole source.

The actual weighted spatial collars were retained and checked. Their raw conductor specializations vanish. This calculation does **not** identify the two native Gysin attachment classes with the two spatial connector 2-cells. It constructs and constrains their source-side counterpart. The full ringed supported-Verdier/PC comparison remains unproved, and no physical reflection parity is assigned.

## 2. The source is the actual two-three-dimensional-branch normalization

Let S be the spectator coefficient ring; for the executable calculation it is the polynomial ring over the integers in the three long occurrence variables. Set

\[
A=S[X_0,X_1,X_2,X_3,X_4,X_5],
\qquad J_E=(X_0,X_2,X_4),\quad J_O=(X_1,X_3,X_5).
\]

In words: all six short occurrence coordinates remain independent in the ambient ring. These are not the separate normal variables or the external Rees parameters.

The source normalization is

\[
B_+=A/J_E,\qquad B_-=A/J_O,\qquad
C=A/(J_E+J_O),\qquad B=A/(J_EJ_O).
\]

In words: the plus sheet carries the odd variables, the minus sheet carries the even variables, and the conductor is their common zero section. This is exactly the source's fiber-product presentation [S1]. In particular, the conductor ideal *within* the plus sheet is generated by the odd variables, whereas the ambient ideal defining that sheet is generated by the even variables. The two are not interchangeable.

The supplied exact sequence is

\[
0\longrightarrow B\longrightarrow B_+\oplus B_-
\xrightarrow{\epsilon_+-\epsilon_-}C\longrightarrow0.
\]

In words: the kernel consists of pairs with matching zero-section values. The difference sign is part of the source data.

For equivariant statements, the quotient C in this sequence carries the polarity character chi: reflection exchanges the branches and negates their difference. Ordinary module statements suppress this one-dimensional character but never the polynomial coefficients.

This source is not the one-dimensional node with equation `xy=0`. The latter remains a useful local model, but its rank-one duality calculation cannot be transferred to a union of two coordinate three-planes without calculating the complete source dual.

## 3. An explicit free resolution of the normalization source

Write K(E), K(O), and K(E,O) for the homological Koszul resolutions of the two ambient branch quotients and their conductor, respectively. All six coordinates form a regular sequence in A [M1]. The actual normalization difference lifts to

\[
f:K(E)\oplus K(O)\longrightarrow K(E,O),
\qquad f(a,b)=\iota_Ea-\iota_Ob.
\]

In words: the two maps are the canonical inclusions of ordered Koszul wedges into the six-variable resolution. Their difference lifts the supplied conductor difference.

An explicit model of its homotopy fibre is

\[
P_n=K_n(E)\oplus K_n(O)\oplus K_{n+1}(E,O),
\]

\[
d_P(a,b,c)=(d_Ea,d_Ob,\iota_Ea-\iota_Ob-d_{E,O}c).
\]

In words: retain the two branch resolutions, the shifted conductor resolution, and the off-diagonal normalization maps. The latter are essential. This construction is a resolution of the actual source B, not a new physical filling cell.

The complex has 80 free generators, initially in degrees minus one through five. Fifteen signed-unit cancellations give a deformation retract with ranks

\[
(1,9,18,15,6,1)
\]

in homological degrees zero through five. In words: the reduced resolution has 50 generators. The nine first differential entries are, up to the recorded orientations, precisely the nine products of an even and an odd occurrence.

The checker retains the projection P, inclusion I, and homotopy H of this reduction and verifies

\[
PI=1,\qquad dH+Hd=1-IP.
\]

In words: this is an integral polynomial deformation retraction, not a rank comparison. All pivots are plus or minus one. Neither occurrence variables nor integers are inverted.

All-polynomial exactness follows from the source exact sequence and the three regular Koszul resolutions. The explicit reduction then supplies a compact model without changing the derived object.

## 4. Its dual has two grades and a mandatory nonzero attachment

Define

\[
\mathbb D_A B=R\operatorname{Hom}_A(B,A).
\]

In words: this is the coefficient dual relative to the ambient ring A. A global dualizing-complex normalization may add a common shift and line; it does not remove the relative separation of the two cohomology grades.

Let L_E and L_O denote the ordered determinants of the even and odd ambient conormal generators, and let L_6 be their six-variable determinant with its explicitly recorded order. Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
H^3(\mathbb D_A B)&\cong
(B_+\otimes L_E^\vee)\oplus(B_-\otimes L_O^\vee),\\
H^5(\mathbb D_A B)&\cong C\otimes L_6^\vee\otimes\chi,\\
H^j(\mathbb D_A B)&=0\qquad(j\notin\{3,5\}).
\end{aligned}
\]

In words: the branch duals survive in degree three and a conductor-supported class survives in degree five. The polarity character is relevant when the source symmetries are retained.

Proof: dualizing the complete normalization triangle gives

\[
\mathbb D_A(C\otimes\chi)
\longrightarrow \mathbb D_AB_+\oplus\mathbb D_AB_-
\longrightarrow\mathbb D_AB
\longrightarrow\mathbb D_A(C\otimes\chi)[1].
\]

In words: both branch maps and the conductor map occur in one exact derived triangle. Koszul duality places the branch terms in degree three and the conductor term in degree six; the last connecting shift places its contribution to the source dual in degree five [M1, M2].

The same answer is checked independently in every occurrence degree. A reduced dual generator labelled by a subset T has occurrence shift minus the sum of the degrees in T. Its homogeneous coefficient exists exactly when every exponent of the compensating monomial is nonnegative. Only the distinction between a coordinate of degree minus one and one of nonnegative degree affects the matrix. The 64 support types exhaust all nonempty homogeneous matrices; a coordinate below minus one gives an empty complex. Every matrix reduces with signed-unit pivots. Its surviving patterns agree with the displayed branch and conductor modules.

Put M equal to the degree-three module and N equal to the degree-five module. The canonical truncation triangle is

\[
M[-3]\longrightarrow\mathbb D_AB\longrightarrow N[-5]
\xrightarrow{\kappa}M[-2].
\]

In words: the last map attaches the conductor class to the two branch classes. It is a class in Ext of degree three, not an optional scalar choice.

The two components of kappa are the **regular three-variable conductor Gysin extensions within the two normalization sheets**. They are primitive and nonzero. On the full Koszul cochain models they are the signed projections

\[
K^\vee(E,O)\longrightarrow K^\vee(E),\qquad
K^\vee(E,O)\longrightarrow K^\vee(O),
\]

with the plus and minus signs supplied by the original normalization difference. In words: discard a wedge containing a generator outside the indicated factor, and retain every other wedge with its native sign. The checker verifies both cochain equations on all 64 source wedges.

After factoring out the common ambient branch-normal resolution, each projection is the bottom-component map from the three-variable within-sheet Koszul cochain complex to the sheet ring. Its Ext class has coefficient one modulo the three within-sheet coordinates. A nullhomotopy changes that coefficient only by their ideal. It therefore cannot remove it.

Consequently the source dual is **not** quasi-isomorphic to the direct sum of the two displayed cohomology modules with zero attachment.

## 5. The source conductor support makes the coupling visible before dualization

Let the support be the actual full conductor ideal

\[
\mathfrak m=(X_0,X_1,X_2,X_3,X_4,X_5).
\]

In words: this ideal is supplied by the source normalization, not chosen to make a desired trace nonzero.

Apply its extended Cech complex to the entire normalization sequence [M3]. A localization involving an even and an odd occurrence is zero on B, since their product is zero there. A localization involving only odd variables belongs to the plus sheet; one involving only even variables belongs to the minus sheet. This determines every coefficient module and every map.

The result has

\[
\begin{aligned}
H^1_{\mathfrak m}(B)&\cong C\otimes\chi,\\
H^3_{\mathfrak m}(B)&\cong
H^3_{J_O}(B_+)\oplus H^3_{J_E}(B_-),\\
H^j_{\mathfrak m}(B)&=0\qquad(j\notin\{1,3\}).
\end{aligned}
\]

In words: the conductor difference gives a degree-one supported class, while each three-dimensional branch supplies its top residue channel. There is no assertion that the complete supported complex splits into these groups.

In the constant occurrence degree, the supported complex has ranks

\[
(1,6,6,2).
\]

In words: it retains the common coefficient, six single localizations, six same-branch double localizations, and the two branch triple localizations.

A literal representative of its primitive conductor class is

\[
\sigma_+=e_{\{1\}}+e_{\{3\}}+e_{\{5\}}.
\]

In words: take the unit in each of the three odd single-localization summands. It is the boundary of the plus-sheet lift of the conductor unit in the supported normalization sequence, not a new inserted cycle.

Writing sigma-minus for the sum on the three even single localizations,

\[
d\sigma_+=d\sigma_-=0,\qquad
\sigma_++\sigma_-=d(1).
\]

In words: the two source-induced representatives differ by the prescribed polarity. Either represents a primitive nonzero class; their sum is the existing bottom boundary. Reflection exchanges the two representatives and acts by minus one on this cohomology class.

The checker evaluates all 729 negative/zero/positive occurrence-support patterns, retaining both source-sheet maps and their difference before computing cohomology. The exponent magnitudes do not affect the matrices, so the finite classification covers arbitrary monomial degrees and pole orders. This source calculation supplies a concrete geometric reason for the conductor contribution to the native dual; it is not an independently introduced detector.

## 6. The missing X0 factor is forced by both the source and the endpoint collar

The preceding four-row map used the ordered residual occurrence pair `(X2,X4)`. On the actual minus normalization sheet, the conductor has **three** coordinates:

\[
J_EB_-=(X_0,X_2,X_4)B_-.
\]

In words: the previous support is a proper part of the actual within-sheet conductor support. X0 is not a spectator for that attachment.

Let rho for an ordered set of coordinates denote the full Koszul-to-Cech comparison, including its bottom component. The native minus-sheet residue is

\[
\rho_E=\rho_{X_0}\otimes\rho_{(X_2,X_4)}.
\]

In words: it is the old four-row map together with one additional source-prescribed conductor Gysin factor. The tensor is ordered `(X0,X2,X4)` and includes its Koszul differential sign.

Its eight components are

\[
\rho_E(e_H)=\left(\prod_{i\in H}X_i^{-1}\right)e_H,
\qquad H\subseteq\{0,2,4\}.
\]

In words: the bottom component is one and every other component is a pole only in its declared **output Cech summand**. No such pole is inserted into an original PC target stalk. The analogous plus-sheet formula uses `(X1,X3,X5)`.

At the conductor, every nonempty localized output vanishes. The complete map still has its bottom unit. It represents the three-variable Gysin extension; it is not an unshifted evaluation of a triple-pole element. Its determinant and degree are part of the map.

The actual collars independently produce the same endpoint monomials. For the marked midpoint m and the two physical endpoints, the reconstructed chains satisfy

\[
\begin{aligned}
\partial h_+&=X_{D03}X_1X_3[m]-X_1X_3X_5[v_+],\\
\partial h_-&=X_{D03}X_1X_3[m]-X_0X_2X_4[v_-].
\end{aligned}
\]

In words: the plus collar is the source's one-edge path to the midpoint, and the minus collar is its three-edge path. In normalized flags they have two and six terms. The checker derives these equations from the actual noncrossing faces and their occurrence-weighted incidences [S2, S3], and verifies all six transported versions.

In particular,

\[
X_0X_2X_4=X_0\,(X_2X_4).
\]

In words: the extra occurrence factor identified by the source conductor is also exactly the factor missing from the negative endpoint weight of the old residual-pair trace. This is a match of labelled coefficient data, not yet an identification of morphisms.

Raw specialization of both collar equations to the conductor gives zero on both sides. Thus scalar endpoint equations cannot decide the primitive Gysin attachment. The complete lower Koszul/Cech components and their degree shift must participate.

## 7. Matching degree-five values does not give a comparison

The preceding combined occurrence/selected-Rees operation had five occurrence directions, omitting X0. Its degree count suggested comparing it with the native degree-five conductor class. That suggestion has now been tested at the chain level rather than assumed.

Let

\[
J_5=(X_1,X_2,X_3,X_4,X_5),\qquad
K_5^\bullet=K^\bullet(X_1,X_2,X_3,X_4,X_5).
\]

In words: keep the entire five-variable Koszul cochain complex, not just its top cohomology. The order used previously differs only by its explicit exterior permutation.

A map whose top coefficient is one must have occurrence degree

\[
\nu=-e_{X_0}.
\]

In words: the native conductor determinant contains the missing X0 degree. This degree is allowed in the Hom calculation; the candidate is not rejected merely for a frame mismatch.

The complete homogeneous mapping complex has 0, 28, and 76 free coefficient columns in Hom degrees minus one, zero, and one. Its degree-zero differential has 28 unit pivots. Therefore

\[
Z^0\operatorname{Hom}_A(K_5^\bullet,\mathbb D_AB)_\nu=0.
\]

In words: no degree-zero cochain map in this frame exists at all, including one with top coefficient one. Every admissible polynomial matrix entry and replacement homotopy is included. The top unit entry is one of the 28 tested columns.

Completeness of the finite calculation follows from the fine grading. If S labels a source wedge and T a target dual generator, the coefficient of a homogeneous entry is forced to have exponent vector

\[
\nu-\sum_{i\in S}e_{X_i}+\sum_{j\in T}e_{X_j}.
\]

In words: each matrix entry is either forbidden by a negative polynomial exponent or is a unique monomial times an arbitrary integer/spectator coefficient. Hence the integer unit-rank calculation exhausts the homogeneous polynomial Hom complex; it is not a polynomial-degree truncation.

There is a second, intrinsic test. A section of the native truncation would have to lift the primitive map from its conductor module in degree five. Resolve that module by the full six-variable Koszul cochain complex shifted to have cohomology in degree five. Its complete degree-zero homogeneous Hom differential into the native dual has 49 columns and 49 unit pivots; the next group has 169 columns. There is no degree-minus-one column in the frame.

Thus the projection from the source dual to its conductor cohomology has **no primitive derived section**. This is the chain-level confirmation of the nonzero attachment in Section 4. It is not a no-go for all mixed-variance physical operations; it rules out replacing the complete native object by its isolated conductor class.

## 8. Why this still does not instantiate the physical connector 2-cells

The native normalization dual, its two attachment classes, and their conductor determinants are now explicit. The actual spatial endpoint collars are also explicit. They have not yet been identified as one morphism in the full PC/occurrence/Rees mapping diagram.

The inherited critical generic normal frame illustrates the problem. Its complete endpoint source-Hom complex is the two-term signed-unit complex on the residual-pair inputs:

\[
A\xrightarrow{-1}A.
\]

In words: the unmarked minus endpoint occurs twice, once on the excess-only input and once on the input including the complementary normal. Its differential is the existing source-normal multiplication after passing to the fixed normal degree. This contraction was verified in the preceding full polynomial calculation, which was rerun before the present continuation.

Changing the output to an occurrence-supported Cech complex cannot make an A-linearly contractible complex noncontractible. Consequently, simply tensoring that fixed generic-frame endpoint calculation with the new X0 support factor does not manufacture a primitive endpoint map. The separate nonzero endpoint residue channels use different source/normal determinant frames, as already recorded.

A valid spatial comparison must therefore do two things together: transport the **native nonzero attachment** and transport the **actual source normal frames**. Matching endpoint monomials or selecting a native conductor cohomology generator is not enough.

The old ordinary-blowdown generic morphism is still nullhomotopic. None of the constructions here rename that morphism or alter its nullhomotopy.

## 9. Symmetry and Rees tests

The native resolution carries the source symmetry strictly. Rotation permutes short coordinates by two steps; reflection sends short index i to `1-i`, exchanges the two branch resolutions, and changes the conductor-difference sign. These operations commute with every differential and satisfy every group composition on the full 80-column resolution. No averaging is used.

On the full six-coordinate conormal determinant, reflection has sign minus one. The conductor-difference character also has sign minus one. Therefore the framed native degree-five conductor class is reflection-even. The two degree-three branch classes are exchanged with their determinant signs.

This is a calculation of the **native source-dual character**. It is not a selection of the physical comparison torsor, which depends on an as-yet-unconstructed map to the framed spatial target.

For the declared Rees equations on the three odd coordinates, the occurrence residues may be expressed as `t_i/u_i` in a target summand localizing the product `u_i=t_iX_i`. At a central Rees face such a localized column disappears; the bottom column remains. The complete six-occurrence tensor comparison is checked on all eight Rees faces. The independent excess factor is retained with coefficient one in both of its grades, not replaced by a product of target normal parameters.

These tensor checks concern the specified support operations. They do not assert that adding the missing occurrence factor constructs a new native six-functor correspondence. In particular a nonflat specialization of the singular normalization source must retain its derived resolution rather than treating the displayed cohomology groups as a split substitute.
