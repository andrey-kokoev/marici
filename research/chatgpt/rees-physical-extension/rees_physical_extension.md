# Rees-chart lift audit and the corrected endpoint extension

## Verdict

There are three distinct conclusions.

1. A proper two-chart completion of the source Rees chart has an explicit relative-dualizing coefficient trace. It preserves the base coefficients, their generic localizations, and derived Cartier restriction. This repairs the raw single-chart coefficient mismatch.
2. That scalar trace does **not** supply the missing branch-selected endpoint comparison. The ordinary crossing comparison kills the first Tor generator for every possible chain-level choice. Independently, the relative normalization quotient does not commute with specialization of the Rees family. These are explicit obstructions to two candidate identifications, not a no-go theorem for logarithmic or nearby-cycle constructions.
3. Using the correct sheet-difference/road-sum marking, extension in the already specified endpoint coefficient models is contractible. This conclusion is not obtained by renaming the earlier common-conductor-value marking. It does not establish extension in an as-yet-unidentified full geometric support-PC/Q model.

Repository inputs are pinned to `andrey-kokoev/marici` at commit `d1947b67a60d3e88ba77f4ca60ea02c2a306ee61`. No repository files were changed.

## 1. The actual Rees chart

Let k be the integers, or a regular polynomial/Laurent coefficient base of the kind used in the source. Put

\[
S=k[X,u],\qquad B=S[t]/(u-Xt)\cong k[X,t].
\]

In words: the occurrence coordinate X and normal coordinate u are independent on the base; on the Rees chart the new coordinate t satisfies u equals X times t. This is the chart used in the source's line-cancellation and raw-stalk audits. [S1, S2]

On the radial open where u is inverted,

\[
B[u^{-1}]=k[X^{\pm1},u^{\pm1}],\qquad C=S[u^{-1}]=k[X,u^{\pm1}].
\]

In words: the raw chart inverts X as well, whereas the prescribed finite coefficient stalk does not. The map from C to the raw ring is localization at X, not an equivalence. The source already detects this distinction. [S1]

The precise quotient is

\[
L=C[X^{-1}]/C.
\]

In words: L consists of the additional negative occurrence powers. The class of X to the power minus m is nonzero and is annihilated by X to the power m. This is occurrence-parameter torsion, not integer-prime torsion.

Applying derived restriction to X=0 gives

\[
(C[X^{-1}])\otimes_C^{\mathbf L} C/(X)=0,
\qquad
L\otimes_C^{\mathbf L}C/(X)\simeq C/(X)[1].
\]

In words: the raw localized chart loses the whole Cartier fibre. The quotient retains it in a shifted degree. These are cohomological shifts. Thus replacing the finite stalk by the one raw chart would destroy exactly the data needed for a closed comparison.

There is a second direct warning. The earlier split node over S has independent branch coordinates xi and eta with their product zero. Assigning xi to X and eta to t cannot define an S-algebra map into B, because

\[
X t=u\ne0.
\]

In words: identifying a branch with an occurrence coordinate is not a coefficient-only base change. That substitution becomes available only after specialization to u=0.

## 2. A proper coefficient repair, constructed explicitly

The source identifies this chart as the X-chart of the blowup of the ideal (X,u). [S2] Retain both charts of that blowup:

\[
p:Y=\operatorname{Bl}_{(X,u)}\operatorname{Spec}S\longrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}S,
\]

\[
U_X=\operatorname{Spec}S[t]/(u-Xt),\qquad
U_u=\operatorname{Spec}S[s]/(X-us).
\]

In words: the missing second chart has coordinate s, and on the overlap s is the inverse of t. The blowup is the proper Rees completion determined by the given ideal. Its construction and affine charts are standard. [M1]

Let E be its exceptional divisor and T the strict transform of u=0. Their Cartier divisors satisfy

\[
p^*(u=0)=E+T,
\qquad
\kappa=\mathcal O_Y(-T)\otimes p^*(u)^\vee\cong\mathcal O_Y(E).
\]

In words: tensor the strict-transform ideal with the dual of the base normal ideal. On the X-chart its generator is t divided by the *line* generated by Xt, so it is the dualizing symbol 1/X; on the u-chart its generator is 1/u. This notation describes invertible sheaves. It does not invert X or u in the base ring.

Both Y and the base are smooth of the same relative dimension over k. Taking the ratio of their top differential lines gives

\[
p^!\mathcal O_S\simeq\kappa.
\]

In words: kappa is the relative dualizing line, with no dimension shift. Explicitly, pullback of dX wedge du is X times dX wedge dt on the first chart. Choosing the opposite ordered differential on the second chart gives its generator 1/u and transition 1/t. Smoothness of the blowup in this regular centre is also covered by [M2].

### Exact two-chart computation

On the overlap, use the monomials

\[
X^a t^b e_X,\qquad a\ge0,\quad b\in\mathbb Z,
\qquad e_X=1/X.
\]

In words: these are all overlap sections of the dualizing line. A monomial extends to U-X precisely when b is nonnegative. It extends to U-u precisely when b is at most a minus one.

Every overlap monomial extends to at least one chart. A section extends to both exactly when

\[
0\le b\le a-1,
\qquad
X^a t^b e_X=X^{a-b-1}u^b.
\]

In words: the common sections are exactly the base polynomials. Every other monomial contributes a two-term Cech block with differential plus or minus one. Therefore

\[
\mathbf R p_*\kappa\simeq S[0].
\]

In words: the complete proper trace retains all base coefficients and has no higher direct image. This argument applies coefficientwise in every polynomial degree and introduces no integer denominators.

There is an S-linear quasi-isomorphism from S into the two-chart Cech complex, sending f to its two local representatives fX e-X and fu e-u. Its inverse in the derived category is the relative-dualizing trace: on the dense open where the blowup is an isomorphism it is the identity, and this fixes its multiplication factor globally. We do not claim that a monomial projection from arbitrary Cech cochains is an S-linear chain map.

The analogous calculation with the structure sheaf has chart conditions b nonnegative and b at most a. It proves that the derived pushforward of the structure sheaf is S as well.

### What this repairs and what it does not

Over either X nonzero or u nonzero, the complete blowup is an isomorphism. In particular, retaining the u-chart restores the occurrence divisor missing from the raw radial X-chart. This is why the proper pushforward recovers C rather than C with X inverted.

Tensoring the S-linear trace roof with any coefficient Koszul complex preserves it. For example,

\[
(\mathbf R p_*\kappa)\otimes_S^{\mathbf L}K_S(X)
\simeq K_S(X).
\]

In words: the trace preserves derived Cartier restriction, not just its value on the generic unit. The same holds with K of u or their tensor product. For restriction along either single divisor, its equation remains a non-zero-divisor on both charts, so the ordinary fibre square is Tor-independent and proper duality base change applies. [M3] At the simultaneous centre, one must retain the derived fibre; an underived replacement would drop excess Tor.

A local branch power t to the m is not a global scalar function on the blowup. Multiplying it by X to the m gives u to the m, which does extend and is retained. For dualizing sections the analogous extending representative is X to the m-plus-one times t to the m times e-X. Thus global gluing—not arbitrary scalar evaluation—separates local branch coordinates from genuine base coefficients.

This construction does not give a map from the single affine ideal tB to S normalized by t mapping to one. That local symbol alone does not give a global dualizing section. It therefore does not evade or contradict the source's direct-affine trace no-go.

There is also a special-fibre caution. The restriction of the invertible ideal of T to the nodal divisor is not automatically its image ideal (t). On the X-chart multiplication by t on k[X,t]/(Xt) has kernel (X). Derived restriction retains this kernel. Silently replacing the invertible restriction by the image ideal would again lose a grade.

## 3. The endpoint crossing: a complete ordinary comparison calculation

The scalar repair does not identify the product divisor with the intersection of its two branches. The source explicitly distinguishes these support problems. [S2]

Work over B=k[X,t] and write homological Koszul complexes

\[
K(Xt)=[B e_u\xrightarrow{Xt}B],
\]

\[
K(X,t)=[B(e_X\wedge e_t)
\xrightarrow{(-t,X)^T}B e_X\oplus B e_t
\xrightarrow{(X,t)}B].
\]

In words: the first resolves the product divisor, while the second resolves the crossing. Their chain degrees run down to zero on the right. These are different support objects.

The two natural choices extending the degree-zero identity are

\[
f_X(e_u)=t e_X,\qquad f_t(e_u)=X e_t.
\]

In words: choose which factor of u supplies the normal comparison. They already admit a homotopy:

\[
f_t(e_u)-f_X(e_u)
=d(e_X\wedge e_t).
\]

In words: changing the branch choice is accounted for by the actual degree-two Koszul generator. The problem is not an absence of a homotopy between these two choices.

In fact every B-linear chain map with degree-zero identity has the form

\[
f_h(e_u)=t(1-h)e_X+Xh e_t,\qquad h\in B.
\]

In words: the regular-sequence syzygy describes all possible choices. To prove this, the chain equation is X a plus t b equals Xt. Subtracting the particular solution (t,0) leaves a syzygy of the regular sequence (X,t), hence a unique multiple of (-t,X).

At the conductor C defined by X=t=0,

\[
H_1(f_h\otimes_B k)=0
\quad\text{for every }h.
\]

In words: every ordinary comparison kills the first Tor class, including after all branch-choice homotopies are retained. A demand that this map preserve a nonzero first-Tor generator therefore has no solution in this ordinary comparison space. This is a scoped statement about this specified support comparison, not about every possible physical construction.

The derived conductor fibres of the source and target have ranks one-one and one-two-one. The induced degree-zero map is the identity and the degree-one map is zero. The cone has

\[
H_1(\operatorname{Cone}(f_h)\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}k)\cong k^2,
\qquad
H_2(\operatorname{Cone}(f_h)\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}k)\cong k^2.
\]

In words: the failure is measured by explicit free classes, not an integer-prime torsion effect. There is no other homology in this derived fibre. Before taking that fibre, the cone is the ideal (X,t) in the product-divisor ring, placed in homological degree one.

### Independent normalization base-change obstruction

The total affine Rees chart B is normal, so its relative normalization cofiber is zero. After specialization u=0 the ring becomes the split node,

\[
A_0=k[X,t]/(Xt),\qquad
N_0=k[X]\oplus k[t],
\qquad
\operatorname{cofib}(A_0\longrightarrow N_0)\simeq k_{\mathrm{or}}[0].
\]

In words: the special fibre acquires the conductor difference class. Derived specialization of the zero total normalization cofiber is still zero; relative normalization of the special fibre is not. The defect of this candidate base-change identification is exactly the displayed conductor line.

This is the endpoint difference class, not the common conductor-value unit. A nonzero generic Q datum cannot be supplied by simply using the relative normalization cofiber of the smooth total chart and declaring that it specializes to this class.

Thus a proper scalar trace, a special nodal normalization quotient, and the generic logarithmic interval computation are not yet one identified functor. The remaining map must use an explicitly defined logarithmic/nearby-cycle or other variance-sensitive excess construction. We have not manufactured that map by an equality of primitive signatures.

## 4. Corrected boundary-to-interior extension

This part is a new calculation of the specified coefficient model, with its correct endpoint marking. It does not assume that the obstruction in Section 3 has disappeared.

Let P-R be the full polynomial conductor-road complex from the preceding construction. Write its degree-zero cohomological version as

\[
\mathcal E_R^q=(P_R)_{1-q}.
\]

In words: its physical degree-one homology is placed at cohomological degree zero. Retain the original differential with these explicit indices.

The source readout and primitive representative are

\[
\epsilon(s,q)=q_0+q_1+q_2,
\qquad z=((1,0);(1,0,0)).
\]

In words: the sheet difference and road sum are both one. The former common-conductor pair with zero roads is a boundary, not z.

There is an explicit integral R-linear strong contraction of E-R onto R with section z. Besides sending a positive sheet term to its identical node relation, it sends the endpoint generator to the first constant sheet, the second constant sheet to the diagonal node, road one to tags one plus two, road two to tag two, and tag zero to the top norm generator. All other basis values vanish. Direct substitution gives

\[
dh+hd=1-z\epsilon,\qquad h^2=0,\qquad h(z)=0.
\]

In words: the complete endpoint complex—not only the positive branch ideal—retracts onto the correct physical readout line. This particular contraction is only R-linear and fixes labels; it is not asserted to preserve all unspecified support conditions or provide an invariant representative under every admitted symmetry.

### The framed primitive loaded model

Let I and B-boundary be the previously computed full and boundary loaded complexes, with the constructed comparison Phi. Define

\[
W_I=I_R\otimes_R\mathcal E_R,\qquad
W_\partial=B_{\partial,R}\otimes_R\mathcal E_R,
\qquad
\Psi=\Phi\otimes1.
\]

In words: attach the actual endpoint coefficient packet to each loaded generator. This is an explicitly defined coefficient construction. Identifying it with the entire geometric support-PC model is not assumed.

The corrected markings are

\[
\widetilde\omega_I=\omega_I\otimes z,\qquad
\widetilde\omega_\partial=\omega_\partial\otimes z,
\qquad
\Psi(\widetilde\omega_I)=\widetilde\omega_\partial.
\]

In words: the extension now carries a nonzero sheet-difference/road-sum state. It does not transport a common-conductor unit into that state.

The endpoint contraction tensored with a cochain a acquires the sign given by the parity of the degree of a. This gives a contraction natural under degree-preserving restriction. The polynomial comparison and boundary restriction therefore form a commuting square, including its overlap homotopies.

Both unshifted marking spaces have components R and no higher homotopy; restriction is the identity on R. Consequently

\[
\operatorname{hofib}_{\widetilde\omega_\partial}
\left(\operatorname{Map}_{D(R)}(R,W_I)
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Map}_{D(R)}(R,W_\partial)\right)
\simeq *.
\]

In words: the fixed corrected boundary marking extends uniquely up to homotopy in this model.

The stable relative complex nevertheless satisfies

\[
\operatorname{fib}(\Psi)\simeq R^5[-2].
\]

In words: the five positive degree-two relative classes persist. They are not ambiguities of the degree-zero physical marking. This formula is for the framed primitive loaded model with fixed coefficient ring, not a claim that every nonconstant localization diagram has only five relative classes.

### The nonconstant coefficient diagram

For the already specified localization diagram, the endpoint quasi-isomorphisms E-R-j to R-j form a natural transformation. Taking derived sections commutes with restricting the indexing category to its boundary. The previous scalar computation gives degree-zero restriction equal to the identity on the unlocalized base ring R-zero, with no negative cohomology.

Thus every fixed endpoint coefficient also has a contractible extension space in this second model. Branch tails have no extra homology because they are matched by their actual node relations. Base occurrence/Rees polynomials remain present, and the positive relative cohomology retains its support-dependent structure. This argument does not replace the localization diagram by a constant one.

The proper scalar completion in Section 2 can be applied to these coefficient objects: the projection formula identifies pushforward of kappa tensored with their derived pullbacks with the same coefficient objects. Its trace is natural under the displayed restrictions. This preserves the computed extension but still does not identify the special branch-selected support functor rejected by the ordinary comparison in Section 3.

## 5. Scope of the result

What now works is a proper coefficient trace on the actual two-chart Rees blowup and a corrected endpoint-marking extension in the explicit linear models.

What fails is ordinary identification of the product-divisor Tor packet with the branch-intersection packet, and naive specialization of the total normalization quotient. The first failure persists for every branch-choice homotopy; the second is a nonzero special conductor line born on degeneration.

What remains unproved is an identified logarithmic branch-selected excess map retaining the required grades, the generic Q datum, both literal endpoint connectors, and their complete geometric comparison squares. Neither the original formal polynomial quasi-isomorphism nor the present proper scalar trace supplies that missing map automatically.

There is no asserted no-go theorem for all physical realizations, no RH consequence, and no claim of a new prime obstruction from the free ordinary cone groups.

The bundle contains the two original checkers and the new checker. Run:

```sh
python check_source_defined_branch_comparison.py --output endpoint_recheck.json
python check_boundary_interior_extension.py --output interior_recheck.json
python check_rees_physical_extension.py --output rees_physical_extension_certificate.json
```

The two re-executed checkers pass 2,966 and 292,493 exact assertions. The new checker passes 27,310 assertions. It tests the two-chart monomial conditions, the all-polynomial formula for ordinary crossing maps and their homotopy, induced conductor maps and cone ranks, normalization matrices, the strong endpoint contraction, cochain tensor signs, all eight corrected marking restrictions, and all twelve marking overlaps.

Polynomial degree twelve bounds test inputs, not the ring or a quotient by higher powers. The all-degree proofs are the regular-sequence syzygy calculation, the exact normalization row, the explicit contractions, and the monomial partition argument. These are executable checks with mathematical proofs, not proof-assistant certification. The test count is not a substitute for those arguments.
