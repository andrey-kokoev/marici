# Finite Spectral Completion and Scale-Selection Audit

## Question

Does enriching the WP823 chain complex by its complete finite Dirac spectrum
make every acyclic mass unavoidable and thereby fix RG thresholds and the
physical portal scale?

## Minimal acyclic spectral sector

Replace the unlabelled unit contractible summand by the graded Dirac operator

\[
D_m=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&m\\
m&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad m>0.
\]

Its spectrum is \(\{-m,m\}\) and

\[
D_m^2=m^2I.
\]

Every \(m>0\) gives zero homology, so homology erases the mass. The full
spectral object retains it. For calibrated heat time \(\tau>0\),

\[
K(\tau)=\operatorname{Tr}e^{-\tau D_m^2}
=2e^{-\tau m^2}
\]

is strictly decreasing in \(m\). Spectral completion therefore repairs
WP823's mass fiber at the level of mathematical readout.

The sign \(m\mapsto-m\) is a grading conjugation and is not a physical portal
orientation. WP820's oriented charge source remains responsible for sign.

## Recording is not selecting

The spectral object contains \(m\); it does not explain its value. A normalized
heat-kernel spectral action,

\[
S(m,\Lambda)=2e^{-m^2/\Lambda^2},
\]

is invariant under

\[
(m,\Lambda)\mapsto(\lambda m,\lambda\Lambda).
\]

It has no positive finite stationary point in \(m\). It distinguishes the
dimensionless ratio \(m/\Lambda\) but cannot fix the common physical scale.

A polynomial spectral action

\[
S(z)=\alpha z+\beta z^2,
\qquad
z=\frac{m^2}{\Lambda^2},
\]

has the nonzero stationary ratio

\[
z_*=-\frac{\alpha}{2\beta}.
\]

The packets \((\alpha,\beta)=(-2,1)\) and \((-8,1)\) select \(z_*=1\) and
\(z_*=4\). Thus stationarity moves the tuning into the action coefficients.
Even after fixing \(z_*\), the cutoff \(\Lambda\) remains free.

Likewise, a direct sum of \(k\) identical acyclic sectors changes the heat
trace by \(k\). The spectrum faithfully records multiplicity but supplies no
principle selecting it.

## Threshold and RG parallelization

The eigenvalue \(m\) is a physical threshold coordinate. To complete WP821,
the source must prove that this same \(m\), the RG fixed point, and the
low-energy portal are related by one normalization. A list of Dirac
eigenvalues plus a separately chosen beta function does not provide that
parallelization.

The common rescaling kernel shows why a dimensionless spectral action cannot
set an absolute threshold. A gravitational, condensate, or clock scale could
break the kernel, but that scale must be source-derived and changes the
experiment's admitted reference structure.

## Physical instrument

The formal heat trace is not automatically a flavor instrument. The pair

\[
(m,\tau)=(1,1),
\qquad
(m,\tau)=(2,1/4)
\]

gives the same heat record. Calibrated heat time is a physical clock port.
Two calibrated heat moments are locally faithful on \((m,\tau)\), but no
current detector realizes them or maps them into physical16. Detector gain is
another independent attachment.

## Aspect germ audit

The native object contains the full complex, Dirac operator, spectral action,
cutoff clock, oriented inflow, mediator, physical16 map, and detector. Spectral
completion passes the fiber-repair gate. Dirac-mass selection, action-
coefficient authority, absolute cutoff, threshold/RG parallelization,
physical16 descent, and calibrated instrumentation remain open.

## Classification and falsifiers

Finite spectral completion is a faithful carrier and threshold record, not a
selector.

The smallest exact falsifiers are:

1. the common rescaling \((m,\Lambda)\mapsto(\lambda m,\lambda\Lambda)\);
2. the absence of a finite positive stationary point for the heat action;
3. the two polynomial coefficient packets selecting different ratios;
4. arbitrary acyclic multiplicity \(k\);
5. the mass–clock heat-record pair;
6. independent detector gain.

## Deutschian appraisal

“The complete spectrum is the source” is better than “homology is the source”
because it retains every physically active contractible sector. But it still
describes rather than explains the spectrum. A hard-to-vary explanation must
derive the finite Dirac operator, the spectral-action coefficients, and the
absolute clock or cutoff from a deeper constructor.

The sharp successor is to test whether a normalized index pairing or
quantized spectral-flow condition can fix a dimensionless Dirac eigenvalue
without a freely chosen potential, and whether dimensional transmutation from
the same gauge–Yukawa action can fix the common scale. The two mechanisms must
share one source clock before their outputs can be multiplied.

## Disposition

Progressive fiber repair, negative selection result. Full spectral data
restores mass faithfulness, but spectral action and heat probes leave
coefficient, scale, clock, threshold-parallelization, and detector gates open.
