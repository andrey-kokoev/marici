# Common-gain reweighting-map classification: WP1142

## Question

Do \(q\), \(r\), and common gain \(3/2\) uniquely classify the reweighting
map?

## DPC resolution

- **Problem:** determine whether target compatibility selects complete uniform
  mixing.
- **Conjecture:** the common-gain constraint uniquely selects \(U=J_6/6\).
- **Rivals:** rank-one complete mixing; rank-two stochastic map; unique
  source-compatible map; underdetermined convex map polytope.
- **Risky consequences:** \(Mq=r/g=u\), row-stochastic nonnegative \(M\),
  exact rank-one and rank-two solutions, and different responses to a
  concentrated hostile source.
- **Falsification attempt:** a rank-two stochastic matrix maps \(q\) to \(u\)
  and distinguishes a concentrated source, so \(q,r\) do not uniquely select
  \(U\).
- **Residual:** physical production/decay locality may select a map inside the
  convex polytope.
- **Disposition:** reject uniqueness of complete mixing and classify the
  executable map family.

## Exact classification

The common-gain condition is

\[
Mq=\frac{r}{3/2}=\left(\frac16,\ldots,\frac16\right)=u.
\]

Every solution has the form \(M=U+A\) with \(A\mathbf 1=0\) and \(Aq=0\).
Before positivity this is a 24-dimensional affine space; nonnegativity cuts
out a convex polytope but does not restore uniqueness. A rank-two nonnegative
example is generated by rows

\[
p_+=\left(\frac5{24},\frac18,\frac5{36},\frac7{36},\frac16,\frac16\right),
\quad
p_-=\left(\frac18,\frac5{24},\frac7{36},\frac5{36},\frac16,\frac16\right).
\]
