# Dual gauge-running selector gate

## Common high-scale domain

WP490 audits the full WP489 matter content above both messenger thresholds.
It uses the same one-loop convention as WP483,

\[
\beta_g=-{b_0g^3\over16\pi^2}.
\]

For \(SO(3)_P\), the vector representation has Dynkin index two. There are 18
Dirac port vectors from the second messenger stage: two charge sectors, three
colors, and three flavor components. The real scalar port vectors are the eight
flavor-adjoint components of \(X_i\) plus the three connector rows.

## Exact coefficients

The admitted flavor coefficient and the newly derived port coefficient are

\[
b_{0F}=-{37\over2},
\qquad
b_{0P}=-{133\over3}.
\]

Both gauge beta functions are strictly positive at every positive nonzero
coupling. Gauge-only one-loop running therefore has no finite interacting
fixed point for either factor.

## Ratio ray

The simultaneous system does possess the exact one-loop ratio trajectory

\[
{g_P^2\over g_F^2}={111\over266}.
\]

It is the asymptotic ratio near the Gaussian infrared limit. It does not fix a
finite nonzero value of \(g_F\), and therefore cannot select a nonzero numerical
value of \(g_Ff_{\rm phys}/v\). Threshold matching also interrupts the
full-matter ray at the messenger scales.

WP489's existence witness \(g_P^2/g_F^2=1/68\) is not on this ray. Its role
remains proof that the width cone is nonempty, not RG selection.

## Disposition

Gauge running supplies a source-derived ratio ray but no finite coupling
selector. The successor must derive the complete gauge–Yukawa beta system and
threshold matching. Only a controlled interacting fixed point or an
independently declared boundary operation can select the finite gauge coupling
needed by the physical clock prediction.
