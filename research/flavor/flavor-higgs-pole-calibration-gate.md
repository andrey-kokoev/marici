# Higgs-pole calibration gate

## Physical instrument

WP470 attaches WP469's benchmark scalar packet to the first calibrated
instrument it necessarily addresses: the observed Higgs-channel pole. The 2025
Particle Data Group listing gives

\[
m_H=125.20\mathbin{\pm}0.11\ {\rm GeV}.
\]

For a bounded exact comparison this packet uses `v=246.22 GeV` and the
Gaussian working interval `m_H plus or minus 1.96 times 0.11 GeV`. No Higgs
measurement is used to choose a source coefficient.

## Benchmark poles in detector units

At the WP469 unit benchmark, `v^2=2w^2`. The three radial masses are therefore

\[
m_-={v\over\sqrt2}\sqrt{206-2\sqrt{10009}},
\qquad m_D=\sqrt6v,
\qquad
m_+={v\over\sqrt2}\sqrt{206+2\sqrt{10009}}.
\]

Numerically they are approximately `423.256`, `603.113`, and `3508.484 GeV`.
Every pole has a strictly positive Higgs-radial residue. The lightest possible
Higgs-channel pole is therefore already more than `298 GeV` above the upper
edge of the frozen 95-percent working interval.

## Disposition

The unit WP467–WP469 benchmark is rejected as a physical Higgs-sector model.
This does not refute the common-dilaton relational selector family: varying
`eta`, `a`, `kappa`, or other source coefficients can move the radial poles.
But choosing them to reproduce 125.20 GeV would be detector calibration, not
source-derived numerical selection. It also changes the pole projectors, so
WP468–WP469 residues cannot be retained.

The first nonfaithful arrow is not detector resolution; it is the absence of a
model pole in the calibrated Higgs support. Computing widths for the rejected
benchmark would not repair this failure.

## Smallest falsifier and reopening rule

The smallest exact falsifier is

\[
m_->125.20+1.96(0.11)\ {\rm GeV}.
\]

Reopening requires an independently frozen coefficient-selection mechanism,
followed by a fresh full Hessian and residue calculation. Only then may the
observed Higgs mass and width be used as withheld tests, and scalar decay widths
be composed with detector likelihoods.
