# Dissipative-Attractor Reservoir-Port Audit

## Question

Can an autonomous irreversible operation evade WP810's conjugation pair by
driving every admissible flavor state to one signed attractor, while also
providing the required RG basin, threshold survival, and physical instrument?

## Admitted source and physical-time map

The effective source is a two-sector open system with orientations \(-\) and
\(+\), coupled to a Markovian reservoir. Physical time is explicit: the source
semigroup parameter \(\tau\) is calibrated by the reservoir clock. The forward
population generator is

\[
L_+=
\begin{pmatrix}
-\gamma&0\\
\gamma&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \gamma>0.
\]

This is the population restriction of a one-way GKLS jump from \(-\) to
\(+\). The faithful orientation observable is

\[
G=\operatorname{diag}(-g_0,+g_0),
\]

which stands for a weak-basis-invariant signed portal coordinate in the
`physical16` quotient, not a texture label.

## Exact positive result

The stationary kernel of \(L_+\) is one-dimensional and generated by
\(p_*=(0,1)^T\). Its nonzero eigenvalue is \(-\gamma\). For initial negative
population \(r\),

\[
p(\tau)=
\begin{pmatrix}
r e^{-\gamma\tau}\\
1-r e^{-\gamma\tau}
\end{pmatrix}
\longrightarrow
\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Thus the effective operation is a genuine selector, not a rigidifier: every
initial probability state lies in the basin of the same signed attractor. A
positive renormalization \(\gamma\mapsto c\gamma\) preserves the stationary
state for \(c>0\), so the selection survives any admitted threshold that does
not close or reverse the dissipative channel.

Jump counting is an executable instrument. Over a physical duration \(\tau\),
its click probability is

\[
P_{-\to+}=r(1-e^{-\gamma\tau}).
\]

This is more than a formal coordinate: the operation, disturbance, temporal
support, and record are all typed.

## Microscopic reversal audit

The effective arrow is not self-authorizing. An inverted reservoir produces

\[
L_-=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&\gamma\\
0&-\gamma
\end{pmatrix},
\]

whose unique attractor is the mirror state \((1,0)^T\). The two generators have
identical spectra and basin dimensions. Their difference is the physical state
of the reservoir: relaxing versus inverted. A unitary system--reservoir
dilation therefore relocates the conjugation choice into reservoir preparation
rather than eliminating it.

This is not a merely philosophical objection. The reservoir state fixes which
jump is present, its rate \(\gamma\), and the direction of the physical-time
contract. Unless that state and its low-entropy orientation are themselves
derived, the effective selector is a reference/preparation port.

## Magnitude, thresholds, and readout

The attractor fixes the sign of \(G\) but not its eigenvalue scale \(g_0\).
The steady detector record is

\[
R=s g_0,
\]

where \(s) is detector gain. The exact hostile pair

\[
(g_0,s)=(1,2),\qquad(2,1)
\]

gives the same record. Its response Jacobian has rank one on a two-dimensional
source--calibration domain. Jump counting separately calibrates \(\gamma\),
not \(g_0\).

A threshold change can preserve the attractor while changing
\(\gamma\) and hence the relaxation clock. Therefore stationary-state
survival is weaker than survival of the complete executable experiment. The
detector duration, bandwidth, and gain must be transported through the same
threshold packet.

## Contextual partition and classification

- Effective semigroup with fixed reservoir: singleton attractor; genuine
  selector with a global basin.
- Microscopic source including reservoir preparation: two mirror packets,
  relaxing and inverted.
- Positive rate-renormalizing thresholds: preserve sign selection but not its
  clock.
- Jump-count instrument: executable and calibrated for rate response.
- Signed steady readout: retains a one-dimensional magnitude--gain kernel.

The operation is therefore both a selector and a physical probe at the
effective level. It is neither an unconditional microscopic selector nor a
complete portal-magnitude instrument.

## Smallest exact falsifier

The pair \((L_+,L_-)\) is the smallest falsifier. Both are completely positive
one-way dynamics with the same spectrum and opposite unique attractors. A
claim that irreversibility alone selects the observed orientation must explain
why the source prepares one reservoir and excludes the other.

## Deutschian appraisal

The one-way channel explains the sign, basin, and operational readout once its
reservoir is admitted. It is a good effective explanation because those
features are linked and hard to vary independently. It is not yet the desired
fundamental explanation: changing only the reservoir preparation reverses the
answer without violating the microscopic grammar, while \(g_0\) and detector
gain remain free.

The progressive successor is not another equilibrium potential. It is a
source-derived low-entropy boundary condition or autonomous chiral reservoir
whose preparation, jump normalization, threshold clock, and detector coupling
all descend from one constructor. That proposal must state explicitly that
physical time and orientation enter through the reservoir state.

## Assumptions and falsifiers

The result assumes Markovian GKLS dynamics, a positive one-way rate, and an
admitted physical-time calibration. A closed microscopic construction that
uniquely prepares the relaxing reservoir and forbids its inverted partner
would falsify the residual preparation fiber. To solve the flavor problem it
must additionally derive \(g_0\), the complete threshold response, and the
`physical16` detector gain.

## Disposition

Mixed. Dissipative attraction is the first tested operation that genuinely
selects a sign, supplies a global basin, and has an executable instrument. Its
microscopic authority still comes from an oriented reservoir preparation, and
it leaves portal magnitude and detector calibration underdetermined.
