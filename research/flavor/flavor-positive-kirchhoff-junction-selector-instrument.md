# Positive Kirchhoff Junction Selector and Instrument

## Question

Can one positive source constructor simultaneously fix WP858's spectral
ordering and supply WP855's complementary readout port?

## Unweighted Kirchhoff incidence

For unit return phase \(z\), take the primitive normalized common-junction row

\[
M_z=\frac1{\sqrt2}(1,z).
\]

Its positive Kirchhoff Gram is

\[
H_z=M_z^*M_z
=\frac12
\begin{pmatrix}
1&z\\
\overline z&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

This is an orthogonal rank-one projector. Its unique zero ray and positive ray
are

\[
d_z=\frac1{\sqrt2}(-z,1)^T,
\qquad
b_z=\frac1{\sqrt2}(z,1)^T.
\]

Therefore positivity fixes the ordering that WP858 previously had to assume:
the antisymmetric ray has zero junction cost, while the symmetric ray has unit
cost. Multiplying the Gram by any positive source coefficient changes the gap
but not the selected ray.

The equal weights are forced if the common junction is reciprocal under
exchange of its two marked inputs. For a positive normalized row \((a,b)\),
exchange invariance gives \(a=b\), while \(a^2+b^2=1\) then gives

\[
a=b=\frac1{\sqrt2}.
\]

Thus the remaining source assumption is endpoint reciprocity, not a fitted
equality of two junction couplings.

## Canonical zero-temperature lowering

Because both eigenspaces are one-dimensional, the lowering dissipator
generated by

\[
L=\sqrt\kappa\,|d_z\rangle\langle b_z|
\]

is unique up to a phase that cancels from the Lindblad generator. Its
Liouvillian spectrum is

\[
0,
\quad -\kappa,
\quad -\kappa/2,
\quad -\kappa/2.
\]

The unique stationary state is
\(|d_z\rangle\langle d_z|\), and every density matrix on the normalized
two-path carrier converges to it. This supplies a complete basin once the
unit-norm two-path carrier is admitted by the lossless WP854 source.

This is a zero-temperature ordering statement. Adding the reverse jump gives
a mixed finite-temperature fixed point and reopens WP858's coherence hostile.

## Complementary physical port

The Kirchhoff row has a unique orthonormal completion up to output phases:

\[
U_z=\frac1{\sqrt2}
\begin{pmatrix}
1&z\\
-1&z
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The first output is the common bright port. The second is the complementary
difference port. On the selected ray,

\[
M_zd_z=0,
\qquad
D_zd_z=z.
\]

Thus the same source junction makes the selected state dark in the ordinary
contact channel and unit-bright in a complementary channel. The return-port
phase supplies the relational reference. No projector is fitted from the
desired answer: the second row is forced by lossless completion of the
source-derived first row.

This is an executable source-level two-port instrument. A flavor experiment
must still realize both outputs with the same external quantum numbers,
finite-width dynamics, and calibrated detector response.

## Weighted-junction hostile

Positivity and rank one alone do not fix equal magnitudes. The legal weighted
row

\[
M_{1,2}=\frac1{\sqrt5}(1,2)
\]

selects the different dark ray \((-2,1)^T/\sqrt5\). The unweighted primitive
incidence must therefore descend from WP854's unit link amplitudes and
microscopic endpoint reciprocity. If edge conductances renormalize independently,
the predicted \(1/\sqrt2\) is lost.

## Threshold condition

A threshold map preserves the full result only if it is an isometry
intertwining both rows of \(U_z\). Merely preserving the dark ray is weaker:
WP856's \(T_{3/4}\) maps the dark vector to half itself, leaving its projective
class unchanged while halving the physical amplitude. Threshold survival must
therefore be stated for the normalized two-port scattering matrix, not just
the vacuum ray or stationary density matrix.

## Classification

Conditional source-generated selector and source-level instrument. The
positive unweighted Kirchhoff junction fixes relative sign, component
magnitude, spectral ordering, and a complete lowering basin; its lossless
completion supplies the necessary complementary readout. Remaining authority
is concentrated in four interfaces: microscopic equality of the two junction
weights, zero-temperature flavor-RG interpretation, isometric threshold
transport of the complete two-port matrix, and calibrated `physical16`
realization. Reciprocity must itself survive the oriented boundary
construction; it cannot be inferred merely from the selected equal weights.
