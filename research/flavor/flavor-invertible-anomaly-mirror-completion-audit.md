# Invertible-Anomaly Mirror-Completion Audit

## Question

Can a nonzero global anomaly or cobordism index evade WP808 by making one
orientation intrinsically consistent while excluding its mirror, thereby
supplying the asymmetric flavor portal?

## Admitted source and faithful quotient

The source packet consists of a boundary anomaly class \(a\) in a finite
abelian group \(A\), together with an invertible bulk phase whose class cancels
it. This is the finite exact model of the general formulation in which an
anomalous theory is relative to an invertible field theory. Orientation
reversal acts by inversion:

\[
\iota(a)=-a.
\]

The faithful flavor coordinate remains the weak-basis invariant orientation
\(J=\operatorname{Im}\det[H_u,H_d]\). Anomaly labels descend under weak-basis
changes, unlike texture-loop phases. The relevant second quotient is the
orientation groupoid acting on the full boundary--bulk source, not the
measured-ten projection.

## Exact mirror-completion theorem

Cancellation pairs a boundary class \(a\) with bulk class \(-a\):

\[
a+(-a)=0.
\]

Orientation reversal produces the equally consistent packet

\[
(a,-a)\longmapsto(-a,a).
\]

There are only two cases.

If \(a\ne-a\), the anomaly is signed, but the two cancellation packets form a
distinct mirror pair. Consistency does not choose either member.

If \(a=-a\), then \(2a=0\). The class is self-inverse and supplies no pair of
opposite anomaly labels from which an absolute sign could be selected.

Therefore no finite invertible anomaly class is simultaneously signed and a
singleton under orientation reversal. This is not a numerical scan claim; the
checker scans finite examples only as exact witnesses of the two-case proof.

The smallest signed example is \(A=\mathbb Z_3\): \(1\leftrightarrow2\). Both
packets \((1,2)\) and \((2,1)\) cancel. The smallest nonzero self-inverse
example is \(A=\mathbb Z_2\), where \(1=-1\), so the anomaly label itself has
no oriented sign.

## What anomaly data really provide

Anomaly matching protects \(a\) under RG transport, and gapped vectorlike
threshold pairs shift the class by zero. This is genuine source authority for
topological persistence. It does not determine a continuous portal magnitude,
the relevant RG trajectory or crossover scale, a massive vacuum, or detector
gain.

An oriented character measurement can distinguish \(a\) from \(-a\) when
they differ. But it is a readout of a prepared member, not a selector. The
instrument must already know which orientation evaluates the phase rather
than its conjugate. Fixing that orientation replaces the full groupoid by a
stabilizer groupoid and creates a relational experiment.

## Contextual partition

- Full orientation groupoid: every signed class lies in the orbit
  \(\{a,-a\}\).
- Two-torsion sector: the orbit is a singleton, but the anomaly label is
  sign-blind.
- Fixed-orientation stabilizer: an oriented character can separate the signed
  pair, conditional on the added port.
- RG and symmetry-preserving gapped thresholds: preserve each admitted anomaly
  class and hence preserve, rather than remove, the mirror partition.
- Calibrated `physical16` probes: absent. Anomaly response has a kernel along
  continuous portal magnitude and detector calibration.

## Selector classification and smallest falsifier

The invertible bulk is an anomaly canceller, topological rigidifier, and
RG/threshold protector. It is not an absolute orientation selector, portal
magnitude selector, or calibrated physical instrument.

The smallest exact falsifier is the \(\mathbb Z_3\) pair

\[
(a,b)=(1,2),\qquad(2,1).
\]

Both satisfy \(a+b=0\), have conjugate oriented responses, and are exchanged
by the full orientation groupoid. Choosing the first is additional source
data.

## Deutschian appraisal

Invertible-anomaly consistency explains why boundary and bulk indices must
cancel and why the relation survives RG and gapped thresholds. It cannot
explain why one absolute orientation exists: its own group law constructs the
inverse completion. The explanation is hard to vary, but it explains the
failure of the proposed selector rather than the observed portal.

The next source candidate must fall outside this invertible-pair architecture.
It must either provide a non-invertible chiral construction whose opposite is
not an object of the same admitted source category, or derive an orientation-
breaking microscopic law together with its coefficient. In either case, the
absence of the mirror must be proved, not stipulated.

## Assumptions, instrument, and falsifiers

The theorem applies to invertible anomaly classes forming an abelian group and
to ordinary orientation reversal by the group inverse. It does not by itself
classify non-invertible topological orders or nonunitary theories. A decisive
falsifier would be a unitary, anomaly-complete source whose signed global class
has no inverse object in its admitted physical category, together with an
executable preparation and calibrated `physical16` response.

Primary sources: Freed's [Anomalies and Invertible Field
Theories](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.7224), Witten and Yonekura's [Anomaly
Inflow and the Eta-Invariant](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08775), and the
flavor-facing finite-group analysis by Davighi, Gripaios, and Lohitsiri,
[Anomalies of Non-Abelian Finite Groups via
Cobordism](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.10700).

## Disposition

Negative but progressive. A global invertible anomaly can protect a selected
relative class but cannot make its absolute sign unavoidable. The surviving
frontier is a demonstrably non-mirror-completable non-invertible or microscopic
chiral source with co-generated portal magnitude and instrument.
