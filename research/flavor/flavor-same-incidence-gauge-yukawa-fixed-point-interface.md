# Same-Incidence Gauge–Yukawa Fixed-Point Interface

## Question

Can the same oriented chiral incidence that fixes the WP820 charge contrast
also derive a controlled nonzero gauge–Yukawa fixed point, attractive basin,
threshold-stable magnitude, and physical readout?

## Exact conditional fixed point

Write \(x=e^2\) for the normalized gauge coordinate and \(y=h^2\) for one
Yukawa coordinate. The minimal coupled flow

\[
\begin{aligned}
\beta_x&=2x^2(-b+cx-dy),\\
\beta_y&=2y(ay-fx)
\end{aligned}
\]

has the nonzero solution

\[
x_*=\frac{ab}{ac-df},
\qquad
y_*=\frac{bf}{ac-df}.
\]

It is positive when \(ac-df>0\). For the exact coefficient packet

\[
(a,b,c,d,f)=(1,1,3,1,1),
\]

the fixed point is

\[
(x_*,y_*)=(1/2,1/2).
\]

Its stability matrix is

\[
J_*=
\begin{pmatrix}
3/2&-1/2\\
-1&1
\end{pmatrix}
\]

with eigenvalues \(1/2\) and \(2\). For RG time \(t=\log\mu\), both directions
are locally attractive toward the infrared. Combined with WP820's selected
charge contrast \(q_3-q_2=1\), this conditional packet predicts a positive
portal magnitude \(e_*=1/\sqrt2\).

This is a complete algebraic sign-and-magnitude mechanism only after the five
beta coefficients are admitted.

## The missing common-source interface

WP820's integer incidence fixes charges. It does not determine gauge group
Casimirs, chiral multiplicities, scalar representations, or Yukawa incidence
coefficients entering \((a,b,c,d,f)\). Two microscopic packets can share the
same charge vector and incidence while producing different flows.

At

\[
(a,b,c,d,f)=(1,1,1,1,1),
\]

the denominator \(ac-df\) vanishes and no finite interacting solution exists.
Changing only \(c\) from \(3\) to \(4\) moves the fixed point from
\((1/2,1/2)\) to \((1/3,1/3)\). Thus the charge incidence does not select the
fixed-point magnitude, and threshold changes in the matter content can move or
remove it.

This is the same parallelization obstruction found in other sectors:
a valid oriented charge source plus a valid fixed-point polynomial does not
imply a source-authorized map between them.

## Basin and perturbative control

The positive stability exponents establish a two-dimensional local infrared
stable tangent for the toy packet. They do not prove a global basin, absence
of other attractors, or survival across all thresholds.

The known integer anomaly-complete chiral source in WP805 supplies an exact
polynomial fixed point in one action, but its normalized gauge coordinate is
\(3163/2234>1\) and its chiral-tensor Yukawa coordinate is
\(34182/5585>6\). That is not a controlled quantitative realization. Its
squared coupling coordinates also erase orientation.

The two positive statements therefore do not yet overlap:

- the toy packet is controlled algebraically but lacks common-source
  coefficient authority;
- the known chiral packet has common-action algebra but lacks perturbative
  control and orientation-sensitive readout.

## Threshold and readout

The oriented anomaly survives threshold decoupling topologically, but the
fixed point depends on the beta coefficients of the active spectrum. Exact
matching must show that the low-energy physical portal stays at the predicted
value; anomaly matching alone is insufficient.

A detector record \(r=s e_*\) retains the exact source–gain ambiguity
\((e_*,s)=(1/\sqrt2,2)\) versus \((\sqrt2,1)\). The RG coordinate is not itself
a physical instrument, and no map to physical16 has been derived.

## Aspect germ audit

The minimal common-source experiment contains the oriented bulk, integer
incidence, complete matter spectrum, mediator, three flavor operators, and
detector. WP820's charge selector passes. Beta-coefficient derivation from the
same incidence, controlled nonzero fixed point, global basin, threshold
survival, physical16 descent, and detector calibration remain open.

## Classification and falsifiers

A specified coefficient packet can conditionally fix sign, magnitude, and a
local RG basin. It is not yet a source-generated flavor selector.

The smallest exact falsifiers are:

1. the same-incidence coefficient packet with \(ac-df=0\);
2. the threshold shift \(c:3\mapsto4\), which changes \(e_*\);
3. the strong coordinates of the known WP805 chiral fixed point;
4. the distinction between local stability and a global basin;
5. the exact fixed-point-source/detector-gain pair.

## Deutschian appraisal

The remaining explanatory task is no longer “find a fixed point.” It is to
explain why the oriented incidence uniquely generates a particular complete
matter spectrum whose loop coefficients land in a controlled positive
fixed-point chamber and remain there under decoupling. Without that
constructor, the five coefficients are just a higher-level tuning of the
original portal.

The sharp successor is a finite matter-spectrum census constrained by the
WP820 charge lattice and anomaly inflow. It must compute the beta coefficients
from each admitted spectrum, reject strong or threshold-unstable packets, and
test whether exactly one controlled fixed-point packet survives before any
physical16 fit is consulted.

## Disposition

Negative common-source result with a progressive conditional model. A
controlled fixed point could supply magnitude and a local basin, but the
oriented incidence does not yet make its coefficients unavoidable.
