# Two required boundary decompositions can mix but cannot generate three-family CP: WP952

## Question

Deutsch's pressure suggests that flavor noncommutativity should arise because two independently required source decompositions cannot be simultaneously diagonalized. Do two boundary parity involutions provide the minimal hard-to-vary constructor?

## Involution grammar

Let `P` and `Q` be Hermitian involutions on three-family space:

\[
P^2=Q^2=I,
\qquad
P^\dagger=P,
\qquad
Q^\dagger=Q.
\]

Any function of one involution is affine in it. Hence the strongest coefficient-independent two-boundary grammar has Gram matrices

\[
H_u=\alpha_u I+\beta_u P,
\qquad
H_d=\alpha_d I+\beta_d Q,
\]

with positivity requiring `alpha_f > |beta_f|` for a strictly positive packet.

The decompositions can genuinely fail to commute:

\[
[H_u,H_d]=\beta_u\beta_d[P,Q].
\]

Thus this construction improves on a single holonomy by making a relative mixing plane source-geometric rather than fitted.

## Three-dimensional obstruction

Each nontrivial involution in three dimensions has a one-dimensional eigenspace of one sign and a two-dimensional eigenspace of the other. A pair of such decompositions admits the standard two-projection block reduction: one common one-dimensional block plus at most one nontrivial two-dimensional principal-angle block.

Consequently `[P,Q]` has rank at most two and spectrum of the form

\[
0,
\quad i\lambda,
\quad-i\lambda.
\]

Therefore

\[
\operatorname{Tr}[P,Q]^3=0
\]

and hence

\[
\operatorname{Tr}[H_u,H_d]^3=0.
\]

Each individual affine Gram also has at most two distinct eigenvalues, so one family pair remains degenerate before additional source objects are introduced.

## Exact noncommuting witness

Take

\[
P=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-1),
\qquad
v=\frac13(1,2,2)^T,
\qquad
Q=I-2vv^T.
\]

Then `P` and `Q` are exact Hermitian involutions. Their commutator is nonzero with rank two, but its cubic trace is zero and its kernel is generated by `(-2,1,0)^T`.

The positive Grams

\[
H_u=3I+P,
\qquad
H_d=4I+2Q
\]

have leading principal minors `(4,16,32)` and `(50/9,68/3,72)`. They mix nontrivially but still have CP cubic zero.

## Claim boundary and disposition

This result concerns two Hermitian order-two decompositions acting on three family dimensions. It does not exclude three or more independently required decompositions, higher-order source operators, or a boundary defect that breaks one parity algebra. Whether any boundary parities are actually forced by an admitted flavor source remains a separate authority gate.

The construction descends under simultaneous weak-basis conjugation, but its image is the wrong `physical16` locus. Two required decompositions can explain one relational angle, not the irreducible three-family CP obstruction. The next minimal constructor must contain at least three source-related eigenspace decompositions or one operator with three simple eigenvalues together with an independently fixed noncommuting partner. A calibrated parity-sensitive instrument is still required. No algebraic composition is assigned physical time.
