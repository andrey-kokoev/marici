# Flux--modulus scale-selector audit (WP140)

Owner: `marici.Figueiredo`.

## Bounded question

Can quantized flux and modulus stabilization supply the source-selected compact
support scale missing from WP138, relative to a gravitational clock?

Pre-objective process report: excitement `9/10`, confidence `8/10` that flux
stabilizes a scale sector-by-sector but leaves a discrete boundary label,
expected information gain `9/10`. Confounds are the toy modulus potential and
absence of a derived compactification/reheating model. These reports are
non-evidential.

Freeze the bounded source class

\[
V_n(r)=a r^2+\frac{n^2}{r^2},
\qquad a>0,\quad n\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\},\quad r>0,
\]

where `r` is measured relative to an admitted gravitational clock and `n` is
a topological flux label. Stationarity gives

\[
r_n^4=\frac{n^2}{a},\qquad V_n''(r_n)=8a>0.
\]

The action therefore selects a unique stable scale inside each fixed flux
sector. This is a physical selector, not a presentation rigidifier.

For `a=1`, sectors `n=1,4` give

\[
r_1=1,\quad r_4=2,
\qquad M_{\rm KK}^{(1)}=1,\quad M_{\rm KK}^{(4)}=\frac12.
\]

With relational detector reach `3/4`, the first threshold is inaccessible and
the second accessible. Both sectors obey the same grammar. Quantization has
replaced a continuous scale fiber by a discrete non-singleton fiber; it has
not selected a unique sector. The scale also depends on `n^2`, leaving an
`n` versus `-n` kernel unless flux reversal is source-authorized gauge.

WP140 is progressive relative to WP138: once `(n,a)` is admitted, the support
scale is stable and no longer adjustable. But neither stabilization nor
quantization selects the realized integer. A cosmological nucleation measure,
topological boundary condition, or superselection theorem must supply it.

Classification: **sector-conditional gravitational scale selector; no flux-
sector selector**. Choosing `n` after inspecting detector reach is target
fitting. The smallest falsifier of unique selection is `n=1,4`; the remaining
gate is a normalized sector-preparation law or a theorem admitting one sector,
then equivariant flavor matching and an executable orientation-sensitive
multi-point instrument.

stable radius; two sectors give opposite accessibility classes; one sign
