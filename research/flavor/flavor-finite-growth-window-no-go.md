Owner: `marici.Figueiredo`.

## Question

WP170 identified a closure-order or recurrence-growth readout as the natural
nonlocal escape from bounded relation testing. This packet asks whether a
finite-radius growth instrument can already certify infinite closure.

## Frozen domain

- State domain: two labelled closure stories `Z^2` and finite tori
  `(Z/NZ)^2`.
- Probe family: Cayley-ball return volume in the same generators
  `+e1,-e1,+e2,-e2`.
- Instrument resource: maximum observed radius `R`.
- Audited radius: `R=3`.

## Exact claim

The radius-`R` Cayley ball in `Z^2` has size `1 + 2R(R+1)`. In `(Z/NZ)^2`, the
same ball is identical through radius `R` whenever `N > 2R`, because no two
integer representatives inside the radius-`R` diamond can differ by a nonzero
multiple of `N`.

For `R=3`, the hostile finite closure is `(Z/7Z)^2`, order `49`. Its growth
sequence through radius three is exactly the `Z^2` sequence:

`1, 5, 13, 25`.

The first growth difference appears at radius four.

## Disposition

Finite recurrence growth is not the closure-order oracle. It is still a
finite-window probe and inherits a large-finite-rival obstruction. It can
become a conditional selector only if the source supplies a diameter, period,
or exponent bound that the instrument radius exceeds.

Thus the live instrument gate is:

`source finite-size bound + growth radius beyond the bound -> closure selector`.

Without that source bound, recurrence growth is only a rigidifier of the
observed local presentation.
