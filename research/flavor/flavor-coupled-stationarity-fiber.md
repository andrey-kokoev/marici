## Minimal completed potential

Use the relational coordinate

\[
z=QR=36s(1-r^2)
\]

and add the smallest bounded polynomial:

\[
V(z)=-\lambda z+\kappa z^2,
\qquad
\lambda>0,\quad\kappa>0.
\]

At the target \((s,r)=(1,3/5)\),

\[
z_0=\frac{576}{25}.
\]

Stationarity away from \(r=0\) requires

\[
z=\frac{\lambda}{2\kappa}.
\]

Consequently the target is stationary only when

\[
\frac{\kappa}{\lambda}=\frac{25}{1152}.
\]

At this ratio the potential is a complete square,

\[
V=\frac{25\lambda}{1152}(z-z_0)^2+C.
\]

The coefficient ratio is numerically equivalent to inserting the target
relational coordinate. No symmetry, representation multiplicity, or mediator
count presently derives it.

## Full stationary fiber

\[

\left(1,\pm\frac35\right),
\qquad
\left(-1,\pm\frac{\sqrt{41}}5\right).
\]

The points \(r=0\) on both sign sheets are maxima. The target pair is related
by the permutation of the first two family ports. The outer pair is not in
that permutation orbit and has a different root-vector mass spectrum.

The cubic vanishes at

\[
|r|=1.
\]

components. Restricting the admitted source domain to \(s=1\) and \(|r|<1\)
removes the hostile outer pair and leaves the target permutation orbit.
However, that restriction must itself be generated by the source. Selecting
