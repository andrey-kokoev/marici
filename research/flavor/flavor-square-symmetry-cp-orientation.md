# Square-symmetry CP-orientation selector

Work package: WP595  
Owner: marici.Figueiredo

## Source architecture

WP594 shows that continuous singlet rotation symmetry removes CP selection.
Replace it by the square symmetry generated by

\[
(\sigma,s)\longmapsto(-s,\sigma)
\]

and CP reflection

\[
(\sigma,s)\longmapsto(\sigma,-s).
\]

The real renormalizable quartic invariant space has dimension two. It may be
spanned by the radial quartic and the square anisotropy. Consider the positive
source potential

\[
V={\lambda\over4}(\sigma^2+s^2-a^2)^2
+{\kappa\over4}(\sigma^2-s^2)^2,
\qquad \lambda>0,\quad\kappa>0.
\]

No observed flavor number occurs in this action.

## Exact selector

Both positive squares vanish precisely when

\[
\sigma^2+s^2=a^2,
\qquad
\sigma^2=s^2.
\]

The vacuum therefore consists of four points,

\[
(\sigma,s)=
\left({\epsilon_1a\over\sqrt2},
{\epsilon_2a\over\sqrt2}\right),
\qquad \epsilon_1,\epsilon_2\in\{-1,1\}.
\]

Every vacuum has \(s\ne0\), while the declared bare CP reflection permutes
distinct vacua. The selected orientation is

\[
{s^2\over\sigma^2+s^2}={1\over2}.
\]

It is independent of the magnitudes of \(\lambda\) and \(\kappa\). The
Hessian eigenvalues are \(2a^2\lambda\) and \(2a^2\kappa\), so there is
no angular zero mode.

This is the first source architecture in this branch whose numerical
orientation is hard to vary within the declared renormalizable invariant
grammar. Changing one half requires removing the anisotropy, changing the
representation or symmetry, or adding new higher operators.

## Generalized-CP correction

The scalar potential alone does not establish physical spontaneous CP
breaking. Let \(R\) be the quarter-turn square rotation and \(C\) the bare
reflection \(s\mapsto-s\). At the diagonal vacuum,

\[
RC
\begin{pmatrix}a/\sqrt2\\a/\sqrt2\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}a/\sqrt2\\a/\sqrt2\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Thus a generalized CP symmetry remains unbroken. The earlier statement that
all four vacua break CP was too strong: they break the chosen bare reflection
but each is stabilized by another reflection in the square group.

WP595 therefore proves a coefficient-independent orientation selector, not a
physical CP-breaking selector. A portal embedding must show that no admitted
internal transformation can compose with CP to stabilize the full source and
physical flavor vacuum.

## Load-bearing assumptions

The anisotropy is essential. Setting \(\kappa=0\) restores the WP594 vacuum
circle and admits the CP-conserving point \((a,0)\).

The theorem is bounded to the renormalizable scalar grammar. Square-symmetric
higher operators can introduce additional angular harmonics and must be
controlled by a UV completion. The complete coupled RG must also prove that
\(\kappa\) remains positive and that thresholds preserve the vacuum
relation.

The singlet construction descends under the weak-basis groupoid. WP87's
linear map from \(s\) to a physical CP invariant is not by itself compatible
with treating the full square rotation as an invisible source symmetry.
The complete portal representation and generalized-CP stabilizer must be
derived. The scalar sector selects one orientation relation, not the remaining
physical16 flavor coordinates.

## Independent criticism

After a generalized-CP-breaking portal and source-to-pole transport are
derived, the architecture predicts both:

1. the orientation fraction \(s^2/(\sigma^2+s^2)=1/2\);
2. a nonzero angular scalar pole with curvature \(2a^2\kappa\).

A joined calibrated CP-invariant and singlet/flavor-clock measurement could
refute either consequence. The current CP and common-clock packets do not yet
form that executable joint instrument.
