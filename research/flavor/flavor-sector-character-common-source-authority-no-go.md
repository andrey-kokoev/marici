# WP350's two coefficient rays require different symmetry objects: WP953

## Question

Can one admitted projector-permutation source symmetry independently force WP350's up-sector ray `(1,1,1)` and down-sector ray `(1,-1,0)`?

## Natural coefficient module

Let `S3` permute the three projector coefficients in its natural three-dimensional permutation representation. This module decomposes as

\[
\mathbb C^3\cong\mathbf1\oplus\mathbf2.
\]

The full invariant subspace is the unique democratic line

\[
L_{\rm triv}=\operatorname{span}(1,1,1).
\]

There is no sign-representation line in this module. Solving

\[
T_{12}v=-v,
\qquad
T_{23}v=-v
\]

gives only `v=0`.

## The down ray is only locally odd

The WP350 vector

\[
v_d=(1,-1,0)
\]

obeys

\[
T_{12}v_d=-v_d,
\]

but a three-cycle sends it to a different line. Its full `S3` orbit spans the two-dimensional standard representation. Therefore `v_d` is a character line only for the chosen subgroup generated by `T12`, not for the same full `S3` action that selects the up ray.

The WP350 construction consequently uses two distinct symmetry objects:

\[
S_3\text{-invariance for the up sector},
\qquad
\langle T_{12}\rangle\text{-oddness for the down sector}.
\]

Choosing `T12` also distinguishes the third projector from the first two. That flag is additional source data.

## Gauge typing does not supply the flag

Standard Model gauge transformations act on the up- and down-sector gauge factors, not by permuting the three projector labels. On the coefficient module they are scalar and commute with every permutation matrix. Distinct scalar gauge charges therefore do not convert the natural permutation module into an `S3` sign module and do not choose one transposition inside `S3`.

A mediator or boundary defect transforming under the standard two-dimensional representation could select a direction such as `(1,-1,0)`, but its vacuum direction and coupling are new source objects. They must be derived before flavor readout and tested under completion.

## Disposition

WP350 remains a valid conditional capability theorem, but its phrase “different permutation characters” does not by itself name one common source symmetry. The up ray is full-`S3` trivial; the down ray is odd only under a marked `Z2`. A common source must therefore provide a symmetry-breaking flag

\[
S_3\longrightarrow\langle T_{12}\rangle
\]

or an equivalent standard-doublet order parameter. Without that object, the relative coefficient directions are stipulated.

This audit concerns representation and subgroup typing, not physical time. The smallest exact falsifier is that the simultaneous full-`S3` sign equations have zero-dimensional solution space while the orbit of `(1,-1,0)` has dimension two. A calibrated instrument for the resulting Gram geometry remains open.
