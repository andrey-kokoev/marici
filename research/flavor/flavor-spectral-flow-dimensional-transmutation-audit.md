# Spectral-Flow and Dimensional-Transmutation Audit

## Question

Can a quantized spectral-flow condition fix the dimensionless Dirac eigenvalue
while the same gauge–Yukawa action fixes the absolute scale by dimensional
transmutation?

## Spectral flow forgets crossing location

Consider the oriented one-level path

\[
H_\rho(t)=t-\rho,
\qquad
-1\leq t\leq1.
\]

For every \(-1<\rho<1\), one eigenvalue crosses zero with positive
orientation, so

\[
\operatorname{sf}(H_\rho)=1.
\]

The hostile pair

\[
\rho=\frac14,
\qquad
\rho=\frac34
\]

has identical endpoint signs and spectral flow but different crossing
locations. Small deformations of \(\rho\) preserve the index until the crossing
hits an endpoint. Reversing the path orientation reverses the index.

Spectral flow can therefore select relational orientation and crossing count.
It cannot select the continuous eigenvalue ratio or threshold location. This
is not an accidental weakness: local constancy under gap-preserving
deformation is the property that makes the index topological.

## Dimensional transmutation trades rather than predicts

For the one-loop asymptotically free flow

\[
\frac{dg}{d\log\mu}=-b g^3,
\qquad b>0,
\]

the quantity

\[
\Lambda=\mu\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2bg(\mu)^2}\right)
\]

is exactly RG invariant. This produces a dimensional scale from a
dimensionless coupling. It does not predict its value. For every
\(0<\Lambda<\mu\),

\[
g(\mu)^2=
\frac{1}{2b\log(\mu/\Lambda)}
\]

is a corresponding boundary condition.

Dimensional transmutation exchanges one continuous datum for another. Common
rescaling of \(\mu\) and \(\Lambda\) changes physical units without affecting
the dimensionless coupling relation.

## The two mechanisms do not close each other

If the spectral mass is

\[
m=\rho\Lambda,
\]

spectral flow leaves a continuum of allowed \(\rho\) while transmutation leaves
a continuum of allowed \(\Lambda\) through the boundary coupling. Their product
therefore has two independent continuous inputs. Juxtaposing a valid index and
a valid RG invariant is not a selector.

A source equation tying the crossing location to the RG trajectory could
repair this, but it must be derived from the same action. Declaring
\(\rho=1/4\) or choosing a boundary coupling to match the desired portal would
reintroduce the original tuning.

## Threshold survival and physical readout

The integer index survives threshold displacements that move \(\rho\) within
the open interval. Hence it protects the existence and orientation of a
crossing, not its numerical energy.

Measuring the crossing location requires a calibrated path parameter. The
record \(r=s\rho\) has the exact hostile pair
\((\rho,s)=(1/4,2)\) and \((1/2,1)\). No present flavor operation supplies this
clock, a physical16 descent map, or detector calibration.

## Aspect germ audit

The native comparison contains the oriented bulk, spectral path, index
pairing, gauge–Yukawa action, renormalization clock, mediator, threshold map,
physical16 map, and detector. Orientation selection passes. Crossing-location
selection, boundary-coupling selection, absolute scale, threshold-location
protection, quotient descent, and detector calibration remain open.

## Classification and falsifiers

The combined construction is an orientation selector and scale generator, not
a numerical portal selector.

The smallest exact falsifiers are:

1. \(\rho=1/4\) and \(3/4\), which share spectral flow one;
2. two boundary couplings producing two transmutation scales;
3. common unit rescaling of \(\mu,\Lambda\);
4. an index-preserving threshold displacement;
5. the crossing-location/clock-gain pair.

## Deutschian appraisal

The index explains why a crossing cannot disappear without a wall event. RG
transmutation explains how a scale can arise without an explicit mass term.
Neither explains which crossing location or which transmutation scale nature
chooses.

The next source candidate must make the spectral path itself an RG trajectory,
so its zero occurs at a dynamically selected event rather than at a free
parameter. A source-derived boundary condition or global regularity condition
must then pick one trajectory from the RG family. That same event must be the
physical threshold and detector clock mark. Without this identification, the
remaining pieces occupy compatible but unjoined frames.

## Disposition

Negative numerical-selection result. Spectral flow fixes orientation and
topological survival; dimensional transmutation supplies an RG-invariant scale;
their crossing-location and boundary-condition fibers remain independent.
