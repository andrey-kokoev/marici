# Faithful readout is not selection (WP299)

## Typed arrows

Let $X$ be the admitted `physical16` family. A faithful readout
$R:X\to Y$ has singleton fibers on its declared domain. It preserves the
cardinality and distinctions of $X$; it does not reduce the admissible
family.

A selector is a different arrow $S:X\to X$ with proper image. In the finite
audit, $S$ is also idempotent, so applying it again does not change the
selected family. A faithful readout after selection reports the reduction
already made by $S$: the reduction is not generated by $R$.

## Postselection is not source selection

After observing a record $y$, the inverse fiber $R^{-1}(y)$ may be a
singleton. That is retrospective conditioning on an obtained outcome. It does
not alter the prior source image, prepare the state, or predict its numerical
value before readout.

The exact four-state audit demonstrates all three cases:

- a faithful readout with four singleton fibers and full four-state image;
- a binary branch readout with two nonsingleton fibers;
- an idempotent selector with a proper two-state image.

## Flavor consequence

WP298 closes the reconstruction problem on its standard chart. It supplies a
faithful separator/readout, neither a selector nor a rigidifier. The flavor
objective remains open at the earlier arrow: an admitted source operation must
produce a proper `physical16` image independently of the reconstructed values.
