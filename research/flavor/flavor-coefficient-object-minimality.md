# Coefficient-object minimality

## Scope

WP972 asks whether one shared dynamical coefficient object can supply the
missing WP452 coefficient vacuum. The admitted carrier is the WP447 spin-one
embedding, whose word algebra is \(M_3(\mathbb C)\). No measured flavor value
is used. The faithful readout remains the full weak-basis physical16 quotient.

## One-object obstruction

Let one Hermitian source field feed both sectors through scalar-coefficient
functional calculus:

\[
Y_u=f_u(C),\qquad Y_d=f_d(C).
\]

Both outputs lie in the commutative algebra generated by \(C\), hence
\([Y_u,Y_d]=0\) identically. The mixing is trivial up to degeneracies and the
CP-odd commutator cubic vanishes. Higher polynomial degree cannot repair this.
Simultaneous weak-basis conjugation preserves commutator vanishing, so the
obstruction descends to physical16.

## Smallest hostile control

Two Hermitian objects evade the algebraic obstruction:

\[
A=\operatorname{diag}(1,2,4),\qquad
B=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&-i\\1&0&1\\i&1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Their commutator determinant and cubic are nonzero. This proves capacity only;
it does not authorize the pair or select its relative orientation.

## Classification

At least two noncommuting coefficient objects are necessary but not sufficient.
A successor must derive their relative vacuum from an \(SO(3)\)-invariant
source action, descend to physical16, survive the complete fitted ensemble,
and expose a typed instrument.

The smallest falsifier would be a legal single-object scalar functional
calculus with \([f_u(C),f_d(C)]\ne0\).
