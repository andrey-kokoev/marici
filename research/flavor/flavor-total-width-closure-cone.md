# Tree-level total-width closure cone

## Purpose and order

WP486 calibrates quark-current residues and quark partial widths. WP487 asks
when those partial widths are also the total widths. The claim is restricted
to tree level and six effectively massless quarks.

The relevant two-body nonquark channel classes are vector pairs,
vector–physical-scalar pairs, physical-scalar pairs, and vectorlike-messenger
pairs. Goldstone and gauge-fixing fields are not physical daughter states.

## Sufficient closure cone

Let \(m_i\) be the three distinct vector-pole masses. Let \(\tau\) be the
minimum mass among all physical connector/flavon scalars and both stages of
vectorlike messengers. Impose

\[
4m_i^2>m_j^2
\]

for every ordered pair of distinct vector poles, and

\[
4\tau^2>m_i^2
\]

for every vector pole. Every potential nonquark daughter is then heavier than
half every parent, so all listed two-body channels are kinematically closed.
No coupling or selection rule is fitted to remove an unwanted channel.

This cone is sufficient, not necessary. Outside it, a channel-aware coupling
calculation is required. Failure of one inequality invalidates this certificate
but does not alone prove that the corresponding interaction vertex is nonzero.

## Nonempty exact witness

The source point

\[
g_F^2=\mu^2=\tau^2=1,
\qquad g_P^2={1\over68},
\qquad s^2=32
\]

has squared vector masses

\[
1-{1\over\sqrt{17}},
\qquad 3,
\qquad 1+{1\over\sqrt{17}}.
\]

Every strict closure inequality holds. This witness proves only that the cone
is nonempty; it is not a selected numerical source point.

## Width authority

On the cone, WP486’s calibrated six-quark partial widths are the complete
tree-level widths. The two mixed fractional widths retain their exact sum rule
with the quintet fractional width.

The present source has not yet been shown to select or occupy this cone. The
connector-scalar and two messenger-stage masses must be derived from the same
source trajectory. Loop-induced and off-shell channels require a separately
declared perturbative order and detector resolution.
