# Integer Incidence and Anomaly-Inflow Selector

## Question

Can an independently derived integer incidence and oriented quantized anomaly
inflow fix the asymmetric mediator charge ray, its sign, and its primitive
normalization—and then carry that selection through RG, thresholds, and
physical readout?

## Primitive integer ray

Consider the rank-two integer incidence

\[
B=
\begin{pmatrix}
2&-1&0\\
3&0&-1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its real kernel is one-dimensional and its primitive integer kernel consists
of the orientation pair

\[
q=\pm(1,2,3)^T.
\]

Unlike WP819's arbitrary normalized line, the lattice condition supplies a
primitive relative normalization. It still leaves the orientation pair.

## Oriented anomaly inflow

On the kernel \(q=s(1,2,3)^T\), the declared cubic anomaly is

\[
\mathcal A(q)=q_1^3+q_2^3+q_3^3=36s^3.
\]

An oriented bulk inflow \(k=36\) imposes

\[
\mathcal A(q)=k
\]

and selects \(s=1\) uniquely over the reals. The reversed bulk \(k=-36\)
selects \(s=-1\). Conditional on the oriented inflow and incidence, the
boundary charge packet is therefore

\[
q=(1,2,3)^T,
\qquad
q_3-q_2=1.
\]

This is the first architecture in the branch that conditionally fixes both
orientation and primitive charge magnitude. It is explicitly relational: the
oriented bulk is a boundary reference and changes the physical groupoid. It
does not reveal an absolute sign in the experiment without that bulk.

## Remaining continuous portal magnitude

If the mediator gauge coupling is \(e\), the linear portal is

\[
e(q_3-q_2)=e.
\]

The topological packet quantizes the charge contrast, not the gauge coupling.
The same selected charges allow every positive value of \(e\). Hence the
physical portal magnitude remains continuous.

## RG basin

For a positive one-loop Abelian coefficient \(b\),

\[
\frac{de}{dt}=be^3,
\qquad
e(t)=\frac{e_0}{\sqrt{1-2be_0^2t}}.
\]

The only fixed point is \(e=0\), and finite-scale evolution retains \(e_0\).
The charge lattice is protected while the portal magnitude has no selected
nonzero RG basin. A complete construction needs an interacting fixed point
whose coefficients are derived from the same chiral matter and whose physical
normalization is frozen.

## Threshold survival

Anomaly matching is nondecoupling. After heavy charged modes are removed, a
Wess–Zumino term can satisfy

\[
\mathcal A_{\mathrm{light}}+\mathcal A_{\mathrm{WZ}}=k.
\]

This preserves the oriented topological charge record but permits a continuous
partition between light and Wess–Zumino sectors. It does not by itself preserve
the mediator pole residue, gauge coupling, or detector amplitude. Topological
survival and numerical threshold survival are distinct.

## Authority and hostile incidences

Integer structure alone does not authorize this particular matrix \(B\).
For example,

\[
B'=
\begin{pmatrix}
3&-1&0\\
4&0&-1
\end{pmatrix}
\]

selects the different primitive ray \((1,3,4)^T\). The incidence must be
derived from a declared topology, quiver, anomaly complex, or microscopic
action before the desired portal is inspected. Likewise, the oriented inflow
must be a source boundary condition rather than a sign chosen after fitting.

## Physical readout

A detector record \(r=s\,e(q_3-q_2)=se\) has the exact hostile pair
\((e,s)=(1/5,2)\) and \((2/5,1)\). The source charge is fixed, but gauge
coupling and detector gain remain confounded. No current constructor proves
that this charge contrast descends to a faithful physical16 coordinate or
supplies the calibrated detector.

## Aspect germ audit

The minimal relational experiment contains the oriented bulk, incidence
complex, mediator, three flavor operators, and detector. Primitive charge
selection passes conditionally. Incidence provenance, bulk-orientation
authority, gauge normalization, nonzero RG basin, numerical threshold
protection, physical16 descent, and detector calibration remain open. The
construction must therefore remain outside acquisition scheduling.

## Classification and falsifiers

Conditional on independently derived \(B\) and \(k=36\), this operation is a
genuine oriented primitive-charge selector. It is not yet a complete physical
portal selector.

The smallest falsifiers are:

1. \(B'\), showing that integer incidence can encode another desired ray;
2. \(k=-36\), showing the mirror bulk selects the opposite orientation;
3. two positive gauge couplings on the same selected charge packet;
4. the absence of a nonzero one-loop Abelian fixed point;
5. continuous light/Wess–Zumino anomaly partition at threshold;
6. the exact gauge-coupling/detector-gain pair.

## Deutschian appraisal

This source has more explanatory reach than a symmetry projector. A primitive
integer kernel plus oriented quantized inflow can make a relative charge
contrast and its sign unavoidable. What it cannot explain is the continuous
strength with which that charge couples.

The sharp successor is therefore forced: derive a nonzero interacting gauge-
Yukawa fixed point from the same chiral incidence and test whether its
attractive basin, threshold matching, and calibrated physical16 detector all
inherit the oriented topological packet. Importing an unrelated fixed point
would merely parallelize two valid frames without a source-authorized
constructor.

## Disposition

Progressive conditional selector. Charge direction, orientation, and primitive
normalization can be fixed topologically; physical coupling magnitude, RG
basin, numerical threshold survival, quotient descent, and instrument remain
open.
