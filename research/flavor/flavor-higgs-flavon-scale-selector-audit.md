## Minimal relative-scale selector

Let `h^2=2 H^dagger H` and let `R^2` be the full weak-basis-invariant flavon
norm. The classically scale-invariant portal is

\[
V(h,R)=\frac{\lambda_h}{4}h^4+
\frac{\lambda_f}{4}R^4+
\frac{\kappa}{2}h^2R^2.
\]

At a stationary point with both norms nonzero,

\[
\lambda_hh^2+\kappa R^2=0,
\qquad
\lambda_fR^2+\kappa h^2=0.
\]

Therefore a nonzero branch requires

\[
\kappa<0,qquad \kappa^2=\lambda_h\lambda_f,
\qquad
\frac{R^2}{h^2}=-\frac{\lambda_h}{\kappa}.
\]

The portal conditionally selects a relative scale. It does not select the
common magnitude. The radial Hessian determinant is proportional to
`lambda_h lambda_f-kappa^2` and vanishes identically on the branch: one
dilation direction remains flat.

For the exact benchmark

\[
(\lambda_h,\lambda_f,\kappa)=(1,4,-2),
\]

both `(h^2,R^2)=(1,1/2)` and `(100,50)` are stationary zero-potential points
with the same ratio. Their mediator thresholds differ by a factor of ten.
They are not weak-basis copies; they are distinct embeddings relative to the
electroweak/detector clock.

## Explicit masses and dimensional transmutation

Adding

\[
-\frac{m_h^2}{2}h^2-\frac{m_f^2}{2}R^2
\]

lifts the dilation direction and can select an absolute scale. But that scale
is then inherited from the dimensionful source packet `(m_h^2,m_f^2)`. The
operation is a valid conditional scale selector only if those masses are
derived independently; it does not explain them by flavor geometry.

Coleman--Weinberg lifting changes the mechanism but not the authority gate.
Dimensional transmutation produces a scale relative to a renormalization
boundary and running-coupling data. Unless that boundary packet is supplied
by an independently admitted source, the calculation transports a scale; it
does not create source authority for one. RG algebra is not an absolute clock.

selector**. It descends under the full weak-basis group and uses no texture
