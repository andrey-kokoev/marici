# Null-outcome rank restoration

Work package: WP570  
Owner: marici.Figueiredo

## Question

WP569 gave the robust smallest-singular-value gate for two source directions.
Its response matrix was defined on three detector outcomes. This packet asks
whether the third outcome is dispensable bookkeeping or part of the physical
probe.

The answer is exact: after normalization, an \(m\)-outcome probability record
has tangent dimension at most \(m-1\). A rank-two local flavor probe therefore
requires at least three typed detector outcomes. A null or failed-selection
outcome can be the necessary third record.

## General rank ceiling

Let \(q(\epsilon)\) be an \(m\)-outcome normalized detector distribution and
let \(D\) be its Jacobian with respect to \(r\) source perturbations. Probability
normalization gives

\[
\mathbf 1^T D=0.
\]

Every column of \(D\) lies in the \((m-1)\)-dimensional probability tangent
space. Hence

\[
\operatorname{rank}D\le\min(r,m-1).
\]

For \(r=2\), any normalized binary record has rank at most one. No choice of
classifier, metric, or covariance can evade this support bound.

## Exact source-channel realization

Take three equally likely source cells,

\[
p_0=(1/3,1/3,1/3)^T,
\]

the identity detector channel, and two centered source-score columns

\[
S=3
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0\\
-1&1\\
0&-1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Then the transported detector response is

\[
D=K\operatorname{diag}(p_0)S
=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0\\
-1&1\\
0&-1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Thus WP569's abstract response has an exact source-score and detector-channel
factorization. The full three-outcome response has rank two. In the multinomial
Fisher metric \(W=3I_3\), its Gram eigenvalues are \(3\) and \(9\).

## Deleting the completed-trial outcome

Interpret the third record as a completed trial with no selected candidate.
If it is discarded, the remaining first two outcomes must be renormalized by
their selected probability \(Q=q_1+q_2\). For a tangent column \(d\), the
conditional derivative is

\[
\dot r_i={d_iQ-q_i(d_1+d_2)\over Q^2},
\qquad i=1,2.
\]

At the exact baseline above, the two conditional response columns are

\[
D_{\mathrm{selected}}=
\begin{pmatrix}
3/2&-3/4\\
-3/2&3/4
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

They are proportional. The rank is one and the conditional Gram determinant
is zero. The lost direction changed the probability of completing without a
selected candidate; conditioning erased that information.

## Physical interpretation

“No selected event” is not absence of a trial. It is a typed null observation
whose probability changes under the source perturbation. Recording it does
not add a reference port or recover an absolute quantity. It completes the
outcome alphabet of the existing experiment.

The three-outcome record jointly separates the two declared local source
directions. The selected-only projection identifies only one combination.
Consequently:

- full-record rank two is source-channel realizable;
- null-outcome deletion is the first nonfaithful arrow;
- covariance on the selected sample cannot reconstruct the erased direction;
- exposure, trigger failure, reconstruction failure, and rejected-candidate
  counts must be included wherever they carry source-dependent probability.

## Experimental contract

A two-error portal-score experiment must publish:

1. the completed-trial denominator or equivalent exposure measure;
2. all outcome categories needed to close probability normalization;
3. the source-conditioned efficiencies for selected and null records;
4. correlations between migration, loss, and selected-bin responses;
5. the robust smallest-singular-value bound on the completed alphabet.

The null record is a source-derived complementary probe because its response is
generated by the same preparation and detector channel. An independently
invented third coordinate would not have this authority.

## Classification and present gate

This is a support-completion theorem for the WP569 separator constructor. It
does not select a source value and is not a presentation rigidifier. The
source scores follow invariant physical16 paths and the detector outputs are
probabilities, so full weak-basis descent passes.

Current HHH releases report selected event categories and efficiencies but do
not publish the portal-score-conditioned completed-trial channel needed to
evaluate the two-direction response, including its null-outcome covariance.
The theorem therefore remains prospective or applicable to an independently
admitted surrogate.
