# Mixed relative grade points to reflection positivity

## New orientation candidate

The determinant route closes because scalar factorization, regularization, and
primitive continuation eventually return to the zero-free logarithm problem.
The surviving mixed relative class suggests a different law.

Suppose two source sectors carry finite-part readouts (F_L) and (F_R), and
their bridge produces the mixed grade

\[
\Phi_{LR}=2yF_LF_R.
\]

This expression is not positive for arbitrary independent complex readouts.
It becomes positive if the source supplies both:

\[
F_R=\overline{F_L}
\]

and

\[
y\ge0.
\]

Then

\[
\Phi_{LR}=2y|F_L|^2\ge0.
\]

The orientation law is therefore not sector exchange alone. It is an
anti-linear reflection identifying one readout with the adjoint of the other,
together with a positive bridge measure.

## Why this differs from earlier positivity attempts

This law does not treat endpoint, gamma, prime, or sector channels as
independently positive. Positivity appears only after a global relation joins
the two source sectors.

It also does not assign positive real part to the completed entire scalar.
The positive object is a mixed Hermitian pairing before scalar projection.

The distinction is:

- exchange symmetry gives (F_L\leftrightarrow F_R);
- reciprocal sewing gives a comparison between the two sectors;
- reflection positivity gives (F_R=\overline{F_L}) in the physical pairing;
- positive bridge incidence gives (y\ge0).

Only the last two orient the mixed grade.

## Abstract operator form

Let (V_+) and (V_-) be the two sector spaces. Let

\[
\Theta:V_+\longrightarrow V_-
\]

be an anti-linear source involution. Let

\[
B:V_-\times V_+\longrightarrow\mathbb C
\]

be the bridge pairing. Reflection positivity requires

\[
B(\Theta v,v)\ge0
\]

for every source-admissible (v).

The null space

\[
\mathcal N=\{v:B(\Theta v,v)=0\}
\]

must then be quotiented before completion. A distinguished scalar zero is
excluded only if the source state associated with that scalar cannot lie in
(mathcal N) away from the seam.

This last injectivity statement is separate from reflection positivity and
must not be assumed.

## Immediate hostile pair

Mere equal magnitude does not orient the mixed grade. The assignments

\[
(F_L,F_R)=(1,1)
\]

and

\[
(F_L,F_R)=(1,-1)
\]

have identical magnitudes and can obey the same linear exchange census, but
their mixed grades have opposite signs.

Likewise, a unitary phase twist

\[
F_R=e^{i\theta}\overline{F_L}
\]

preserves norms and many scalar symmetries while destroying positivity unless
the source fixes the phase.

Thus hostile symmetric multipliers are rejected only if they fail the
source-derived anti-linear reflection law or the positive bridge incidence.

## Relation to the two-sector RH architecture

The critical seam is already the locus where the two valuation sectors are
unitarily compared. The new question is whether the labelled theta/Tate source
upgrades that unitary comparison to a reflection-positive pairing.

A viable construction must provide:

1. the two source spaces before completion;
2. the anti-linear reflection (Theta);
3. the bridge pairing (B);
4. positivity of (B(\Theta v,v));
5. compatibility with prime transport, endpoint, gamma, and seam currents;
6. a proof that the distinguished off-seam zero-state is not in the null
   quotient;
7. stability under restricted-product completion.

The first four would supply orientation. The sixth is the zero-exclusion gate.
Neither follows from the scalar functional equation.

## Finite falsifier

At one finite labelled cutoff, form the mixed Gram matrix

\[
G_{ij}=B(\Theta e_i,e_j).
\]

The proposal fails if:

- (G) is not Hermitian;
- a principal minor is negative;
- a prime-transported source vector enters the null space unexpectedly;
- the endpoint or gamma correction breaks adjoint compatibility;
- an allowed phase twist changes the sign while preserving all declared source
  data;
- positivity holds only after using the completed scalar or its zeros.

## Decisive boundary

Reflection positivity is a genuinely different orientation mechanism from
positive-real impedance, determinant zero-freeness, or scalar Weil positivity.
It acts on a mixed two-sector relation before projection.

The route remains open only if the anti-linear reflection and positive bridge
pairing can be derived from labelled theta/Tate source operations. Otherwise
the mixed relative grade is another faithful lift without RH-bearing
orientation.
