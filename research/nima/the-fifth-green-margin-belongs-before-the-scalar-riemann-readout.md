# The fifth Green margin belongs before the scalar Riemann readout

## Type correction

The completed labelled wall--jump carrier and the four-character Fourier boundary packet are higher-dimensional. A terminal Riemann matrix coefficient

\[
r_s:Y\longrightarrow\mathbb C
\]

cannot be bounded below on either full packet whenever \(\dim Y>1\). Its kernel is structural, not a defect that a positive output-angle margin can remove.

Therefore the fifth Green margin cannot be imposed on the scalar Riemann readout. It belongs to the last faithful representation-valued Green evaluator before scalar projection.

## Rank obstruction

For any nonzero bounded functional \(r:Y\to\mathbb C\),

\[
\operatorname{codim}\ker r\le 1.
\]

If \(Y\) contains two independent typed ports, then \(\ker r\ne0\). Consequently there is no \(c>0\) such that

\[
|r(y)|\ge c\|y\|
\qquad(y\in Y).
\]

The obstruction already occurs on one prime's wall--jump plane, and it becomes infinite-dimensional after prime-label completion. Calling this kernel mixed cancellation does not turn it into a coercivity margin: scalar augmentation is intentionally nonfaithful.

## Faithful packet level

Let

\[
J_{mathrm{pkt}}:
H_{\mathrm{coh}}\oplus H_{\mathrm{dis}}
\longrightarrow
Y_c\oplus Y_d
\]

be the source-authorized representation-valued Green observer, with the two terminal types retained as an orthogonal direct sum. Write

\[
J_{mathrm{pkt}}(c,d)=(J_c c,J_d d).
\]

Then

\[
J_{mathrm{pkt}}^*J_{mathrm{pkt}}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
J_c^*J_c&0\\
0&J_d^*J_d
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

At this typed level the normalized mixed block is zero, so

\[
\delta_{\mathrm{mix}}=1.
\]

This is not an estimate. It is a consequence of retaining the authorized output types.

The same mechanism appears in the Pauli two-port lift: its direct-sum norm cancels endpoint cross-correlation exactly. Mixed cancellation reappears only if a later map identifies or adds the two outputs.

## Nonorthogonal faithful comparison

A source constructor may instead place the two sector images in one Green target before scalar projection. Then the existing Schur criterion remains correct:

\[
R=A^{-1/2}CB^{-1/2},
\qquad
\|R\|<1.
\]

But the target map must still be faithful on the declared reduced joint state space. The fifth margin audits this representation-valued identification. It does not audit the scalar matrix coefficient that follows it.

Thus there are two legitimate regimes:

1. typed direct-sum terminal packet: \(C=0\) and \(\delta_{\mathrm{mix}}=1\);
2. source-authorized common Green target: prove \(\|R\|\le1-\delta_{\mathrm{mix}}\).

A scalar target is neither regime unless the admissible joint state space has already been reduced, by a source theorem, to at most one dimension.

## Correct role of the scalar section

The scalar section may have zeros because it is a determinant, matrix coefficient, or augmentation of an already constructed operator packet. Its kernel need not represent invisible Green states.

The RH-bearing bridge must therefore be

\[
\Xi(s_0)=0
\Longrightarrow
0\ne h\in\ker\mathcal D(s_0)
\Longrightarrow
\text{closed reciprocal boundary state},
\]

where \(\mathcal D(s)\) acts on the faithful completed Green domain. It cannot be

\[
\Xi(s_0)=0
\Longrightarrow
r_{s_0}\text{ is coercive}.
\]

The first statement is spectral identification. The second is impossible for a multidimensional packet.

## Consequence for the five-margin theorem

The five-margin factorization must stop before scalar augmentation:

\[
\text{constructor coherence}
\longrightarrow
\text{faithful representation-valued Green packet}
\longrightarrow
\text{five-margin coercivity}
\longrightarrow
\text{operator pencil}
\longrightarrow
\text{scalar determinant or matrix coefficient}.
\]

If the completed construction preserves the wall, jump, Fourier-character, and prime labels as orthogonal output types, the fifth margin is automatically maximal. The remaining burden then moves to:

- lower bounds on each retained sector;
- radical descent;
- the spectral-identification theorem linking the scalar section to the operator pencil.

If a source-authorized merge occurs before the pencil, its normalized cross block must be audited there.

## Minimal hostile

Let \(Y=\mathbb C^2\) with faithful packet observer \(J=I\), and let

\[
r(y_1,y_2)=y_1+y_2.
\]

The packet has perfect coercivity and zero mixed block. Yet \(r(1,-1)=0\). This is a valid scalar cancellation, not a failure of the packet.

Treating it as failure would forbid every scalar determinant or matrix coefficient from vanishing and would therefore erase the very zero locus the programme is meant to identify.

## Frontier contraction

The next global audit is not a lower bound for the Riemann scalar readout. It is:

1. identify the final representation-valued Green target before scalar projection;
2. determine whether coherent and disagreement outputs remain typed-direct-sum or undergo an authorized merge;
3. in the direct-sum case, record \(\delta_{\mathrm{mix}}=1\);
4. in the merged case, compute the normalized cross block;
5. separately prove that the scalar Riemann section is the determinant or authorized matrix coefficient of the resulting closed operator pencil.

This removes an impossible terminal coercivity demand while preserving the genuine mixed-cancellation gate exactly where it is mathematically typed.
