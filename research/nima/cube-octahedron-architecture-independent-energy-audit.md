# Cube versus octahedron: source eigenlines and an architecture-independent energy trial

## Outcomes

Two results emerge from an exact comparison using the same 24 spatial rotations:

1. **The native cube construction does not satisfy the full-support hypothesis.**
   Its face-derived cyclic eigenline has a zero coordinate. Its three operator
   supports total 4128, not 7128. Only 3552 of those arrows belong to the selected
   closed traversals; 576 alignment arrows are unused by that mode.
2. **Source incidence distinguishes the geometries without counting circuit
   arrows.** A declared shared-edge connection-Laplacian Hamiltonian has different
   exact excitation gaps for cube and octahedron, and is unchanged by replacing
   dense feedback with identity feedback. Its couplings and physical scale are
   not derived.

This is a constructive energy-model audit, not a new particle or mass prediction.
It refines the earlier suggestion that cube and octahedron would automatically
have the same 7128 count. Their symmetry representations agree; their native
geometric selected vectors do not have the same coordinate support.

Sources: [support theorem](monomial-projector-support-theorem.md),
[octahedral realization](octahedral-monomial-projector-realization.md), and
[tetrahedral implementation/minimality audit](twelve-triangle-net-minimality.md).

## One group, two geometric carriers

Both constructions use the proper octahedral group S4 generated by the
quarter-turn and coordinate-cycle matrices in the octahedral checker. Its order
is 24 and its signed spatial representation has degree three. No new seed group
is fitted separately for the cube.

For the cube, use vertices (±1,±1,±1), and split each square face into four
triangles through its centroid. For the octahedron, use vertices ±e_i, and split
each triangular face into three centroid triangles. The respective reference
triangles, with vertices written as columns of X0, are

    cube:        (e1, (1,1,1), (1,-1,1));
    octahedron:  ((1,1,1)/3, e1, e2).

Each reference has a free orbit of 24 outward-oriented triangles under the same
group. Both surfaces have 14 vertices, 36 edges and 24 triangles; their oriented
boundaries vanish. They are nevertheless different triangulations:

| Quantity | Cube construction | Octahedral construction |
|---|---|---|
| Original vertices / faces | 8 / 6 | 6 / 8 |
| Face centroids added | 6 | 8 |
| Subdivided vertex degrees | six of degree 4, eight of degree 6 | eight of degree 3, six of degree 8 |
| Origin-cone volume per triangle | 1/3 | 1/18 |
| Total volume in the stated coordinates | 8 | 4/3 |

The coordinate sizes are explicit conventions, not matched physical radii or
energy scales. Every supporting-plane and volume check is rational. The trial
Hamiltonian below uses unweighted incidence, not these volumes or edge lengths.

## The cube's geometric eigenline is not full-support

In both cases use c_cycle=(1,omega,omega^2), omega=exp(2*pi*i/3), and

\[
v_0=X_0c_{\mathrm{cycle}},\qquad A_0=X_0 C X_0^{-1}.
\]

Both satisfy A0 v0=omega v0. But their spatial vectors are

\[
v_0^{\mathrm{cube}}=(0,i\sqrt3,-1),\qquad
v_0^{\mathrm{oct}}=
\left(-\frac16+\frac{i\sqrt3}{2},
      -\frac16-\frac{i\sqrt3}{2},\frac13\right).
\]

The zero is forced by the cube's face plane, not numerical cancellation. If
n^T p=d for every vertex column of X0, then

\[
n^T X_0c_{\mathrm{cycle}}=d(1+\omega+\omega^2)=0.
\]

Thus the nontrivial cyclic mode is tangent to its supporting face. Cube face
normals are coordinate axes in the monomial frame, forcing one spatial
coordinate to vanish. Octahedral face normals are diagonal directions, so the
same tangency equation need not set an individual coordinate to zero.

Every transported cube vector has two nonzero coordinates. Its local squared
norm is 4, and the assembled vector u has squared norm 96, with 48 nonzero
coordinates in an ambient 72-dimensional space. For the octahedron those values
are 5/3, 40 and 72 respectively. Raw vector norms are not assigned physical energy:
normalizing the projectors removes an arbitrary common vector scale.

Adding a normal component to the cube vector, for example replacing it by
(1,i*sqrt(3),-1), restores full support and the 7128 architecture. The checker
also verifies that this supplied vector is no longer an omega eigenvector of
the cube's A0. That repair changes the geometric selection; it is not merely
a different description of the same cyclic mode.

## Support counts versus closed-traversal counts

Let L select the local lines, R align fields using the signed rotations, and
N=RL=LR be the collective rank-one projector. Direct sparse products verify
these equalities for both geometries, including the cube's zero coordinates.

| Count | Native cube | Native octahedron |
|---|---:|---:|
| Local selection L | 96 | 216 |
| Alignment R | 1728 | 1728 |
| Dense collective feedback N | 2304 | 5184 |
| Sum of full typed stage supports | **4128** | **7128** |
| Closed-traversal covered stage supports | 96 + 1152 + 2304 | 216 + 1728 + 5184 |
| Closed-traversal arrow union | **3552** | **7128** |
| Identity-feedback implementation, keeping full L and R | **1896** | **2016** |

Each nonzero collective feedback entry closes a unique three-step traversal.
Their positive weights sum to one in both cases. In the cube, 576 alignment
arrows transport the unused face-normal coordinates and occur in none of those
traversals. Removing those R entries preserves RL=N, as checked directly.

This distinction matters: full support was needed both for the theorem's dense
count and for complete closed-traversal coverage of the three-stage graph.
The cube experiment must not silently replace one counting convention by the
other.

The earlier tetrahedral values 1836 versus 576, and the present alternatives,
therefore remain implementation counts. They do not define invariant energies
of the common return operator.

## First energy audit: use the return operator alone

A simple positive penalty is

\[
H_{\mathrm{return}}=I-N.
\]

For each geometry the exact checker verifies its idempotence, trace 71, and
annihilation of u. Its spectrum is zero once and one 71 times. Replacing dense
feedback by identity feedback does not change N, so it does not change this
penalty. But its spectrum also does not distinguish cube from octahedron.

Replacing raw arrow counts by sums of squared coefficient magnitudes does not
solve the general architecture problem either. The sum of squared
Hilbert-Schmidt norms is

    ||L||_HS^2 + ||R||_HS^2 + ||N||_HS^2 = 24+3+1 = 28,

whereas identity feedback gives 24+3+72=99 for the same return. These quantities
could measure a declared implementation cost; they are not thereby invariant
rest energies.

## Second energy audit: retain the source adjacency

Give each small triangle one graph node, and join two nodes when their triangles
share an edge. Both source graphs have 24 nodes and 36 edges and are connected
and three-regular. Their adjacencies are checked to be equivariant under the
same left group action on triangles. They are not the same graph.

Choose uniform graph weights as a **trial interaction law**. Let D be its scalar
combinatorial Laplacian, D=3I-A. Exact integer characteristic polynomials factor
as follows, with t the eigenvalue variable:

\[
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{cube}}(t)={}&t(t-2)^3(t-4)^3(t-6)\\
 &\cdot(t^2-8t+14)^3(t^2-6t+6)^2(t^2-4t+2)^3,
\end{aligned}
\]

\[
p_{\mathrm{oct}}(t)=t(t-1)^3(t-2)(t-3)^5(t-4)^3(t-5)^5(t^2-5t+2)^3.
\]

Both have a simple zero eigenvalue. Their first positive eigenvalues are

\[
\lambda_1^{\mathrm{cube}}=2-\sqrt2\approx0.5857864376,
\qquad
\lambda_1^{\mathrm{oct}}=\frac{5-\sqrt{17}}2\approx0.4384471872.
\]

Both first positive eigenvalues have multiplicity three. Factorization and its
reconstruction are exact; the checker also certifies rational bounds enclosing
the printed decimal gap values. No numerical eigensolver or external algebra
package is used.

### Frame-aware local Hamiltonian

For triangle positions x,z, with spatial rotations rho(x), define the connection
Laplacian

\[
K_{xz}=D_{xz}\rho(x)\rho(z)^\dagger.
\]

With U=direct-sum_x rho(x),

\[
U^\dagger K U=D\otimes I_3,\qquad
U^\dagger L U=I_{24}\otimes P_{v_0}.
\]

The checker verifies both frame reductions exactly. K and L commute. The trial
Hamiltonian is

\[
\boxed{H(a,b)=aK+b(I-L),\qquad a,b>0.}
\]

Its quadratic form is

\[
\langle v,H(a,b)v\rangle
=a\sum_{\{x,z\}\in E}
\|\rho(x)^\dagger v_x-\rho(z)^\dagger v_z\|^2
+b\sum_x\|(I-P_x)v_x\|^2.
\]

This is nonnegative and uses only shared-edge interactions plus a local line
penalty. Connectedness of the graph and the rank-one local line give a unique
zero-energy line, span(u). The spectrum follows from the frame reduction: for
each scalar eigenvalue lambda, there is a branch a*lambda once and a*lambda+b
twice, with the scalar multiplicities retained. Hence

\[
\Delta(a,b)=\min\{a\lambda_1,b\}.
\]

At a=b=1, the gaps are the two distinct values above. The larger arrow count
belongs to the smaller gap: energy ordering does not follow support ordering
in this explicit model. At a=1 and b=1/10, both gaps instead become 1/10.

This H is independent of which circuit implements the same L/R collective
return: neither a materialized dense feedback stage nor its arrow support
appears in the formula. It does depend on the chosen source adjacency, flat
frame transport, selected line and coupling law. Architecture independence here
is not invariance under changing those physical-model inputs.

## What has and has not been gained

We have an explicit positive, graph-local trial Hamiltonian that distinguishes
geometries, has an isolated collective ground-state line, and passes the feedback
implementation test. The source graph supplies meaningful dimensionless spectral
data that the group order and representation dimension alone do not supply.

However:

- Uniform shared-edge weights and the positive local penalty are declared,
  not uniquely selected by the carrier. Alternative geometric weights remain
  possible; shared edges need not have identical geometric lengths.
- The coupling ratio a/b is unspecified. Its variation can remove the gap
  distinction, as the 1/10 control demonstrates.
- The overall energy scale and absolute offset remain free.
- The selected line has ground-relative energy zero by construction. This is
  neither a rest-mass calculation nor a prediction of a massless particle.
- Unitary evolution under this finite Hamiltonian does not make the selected
  eigenstate an attractor. Preparation and relaxation require further dynamics.
- No electron reference, proton sector, particle statistics or relativistic
  dispersion law has been identified.

The next useful question is whether the actual retained-path/edge process fixes
these local couplings or instead falsifies this quadratic rule. It is not yet
useful to fit the gaps or support counts to a particle mass table.
