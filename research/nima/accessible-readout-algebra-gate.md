# Accessible readout algebra on a protected quotient

## Construction

Let \(\mathcal H_{\mathrm{phys}}\) be a protected quotient or code space and
let \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{source}}\) be the algebra generated by admitted
source operations. The accessible operational algebra is the induced image

\[
\mathcal A_{\mathrm{acc}}
=\operatorname{im}
\left(
\mathcal A_{\mathrm{source}}	o
\operatorname{End}(\mathcal H_{\mathrm{phys}})
\right).
\]

A proposed port is operationally available only if its observable or
instrument is realized in \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{acc}}\), or in a declared
completion equipped with an explicit limiting protocol.

This improves the constructibility criterion. Nontrivial support is necessary
in the toric model, but support alone does not say which logical operations
the source can implement.

## Exact two-logical-qubit census

The toric ground space on a torus carries two logical qubits. Modulo phases,
its Pauli operators are labelled by

\[
\mathbf F_2^4
=\langle X_1,X_2,Z_1,Z_2\rangle.
\]

The exact generated-algebra census gives:

| Admitted ports | Pauli-label rank | Basis size | Capability |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| local stabilizers only | 0 | 1 | no logical readout |
| one loop port \(Z_1\) | 1 | 2 | detect one binary grade |
| commuting \(Z_1,Z_2\) | 2 | 4 | separate one logical basis |
| \(X_1,X_2,Z_1,Z_2\) | 4 | 16 | full logical operator basis |

The commuting pair distinguishes computational-basis labels but cannot read
arbitrary coherences. Full reconstruction requires the intersecting primal
and dual ports whose symplectic pairing makes the algebra noncommutative.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{global support creates access;
complementary noncommuting ports create tomography.}
}
\]

## Architectural consequence

The sequence is now:

\[
\text{source constructors}
\to
\mathcal A_{\mathrm{acc}}
\to
\text{available instruments}
\to
\text{record algebra}.
\]

A groupoid-descending functional outside \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{acc}}\) is an
unimplemented API: mathematically specified, perhaps internally consistent,
but not callable by the physical system.

The distinction between detection, basis separation, and reconstruction is
therefore algebraic rather than verbal:

- detection needs one nontrivial element;
- separation of a declared classical basis needs a maximal commuting
  subalgebra;
- quantum reconstruction needs an informationally complete noncommuting
  operator system.

## Limits

This finite Pauli census does not prove that a laboratory protocol implements
all generated logical operators. It classifies the algebra after the relevant
constructors have been admitted. Kitaev's source audit must still derive those
constructors from Hamiltonian dynamics, controls, ancillas, and measurements.

Nor does the criterion say that every physical observable must be a bounded
finite algebra element. Infinite-volume or continuum sectors may require a
source-defined topology and completion. That completion is part of the type,
not permission to insert a desired readout by closure.

## Cross-sector test contract

For another sector:

1. identify the protected or physical quotient;
2. enumerate source-admitted constructors;
3. compute their induced operational image;
4. locate the proposed readout in that image or a typed completion;
5. distinguish detection, separation, and reconstruction ranks;
6. reject ports requiring an operation absent from the source.

This is the operational form of the claim that sector constraints and
readouts are generated rather than freely supplied parameters.
