# Flavor Staged Observers Reconcile by Lineage Pullback

## Result

Flavor observers at different stages should reconcile only when their records
come from the same preparation lineage. The terminal operation is a pullback,
not a Cartesian join and not a fit based on similar numerical outputs.

The minimum common key contains:

- preparation identity;
- observation epoch;
- resolved messenger or transition path.

Additional frame revisions may be required by a concrete experiment.

## Staged records

An early unsigned observer may record:

- hierarchy exponents;
- spectral moments;
- projective seed weights;
- positive cyclicity.

A later signed observer may record:

- family-relative orientation;
- volume-relative phase;
- a CP-sensitive character.

These records are produced at different construction stages, but they refer to
one evolving physical preparation.

## Pullback law

Let \(U\) be the unsigned-record object and \(S\) the signed-record object.
Both map to a lineage object \(L\):

\[
U\longrightarrow L
\longleftarrow S.
\]

The terminal Flavor packet lies in

\[
U\times_L S.
\]

Only pairs with equal lineage images are admitted. This retains records from
different stages without pretending they were simultaneous or independently
composable.

## Exact record-swapping hostile

The checker uses two preparations. They share the same (1/2/3) hierarchy
pattern but have different moment packets, messenger paths, and signed sheets.

An unkeyed Cartesian join produces four terminal packets. Only two correspond
to real preparations. The other two attach each signed record to the other
preparation's unsigned record.

The keyed pullback returns exactly the two physical packets.

Matching the hierarchy exponents is not enough. WP614 already shows that one
charge pattern admits continuously different Wilson coefficients and both
zero and nonzero CP phase. The hierarchy lens therefore cannot serve as a
preparation identifier.

## Temporal requirement

Matching a run label is also insufficient if the observations belong to
different epochs. The source may evolve, the phase reference may drift, or the
messenger realization may change. The pullback must compare the full declared
lineage key.

This is the temporal version of ordered-port typing. Early and late records are
not commutative values in a bag; they occupy positions in one construction
history.

## Interaction Net interpretation

The history-retaining Interaction Net emits typed records at several depths.
Each record carries a pointer to the same net execution and the path by which
it was produced. The terminal reconciliation node consumes those pointers as
part of its input signature.

Deleting the lineage pointer and retaining only output values quotients the
execution graph. No downstream scalar can reconstruct which early and late
records originally belonged together.

## WP614 placement

The one-spurion charge grammar with charges \((3,2,0)\) conditionally supplies
an early preparation topology: one-, two-, and three-insertion paths. It does
not select Wilson coefficients or the CP phase.

Accordingly:

1. the early observer may verify the resolved messenger topology;
2. later observers measure moments and signed CP-sensitive data;
3. terminal reconciliation must retain the specific messenger lineage;
4. none of these observations derives the charge vector or prepares the
   numerical coefficients.

## Finite falsifiers

A staged observer proposal fails if:

- it admits the Cartesian product of early and late records;
- it joins records solely by equal hierarchy powers;
- it joins the same nominal run across incompatible epochs;
- it discards the messenger path before terminal reconciliation.

## Status

The two-stage observer intuition is now operational. Early unsigned and later
signed measurements can coexist without collapsing their types, provided the
terminal constructor forms a lineage pullback. The remaining source problem is
still preparation: derive the charge and messenger grammar rather than infer
it from the observed hierarchy.
