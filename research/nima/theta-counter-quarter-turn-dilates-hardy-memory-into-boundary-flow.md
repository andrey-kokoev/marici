# The theta counter-quarter-turn dilates Hardy memory into boundary flow

## Ordered-port correction

Generic dilation restores abstract defect ports but does not identify them with
the ordered theta endpoint and source ports. Any theta use must carry the flag
\((b_0,b_f)\) and prove its incidence with the dilation. An unframed unitary
boundary completion has the wrong observable authority.

Exact contraction-dilation theorem and architectural correction. The forward
tenth turn compresses a causal inner factor into its model-space state machine.
Turning the other way does not erase that state. It opens the state machine by
adjoining the defect channels required for a unitary boundary evolution.

The resulting object is a conservative colligation. It restores the boundary
ports forgotten by compression, but it does not by itself exclude hostile
zeros.

## Fixed memory contraction

Let \(A\) be the canonical contraction acting on the Hardy model space
\(\mathcal K\). Define its two defect operators

\[
D_A
=
\left(I-A^*A\right)^{1/2},
\qquad
D_{A^*}
=
\left(I-AA^*\right)^{1/2}.
\]

They measure two different failures:

- \(D_A\) measures loss seen from the incoming state geometry;
- \(D_{A^*}\) measures loss seen from the outgoing state geometry.

Neither defect may be erased if the compressed evolution is to be reopened as
a lossless process.

## The counter-quarter-turn

Form the Julia--Halmos operator

\[
\mathcal J_A
=
\begin{pmatrix}
A & D_{A^*}\\
D_A & -A^*
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The intertwining identities

\[
AD_A=D_{A^*}A,
\qquad
A^*D_{A^*}=D_AA^*
\]

imply

\[
\mathcal J_A^*\mathcal J_A
=
\mathcal J_A\mathcal J_A^*
=
I.
\]

Thus every contraction can be reopened as a unitary two-port evolution after
its incoming and outgoing defect channels are adjoined.

This is the opposite-direction rotation:

\[
A
\longrightarrow
\left(\mathcal D_A,\mathcal D_{A^*}\right)
\longrightarrow
\mathcal J_A.
\]

In mathematical symbols,

\[
A
\longrightarrow
\left(D_A,D_{A^*}\right)
\longrightarrow
\mathcal J_A.
\]

## Why this is not an inverse

Compression forgets how an internal defect was attached to its environment.
Many unitary systems can have the same compressed contraction. Dilation must
therefore add a boundary space and an attachment map; it cannot recover them
from the compressed bytes alone without a minimality or source condition.

The counter-turn is a lift along a forgetful map, not an inverse isomorphism.

## One-pole model

For a degree-one Blaschke factor, the model space is one-dimensional. Its
compressed model operator is multiplication by the conjugate zero coordinate,

\[
A=\overline a,
\qquad
|a|<1.
\]

The two scalar defect magnitudes agree:

\[
D_A=D_{A^*}=\sqrt{1-|a|^2}.
\]

The unitary dilation is

\[
\mathcal J_a
=
\begin{pmatrix}
\overline a & \sqrt{1-|a|^2}\\
\sqrt{1-|a|^2} & -a
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The hostile zero has now become the internal reflection coefficient of a
perfectly lossless two-port scatterer. Its displacement from the seam is paid
for by nonzero coupling to the boundary channel.

Therefore lossless dilation also does not exclude off-seam zeros.

## What the reverse direction restores

The forward compression makes a zero look like internal memory. The reverse
dilation reveals the same datum as a relationship among:

- an internal state;
- an incoming boundary defect;
- an outgoing boundary defect;
- and a unitary conservation law joining them.

This is closer to the source architecture already found in the theta/Tate
lane, where the seam cannot be reconstructed boundedly from the retained tail
and must remain an independent physical component.

## Source-derived gate

Generic dilation is too permissive: it unitary-realizes every contraction. The
RH-bearing question is not whether a unitary dilation exists. It is whether the
labelled arithmetic source supplies a particular dilation whose defect ports
are exactly the primitive, square, seam, and archimedean currents.

The required incidence maps are

\[
\iota_{\rm in}:\mathcal D_A\longrightarrow\mathcal B_{\Theta},
\qquad
\iota_{\rm out}:\mathcal D_{A^*}\longrightarrow\mathcal B_{\Theta},
\]

where \(\mathcal B_{\Theta}\) is the complete typed theta boundary object.

They must satisfy:

1. source provenance at every finite cutoff;
2. equality with the actual defect quadratic forms;
3. reciprocal compatibility between the two analytic sectors;
4. continuity under restricted-product completion;
5. no extra boundary dimensions fitted to hostile zeros.

## Finite falsifiers

A proposed source dilation fails if:

- its declared boundary incidence does not reproduce \(D_A^2\) and
  \(D_{A^*}^2\);
- it needs a new port for each hostile zero without arithmetic provenance;
- incoming and outgoing defect ranks disagree with the typed source currents;
- the completed dilation is unitary only after scalar cancellation;
- or two inequivalent boundary attachments yield the same compressed model
  with no source selector.

The degree-one matrix \(\mathcal J_a\) is the minimal hostile: it proves that
unitary boundary completion alone realizes every interior zero.

## Decisive conclusion

Turning the other way replaces hidden memory by explicit boundary exchange.
This is structurally preferable for the theta/Tate programme because it moves
toward source incidence rather than further into scalar readout. But generic
unitary dilation still adds no RH selectivity. The next decisive object is the
source-derived identification of the two defect ports with the complete
arithmetic and archimedean boundary currents.
