# A scalar Weyl realization would prove simplicity as well as RH

The rank-one dark-channel route is stronger than seam confinement.

Let
\[
a(z)=\theta_d-m_d(z),
\]
where \(m_d\) is a nonconstant scalar Nevanlinna function and \(\theta_d\in\mathbb R\). At a real zero \(x_0\) away from poles and continuous-density singularities,
\[
m_d(x_0)=\theta_d.
\]
The derivative satisfies
\[
m_d'(x_0)
=
\beta
+
\int_{\mathbb R}\frac{d\mu_d(t)}{(t-x_0)^2}
>0
\]
for a minimal active channel. Therefore
\[
a'(x_0)=-m_d'(x_0)<0.
\]
Every zero is simple.

Thus an identification
\[
a(z)
=
N(z)\,
\xi\left(\frac12-iz\right),
\qquad
N(z)\neq0,
\]
would prove not only RH but also simplicity of all nontrivial zeta zeros represented in the regular scalar Weyl regime.

This is logically permissible, but it is a major additional burden. Simplicity is not known, so the model must not present it as a routine byproduct without exposing the strengthened claim.

There are only several ways multiple zeros could survive:

1. The dark boundary space has dimension greater than one, allowing geometric multiplicity.
2. The boundary parameter \(\Theta(z)\) depends on \(z\), and its derivative can cancel \(M'(z)\).
3. The zero occurs at a pole, threshold, or singular-continuous point where the regular derivative formula fails.
4. The scalar observer is nonminimal and misses a reducing multiplicity space.
5. The zero order of the completed scalar includes a normalization factor that is not actually zero-free.

Each option changes the clean rank-one theorem.

For a scalar boundary triple with a cyclic boundary vector, self-adjoint rank-one extension spectra are simple. Hidden multiplicity would contradict cyclicity or observer faithfulness. Therefore one cannot retain both:

\[
\text{faithful minimal scalar Weyl channel}
\]
and
\[
\text{arbitrary zero multiplicities}.
\]

The programme should choose its declared target.

## Strong target

Prove a minimal scalar Weyl realization and accept that the theorem implies RH plus simplicity. Then the determinant identification must preserve zero order exactly.

## Multiplicity-safe target

Retain a matrix-valued dark Weyl function
\[
M_d(z)
\]
on a boundary space large enough to carry possible multiplicity. Seam confinement follows from Nevanlinna positivity, while
\[
\det(\Theta_d-M_d(z))
\]
can have higher-order zeros through kernel dimension.

Even in the matrix case, positive derivative on the kernel makes crossings semisimple and the determinant order equals kernel dimension. Multiplicity is bounded by the dark boundary dimension unless the model has variable or infinite rank.

This yields a new rank constraint. A fixed two-dimensional full boundary with a one-dimensional dark sector can represent only simple regular zeros. If a multiple zeta zero exists, that exact architecture is falsified.

The smallest hostile claims to preserve unknown multiplicities while using
\[
m_d'(x)>0
\]
and a zero-free scalar normalization. The claim is inconsistent.

A second hostile inserts multiplicity into a scalar determinant by raising it to a fitted power at selected points. This breaks analytic constructor authority.

A third hostile invokes a hidden orthogonal eigenspace while maintaining that the scalar boundary vector is cyclic.

The immediate packet should record:

- declared dark boundary rank;
- minimality or cyclicity status;
- regularity class of seam crossings;
- maximum representable multiplicity;
- whether the final theorem intentionally includes simplicity.

This is not a peripheral issue. It decides whether the elegant scalar reduction is a plausible RH architecture or a stronger conjectural programme.
