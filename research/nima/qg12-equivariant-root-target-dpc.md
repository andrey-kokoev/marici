# Equivariant unordered root target: new cycle 1

## Problem

The ordered-root requirement was too strong. Root transposition canonically leaves the rank-one target generated by the even class, but a target object need not construct a source chain map.

## Bold conjecture

The canonical invariant submodule of the unordered Cayley–Menger root pair is sufficient by itself to construct the physical pairing of the unsplit source contour.

## Named rivals

1. target invariance supplies the missing pairing;
2. target invariance constrains any equivariant map but does not establish its existence or normalization;
3. a non-equivariant source specialization may still land on an odd root class.

## Risky consequences

If the bold conjecture is correct, root transposition invariance must exclude the zero map and determine the primitive coefficient of the even generator.

## Strongest falsification attempt and residual

For a trivial rank-one source and root-pair lattice with transposition, write `f(1)=(a,b)`. Equivariance gives

\[
(a,b)=(b,a),
\]

so every equivariant map has the form

\[
f(1)=n(\gamma_-+\gamma_+).
\]

Execution `structured_command_execution:e_31668_1788304071950546200_4` confirms that every such image has even total parity, but also admits `n=0` and every nonprimitive multiplier. Thus the invariant target determines neither existence nor normalization. The bold conjecture is falsified.

## Disposition and residual conjecture

Ordering is unnecessary for the even target class. The exact remaining condition is weaker and sharper: a source-derived, nonzero, primitive, root-transposition-equivariant specialization would land uniquely on the even generator up to orientation. Existing evidence provides none of nonzero existence, primitivity, or source equivariance.

The next executable test is to audit whether the source-sewn unsplit canonical form carries an occurrence-exchange involution compatible with root transposition. Success would prove conditional evenness while leaving existence and primitive normalization as separate gates.
