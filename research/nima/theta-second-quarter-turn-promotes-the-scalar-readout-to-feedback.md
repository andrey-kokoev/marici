# The second theta quarter-turn promotes the scalar readout to feedback

## Status

Exact linearization theorem and source-specific candidate. The full-history
Dirac operator is held fixed. A second rotation converts a scalar transfer
zero into an eigenstate of a one-port feedback extension, rather than forcing
it to be an eigenstate of the bare Dirac block.

## Fixed invariant core

Retain the canonical history Dirac operator

\[
D_H
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&H^*\\
H&0
\end{pmatrix}
\]

on

\[
\mathcal K=\mathcal C\oplus\mathcal S.
\]

The first round of rotations is now frozen:

- \(H\) is the source-history incidence;
- \(H^*\) is generated by graph-conormal rotation;
- \(D_H\) is selfadjoint when \(H\) is closed and densely defined;
- \(D_H^2\) contains the two positive Gramians.

No later rotation may redefine these objects.

## Why the bare Dirac eigenstate is too strong

A bare eigenstate would satisfy

\[
H^*u=\lambda v,
\qquad
Hv=\lambda u,
\]

and therefore

\[
H^*Hv=\lambda^2v.
\]

A scalar Mellin zero currently supplies an endpoint or orthogonality condition,
not this singular-vector equation. Essential-spectrum saturation of a
correlation also need not produce an eigenvector.

The correct linearization should retain the scalar port that generated the
readout.

## Feedback extension

Let

\[
b:\mathbb C\longrightarrow\mathcal K
\]

be the source incidence of one scalar port, and let

\[
\ell:\mathcal K\longrightarrow\mathbb C
\]

be its readout. Introduce a scalar feedback coordinate \(c\) and the block
operator

\[
\mathbb A
=
\begin{pmatrix}
D_H&b\\
\ell&\alpha
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The eigenvalue equation

\[
(\mathbb A-\lambda)
\binom{\psi}{c}=0
\]

is equivalent, away from \(\operatorname{spec}D_H\), to

\[
\psi=-(D_H-\lambda)^{-1}bc
\]

and

\[
M(\lambda)c=0,
\]

where

\[
M(\lambda)
=
\alpha-\lambda
-
\ell(D_H-\lambda)^{-1}b.
\]

Thus

\[
M(\lambda)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\lambda\in\operatorname{spec}\mathbb A
\]

subject to the usual pole and multiplicity bookkeeping.

The scalar zero has been rotated into a feedback eigenstate.

## Selfadjointness gate

The feedback extension is selfadjoint precisely when the source data satisfy

\[
\ell=b^*
\]

in the declared metrics and domains, and

\[
\alpha\in\mathbb R.
\]

Then

\[
\mathbb A
=
\begin{pmatrix}
D_H&b\\
b^*&\alpha
\end{pmatrix}
\]

is a selfadjoint one-port extension. Every discrete characteristic value is
real in the rotated spectral coordinate.

This is the exact place where source orientation enters. A forward incidence
and an unrelated scalar readout give a nonselfadjoint colligation even though
the internal Dirac block is selfadjoint.

## Second-round rotations

With \(D_H\) fixed, the remaining quarter-turns have the following roles.

### Readout to feedback

The covector equation

\[
\ell\psi=0
\]

is promoted to a dynamical scalar coordinate. The transfer function becomes a
Schur complement of the enlarged operator.

### Boundary condition to characteristic determinant

The boundary compatibility condition becomes

\[
\det M(\lambda)=0.
\]

For one port this is scalar; for the complete arithmetic boundary packet it is
matrix-valued.

### Skew flow to selfadjoint spectrum

Multiplication by \(i\) rotates the conservative generator into the
selfadjoint spectral convention while leaving the fixed history incidence
unchanged.

### Scalar phase to determinant line

Instead of treating the scalar section as a matrix coefficient of the bare
history transport, treat it as the determinant section of the feedback Schur
complement. This retains multiplicity and kernel/cokernel profiles.

## Source-specific theta instantiation

The source must provide, before scalar comparison:

1. the full-history Dirac operator \(D_H\);
2. a labelled input incidence \(b_X\);
3. a labelled output covector \(\ell_X\);
4. the real boundary parameter \(\alpha_X\);
5. completion-compatible bonding maps;
6. a determinant comparison with the completed theta section.

The first finite test is

\[
R_X^{\mathrm{port}}
=
\ell_X-b_X^*.
\]

If this residual is nonzero, the scalar port does not close the fixed Dirac
core selfadjointly.

## Endpoint evaluation is not automatically the adjoint port

In the current tail system, forward forcing is multiplication by \(f\), while
the scalar zero is expressed through endpoint evaluation of the tail. The
Hilbert adjoint of multiplication by \(f\) is an integral pairing with
\(\overline f\), not generally endpoint evaluation.

Therefore the one-port residual is expected to be nontrivial unless the full
seam, primitive, square, and archimedean boundary packet converts endpoint
evaluation into the adjoint source readout.

This conversion is the finite source theorem to seek. It cannot be supplied by
the scalar functional equation alone.

## Matrix-valued boundary packet

The complete system may require a port space \(\mathcal U_X\) of growing rank.
Then

\[
b_X:\mathcal U_X\longrightarrow\mathcal K_X,
\qquad
\ell_X:\mathcal K_X\longrightarrow\mathcal U_X,
\]

and the Schur complement is

\[
M_X(\lambda)
=
\alpha_X-\lambda I
-
\ell_X(D_{H,X}-\lambda)^{-1}b_X.
\]

Selfadjoint feedback requires

\[
\ell_X=b_X^*,
\qquad
\alpha_X=\alpha_X^*.
\]

The scalar completed section may then be a determinant of \(M_X\), but equal
determinants do not identify Smith profiles or intermediate residue flags.

## Essential spectrum gate

If the normalized correlation reaches its extremal value only through
essential spectrum, a finite-rank feedback extension may produce a resonance
or threshold state rather than an \(L^2\) eigenvector. The determinant bridge
must distinguish:

- discrete eigenvalues;
- embedded eigenvalues;
- resonances;
- threshold singularities;
- essential-spectrum boundary values.

Calling all of these characteristic states would erase the exact obstruction
found in the history audit.

## Finite falsifiers

The feedback route fails if:

1. \(R_X^{\mathrm{port}}\ne0\);
2. \(\alpha_X\) is not Hermitian;
3. port rank is too small for the boundary residual quotient;
4. the determinant matches while kernel/cokernel profiles differ;
5. the required port changes with \(\lambda\) without source authority;
6. a scalar zero maps only to essential-spectrum saturation;
7. the determinant comparison is fitted backward from \(\Xi\);
8. the selfadjoint finite extensions lose closedness or convergence at
   completion.

## Verdict

The second round of quarter-turns produces a better zero-to-state target. A
theta zero need not be an eigenstate of the bare history Dirac operator. It
should be a characteristic state of a source-derived selfadjoint feedback
extension of that fixed operator. The decisive new equality is not
Fourier–Tate reflection alone but adjoint port closure:

\[
\ell=b^*.
\]

If the completed arithmetic boundary packet derives this equality and its
Schur determinant is the completed theta section up to a zero-free factor,
the spectral bridge closes noncircularly. Otherwise the second rotation exposes
the exact port mismatch that blocks it.
