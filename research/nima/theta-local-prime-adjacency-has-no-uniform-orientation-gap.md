# Local prime adjacency has no uniform orientation gap

## Status

Research packet. It proves a completion obstruction for source-local
cross-label energies generated by finitely many prime shifts. It does not
exclude a nonlocal boundary-bearing arithmetic operator.

## One prime-power orbit

Fix a prime (p) and write

\[
e_k=e_{p^k},
\qquad
Se_k=e_{k+1}.
\]

Here (S) is the prime-multiplication shift along the (p)-power orbit. The
most basic cross-label difference is

\[
D=I-S.
\]

For

\[
v_N=\frac1{\sqrt N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}e_k,
\]

one has

\[
\lVert v_N\rVert=1,
\qquad
Dv_N=\frac{e_0-e_N}{\sqrt N},
\qquad
\lVert Dv_N\rVert^2=\frac2N.
\]

Therefore the positive adjacency energy (D^*D) has no uniform lower bound
on the completed prime-power orbit.

## Finite-range extension

The same obstruction applies to every fixed finite-range translation-covariant
difference operator

\[
P(S)=\sum_{j=0}^{r}a_jS^j
\]

that annihilates constant bulk packets, equivalently (P(1)=0). On (v_N),
all interior coefficients cancel. Only (O(r)) boundary labels remain, each
with size (N^{-1/2}). Hence

\[
\lVert P(S)v_N\rVert^2=O(N^{-1}).
\]

No fixed local prime-difference law of this type can be coercive after
completion.

## Several admitted primes

For a fixed finite prime family (F), use rectangular packets in the exponent
lattice

\[
\{0,\ldots,N-1\}^{F}.
\]

Their volume is (N^{|F|}), while the boundary seen by any fixed-range prime
operator is (O(N^{|F|-1})). After normalization, every such boundary energy
tends to zero.

This is the Følner obstruction for the free commutative prime-constructor
monoid. It matches the pro-Gram requirement that continuity be witnessed by
one fixed finite authorized constructor family: every such family has
asymptotically invisible bulk packets.

## Consequence for RH orientation

Prime adjacency genuinely mixes arithmetic labels, unlike the diagonal
Gaussian oscillator. But local mixing alone still cannot provide a uniform
orientation reserve. Finite cutoffs may be faithful and positive while their
smallest energy tends to zero.

Thus neither of the two simplest positive source energies works:

- the local oscillator energy vanishes on the whole Gaussian-labelled
  coefficient module;
- the local prime-adjacency energy is positive on finite differences but has
  spectral bottom zero in completion.

## What could escape the obstruction

A surviving operator must violate at least one hypothesis of the no-go in a
source-authorized way. It could be:

1. nonlocal across arbitrarily many prime scales;
2. anchored by a boundary term whose strength does not vanish on Følner
   packets;
3. label-dependent rather than translation-covariant;
4. defined in a stronger constructor topology that does not normalize long
   Følner blocks;
5. or coupled to the seam and archimedean state so that arithmetic escape
   creates a compensating physical cost.

Each option must still preserve unique-factorization typing and reject signed
hostile sources.

## Boundary anchoring gate

Adding one finite-rank endpoint detector does not automatically repair the
gap. A long block may be chosen far from the anchored labels or orthogonal to
the detector. A successful boundary anchor must be transported through the
entire prime orbit or coupled through a nonlocal current.

The primitive and prime-square currents are natural candidates because their
completion defects already live at the boundary between local Euler
factorization and global sewing. The exact question is whether they measure
the Følner boundary with a cutoff-independent lower bound.

## Finite falsifier

The single-prime packet (v_N) is the minimal falsifier. For every proposed
fixed-range prime energy, compute its exact value on (v_N). If it tends to
zero while the declared source norm stays one, the energy cannot orient the
completed arithmetic module.

## Conclusion

Cross-label mixing is necessary but not sufficient. The prime constructor
graph is amenable, so every fixed local difference energy admits normalized
packets whose cost escapes to the boundary. Any RH-bearing arithmetic law
must be nonlocal, boundary-anchored, topologically stronger, or genuinely
coupled to the seam and archimedean sectors.
