# Flavor Alignment Needs an Alternating Cubic Carrier

## Result

The bipartite Flavor construction requires two distinct source structures:

1. a shared pairing that aligns the labels of the two families;
2. an alternating cubic carrier that orients the aligned three-cycle.

Alignment alone does not distinguish clockwise from counterclockwise transport.

## Independent-family quotient

Let the two three-element families admit independent relabellings

\[
(L,R)\in S_3\times S_3.
\]

A cross-block \(K:A\to B\) transforms as

\[
K\longmapsto L^T K R.
\]

For any permutation cross-block \(K\), an independent relabelling of \(B\)
can turn \(K\) into the identity. The six permutation matrices therefore form
one orbit under the 36-element independent-family group. In particular,
\(P\), \(P^T\), and \(I\) are gauge-equivalent.

Bare family typing supplies two carriers but no common coordinate system.

## Alignment reduces the gauge group

Adjoin a nondegenerate reference pairing

\[
E:A\longrightarrow B.
\]

In aligned coordinates take \(E=I\). Preserving it requires

\[
L^T R=I,
\]

so \(R=L\). The independent group reduces to diagonal \(S_3\).

This makes the cross-block a genuine endomorphism of one aligned label set.
But it still does not orient that label set.

## Reflection remains after alignment

Under the residual diagonal action,

\[
P\longmapsto L^T P L.
\]

The orbit of \(P\) under diagonal \(S_3\) is

\[
\{P,P^T\}.
\]

Every odd permutation reverses the three-cycle and conjugates the two
orientations. Thus an un-oriented alignment still treats clockwise and
counterclockwise as the same source orbit.

## Cubic orientation repair

Let \(\omega\) be an alternating trilinear carrier on the aligned family. Under
a relabelling \(L\),

\[
\omega\longmapsto \operatorname{sgn}(L)\omega.
\]

Preserving \(\omega\) reduces the residual group to

\[
A_3\cong C_3.
\]

Because this subgroup is generated by \(P\), conjugation fixes \(P\) and
\(P^T\) separately. Their oriented orbits are disjoint singletons.

The alternating carrier supplies exactly the missing orientation wall. It is
not another quadratic width or two-port scalar; it is a cubic relation among
the three aligned labels.

## Stabilizer chain

The source reduction is

\[
S_3\times S_3
\supset
S_3^{\mathrm{diag}}
\supset
A_3^{\mathrm{diag}}.
\]

The first reduction is generated by the shared pairing \(E\). The second is
generated by the alternating carrier \(\omega\).

The corresponding information gain is:

- before alignment, all six permutation cross-blocks are one orbit;
- after alignment, the two cyclic orientations remain one orbit;
- after cubic orientation, they become two distinct orbits.

## Relation to the prior torsor result

The ordered \(A/B\) sign local system remains necessary, but it is not the
whole frame. Family exchange controls which carrier is called \(A\) or \(B\).
The alternating cubic carrier controls orientation inside their shared
three-label coordinate system.

These are separate frame coordinates. Fixing one does not fix the other.

## Finite falsifiers

A proposed microscopic mediator fails if either condition holds:

1. its reference pairing is absent, so an independent relabelling sends its
   directed cross-block to the identity;
2. its pairing exists but no alternating carrier is present, so an odd
   diagonal relabelling conjugates \(P\) to \(P^T\).

The exact checker exhausts all 36 independent relabellings and all six residual
diagonal relabellings.

## Revised source target

The smallest viable Flavor Carrier now needs:

1. two inequivalent three-state families;
2. a source-derived nondegenerate pairing aligning them;
3. a source-derived alternating cubic carrier on the aligned labels;
4. a directed reciprocal cross-block measured relative to that structure;
5. the temporal coherence process and ordered detector ports;
6. calibrated descent to physical16.

This is a concrete microscopic search signature. A quadratic one-family
Gaussian mediator cannot meet it. A bipartite mediator without the cubic
carrier also cannot meet it.
