# The primitive seam line cancels only the simple-zero Clark channel

## Status

Exact multiplicity obstruction. The single primitive seam line surviving the
continuous bilateral Green descent is the first Clark feature. It is nonzero at
a simple scalar zero but vanishes at every zero of multiplicity at least two.
The filtered Clark tower, however, detects a multiplicity-\(m\) zero at its
\(m\)-th jet and carries positive supply there.

Therefore one primitive seam line is rank-sufficient abstractly but not
sensor-sufficient for arbitrary multiplicity. A complete signed balance needs
a filtered hierarchy of negative seam currents, a source-derived recurrence
reducing higher jets to the primitive line, or an independent source theorem
that all relevant zeros are simple.

## Primitive seam readout

Let \(F(z)\) be the scalar endpoint transform and let

\[
\mathcal C_a=1+ia\partial_z.
\]

The surviving primitive seam feature is

\[
r_1(z)=\mathcal C_aF(z)
=
F(z)+iaF'(z).
\]

This is the boundary value of the first Clark-differentiated tail feature. It
is source-derived before any zero is selected.

## Simple zero

If \(z_0\) is a simple zero, then

\[
F(z_0)=0,
\qquad
F'(z_0)\neq0.
\]

Hence

\[
r_1(z_0)=iaF'(z_0)\neq0
\]

for \(a\neq0\). The primitive seam line sees the simple zero-state and has the
correct rank to cancel one positive Clark supply channel.

Exact coefficient and phase matching remain unproved.

## Multiple zero

Suppose \(z_0\) has multiplicity \(m\geq2\). Then

\[
F(z_0)=F'(z_0)=0,
\]

so

\[
r_1(z_0)=0.
\]

The primitive seam supply therefore vanishes on this zero-state.

But the \(m\)-th Clark iterate satisfies

\[
\mathcal C_a^mF(z_0)
=(ia)^mF^{(m)}(z_0)
\neq0.
\]

Its positive Gram supply is

\[
|a|^{2m}|F^{(m)}(z_0)|^2>0.
\]

Thus the first primitive seam line contributes zero precisely when the
source-authorized higher Clark sensor contributes positive supply.

## Minimal finite falsifier

Use

\[
F(z)=z^2
\]

at \(z_0=0\). Then

\[
\mathcal C_aF(0)=0,
\qquad
\mathcal C_a^2F(0)=-2a^2\neq0
\]

for \(a\neq0\).

Any signed-balance compiler that assigns the first primitive seam line as the
sole negative partner of the complete Clark jet supply fails on this two-jet
witness.

## Three surviving architectures

### Filtered negative-current tower

The source supplies seam currents

\[
r_j=\mathcal C_a^jF,
\qquad
j=1,2,\ldots,
\]

with the first nonzero negative partner appearing at the same filtration stage
as the first nonzero positive Clark sensor.

This preserves arbitrary finite multiplicity but requires a typed infinite
filtered completion. It cannot be replaced by one unweighted Hilbert direct
sum.

### Source recurrence

An independently derived differential relation expresses all higher Clark jets
through a finite boundary-current module. Such a relation must precede zero
inspection and remain regular at multiple zeros.

For a generic entire transform no such recurrence exists. It would be genuine
theta-specific structure.

### Source-derived simplicity

A theorem proves

\[
F(z_0)=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
F'(z_0)\neq0
\]

for every relevant zero. This would make the primitive line sufficient, but it
is stronger than RH and cannot be assumed or inferred numerically.

## Relation to the negative canonical-block result

The physical one-block/two-lobe canonical form is negative in a source-derived
small-normal-parameter regime. Therefore the missing higher-multiplicity repair
cannot be recovered by strengthening that scalar block. It must act in a larger
source block or before the two-lobe projection.

The filtered Clark and seam jets are precisely such a pre-projection source
block.

## Constraint-module consequence

At multiplicity \(m\), the first seam generator vanishes to insufficient jet
order relative to the positive \(m\)-th Clark supply. The constrained Hermitian
module must therefore include generators through at least order \(m\), unless a
source recurrence lowers the required order.

The earlier regular-multiplier gate detects this obstruction as failure of
determinantal-ideal membership.

## Completion gate

Every individual zero has finite multiplicity, so a filtered construction can
resolve it at a finite stage. But no uniform finite stage is known to cover all
zeros. A completed theorem must therefore preserve the filtration without
silently asserting boundedness of the infinite jet observation operator.

The negative current at each stage must have source-derived normalization and
must sew compatibly with the primitive, prime-square, and archimedean boundary
types.

## Decisive conclusion

One primitive seam line is exactly sufficient for simple zeros and definitely
insufficient for multiple zeros. The signed-balance programme must now choose
and construct one of three stronger source mechanisms: a filtered negative
Clark-seam tower, a finite theta-specific jet recurrence, or an independent
simplicity theorem.

Until one exists, the claimed cancellation handles only the simple-zero branch
and cannot support an RH theorem with arbitrary multiplicity.
