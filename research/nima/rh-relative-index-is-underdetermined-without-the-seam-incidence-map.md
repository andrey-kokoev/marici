# Relative index is underdetermined without the seam incidence map

## The three carriers do not determine the quotient

Retaining a seam carrier repairs the regularization ambiguity only when its
incidence into the two sector deficiency spaces is source-derived.

At the charge-lattice level, let

\[
K_+=\mathbb Z,
\qquad
K_-=\mathbb Z,
\qquad
K_{\mathrm{seam}}=\mathbb Z.
\]

A seam incidence is a homomorphism

\[
j:K_{\mathrm{seam}}\longrightarrow K_+\oplus K_-.
\]

The residual relative class lies in

\[
\operatorname{coker}j.
\]

The objects alone do not specify `j`.

## Exact charge hostile

Let the reciprocal sector charge be

\[
q=(-1,1).
\]

Consider two primitive incidences:

\[
j_{\mathrm{same}}(1)=(1,1),
\qquad
j_{\mathrm{opp}}(1)=(-1,1).
\]

Under `j_same`, the charge `q` survives in the cokernel because it is not an
integer multiple of `(1,1)`. Under `j_opp`, the same charge is exactly a seam
boundary and vanishes in the cokernel.

Both quotients are abstractly isomorphic to the integers. Their abstract
groups therefore do not reveal which scientific charge survives. The labelled
incidence map carries the operative information.

## Why sign convention is not cosmetic

The two choices correspond to different physical statements:

- the seam state enters both sector deficiency spaces with the same
  orientation;
- the seam state enters them with opposite orientations.

Reciprocal reflection, boundary orientation, and Hardy polarization can each
change the apparent sign. Transporting an incidence convention from another
presentation does not authorize it. The source must derive the ordered pair of
maps.

## Fitted-incidence no-go

For any primitive charge vector `q`, one can choose `j(1)=q` and make that
charge vanish in the relative quotient. Therefore a relative-index proof is
unfalsifiable if the seam incidence is selected after computing the proposed
off-seam obstruction.

This is the higher-categorical analogue of fitting a coherence cell to kill a
known residual.

## Required source packet

The actual fifth-tower object must contain

\[
j_+:K_{\mathrm{seam}}\longrightarrow K_+,
\qquad
j_-:K_{\mathrm{seam}}\longrightarrow K_-,
\]

together with:

- ordered-port typing;
- boundary-orientation conventions;
- reciprocal covariance;
- support and multiplicity of each seam state;
- compatibility with primitive, square, and archimedean currents;
- completion continuity.

Only the combined map `(j_+,j_-)` defines the relative quotient.

## DPC

Before calculating any relative index:

1. construct the seam carrier from the source;
2. derive both incidence maps separately;
3. freeze their signs and ordered ports;
4. verify reciprocal covariance without identifying the ports;
5. calculate the cokernel with labelled generators;
6. test the reciprocal Blaschke charge and both regularizations;
7. reject any incidence introduced after the residual is known.

The finite falsifier is immediate: if changing only an untyped incidence sign
changes whether `(-1,1)` vanishes, the theory has not yet defined its
obstruction.

## Verdict

Relative index remains promising, but the seam carrier by itself is
insufficient. The decisive fifth-tower constructor is the source-derived,
ordered two-port incidence from seam states into the sector deficiency spaces.
Without it, any desired obstruction can be killed by a fitted boundary map.
