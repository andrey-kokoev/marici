# Even entire completion cannot be a positive-real impedance

## Theorem

Let (F) be an entire scalar function satisfying the centered completion law

\[
F(-z)=F(z).
\]

If

\[
\operatorname{Re}F(z)>0
\]

throughout the open right half-plane, then (F) is constant.

The same conclusion holds with non-strict nonnegative real part, provided the
inequality holds throughout the open half-plane.

## Proof

Evenness transports the right-half-plane inequality to the left half-plane.
Continuity supplies nonnegative real part on the imaginary seam. Hence

\[
\operatorname{Re}F(z)\ge 0
\]

on the entire complex plane.

The entire function

\[
e^{-F(z)}
\]

is therefore bounded by one. Liouville's theorem makes it constant, and its
nonvanishing derivative factor then makes (F) constant.

## Application to the completed xi section

With centered coordinate

\[
z=s-\frac12,
\]

the functional equation becomes

\[
\Xi\!\left(\frac12+z\right)
=
\Xi\!\left(\frac12-z\right).
\]

The centered completed section is entire, even, and nonconstant. It therefore
cannot itself be a positive-real driving-point impedance on either open
half-plane.

Multiplication by a fixed nonzero phase does not repair this: the resulting
function remains entire and even, so positive real part on one sector again
forces constancy.

## Operator-valued extension

Suppose an entire operator-valued function (M(z)) is even and satisfies

\[
\operatorname{Re}\langle v,M(z)v\rangle\ge0
\]

in the right half-plane for every vector (v). Each scalar quadratic readout
is entire, even, and positive-real, hence constant. Polarization then makes

\[
M(z)
\]

constant as an operator-valued function.

Thus the obstruction is not caused by taking a scalar trace. A nontrivial
positive-real object must live before even entire sewing, or must cease to be
entire by retaining boundary, pole, or relation data.

## Architectural consequence

There are now three distinct objects:

1. a sector-local impedance or Weyl function, which may be positive-real;
2. a Cayley-transformed scattering or overlap channel, which may have dark
   transmission zeros;
3. the even entire completed section, which cannot be a nonconstant
   positive-real impedance at all.

The familiar logarithmic derivative escapes the theorem because it is
meromorphic and odd rather than entire and even. But its positive-real or
Herglotz property is already the RH-equivalent condition, so it supplies no
independent orientation law.

Therefore any viable source-level proof must locate positivity in a genuinely
sector-local operator before completion and derive a lossless, zero-preserving
passage to the entire section. Positivity cannot be assigned directly to the
completed scalar without contradicting its source-fixed analytic type.

## Finite rejection gate

For any proposed direct impedance realization of the centered completed
section, check that (F) is entire together with the two mathematical
conditions

\[
F(-z)=F(z),
\qquad
\operatorname{Re}F(z)>0\quad(\operatorname{Re}z>0).
\]

If all are asserted and (F) is nonconstant, the proposal is inconsistent
before any zero calculation.
