# Four charts are one periodic object in the canonical homeomorphism groupoid

## Canonical model

Let the even charts be the channel-lattice Hilbert carrier

\[
\ell^2(L_n)
\]

and the odd charts its Pontryagin-dual carrier

\[
L^2(\mathbb T_n).
\]

Use Pontryagin Fourier and inverse/reflection transport around the four phases. On basis atoms the orbit is

\[
\delta_v\to\chi_{-v}\to\delta_{-v}\to\chi_v\to\delta_v.
\]

Every transition is unitary. Consequently the four chart spaces are objects in one connected homeomorphism groupoid, and the composite monodromy satisfies

\[
q^4=I
\]

before graded lift.

The four objects can therefore be replaced by one base carrier plus its `C4` presentation action. Convolution and pointwise multiplication are transported monoidal structures, not independent chartwise choices. Polygon rotation commutes with the chart transport.

## Stable graded lift

When the fourth wrap carries suspension,

\[
q^4=\Sigma,
\]

the correct object is a periodic stable groupoid rather than an ordinary `C4` action. Suspension is an autoequivalence of the graded carrier. Thus all chart arrows remain equivalences, but the monodromy remembers one grading shift. The phase coordinate is reducible to presentation plus monodromy; it cannot be erased without also erasing the stable grading.

## Historical boundary

The canonical statement does not prove that all four historical semilocal chart carriers are homeomorphic in their independently inherited topologies. The fourth-chart inverse and the direct oriented-log-radial conjugacy remain unavailable. Giving an essential image the transported source topology produces a presentation equivalence by definition, but does not identify it with the native analytic topology.

Hence:

- canonical Tate/Pontryagin charts: one periodic object, proved;
- stable graded charts: one object with suspension monodromy;
- historical native semilocal charts: conditional, not yet quotiented.
