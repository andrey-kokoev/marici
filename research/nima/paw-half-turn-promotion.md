# Paw half-turn square and promotion to a fresh identity record

## Question

Can a paw's distinguished leg make a retained half-turn through pi, square that operation, and thereby become a new identity for the next cycle?

The test separates three claims:

1. **Endpoint return:** two same-direction half-turns return a vector.
2. **Retained promotion:** that completed route can receive a fresh identity label without discarding its history.
3. **Dimensional growth:** the route creates a new spatial direction or persistent volume.

The first two are realized in this finite experiment. The third does not follow from rotation or fresh labelling. The triangle does generate an oriented bivector, which is a different algebraic grade, not a third spatial vector.

No quadrature amplitude, proton mass formula, or conservative feedback bank is used here. This returns to the paw and the stated pi-turn operation.

## The paw fixture and its oriented plane

For exact arithmetic take

    A=0, B=e2, C=-e1+e2, D=e1.

The graph is triangle ABC plus leg AD. All vertices are distinct. This is a simple planar fixture, not the earlier all-unit-edge regular-tetrahedron animation.

The oriented triangle plane is generated by its actual two edge vectors:

    (B-A) wedge (C-A)
      = [e2(-e1+e2)-(-e1+e2)e2]/2
      = J.

The normalized area direction J squares to -1. It supplies an intrinsic complex-plane algebra span{1,J}. Acting with J on a triangle-plane vector rotates that vector within the original plane; it does not supply a spatial normal.

There is an important ambiguity in the word "square":

- the plane generator has J²=-1;
- a vector-plane half-turn has (-I)²=I;
- the rotor implementing that physical half-turn has (-J)²=-1.

Those are different objects and must not be substituted for one another.

## Physical angle versus rotor angle

For a physical vector rotation through alpha in the e1/e2 plane, use

    r(alpha)=cos(alpha/2)-J sin(alpha/2),
    d(alpha)=r(alpha) d reverse(r(alpha)).

Thus a positive physical pi-turn has rotor -J and reverses the leg. A negative pi-turn has rotor +J and the same reversed vector endpoint. Their retained paths differ.

| Retained operations | Signed angle | Vector endpoint | Rotor lift |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Empty history | 0 | original | +1 |
| One positive half-turn | pi | reversed | -J |
| Two positive half-turns | 2pi | original | -1 |
| Positive then negative half-turn | 0 | original | +1 |
| Four positive half-turns | 4pi | original | +1 |

In particular a full vector turn is NOT the identity of the signed rotor lift. Calling its endpoint identity does not authorize deleting the minus sign. This is the spin-cover bookkeeping of the chosen algebra, not a derivation of particle spin.

The two same-direction turns have net winding one. The forward/reverse route has winding zero but positive path variation 2pi. The empty route has zero variation. Endpoint, lift, winding and full history are successively different readings.

## Promotion: the part that works

Each half-turn event stores a fresh occurrence ID, direction, and source/target leg orientation. A promoted closed route stores:

- a fresh packet label;
- source and returned target;
- ordered immediate parent IDs;
- every original event occurrence, in order;
- signed angle, path variation and signed rotor lift as derived readings.

Two separately executed full turns get different packet labels even though their vector endpoints and lifts agree. A route followed by a reversal remains different from the original empty identity. Recovering the parent records reconstructs the exact event sequence.

The completed endpoint-identity packet can consequently be used as a new addressed reference record. That is a precise sense in which the cycle supplies a "new identity": new retained provenance over an old endpoint identity. It does not create a second algebraic unit, change the Clifford multiplication or add a physical coordinate.

The checker rejects mismatched endpoints, replay by duplicating the same event occurrence IDs, and promotion beyond depth three. Repeating an operation is allowed through fresh event occurrences; no ancestry is silently discarded. Three binary promotion levels retain eight half-turn events.

## Does rotating into the imaginary direction create an outward dimension?

The leg d=e1 has d²=+1, while the generated plane element J has J²=-1. A putative Euclidean coefficient rotation

    d(theta)=cos(theta)e1+sin(theta)J

therefore satisfies

    d(theta)²=cos(2theta)*1.

At theta=pi/4 it is nonzero but square-zero: (e1+J)²=0. An algebra automorphism preserving the unit cannot map an element whose square is +1 to one whose square is -1 or zero. Such a mixed-grade rotation could be separately declared in coefficient space, but it is not the existing geometric rotor operation. In Cl(2,0), mixing these directions by a square-preserving boost instead gives a hyperbolic, not circular, parameterization.

So the triangle generates an imaginary-like BIVECTOR direction intrinsically. Identifying it with an outward spatial VECTOR would require extra structure. In three dimensions a Hodge dual converts the plane bivector to a normal using the ambient metric and orientation, but that already assumes the third spatial dimension.

## Control with a supplied spatial normal

To test the literal outward picture, explicitly supply n=e3 and rotate only the leg, retaining triangle ABC as its reference:

    d(theta)=cos(theta)e1+sin(theta)e3.

The exact quarter-turn samples are

    e1 -> e3 -> -e1 -> -e3 -> e1.

The leg stays unit length and misses every body vertex. The associated convex hull of A,B,C,D has volume |sin(theta)|/6. It has positive volume halfway through each half-turn, but zero volume at theta=pi and theta=2pi. Thus the outward excursion alone does not leave a persistent tetrahedral volume. The graph still has only the original paw edges; the convex hull is a supplied geometric readout, not a derived simplicial filling.

This control illustrates the retained excursion while making its extra ambient direction explicit. It is not evidence that rotation created that direction.
