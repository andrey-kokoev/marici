# Selector and Rigidifier Are Independent Operations

Let \(p:\mathcal L\to B\) be the family of admissible sector lenses.

A **selector** is source data that determines a section of \(p\), uniquely up
to the declared gauge.  It reduces the admissible lens fiber.

A **rigidifier** instead makes a readout constant, or nearly constant, across
a declared family of presentations.  It reduces readout variation.  It need
not select a point of \(\mathcal L\).

These properties are logically independent.  A finite four-case census
realizes neither, selector only, rigidifier only, and both.  Consequently,
small output variance cannot be used as evidence that a source selected the
underlying lens.

The current sector evidence separates accordingly:

- The positive quadratic Gaussian Hamiltonian is a selector: for fixed
  \(h\), it determines \(V_h\) in an otherwise continuous admissible family.
- The flavor texture atlas is presently only a rigidifier: it reduces the
  spread of \(F_t\) by roughly fortyfold while failing to locate its value
  relative to the complete fitted region.
- Magnetic Hall transport and the RH repair operator remain under owner
  audit; neither should be called a selector merely because it stabilizes or
  repairs a readout.

The operational gate for any future selection claim is therefore:

1. exhibit the admissible lens fiber before the source datum;
2. derive the source map into that fiber;
3. prove uniqueness modulo declared gauge;
4. only then compute readout rigidity.

Verification: dependency-free finite census, four cases out of four.
