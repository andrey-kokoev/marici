# Higher-coherence topology iteration 38: the adelic product formula gives global neutrality, not placewise unitarity

## Candidate topology

Place the reciprocal state in a restricted product of local metric lines

\[
\mathcal K_{\mathbb A}
=
\bigotimes_v'\mathcal K_v
\]

with finite-prime and archimedean norms. Use the adelic product formula to
compare the global reciprocal line with local valuation transports.

## What the product formula states

For a genuine global idele `x`,

\[
\prod_v|x|_v=1,
\qquad
\sum_v\log|x|_v=0.
\]

This allows nontrivial local moduli whose logarithms compensate. It does not
imply

\[
|x|_v=1
\]

at every place.

Therefore global neutral norm does not localize to primewise neutral lines
without an additional sign or independence theorem.

## Mismatch with the spectral multiplier

The local spectral factors in the Haar residual are

\[
p^{-\operatorname{Re}z}
\]

for every prime. They are not the local norms of one rational number or an
ordinary restricted idele with only finitely many nonunit components. For
`Re(z) != 0`, the raw product over all primes is not an ordinary restricted-
product norm and needs zeta regularization.

Using that regularization to assert a product formula would import the same
global analytic continuation whose zero geometry is under study.

## Same-sign finite-place observation

For fixed `a=Re(z)`, every finite-place scalar

\[
1-p^{-2a}
\]

has the same sign. Thus a genuinely positive sum

\[
\sum_p w_p(1-p^{-2a})E_p
\]

with `w_p>0`, `E_p>0`, and nonzero finite value could vanish only at `a=0`.
This is promising, but it is not the ordinary product formula. It requires:

1. a convergent positive weighting;
2. no signed archimedean compensation;
3. identification of the global Xi cycle law with this positive sum;
4. noncollapse of at least one local energy.

Condition 3 is the missing arithmetic energy balance.

## Restricted-product completion

A restricted product can retain labelled local probes and source cutoffs. But
if the global readout is only the scalar product of local norms, local factors
remain nonidentifiable. Jointly faithful coordinate projections or independent
local variations are still required.

Archimedean completion is especially important: its logarithmic norm can
compensate all finite-place contributions while preserving the global product
formula.

## Higher-coherence interpretation

An adelic cone tower may close globally because its placewise curvatures sum to
zero. Such closure is a conservation law for the total object, not flatness of
every local connection. Promoting it to placewise flatness requires positivity
or orthogonality of the local curvature components.

## Verdict for topology 38

Adelic restricted-product topology correctly packages global reciprocal
neutrality but does not imply local prime unitarity. The same-sign finite-prime
factors suggest a possible positive aggregate route, yet identifying the Xi
cycle with that aggregate is precisely the unresolved Green balance.

The next nonredundant topology to test is an orthogonal direct-integral or
Hilbert-module topology over the prime spectrum, where local residuals occupy
orthogonal central fibers and global zero may force fiberwise zero without
using the scalar product formula.
