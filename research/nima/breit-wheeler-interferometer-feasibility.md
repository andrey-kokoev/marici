# Feasibility gate for cross-angle Breit-Wheeler interferometry

## Energy typing

The QED Bell onset occurs at

\[
\sqrt{s}=0.6481956562\,m_e,
\]

whereas real Breit-Wheeler production requires

\[
\sqrt{s}\geq 2m_e.
\]

Thus pair production cannot probe the onset at the same center-of-mass energy.
It can contribute only as the absorptive input to a dispersion reconstruction
of the subthreshold elastic amplitude.

## Hardware audit

Coherent free-electron optics is not fictional. Electron biprisms and grating
beam splitters preserve coherence, and laser/crystal schemes have been studied
at energies extending to roughly 300 keV. This establishes plausibility for
single-electron mode recombination.

The required Bell/EFT readout is substantially stronger:

1. select two distinct momentum modes from the same Breit-Wheeler event;
2. preserve the joint \(e^+e^-\) amplitude and relevant spin channel;
3. coherently recombine the modes for both charged particles;
4. scan a calibrated relative phase; and
5. repeat this across the angular support needed by the nonforward cut.

The literature audit found polarized Breit-Wheeler angular measurements and
single-electron interferometry, but no demonstrated joint \(e^+e^-\)
cross-angle interferometer of this type. Positronium interferometry is itself
described as challenging and does not implement the required continuum-pair
kernel.

## Verdict

The four-phase protocol is a mathematically sufficient specification of the
missing readout, but not a near-term experimental alternative. The practical
route remains hybrid:

\[
\text{source-theoretic phase connection}
+
\text{polarized differential Breit-Wheeler constraints}
\longrightarrow
\text{nonforward dispersive amplitude}.
\]

This is model-assisted amplitude reconstruction, not a direct Bell test and
not a model-independent experimental extraction.
