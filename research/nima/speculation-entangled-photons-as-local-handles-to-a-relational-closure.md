# Entangled Photons as Local Handles to a Relational Closure

## Status

This is a non-evidential architectural speculation. It proposes a Marici vocabulary for an established quantum-mechanical structure. It does not derive quantum mechanics, choose an interpretation of measurement, introduce hidden variables, or permit superluminal signalling.

## Operator intuition

Two photons produced by one entangling preparation and transported to distant locations can be pictured as carrying local handles to one source-produced relational closure. The physically persistent object is not a pair of independently stored spin values. It is the joint state and its law for correlated records under compatible measurement contexts.

For a singlet-like two-port state,

\[
|\Psi^-\rangle
=
\frac{|\uparrow\rangle_A|\downarrow\rangle_B-|\downarrow\rangle_A|\uparrow\rangle_B}{\sqrt 2}.
\]

The reduced state at either port does not contain the full joint relation. Each photon is therefore better compared to a restricted local capability than to a copy of shared data.

## Proposed typing

The preparation constructs a relational state (C) together with two local ports:

\[
h_A:C\rightsquigarrow A,
\qquad
h_B:C\rightsquigarrow B.
\]

A measurement requires both a port and a context (a):

\[
\mu_a:(h_A,C)\rightharpoonup(r_A,C_{a,r_A}).
\]

Here (r_A) is a local physical record and (C_{a,r_A}) is the conditional continuation state. The operation does not reveal a context-independent value already stored in the handle. It produces a record through an admitted measurement interaction and changes the joint continuation possibilities.

For compatible measurements on the singlet state, observing (r_A=+1) along axis (a) fixes the conditional prediction (r_B=-1) along the same axis. It does not assign definite values to all incompatible axes.

## Why “handle” is useful

The local port resembles an affine or linear capability:

- it provides restricted access to a shared relational object;
- it cannot reveal the complete joint state locally;
- it cannot be cloned arbitrarily;
- invoking it through measurement changes the available continuations;
- its relational content is operationally checked only through later comparison of records.

Distance alone does not factor the joint state. Uncontrolled environmental coupling can instead destroy usable coherence. Classical comparison remains necessary to expose the correlations operationally, so the picture supplies no faster-than-light communication channel.

## Carrier analogy

The analogy suggests that Carrier data need not reside as a complete value at either endpoint. A Carrier may instead preserve a source-authorized continuation law across several ports. Local records are projections of that law under typed interactions.

In this reading:

- preparation constructs the closure;
- propagation transports its local handles;
- measurement is a contextual constructor;
- a record is local and definite;
- correlation belongs to the joint closure;
- decoherence is loss of the continuation structure needed to recover the joint relation.

## Immediate falsifiers and limits

The analogy fails if it is used to claim any of the following:

- pre-existing context-independent spin values at both ports;
- a locally readable pointer to the remote record;
- a controllable instantaneous signal;
- duplication of the complete closure into each photon;
- a unique physical collapse mechanism derived from the software metaphor.

Its useful content is narrower: some physically meaningful information is irreducibly relational, locally accessed through restricted ports, and made definite only as typed records under interaction.

## Bell refinement: static local values versus relational closure

The operator intuition is that Bell inequalities distinguish a hidden parameter treated as a static store of local answers from a source-produced closure that governs contextual relations between ports.

The weakest defensible version requires care. Bell's theorem does not exclude every hidden-variable description merely because its variables are static. Under the usual assumptions, it excludes locally causal models whose joint probabilities factor through a shared variable \(\lambda\):

\[
p(A,B\mid a,b,\lambda)
=
p(A\mid a,\lambda)p(B\mid b,\lambda).
\]

Such a model may preassign local answers or local response probabilities for the possible settings. Bell-violating quantum correlations cannot be reproduced while retaining that factorization together with the other assumptions used in the theorem.

A relational closure differs only if it refuses this local decomposition. It types the source state as one joint object whose permitted state--effect pairings depend on both measurement contexts:

\[
p(A,B\mid a,b,C),
\]

without requiring that this joint law factor through two independently evaluable local response functions. The closure is therefore not a richer classical lookup table distributed between the photons. Renaming \(\lambda\) as a closure while retaining Bell-local factorization changes nothing and is immediately falsified by the same inequality.

This makes Bell's result a typing obstruction:

- the source relation cannot descend to two complete local value carriers;
- each local port remains insufficient for reconstructing the joint continuation law;
- the joint law is contextual but still no-signalling at the level of local marginals;
- any successful closure model must reproduce the quantitative quantum correlations, not merely redescribe them.

The important contrast is therefore not simply static versus dynamic. It is locally factorized stored answers versus an irreducibly joint, context-indexed relation.
