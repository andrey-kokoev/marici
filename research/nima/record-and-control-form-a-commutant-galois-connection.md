# Record and control form a commutant Galois connection

## Result

The cross-axis interface has a canonical positive structure when records and
operations live in an operator algebra. For a record algebra \(R\), define its
compatible control algebra by the commutant \(R'\). For a control algebra
\(M\), define its nondisturbing record algebra by \(M'\).

These assignments reverse inclusion and satisfy

\[
R\subseteq M'
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
M\subseteq R'.
\]

Thus record and control form an order-reversing Galois connection. The closure
operations are the double commutants \(R''\) and \(M''\).

This supplies the missing bidirectional law:

- demanding sharper records shrinks the compatible future-control algebra;
- demanding richer future control shrinks the nondisturbing record algebra.

## Exact two-dimensional model

Inside the full real \(2\times2\) matrix algebra, consider the chain

\[
\mathbb R I
\subset
\operatorname{span}(I,Z)
\subset
M_2(\mathbb R).
\]

Their commutants have dimensions four, two, and one respectively. Hence the
inclusion chain reverses exactly:

\[
M_2(\mathbb R)
\supset
\operatorname{span}(I,Z)
\supset
\mathbb R I.
\]

Full endpoint control therefore permits only scalar sharp records without
disturbance. A diagonal sharp record retains only diagonal future control. A
scalar record preserves the full control algebra.

## Relation to the five-gate compiler

The commutant law does not authorize a record or operation. Candidate algebras
must still pass source authority, completion, resource, fault, and modality
checks. It supplies the interface coherencer once both sides are admitted.

The source-rooted proof DAG records which primitive constructors generated
\(R\) and \(M\). The commutant comparison then determines whether their joint
use is compatible without inventing an additional fitted cell.

## Finite falsifier

If a claimed record projector lies outside \(M'\), one executable operation has
a nonzero commutator with it. That commutator is the finite witness that the
record destroys part of the claimed future capability.
