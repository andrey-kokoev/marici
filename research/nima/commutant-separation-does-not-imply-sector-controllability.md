# Commutant separation does not imply sector controllability

## Candidate criterion and falsifier

Consider two equivalent copies of an irreducible carrier \(W\).  Diagonal
noncentral controls act as

\[
K\longmapsto(K,K).
\]

Before a sheet label is supplied, the commutant contains the exchanger

\[
S(w_1,w_2)=(w_2,w_1).
\]

It is tempting to declare the sectors unlocked exactly when this exchanger
leaves the commutant.  That criterion is false.

Adjoin the central sheet label

\[
J=(I,-I).
\]

The exchanger anticommutes with \(J\), so it no longer belongs to the
commutant.  The two sectors are now representation-theoretically
distinguishable and the projectors

\[
E_\pm=\frac{I\pm J}{2}
\]

are defined.

Nevertheless, \(J\) commutes with every diagonal noncentral control.  The Lie
closure remains

\[
\Delta\mathfrak g\oplus\mathbf R J,
\]

not \(\mathfrak g\oplus\mathfrak g\).  No bracket forms \((K,0)\) or
\((0,K)\).

Thus removal of off-diagonal intertwiners is necessary for independent block
control but not sufficient.

## Two distinct splittings

The example separates two operations.

### Carrier splitting

A central label supplies orthogonal or algebraic idempotents and distinguishes
the two summands.  This is a decomposition of the object.

### Action splitting

A noncentral asymmetric tag supplies unequal coefficients inside the
noncentral action algebra.  Together with diagonal controls it generates
sector-selective actions.  This is a decomposition of the executable action.

Carrier splitting can occur without action splitting.  In categorical terms,
splitting an idempotent on an object does not imply that the admitted action
functor is full on the resulting summands.

## Exact criterion in the aligned simple-block model

Assume the diagonal copy of a simple Lie algebra \(\mathfrak g\) is already
available and the additional aligned noncentral controls have coefficient
vectors

\[
(\lambda_1,\lambda_2)\in\mathbf R^2.
\]

Independent sector control is obtained when the coefficient vectors generated
by the noncentral controls and their brackets span \(\mathbf R^2\).  The
diagonal vector \((1,1)\) supplies one direction.  Any noncentral tag with
unequal weights supplies the second.

Central coefficient vectors do not count toward this rank because their
brackets with \(\mathfrak g\) vanish.

The minimal audit therefore reports two invariants:

1. the commutant or idempotent decomposition of the carrier;
2. the coefficient rank of the noncentral action module.

## Sector implications

For reciprocal theta observers, a central sheet sign may distinguish the two
charts and remove their exchanger while leaving all Weyl actions diagonally
locked.  It cannot provide the missing orientation.

For flavor observers, two calibrated labels may split the record carrier while
the available interventions still act through one common response direction.

For endpoint control, central block characters may construct abstract
projectors while the dynamical Lie algebra remains correlated across repeated
simple blocks.

## Falsifiers

Reject a sector-unlocking claim when any of the following occurs:

1. a trivial commutant is used as the sole controllability certificate;
2. central idempotents are treated as switchable noncentral controls;
3. associative multiplication by a projector is treated as an available Lie
   operation;
4. noncentral coefficient rank is less than the number of repeated sectors;
5. algebraic action splitting is promoted to a physical implementation without
   source authority.
