# Higher-coherence topology iteration 10: a separated Rees topology could kill a residual only if each cone raises its filtration order

## Candidate topology

Let `C` carry a decreasing source filtration

\[
C=F^0C\supset F^1C\supset F^2C\supset\cdots
\]

and form the Rees object

\[
\operatorname{Rees}_F(C)
=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}F^nC\,t^n.
\]

The generic fiber `t=1` recovers the physical coherence system, while the
special fiber records the associated graded normal system. Complete `C` in the
`F`-adic topology and require it to be separated:

\[
C\simeq\varprojlim C/F^nC,
\qquad
\bigcap_{n\ge0}F^nC=0.
\]

## Why one special-fiber cancellation is insufficient

Writing a residual as `t r` makes its specialization at `t=0` vanish for every
`r`. This does not imply that the physical generic-fiber residual vanishes.
Even flatness or absence of `t`-torsion does not reverse specialization.

Thus a five-dimensional cone whose boundary cancels only on the normal fiber
can hide, rather than solve, the Haar mismatch.

## Infinite order-raising mechanism

There is nevertheless one genuine way an infinite cone tower could force
vanishing. Suppose the `n`th sourced coherencer proves

\[
r_p(z)\in F^nC
\]

for every `n`, with all witnesses compatible under the Rees bonding maps. Then
separatedness gives

\[
r_p(z)\in\bigcap_nF^nC=0.
\]

This is stronger than merely making the residual exact at every rung. Every
new cone must raise the filtration order of the *same physical residual*.

This is the first tested topology in which an actual infinite higher-coherence
tower could imply pointwise vanishing without quotienting away the positive
state.

## Application to the Haar residual

The scalar observation is

\[
\ell_p(r_p(z))
=
\bigl(1-p^{-2\operatorname{Re}z}\bigr)E_p(b_z).
\]

For the order-raising route to work, the Haar observer must be filtration
continuous, for example

\[
|\ell_p(x)|\le c_n\,\|x\|_{F^n},
\qquad c_n\to0,
\]

or must annihilate the separated intersection. Then infinite filtration depth
would force its scalar value to zero.

No current source filtration has this property for the fixed-prime coordinate.
Prime grade, seam-jet order, and cone degree leave the already present
prime-`p` scalar unchanged rather than moving it into successively deeper
ideals.

## Noncircular acceptance test

A viable Rees unlock must provide, before Xi specialization:

1. a source-defined ideal or filtration `F`, not one generated by the Haar
   residual;
2. a Rees-flat higher cone map `h_n` for every rung;
3. the order-raising law `r_p in F^nC`;
4. compatibility of all `h_n` under restriction;
5. `F`-adic completeness and separatedness;
6. continuity of the positive Haar observer;
7. noncollapse of `b_z` in `C/F^nC` for some fixed finite observation degree.

Failure of item 7 would merely bury the state together with the residual.

## Verdict for topology 10

Rees topology supplies a plausible mathematical realization of the proposed
endless simplex--prism--cone pattern:

\[
\text{higher coherence}=
\text{successive filtration-order improvement}.
\]

Unlike previous topologies, an infinite compatible tower can force a residual
to vanish by separatedness. But the required order-raising maps are new sourced
data, and the present prime/seam/cone filtrations do not raise the Haar
residual's order.

The next nonredundant topology to test is an ultrametric/non-Archimedean
completion, where increasing coherence depth may contract residual norms
geometrically and realize this Rees mechanism quantitatively.
