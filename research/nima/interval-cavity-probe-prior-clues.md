# Prior-research clues for observing the interval process dictionary

## Search result

The prior research supplies a more specific next mathematical test than another general calibration search: compose the existing single-photon/ready-detector interface with the six interval processes. The observation problem can be studied separately from physically executing inverse/replay edits.

This is a source review and a conditional analytic deduction, not an executed new checker, acquired calibration, net admission or apparatus claim.

## Directly reusable same-model source

`physics-geometric-candidate-v1.md` supplies:

- the same ten-mode, three-round retained cavity;
- a passive one-photon lift;
- a local zero/one-photon detector with ready/dead memory;
- explicit routing and complete click/no-click records;
- nominal efficiency eta=9/25.

`physics-observation-resource-interface-v1.md` supplies the outcome laws and explicitly blocks physical execution without apparatus/calibration records. `physics-rival-prediction-table-v1.md` warns against resetting detector readiness silently or dropping no-click trials.

These do not certify a laboratory, but they do provide a mathematical instrument with the correct carrier domain. Unlike a coherent-state probe, a one-photon probe lies exactly within the detector's n_port<=1 domain.

## Proposed conditional probe family

For each k=0,1,2, in a separate freshly prepared trial:

1. prepare one photon in incident mode u_k=1+3k, every other mode vacuum;
2. apply the unknown member E(i,j) of the declared six-process family;
3. detect the corresponding retained slot u_k with an initially ready detector, retaining click and no-click records.

The process dictionary already derives E(i,j)[u_k,u_k]=-3/5 on selected rounds and 1 otherwise. Thus the probability of a photon at that output is 9/25 or 1. Under the supplied detector law,

    P(click | k,i,j) = eta * 9/25  if i-1<=k<=j-1,
                      eta          otherwise.

At eta=9/25 these are 81/625 and 9/25, a nominal gap of 144/625. The three probabilities distinguish the six consecutive nonempty masks. This is joint faithfulness of distributions on a restricted process family, not deterministic identification from one shot and not tomography of arbitrary optical processes. A single finite observed click word need not identify an interval.

Importantly, a wrong or phase-corrupted process may share these intensities. They are sufficient for interval identification only under the independently justified canonical-family promise; they cannot replace the full-matrix validation in the process decoder or certify inverse controls.

The choice of basis preparations and measured slots is a proposed new binding of existing instrument operations. Its physical preparation/routing authority has not been established. Do not silently replace the previous coherent superposition preparation with these probes as if they had already been calibrated.

## Other prior clues and their limits

### Joint probes, not a universal input state

`research/aspect/minimal-jointly-monic-optical-probes.md` proves that a family of allowed probes can distinguish a finite quotient even when individual probes do not. Its deletion-minimality method transfers: test all subsets of the three proposed port probes and exhibit colliding interval pairs. Its twelve-state polarization quotient and its four-probe minimum do not transfer numerically.

### Calibration can remove an efficiency ambiguity

`research/aspect/minimal-calibration-for-lossy-pauli-tomography.md` identifies a full-support independently known reference and shared static efficiencies as the relevant rank condition. Here a separately justified identity/bypass exposure of the same known one-photon input could supply a same-channel reference click rate eta. The sample-level calibration uncertainty and stability of preparation, routing and readiness still need specification; a ratio of observed rates is not automatically an exact calibration.

That paper's nine-setting Pauli apparatus is not this cavity. Transfer the identifiability argument, not its experimental authority. `calibration-is-a-source-extension-that-can-restore-observability.md` explicitly warns that such a reference adds a controlled source; it cannot be inferred from the unknown record itself.

### Readout is not coherent recovery

`research/aspect/coherent-port-versus-classical-log.md` distinguishes repeated-preparation tomography from preservation of an individual system. Detecting these outputs can identify the interval distributionally while destroying the state needed for inverse replay. Use separate runs for readout and coherent-control validation; do not treat the detector log as the retained coherent port.

## Proposed next bounded test

Construct the exact three-probe response table using the existing passive lift and detector outcome maps, rather than just squaring a matrix entry. Check:

- normalization and all six interval signatures;
- complete source-record recovery from the canonical-family response vectors;
- deletion collisions for probe subsets;
- wrong readiness, missing no-clicks, unknown efficiency and phase-alias hostiles;
- an explicit reference-calibration model and a separation bound under stated uncertainty.

Stop at a conditional jointly faithful observation family. This can advance the mathematical observer interface without first supplying inverse hardware. Physical calibration, finite-sample design, causal-map selection and inverse/replay controls remain separate gates.
