# The archimedean completion is a nowhere-zero divisor unit on the open critical strip

## Completed section

Use the standard completed function

\[
\Xi(s)
=
\frac12s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma\left(\frac s2\right)\zeta(s).
\]

Define the archimedean factor

\[
A_\infty(s)
=
\frac12s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma\left(\frac s2\right).
\]

## Open-strip divisor

On

\[
0<\operatorname{Re}s<1,
\]

one has \(s\ne0\) and \(s\ne1\).  The exponential factor
\(\pi^{-s/2}\) is entire and nowhere zero, while the gamma function is
holomorphic and nowhere zero because its poles occur only at nonpositive even
integers after the substitution \(s/2\).

Therefore

\[
A_\infty(s)\ne0
\]

throughout the open critical strip.  It follows that

\[
\Xi(s)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\zeta(s)=0
\]

there, with equal algebraic multiplicities.

## Determinant-line consequence

Suppose the arithmetic and relative determinant compilers produce a section
\(D(s)\) satisfying

\[
D(s)=E_{\rm ar}(s)\zeta(s)
\]

with \(E_{\rm ar}\) holomorphic and nowhere zero on the open strip.  Then

\[
A_\infty(s)D(s)
=E_{\rm ar}(s)\Xi(s).
\]

Thus the archimedean factor does not create, remove, or change the multiplicity
of any nontrivial zero.  It is a divisor unit on the domain relevant to RH.

Equivalently, it may be incorporated into the comparison unit

\[
E(s)=E_{\rm ar}(s)A_\infty(s)^{-1}
\]

depending on which side of the determinant identity carries the completed
normalization.  Both conventions preserve the zero divisor when frozen
consistently.

## Endpoint qualification

At \(s=0\) and \(s=1\), the polynomial and gamma/zeta singularities cancel in
the completed entire function.  The factor \(A_\infty\) is not by itself a
holomorphic unit on a domain containing those endpoints.  A global entire
factorization must treat the trivial/polar boundary divisor explicitly.

G4 concerns completed Xi zeros and must state whether its comparison domain is
the open critical strip or the entire plane.  The unit argument here closes
only the open-strip divisor comparison.

## Reciprocal covariance

The archimedean factor participates in the functional equation

\[
\Xi(s)=\Xi(1-s).
\]

The bare factors \(A_\infty(s)\) and \(A_\infty(1-s)\) are not individually
equal; their ratio is compensated by the arithmetic functional equation.
Therefore reciprocal determinant charts must include the archimedean
transition before claiming a global scalar equality.

The relative \(\det_2\) transition may be the unit one after reciprocal
pullback, while the bare Euler and archimedean determinant lines carry the
nontrivial functional-equation transition.

## G4 consequence

The archimedean completion is not the remaining nontrivial-zero obstruction.
On the open critical strip it is a source-fixed nowhere-zero multiplier.

The unresolved G4 theorem is arithmetic/operatorial: construct the closed-loop
section from the Euler \(\det_3\), its first two cumulants, and the relative
boundary determinant, then prove its ratio with \(\zeta(s)\) is nowhere zero
and identify determinant kernels with Green-pencil collisions.
