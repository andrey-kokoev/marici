# Consecutive-prime shell widths exclude the pairwise label-dilation collision

## Question

Does the explicit cross-shell label-dilation kernel occur in the actual consecutive-prime shell catalogue used by candidate one?

## Claim boundary

No at the level of pairwise proportional histories. Distinct consecutive-prime shells have distinct logarithmic widths. Since width is one component of the unlabelled translated-kernel coordinate, the exact collision triple is injective on the actual shell catalogue. This does not yet exclude longer linear relations assembled by shell concatenation.

## Actual shell catalogue

Candidate one uses consecutive primes \(p<q\) and the logarithmic shell

\[
[a,b]=[\log p,\log q].
\]

For ordered theta labels \((n,m)\), the translated base-kernel coordinates are

\[
A=\log p+\log n=\log(np),
\]

\[
H=\log q-\log p=\log\frac qp,
\]

\[
D=\log\frac mn.
\]

Thus the source tuple maps to

\[
(p,q;n,m)
\longmapsto
\left(
\log(np),
\log(q/p),
\log(m/n)
\right).
\]

## Width uniqueness

Suppose two consecutive-prime shells \((p,q)\) and \((r,s)\) have equal widths. Then

\[
\frac qp=\frac sr,
\]

so

\[
qr=sp.
\]

All four quantities are prime. Unique factorization forces equality of the prime multisets

\[
\{q,r\}=\{s,p\}.
\]

The crossed identification \(q=p\) and \(r=s\) contradicts strict shell order. Therefore

\[
p=r,
\qquad
q=s.
\]

Distinct consecutive-prime shells cannot have equal logarithmic width.

## Full collision-triple injectivity

If two source tuples have the same \((A,H,D)\), width uniqueness first gives

\[
p=r,
\qquad q=s.
\]

Equality of \(A\) then gives \(np=n'p\), hence \(n=n'\). Equality of \(D\) gives

\[
\frac mn=rac{m'}n,
\]

hence \(m=m'\). Therefore the translated-coordinate map is injective:

\[
(p,q;n,m)
\mapsto(A,H,D).
\]

The two-term proportional kernel constructed for arbitrary equal-width shells does not occur between distinct tuples in the consecutive-prime catalogue.

## Remaining linear-dependence question

Pairwise coordinate injectivity is weaker than linear independence. Base kernels obey interval additivity:

\[
K_{[A,C]}=K_{[A,B]}+K_{[B,C]}.
\]

A longer relation could therefore occur if translated consecutive-prime intervals align into a common subdivision while their ratio shifts also agree. Such a relation would require endpoint equations such as

\[
np=n'r,
\qquad
nq=n's
\]

across several tuples, not merely equality of one collision triple.

The pairwise hostile is eliminated, but an interval-incidence census remains necessary before asserting full common-history faithfulness.
