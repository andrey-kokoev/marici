# Additive Differentiation Is the Source Incidence Between Even and Odd Tate Ports

## Two genuinely different Tate ports

Grothendieck's corrected logarithmic Tate analysis produces two operators:

\[
K_+=U\mathcal F_xU^{-1}
\]

on even additive lifts, and

\[
K_-=U\mathcal F_xU^{-1}
\]

on odd additive lifts. Their integral kernels use cosine and sine,
respectively. The positive Mellin ray does not itself choose between them.

The standard even theta source nevertheless generates an odd port through a
named source operation: additive differentiation.

## Logarithmic form of additive differentiation

Use the unitary half-density pullback

\[
(Uf)(q)=e^{q/2}f(e^q).
\]

For a logarithmic state \(g\),

\[
(U^{-1}g)(x)=x^{-1/2}g(\log x).
\]

Differentiating in \(x\) and pulling back gives

\[
P
=
U\partial_xU^{-1}
=
e^{-q}\left(\partial_q-\frac12\right).
\]

Additive differentiation reverses parity, so \(P\) maps the even Tate port to
the odd Tate port.

## Exact Fourier coherence square

For the Fourier convention used in the Tate packet,

\[
\mathcal F_x\partial_x
=
2\pi i\,M_x\mathcal F_x,
\]

where \(M_x\) is multiplication by the additive coordinate. Under logarithmic
pullback,

\[
UM_xU^{-1}=M_{e^q}.
\]

Therefore

\[
K_-P
=
2\pi i\,M_{e^q}K_+.
\]

This is a source-derived incidence between the even and odd additive Tate
ports. The quarter-phase \(i\) is fixed by the Fourier differentiation law,
not fitted after scalar completion.

## Gaussian witness

For the even Gaussian

\[
f_0(x)=e^{-\pi x^2},
\]

the derivative

\[
\partial_xf_0=-2\pi x e^{-\pi x^2}
\]

is an authentic odd Tate state. Its logarithmic profile is

\[
(P\,Uf_0)(q)
=
-2\pi e^{3q/2}e^{-\pi e^{2q}}.
\]

Since \(\mathcal F_xf_0=f_0\), the coherence square gives its odd Fourier
phase and coordinate multiplier exactly.

## What this repairs

The odd Tate port is not authorized merely because an odd phase would be
useful. It is authorized when generated by \(P\) from an admitted even source
state. This preserves the distinction between:

1. logarithmic endpoint pointing;
2. additive parity;
3. the incidence converting the even source into an odd Tate state.

Ordinary logarithmic Fourier transform remains unrelated to this incidence.

## Remaining prime coupling

The new square fixes the archimedean parity incidence and its phase. It does
not yet identify the corresponding map on the prime seam or prove that the
complete Schur return excludes unit eigenvalues off the critical seam.

The next finite question is whether the primitive and square seam currents
carry a source operation \(P_{\mathrm{fin}}\) satisfying the same typed
Fourier covariance pattern. A claimed comparison that matches only the scalar
phase but not the derivative--coordinate square is insufficient.

## Falsifier

Any proposed even--odd Tate incidence must satisfy

\[
K_-P-2\pi i\,M_{e^q}K_+=0.
\]

A nonzero residual on one admitted source atom disproves the incidence. The
Gaussian and its derivative are the smallest exact test packet.
