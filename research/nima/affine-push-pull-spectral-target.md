## 1. Source support theorem

\[

## 3. Bare spectral algebra obstruction

Use co-moving notation for L,G,N. The four orthogonal atoms and their ranks are

| Projector | Rank |
|---|---:|
| N | 1 |
| L-N | 11 |
| G-N | 2 |
| I-L-G+N | 22 |

Every element of C[L,G] is scalar on each atom. Its idempotents are exactly the 16 sums of subsets of these atoms. In particular the unique projectors of ranks 11,14,25 are respectively

\[
E_{11}=L-N,\qquad E_{14}=L+G-N,\qquad K=I-L+N.
\]

Their intersections have ranks

\[
\operatorname{rank}(E_{11}K)=0,\qquad
\operatorname{rank}(E_{14}K)=3,\qquad
\operatorname{rank}(E_{11}E_{14})=11.
\]

Hence this algebra cannot supply complementary rank-11 and rank-14 sectors inside K. In particular the initially observed rank 11 is the wrong subspace for that purpose.

## 4. Geometric normal supplies a genuine refinement

These are orthogonal rank-one projectors; P,R,S resolve I3. Unlike an arbitrary chosen line in a two-dimensional complement, R has an explicit geometric source. It transports covariantly with the native triangle rotations. It is extra structure relative to the bare affine nerve and the algebra C[L,G].

\[

\operatorname{rank}K=25.

The new rank-11 sector lies inside the old rank-22 atom, not in L-N. This is why it evades the bare-algebra obstruction without contradicting it. The labels D and C are candidate target labels, not a proved identification with the witness-helix observables.
