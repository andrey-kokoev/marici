# A unitary wave trace needs a fixed spectral functional

## Correct target

The centered prime-power current has Laplace typing, while a self-adjoint operator supplies resolvent and Fourier-wave typing. These cannot be identified by notation alone.

Let \(H\) be self-adjoint on a source-derived Hilbert space and let

\[
U(q)=e^{iqH}.
\]

To obtain a wave trace, one also needs a fixed functional \(\tau\) on an operator or functional-calculus domain:

\[
W(q)=\tau(U(q)).
\]

The source authority lies in the pair \((H,\tau)\), not in unitarity alone.

## Real-support theorem

If \(\tau\) is a fixed continuous functional on a suitable smooth functional calculus of \(H\), the spectral theorem produces a distribution \(\mu_\tau\) supported on the real spectrum of \(H\) such that

\[
W(q)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{iq\lambda}\,d\mu_\tau(\lambda)
\]

in the declared distributional sense.

The functional need not be positive for the support statement. Positivity would strengthen \(\mu_\tau\) to a positive measure and make \(W\) positive-definite, but the centered prime current is signed and should not be assumed to have that stronger form.

Real spectral support is the relevant mechanism. A contribution

\[
e^{\alpha q}e^{i\lambda q},
\qquad \alpha\ne0,
\]

has complex frequency and cannot arise from a bounded fixed functional of a unitary group. In the zero dictionary, \(\alpha\) is horizontal displacement from the critical seam.

## Regularization gate

An arbitrary regularized trace does not inherit the theorem. If the subtraction or functional depends on \(q\), one can manufacture any desired function:

\[
W_{\mathrm{reg}}(q)=\tau_q(U(q)).
\]

This no longer comes from one spectral distribution and can contain complex frequencies despite the unitarity of \(U\).

Therefore the regularization must be compiled as a fixed source constructor. It must satisfy:

1. linearity on one declared operator domain;
2. independence from the scale parameter \(q\);
3. compatibility with the group law and adjoint;
4. continuity on a source-defined smooth functional calculus;
5. cutoff compatibility and a fixed anomaly subtraction;
6. exact equality of its wave trace with the completed prime-power fluctuation current;
7. retention of the primitive, square, seam, and archimedean anomaly coordinates.

## Positivity separation

If \(\tau\) is positive, every finite matrix

\[
W(q_j-q_i)
\]

is positive semidefinite. This is an immediate hostile test. Failure does not kill the real-support programme; it proves only that the required functional is signed, graded, or distributional rather than a positive state.

The argument must not infer spectral positivity from a signed regularized trace.

## Finite falsifiers

At each cutoff, derive \(H_X\), \(\tau_X\), and

\[
W_X(q)=\tau_X(e^{iqH_X})
\]

before fitting any zero data. Then test:

- all eigenfrequencies of \(H_X\) are real;
- the same \(\tau_X\) is used for every \(q\);
- the declared subtraction is independent of \(q\);
- \(|W_X(q)|\) obeys the norm bound when \(\tau_X\) is bounded;
- the completed source current is recovered with a cutoff-independent topology;
- deletion of any anomaly coordinate produces the predicted residual.

One exponentially growing finite contribution, one scale-dependent subtraction, or one fitted complex frequency falsifies the claimed unitary wave-trace constructor.

## Scope

Constructing such a fixed spectral functional would give the puncture-impossibility mechanism a legitimate operator form: off-seam zeros would require complex spectral frequencies unavailable to a self-adjoint source system. Until the functional and trace identity are derived independently from theta/Tate data, the proposal remains a Hilbert–Pólya target rather than an explanation.
