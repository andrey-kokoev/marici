# The two-ray four-grade packet has an exact two-by-two Hilbert Gram

Let

\[
E=-2\pi L^2f_0+4\pi^2L^2f_2,
\qquad
O=8\pi Lf_1-8\pi^2Lf_3,
\]

and define the two sewn ray vectors

\[
g_+=U_L(E+O),
\qquad
g_-=U_{-L}(E-O).
\]

Using the exact translated four-grade Gaussian table gives equal diagonal energies

\[
\langle g_+,g_+\rangle
=
\langle g_-,g_-\rangle
=
\frac1{\sqrt2}
\left(3\pi^2L^4+7\pi L^2\right).
\]

The cross-ray pairing is

\[
\langle g_+,g_-\rangle
=
\frac{e^{-2\pi L^2}}{\sqrt2}
\left(
16\pi^4L^8
-72\pi^3L^6
+67\pi^2L^4
-7\pi L^2
\right).
\]

The reverse pairing is equal, as required for the real Hilbert Gram. Both formulas retain the \(f_3\) diagonal and \(f_1\)-\(f_3\) interference terms; a three-grade truncation does not reproduce them.

Thus the ordinary ray-level Gram baseline is now fully assembled rather than merely known entrywise. The remaining quadratic theorem must explain how the relative Wronskian boundary form, causal-history graph energy, Fourier-saturated wall/tail topology, and radical quotient modify or represent this \(2\times2\) block.

This result does not identify the ordinary Hilbert Gram with \(\mathfrak G_p^\theta\), and therefore does not close G1.1.
