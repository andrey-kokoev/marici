# Constrained-form cancellation needs determinantal-ideal membership

## Status

Exact regularity correction. At a fixed parameter where the constraint matrix
has constant rank, a Hermitian form vanishing on the constraint kernel admits a
multiplier representation. Across a parameter-dependent rank drop, pointwise
kernel vanishing is insufficient: every multiplier may become singular.

The correct source-local certificate is membership in the Hermitian module
generated by the constraints with regular, cutoff-controlled multipliers. This
is a jet-order or determinantal-ideal condition.

## Fixed-rank theorem

Let

\[
L:V\longrightarrow W
\]

be a finite-dimensional linear map of full row rank, and let \(H=H^*\) be a
Hermitian form on \(V\). Suppose

\[
x^*Hx=0
\]

for every \(x\in\ker L\).

Choose coordinates in which

\[
L=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&I
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The kernel condition forces the upper-left block of \(H\) to vanish, so

\[
H=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&B^*\\
B&C
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Taking

\[
Y=
\begin{pmatrix}
B&C/2
\end{pmatrix}
\]

gives

\[
H=L^*Y+Y^*L.
\]

Thus at fixed full row rank, vanishing on the constrained kernel and existence
of a Hermitian multiplier certificate are equivalent.

## Rank-drop hostile

Let the real parameter be \(a\), and define

\[
L(a)=
\begin{pmatrix}
a^2&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
H(a)=
\begin{pmatrix}
a&0\\
0&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

For \(a\neq0\),

\[
\ker L(a)=\operatorname{span}
\left\{
\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\end{pmatrix}
\right\},
\]

and \(H(a)\) vanishes on that kernel. At \(a=0\), both \(L(0)\) and \(H(0)\)
vanish, so the kernel condition still holds on the enlarged kernel.

Write

\[
Y(a)=
\begin{pmatrix}
y_1(a)&y_2(a)
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The first diagonal entry of

\[
L(a)^*Y(a)+Y(a)^*L(a)
\]

is

\[
2a^2\operatorname{Re}y_1(a).
\]

Matching \(H(a)\) requires

\[
\operatorname{Re}y_1(a)=\frac1{2a}.
\]

Every multiplier is singular at the rank-drop point. Pointwise kernel
vanishing did not supply a regular constrained identity.

## Ideal interpretation

The constraint vanishes to order two while the candidate cancelled form
vanishes only to order one. The latter does not belong to the ideal generated
by the former and its adjoint with regular coefficients.

This is the Hermitian analogue of the earlier scalar hostile

\[
\sigma=z^2,
\qquad
\eta=z.
\]

Zero-fiber agreement does not control multiplicity.

## Application to the theta zero fiber

Let \(\mathcal L_X(z)\) stack the finite source equation and ordered endpoint
constraint, and let

\[
H_X(z)=Q_{{\rm Clark},X}(z)-r_X(z)^*r_X(z).
\]

The desired identity is

\[
H_X
=
\mathcal L_X^*Y_X
+Y_X^*\mathcal L_X.
\]

It is not enough to check numerically that \(H_X\) vanishes on the kernel at
each detected scalar zero. The multiplier \(Y_X\) must be constructed from the
source before locating zeros and must remain regular through every rank change.

For polynomial or analytic finite models, this is module membership over the
parameter ring. Smith valuations, minors, or jet expansions provide exact
finite tests.

## Relation to zero multiplicity

At a zero of multiplicity \(m\), the boundary constraint can lose rank to order
\(m\). A candidate signed repair that vanishes to lower order cannot descend
through the constraint quotient with a regular multiplier.

The Clark jet tower detects the first nonzero derivative at order \(m\). The
primitive seam current must match not only its value on the one-dimensional
fiber but also the transverse jet order required for a regular family
certificate.

This is where arbitrary finite zero multiplicity re-enters the signed-balance
programme.

## Completion gate

Even if every finite cutoff admits a regular \(Y_X\), completion requires one
source topology in which the multipliers remain uniformly controlled. A family
with

\[
\lVert Y_X\rVert\longrightarrow\infty
\]

is the cutoff analogue of the rank-drop pole above.

Therefore the full gate has three levels:

1. pointwise constrained cancellation;
2. regular parameter-module membership;
3. cutoff-uniform multiplier control.

Only the third descends to the completed zero-state system.

## Finite falsifier

The pair

\[
L(a)=
\begin{pmatrix}a^2&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
H(a)=
\operatorname{diag}(a,0)
\]

is the minimal hostile. It passes every pointwise kernel test and fails every
regular multiplier test at \(a=0\).

For theta, the corresponding falsifier is the first cutoff and spectral jet at
which the Clark-minus-seam form has lower vanishing order than the constraint
module.

## Decisive conclusion

The one primitive seam line has sufficient rank, but rank compatibility is not
enough. Exact cancellation must descend regularly through a parameter-dependent
constraint quotient. That requires determinantal-ideal membership with
source-derived multipliers and uniform completion control.

The next finite calculation should compute the Smith or jet profile of the
Clark-minus-seam form relative to the boundary-control constraint. A single
order mismatch closes the signed-balance route.
