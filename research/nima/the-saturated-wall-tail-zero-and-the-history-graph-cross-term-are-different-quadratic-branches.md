# The saturated wall-tail zero and the history-graph cross term are different quadratic branches

The completed theta history decomposes as

\[
H_\Phi=M_\Phi I+B.
\]

Its graph energy is

\[
\|f\|^2+\|H_\Phi f\|^2
=
(1+M_\Phi^2)\|f\|^2
+2M_\Phi\operatorname{Re}\langle f,Bf\rangle
+\|Bf\|^2.
\]

Thus the shifted-history graph contains the wall--tail cross operator

\[
M_\Phi(B+B^*).
\]

Separately, Fourier saturation produces a source-authorized observer Gram whose wall and tail character planes are exactly orthogonal. Its cross block is zero.

These statements are not contradictory because they concern different quadratic constructions. They cannot be identified merely because both are source-authorized or because the Adams operations preserve the saturated topology.

To replace the graph cross term by the saturated zero on the Adams incidence range, one must prove either

\[
\operatorname{Re}\langle f,Bf\rangle=0
\]

for every represented incidence vector, or an explicit block congruence/Schur transformation that cancels the cross term while preserving the remaining form.

The exact hostile checker demonstrates the distinction with rational scalar models: graph energy \(1+(M+B)^2\) differs from orthogonal energy \(1+M^2+B^2\) by \(2MB\).

Therefore the next G1.1 theorem is not only topology preservation. It must select and relate the two quadratic forms. Until then, the wall--tail entry in the first Adams target Gram is branch-dependent rather than fixed to zero.
