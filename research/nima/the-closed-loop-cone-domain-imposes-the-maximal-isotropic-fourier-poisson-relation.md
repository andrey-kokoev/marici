# The closed-loop cone domain imposes the maximal-isotropic Fourier-Poisson relation

## Boundary relation

Let \(W_{\rm FP}\) be the source Fourier--Poisson sewing unitary between the two
endpoint boundary fibres.  Its contragredient action on responses is
\(W_{\rm FP}^{-*}\).  Define

\[
\Lambda_{\rm FP}
=
\left\{
(c_-,r_-;c_+,r_+):
 c_+=W_{\rm FP}c_-,
\quad
 r_+=W_{\rm FP}^{-*}r_-
\right\}.
\]

For the doubled Green flux form

\[
\Sigma=-h_-\oplus h_+,
\]

the graph \(\Lambda_{\rm FP}\) is closed and maximal isotropic.

## Cone domain

Let \(\mathcal C(s)\) be the closed-loop boundary cone and let
\(\operatorname{Tr}_\partial\) be the complete endpoint trace on its common
history domain.  Define

\[
\operatorname{Dom}\mathcal C_{\rm FP}(s)
=
\left\{
\psi\in\operatorname{Dom}\mathcal C_{\max}(s):
\operatorname{Tr}_\partial\psi\in\Lambda_{\rm FP}
\right\}.
\]

The half-density history theorem gives continuity of the endpoint traces in
the graph norm.  Since \(\Lambda_{\rm FP}\) is closed, this domain is a closed
subspace of the maximal graph domain.  The restriction
\(\mathcal C_{\rm FP}(s)\) is therefore closed whenever the maximal cone is
closed.

## Three-stratum continuity

The response coordinate retains primitive, square, and connected strata.  On
the projective source and its dual rungs:

- prime-loop and grade-reindexing maps are continuous;
- Fourier--Poisson transport is unitary labelwise;
- primitive response is transported in the distributional dual rung;
- square response is transported in its Hilbert rung;
- connected response is transported in its nuclear/determinant rung.

Thus \(W_{\rm FP}^{-*}\) acts continuously on the stratified response carrier.
No scalar collapse is used to define the graph relation.

## Compatibility with the cone incidence

The reciprocal covariance identities for \(B_\pm\), \(G_\pm\), and \(L_\pm\)
show that the two endpoint traces of the closed-loop cone are exchanged by
\(W_{\rm FP}\), with responses exchanged contragrediently.  Hence the cone
restriction is invariant under reciprocal chart transport.

This is the full endpoint square that scalar functional-equation agreement
alone could not provide.

## Kernel-state flux

If

\[
0\ne\psi\in\ker\mathcal C_{\rm FP}(s),
\]

then its boundary trace lies in \(\Lambda_{\rm FP}\), so maximal isotropy gives

\[
\Sigma(\operatorname{Tr}_\partial\psi,
       \operatorname{Tr}_\partial\psi)=0.
\]

Inserted into the closed Green identity, this is vanishing oriented boundary
flux for the operator state.  It does not assert \(\Xi'(s)=0\) and is compatible
with a simple scalar determinant zero.

## Combination with Schur reduction

On an invertible open-loop chart, the previous cone theorem gives

\[
\ker\mathcal C_{\rm FP}(s)
\cong
\ker(I-K_{\rm rel}(s)).
\]

Therefore every relative eigenvalue-one collision in the restricted domain
produces a genuine closed kernel state satisfying the maximal-isotropic
boundary condition and hence zero Green flux.

\[

\Longrightarrow
