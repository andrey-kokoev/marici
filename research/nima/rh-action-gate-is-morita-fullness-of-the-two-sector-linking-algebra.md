# The RH action gate is Morita fullness, not off-diagonality

## Result

Treat the two theta/Tate sectors as the objects of a linking category, with
source algebras $A_+$ and $A_-$, forward passage $M$, and backward passage $N$.
The linking algebra is

$$
\mathcal L=
\begin{pmatrix}
A_+ & M\\
N & A_-
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

For the sector grading $\Gamma=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$, diagonal
constructors commute with $\Gamma$ and cross-sector constructors do not.
This detects crossing, not faithful action.

The stronger gate requires source-derived pairings

$$
\mu:M\otimes_{A_-}N\to A_+,
\qquad
\nu:N\otimes_{A_+}M\to A_-,
$$

satisfying the Morita compatibility identities. Faithful finite action requires

$$
\operatorname{im}\mu=A_+,
\qquad
\operatorname{im}\nu=A_-.
$$

Under completion, this must strengthen to a cutoff-independent quantitative
fullness margin.

## Hostile cases

A nonzero off-diagonal link can still generate proper image ideals. A one-way
link lacks a two-sided evaluation. Equal scalar traces can conceal different
ideal support. Finite positive margins can converge to zero.

Therefore the action gate has five ordered tests:

1. grading noncentrality;
2. two-sided source authority;
3. Morita compatibility;
4. finite fullness;
5. completion-stable quantitative fullness.

The product-system carrier passes only the typing stage. Observation and scalar
determinants are later gates and cannot repair a failed image ideal.
