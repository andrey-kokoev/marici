# Inseparable channel kernel

## Question

Can unique field-valued recovery coexist with a nonzero infinitesimal channel kernel?

## Claim boundary

Work over a field of characteristic p, with invertible channel scales. Put m=n-3=p^e r, with p not dividing r. Field points, tangent directions, and scheme length are separate invariants.

Flip equations over any commutative coefficient algebra force all kernel channel scales equal. The remaining equation is z^m=1. Thus the kernel functor is represented by mu_m, not merely the set of field-valued roots. In characteristic p,

\[
z^m-1=(z^r-1)^{p^e}.
\]

Over an algebraic closure there are r distinct roots, each of length p^e. The tangent kernel is one-dimensional if e>0 and zero otherwise: the derivative of z^m at one is m. This does not change the dimension of the image torus.

For a dual-number rescaling 1+epsilon, the channel product is 1+m epsilon. When e>0 every such uniform tangent perturbation is invisible. More precisely, the first nonzero term of (1+epsilon)^m-1 has degree p^e and coefficient r modulo p. Truncation through lower degrees therefore cannot detect the multiplicity; the next degree does.

## Disposition

At n=6 in characteristic three, cubing is bijective on F_3*, yet mu_3 has a tangent direction and scheme length three. The n=9 case retains two prime-field roots and rejects one power-map target, separating the prime-to-p obstruction. Checks at multiplicities two, three, four, and nine pass; n=8 in characteristic three is the deliberate no-tangent control. These computations test modular coefficients, not physical deformations or characteristic-zero root counts. Over imperfect fields a purely inseparable power map need not be surjective; that coefficient-field boundary remains distinct.
