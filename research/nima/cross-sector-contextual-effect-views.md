# Scattering and flavor are contextual families of commutative effect views

## Refinement of the common interface

One measurement context supplies a finite commutative effect algebra. The
ambient state space need not be commutative. Both positive sectors contain
multiple exhaustive projector families:

- scattering: helicity effects and rotated analyzer effects;
- flavor: up-type and down-type mass spectral effects.

Within each family the effects commute. Across the two families, generic
projectors do not commute.

## Exact comparison

For two projector families \(\{P_i\}\) and \(\{Q_j\}\), define

\[
B_{ij}=\operatorname{Tr}(P_iQ_j).
\]

The exact checker verifies in both sectors:

1. each context is internally commutative and exhaustive;
2. the two contexts are mutually incompatible;
3. \(B\) is positive and bistochastic.

Thus each context is a classical database-like view, while transition between
views retains the noncommutative ambient geometry.

## Architectural consequence

\[
\boxed{
\text{noncommutative state/effect substrate}
+\text{sector-selected commutative views}
+\text{bistochastic context transport}.}
\]

Shannon entropy belongs to a selected commutative view. Changing view can
change the probability distribution even when the underlying state is fixed.
The view is not arbitrary: flavor contexts are fixed by mass operators, while
scattering contexts are fixed by helicity geometry or physical analyzers.

This sharpens the relational-database analogy. The substrate is not well
modeled by one global table whose columns are simultaneously readable.
It resembles a family of compatible local query surfaces over a
noncommutative core.

## Scope

This is a shared diagram shape, not a cross-sector physical morphism. It does
not derive quantum theory from the bare Carrier. It identifies the exact
additional structure present in the two established entropy manifestations
and absent from the scalar cosmological period.
