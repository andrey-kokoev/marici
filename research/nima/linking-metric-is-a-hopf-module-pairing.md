# Linking metric is a Hopf-module pairing

On the bilateral Wronskian trace plane, translation acts by

$$
T_L=\begin{pmatrix}e^{L/2}&0\\0&e^{-L/2}\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Let

$$
J_0=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Since `det(T_L)=1`, direct multiplication gives

$$
T_L^\top J_0T_L=J_0.
$$

Thus the Wronskian is an invariant pairing for the half-density translation module. Equivalently, for a group-like Hopf element `Q_L`,

$$
\omega(\rho(Q_L)x,\rho(Q_L)y)=\omega(x,y).
$$

The bounded translation-orbit synthesis extends this identity from the rapid common core to the completed response orbit. If

$$
\omega_{\rm link}(x,y)=\langle J_{\rm link}x,y\rangle,
$$

then

$$
\rho(Q_L)^*J_{\rm link}\rho(Q_L)=J_{\rm link}
$$

on the completed orbit, with adjoints understood in the transported object-indexed Hilbert fibers.

The forced block

$$
K_{\rm link}=-\frac12J_{\rm link}
$$

inherits the same covariance. On the forward/reciprocal doubled output, the polarized metric

$$
G_{\rm link}=\begin{pmatrix}0&K_{\rm link}\\-K_{\rm link}&0\end{pmatrix}
$$

is therefore a morphism of paired translation modules. Reciprocal slot exchange still acts by the orientation character:

$$
S^*G_{\rm link}S=-G_{\rm link}.
$$

Hence linking contributes an invariant Hopf-module pairing together with a separate `Z/2` orientation covariance; the two laws do not conflict.
