# A Readout Quotient Can Erase a Genuine Selection

Let \(q:\mathcal L\to\mathcal O\) be a physical readout and let two sources
select distinct lenses \(s_0,s_1\in\mathcal L\).  It can happen that

\[
q(s_0)=q(s_1).
\]

Then the selections are real at the source level but observationally
indistinguishable through \(q\).  No left inverse of \(q\) can recover both.

This is the exact shape of the cosmological state-line phase under the
standard equal-time adapter: the augmented protocol distinguishes a phase
direction that \(|\Psi|^2\) forgets.

Consequently, selection and observability require separate certificates:

1. **Selection:** a source-derived section chooses a unique lens modulo gauge.
2. **Descent:** the selected distinction survives the physical readout.
3. **Reconstruction:** only a faithful readout can recover the selection.

The third condition is stronger than the second.  A selected quantity may
affect an observable without being uniquely reconstructible from it.

This also proves that the sector architecture cannot be treated as a freely
reorderable pipeline.  Applying a nonfaithful readout before asking which
lift was selected destroys the information required by the selection claim.
