# The three-grade Mertens scalar does not control the boundary corona

## Question

Does the exact scalar decomposition of the Mertens counterterm into primitive,
square, and connected grades suffice to prove that the completed comparison
class vanishes?

## Scalar-blind corona hostile

Consider the bounded sequence

\[
a=(1,-1,1,-1,\ldots).
\]

At every even cutoff \(2N\), its normalized scalar average is exactly zero:

\[
\frac1{2N}\sum_{n=0}^{2N-1}a_n=0.
\]

The zero sequence has the same scalar record at those cutoffs. Nevertheless,
\(a\) does not converge to zero and its class in \(\ell^\infty/c_0\) has norm
one.

Thus exact scalar cancellation at every member of a cofinal cutoff family does
not imply vanishing of the boundary corona class.

The same obstruction survives finitely many scalar moments. One may place
oscillation inside the common kernel of any fixed finite family of linear
readouts while retaining a nonzero tail norm. A finite scalar packet cannot
be jointly faithful on an infinite-dimensional corona without an additional
source restriction on the admissible residual class.

## Consequence for three-grade sewing

The identity decomposing the Mertens constant into primitive, square, and
connected Euler grades is exact and necessary. It proves that deleting any
declared grade changes the scalar normalization. It does not prove that the
state-valued completion residual vanishes.

The roles must remain distinct:

- three-grade Mertens sewing calibrates the scalar origin;
- boundary-corona control determines whether an invisible tail remains;
- a completed comparison 2-cell requires both.

Promoting the scalar identity directly to the completed cell would repeat the
projection error already encountered throughout the programme.

## Minimum repair

The scalar target must be accompanied by one of the following source-derived
structures:

1. a faithful state-valued boundary norm;
2. a positive measure whose total mass vanishes only when the measure does;
3. a separating family of corona characters with uniform bounds;
4. an operator-valued relative determinant whose defect norm controls its
   scalar trace;
5. a source theorem restricting admissible residuals to a class on which the
   scalar readout is injective.

The final option is especially dangerous: the admissible class must be derived
before applying the scalar, not defined retrospectively by the desired
kernel.

## Finite falsifier

For cutoffs \(2,4,8,16\), compare the zero vector with the alternating vector.
Both have scalar average zero at every cutoff. Their maximum tail magnitudes
are zero and one, respectively. Any compiler that declares the completed
boundary cell closed from these scalar averages alone produces a false pass.

## Verdict

The three-grade Mertens decomposition supplies a scalar acceptance target but
not uniform Beck--Chevalley descent. The live RH gate remains state-valued:
construct a source-derived boundary residual carrier and prove that its corona
class vanishes before taking the Mertens/BSY scalar readout.
