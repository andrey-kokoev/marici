# The Real Determinant Orientation Is a Source-Anchored Z2 Torsor

## Local Real line

At an \(I\)-fixed zero, a Real-equivariant Fredholm complex would supply a
complex determinant line with an anti-linear involution. Its fixed vectors
form a one-dimensional real line.

That real line has two orientations. Hermitian norm, complex dimension,
anti-linear involutivity, and the completed scalar determinant do not choose
between them.

## Two indistinguishable Real structures

On the complex line, consider

\[
c_+(z)=\overline z,
\qquad
c_-(z)=-\overline z.
\]

Both are anti-linear isometric involutions. Their fixed real lines are the
real and imaginary axes, respectively. Scalar norm and complex determinant
data do not distinguish which fixed line carries the source-positive frame.

Thus a Real structure is not yet an oriented Real structure.

## Torsor statement

For each fixed local determinant line, admissible orientations form a
\(C_2\)-torsor. Along the seam, comparison maps produce a sign cocycle. A
global positive fixed-point mark requires:

1. vanishing orientation holonomy on the completed seam packet;
2. a source anchor selecting one of the two global orientations;
3. preservation of that anchor through endpoint, gamma, primitive, square,
   and completion maps.

Orientability supplies existence of a coherent frame. It does not supply its
source authority.

## Contractible-base qualification

The open critical seam is topologically a line, so an ordinary real line
bundle over it is orientable. The obstruction is not generic bundle topology
on the open seam. It can enter through:

- the compactification or infinity port;
- singular continuation across zero events;
- typed transition maps between local determinant presentations;
- absence of a canonical source anchor.

Therefore invoking a first Stiefel–Whitney class without specifying the
completed base would overstate the topology.

## Required source anchor

The closest known analogue is the vacuum normalization in the Wilson sector:
the tensor unit selects the positive Wilson sheet. Here the candidate anchor
must arise from labelled theta/Tate data, such as a positive Gaussian vacuum,
an ordered endpoint generator, or a primitive Fock vacuum.

The anchor must live in the same Real determinant system as the zero local
classes. Positivity of an unrelated source vector cannot be transported into
that line without an incidence map.

## Finite falsifier

Present two anti-linear isometric involutions on one complex line that have
identical norm data and different fixed lines. Any compiler that declares a
positive orientation from isometry and involutivity alone must return the same
answer for both and therefore fails.

Adding a source vector fixed by one involution and anti-fixed by the other
separates them. This demonstrates exactly what the missing anchor must do.

## Revised RH gate

Even after constructing the Real zero complex, the fixed-mark theorem needs a
source-anchored orientation functor from its \(I\)-fixed determinant lines to
positive multiplicities. Without that functor, the result is a signed index,
not the unsigned Burnside observer required by RH.
