# Correction: a persistent dark line is allowed; only the closed-state crossing must be discrete

The previous note stated an overly strong target: that the radiation-dark boundary line should exist only at arithmetic zero ordinates. A rank-two boundary system with one open radiation output naturally has a one-dimensional dark line at every regular seam point.

Let
\[
Y(x):\mathcal B\to\mathcal R
\]
be the outgoing radiation map, with
\[
W(x)=Y(x)^{*}Y(x)=\operatorname{Im}M_{+}(x).
\]
If
\[
\dim\mathcal B=2,
\qquad
\dim\mathcal R=1,
\]
and \(Y(x)\neq0\), then
\[
\operatorname{rank}W(x)=1,
\qquad
\dim\ker W(x)=1
\]
for every such \(x\).

This permanent dark line does not produce a continuum of eigenstates. A closed state must satisfy two independent conditions:

1. Radiation cancellation:
   \[
   b\in\ker Y(x).
   \]
2. Reactive phase matching:
   \[
   (\Theta-\operatorname{Re}M_{+}(x))b=0.
   \]

Only their simultaneous satisfaction is a seam zero.

Write
\[
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{dark}}(x)=\ker Y(x)
\]
and
\[
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{react}}(x)
=
\ker(\Theta-\operatorname{Re}M_{+}(x))
\]
when the latter is one-dimensional. The zero condition is
\[
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{dark}}(x)
=
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{react}}(x).
\]

Thus discreteness comes from intersection of two moving lines in the boundary projective space, not from the appearance and disappearance of the dark line itself.

In coordinates, let \(y(x)\) be the nonzero radiation row and let \(r(x)\) be a row whose kernel is the reactive line. Then the Evans section is
\[
D(x)
=
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
y(x)\\
r(x)
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
A zero means the two rows become dependent, equivalently their kernel lines coincide.

This is the exact two-channel interference mechanism anticipated by the coherent/disagreement decomposition:

- the shared radiation evaluator sees one coherent output;
- its orthogonal disagreement combination is dark;
- arithmetic and archimedean propagation rotate the reactive line;
- a zeta zero occurs when the reactive line enters the dark direction.

The transverse margin is
\[
|D(x)|
\]
away from zeros. At a simple zero,
\[
D(x_0)=0,
\qquad
D'(x_0)\neq0.
\]
Higher zero multiplicity is the order of tangency of the two projective lines.

This corrects the source target to:

> The doubled theta-wall boundary system has one persistent source-authorized radiation-dark line, while the independently derived reactive line meets it exactly at the completed arithmetic divisor.

The independence clause is decisive. If \(r(x)\) is fitted from \(y(x)\) and \(\xi\), the construction remains circular.

The one-output hypothesis must itself be derived. Reciprocal sheets may initially produce two independent radiation outputs, giving full-rank \(W\) and no dark line. The seam glue must prove that they share a common terminal evaluator before the rank-one factorization is used.

Completion gates are:

1. \(Y(x)\) has rank one with a uniform nonzero singular value on compact seam intervals away from thresholds.
2. The dark line varies continuously or analytically.
3. The reactive relation is independently source-derived.
4. Their Evans determinant extends analytically off seam.
5. Its zeros have the correct \(\xi\) multiplicities.
6. The orthogonal radiative line remains observed at every crossing.

The smallest hostile has a permanent dark line and chooses the reactive relation equal to it for all \(x\). It produces a continuum of closed states.

A second hostile gives two independent radiation outputs. The supposed dark combination cancels one output but remains visible to the other.

A third hostile proves only scalar cancellation at the terminal port while the full radiation operator has rank two.

So the continuous-spectrum pilot is now finite and geometric: compute the \(1\times2\) radiation row and the independent \(1\times2\) reactive row, then compare their projective winding.
