# Correction: the connection curvature lands in a reciprocal-odd Gaussian module

## Correction to the first curvature packet

The identity

\[
[A(A+1),\partial_x]=-2(A+1)\partial_x
\]

is correct. However, the first packet described its image as living naturally
in the same cyclic dilation module as the even three-grade theta completion.
That statement is too coarse. The ray derivative changes parity.

## Exact action on the Gaussian

Let

\[
f_j(x)=x^j e^{-\pi x^2},
\qquad
A=x\partial_x,
\qquad
P=A(A+1).
\]

Then

\[
\partial_x f_j
=
j f_{j-1}-2\pi f_{j+1},
\]

and

\[
A f_j
=
j f_j-2\pi f_{j+2}.
\]

For the even Gaussian \(f_0\),

\[
\partial_xf_0=-2\pi f_1.
\]

Therefore the curvature operator

\[
\Omega=-2(A+1)\partial_x
\]

acts by

\[
\Omega f_0
=
8\pi f_1-8\pi^2 f_3.
\]

Its image is in the odd Gaussian polynomial sector, not the even theta packet
spanned initially by \(f_0,f_2,f_4\).

## Two-ray typing

The two comoving connections contain opposite ray derivatives. Hence their
curvatures are

\[
\Omega_+=-2(A+1)\partial_x,
\qquad
\Omega_- =+2(A+1)\partial_x.
\]

Reflection exchanges the rays and the signs. The curvature therefore forms a
reciprocal-odd pair. It may cancel under an unoriented symmetric scalar
readout, but it survives in the oriented seam channel.

This is exactly the information that a scalar endpoint comparison can hide.

\[
