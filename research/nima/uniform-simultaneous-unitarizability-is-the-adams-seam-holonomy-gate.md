# Uniform simultaneous unitarizability is the Adams-seam holonomy gate

## Exact finite criterion

Let
\[
\pi_X:\Gamma_X\to GL(H_X)
\]
be the source-authorized holonomy representation generated by the Adams-seam constructor graph at finite cutoff \(X\).

The exact finite question is whether there exists one positive metric
\[
G_X>0
\]
such that
\[
H^*G_XH=G_X
\qquad
\text{for every }H\in\pi_X(\Gamma_X).
\]

This holds exactly when the represented holonomy group is conjugate into a unitary group. In finite dimension, equivalently, its closure in \(GL(H_X)\) is compact.

Checking selected cycle generators separately is only a preliminary filter.

## Three gates

### Generator gate

Every chosen cycle generator is individually similar to a unitary matrix.

This rejects obvious eigenvalue and Jordan defects but does not produce a common metric.

### Group gate

All words generated by the cycle holonomies preserve one common positive metric.

This is simultaneous unitarizability and is the finite constructor theorem.

### Completion gate

The common metrics or conjugators remain uniformly equivalent to the original completed source topology over cutoff and compact parameter regions.

This is uniform simultaneous unitarizability.

## Common metric and conjugator forms

If
\[
G_X=S_X^*S_X,
\]
then
\[
H^*G_XH=G_X
\]
is equivalent to
\[
S_XHS_X^{-1}
\]
being unitary for every represented holonomy \(H\).

Completion stability requires separate bounds
\[
\sup_X\|S_X\|<\infty,
\qquad
\sup_X\|S_X^{-1}\|<\infty.
\]

A bounded condition number alone is insufficient because scalar rescaling of \(S_X\) leaves the conjugated representation unchanged. One may normalize \(S_X\), for example by determinant or trace, but the normalization must be frozen before checking absolute bounds.

## Necessary word invariants

For every holonomy word \(H\), simultaneous unitarizability implies:

- \(|\det H|=1\);
- \(\sigma(H)\subset S^1\);
- \(H\) is semisimple;
- \(\sup_{n\in\mathbb Z}\|H^n\|<\infty\) in a uniformly equivalent norm.

These conditions are necessary word tests, not a replacement for the common metric.

Testing only the generators misses spectral defects appearing in products.

## Two-involution hostile

Let
\[
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0\\
0&-1
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
B=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&t\\
0&-1
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad t\neq0.
\]
Both are involutions:
\[
A^2=B^2=I.
\]
Each is diagonalizable with eigenvalues \(\{1,-1\}\), hence individually unitarizable in some positive metric.

But
\[
AB=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&t\\
0&1
\end{pmatrix}
\]
is a nontrivial unipotent Jordan block. It is not similar to a unitary matrix. Therefore no common positive metric is preserved by both \(A\) and \(B\).

This is the minimal generatorwise hostile: every selected generator passes, while a generated word fails.

A hyperbolic variant chooses two individually unitarizable involutions whose product has reciprocal real eigenvalues \(\lambda,\lambda^{-1}\) with \(|\lambda|>1\).

## Compact-closure test

If the closure
\[
K_X=\overline{\pi_X(\Gamma_X)}
\]
is compact, average any positive seed metric over Haar measure:
\[
G_X=\int_{K_X}H^*G_0H\,d\mu(H).
\]
Then \(G_X>0\) and is invariant under the whole group.

This proves existence but does not automatically prove source authority or uniform equivalence as \(X\to\infty\). The averaging construction must be compatible with cutoff and the frozen source topology.

## Finite computational certificate

For a finite matrix representation, the common metric equations are linear in the Hermitian unknown \(G\):
\[
H_j^*GH_j-G=0
\]
for a generating set \(H_j\).

Solve the joint fixed space
\[
\mathcal F_X=
\{G=G^*:H_j^*GH_j=G\ \forall j\}
\]
and test whether it intersects the positive-definite cone.

This generator equation is sufficient for the entire generated group because invariance composes. The distinction from generatorwise unitarizability is that one solves all equations for the same \(G\), not one \(G_j\) per generator.

The certificate returns:

- one common \(G_X>0\); or
- a proof that \(\mathcal F_X\) misses the positive cone;
- a short word with determinant, spectrum, or Jordan obstruction when found.

## Uniform completion certificate

A finite family of solutions \(G_X\) must be chosen with
\[
m_CI\le G_{X,s}\le M_CI
\]
uniformly for \(s\) in each compact off-seam region and all cutoffs.

Equivalently, after a frozen normalization,
\[
\|S_{X,s}\|\le C_C,
\qquad
\|S_{X,s}^{-1}\|\le C_C.
\]

The feasible positive cone may be nonempty at every finite cutoff while approaching its boundary. Then simultaneous unitarization exists finitely but not uniformly.

## Quantitative hostile

Choose representations \(\pi_X\) conjugate to a fixed unitary representation:
\[
\pi_X(\gamma)=S_X^{-1}U(\gamma)S_X,
\]
where
\[
S_X=
\begin{pmatrix}
X&0\\
0&X^{-1}
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Every finite holonomy group is unitarizable by
\[
G_X=S_X^*S_X.
\]
Yet
\[
\|S_X\|=\|S_X^{-1}\|=X\to\infty.
\]
No uniform equivalence to the source metric follows.

The representation may even have identical abstract unitary image at every cutoff; the failure lies in its embedding into the source topology.

## Adams-seam specialization

Write each edge as
\[
T_\alpha=a(\alpha)U_\alpha
\]
only when the positive scale is central in the relevant typed fiber. The generated holonomy representation then combines:

- geometric moving-seam unitary transport;
- positive Adams scaling;
- grade and sheet permutations;
- endpoint and archimedean factors when admitted;
- associator and unitor transport.

The group test must use ordered full matrices. Testing the scalar Adams cycle product separately is sufficient only in the central scalar case.

## Classification outcome

The concrete audit can return:

1. **source-unitary:** the original source metric is invariant;
2. **uniformly unitarizable:** another source-authorized uniformly equivalent metric is invariant;
3. **merely bi-bounded:** no invariant metric is authorized, but canonical composites have separate uniform bounds;
4. **generatorwise only:** each primitive cycle is individually harmless, but no common metric exists;
5. **intrinsically amplifying:** a word or completion sequence escapes every uniformly equivalent invariant metric.

## Next executable theorem

For each finite cutoff and compact parameter sample:

1. generate a cycle basis;
2. build the full typed holonomy generators;
3. solve their joint invariant-Hermitian space;
4. test positive-definite feasibility;
5. search short words for determinant, spectral, and Jordan witnesses;
6. normalize a feasible \(G_X\);
7. record its extremal eigenvalues and conjugator norms;
8. verify cutoff and source-authority compatibility;
9. take uniform envelopes toward completion.

This directly answers whether Adams-seam transport is uniformly unitarizable in the original completed source topology.
