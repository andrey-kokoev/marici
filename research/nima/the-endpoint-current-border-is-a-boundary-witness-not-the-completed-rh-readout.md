# The endpoint-current border is a boundary witness, not the completed RH readout

## Necessary correction

The bordered operator

\[
D_J(G)
=
\begin{pmatrix}
I&u(G)\\
E_0&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
E_0u(G)=J(G),
\]

satisfies

\[
\det D_J(G)=-J(G).
\]

This is an exact zero-to-state bridge for the additive endpoint current. It
does not make \(J\) the completed RH readout.

The distinction is decisive because \(J\) is a logarithmic boundary
counterterm, whereas the completed determinant section is multiplicative.

## Additive zeros do not propagate

For endpoint translation,

\[
b_G(a+b)
=
b_G(a)+b_{S_aG}(b).
\]

Choose a state \(G\) and a displacement \(a\) such that

\[
b_G(a)=0.
\]

There is no source law forcing

\[
b_{S_aG}(b)=0.
\]

Hence generically

\[
b_G(a+b)=b_{S_aG}(b)\ne0.
\]

A zero of one additive interval current does not propagate through later
translations. Therefore the bordered determinant \(-J\) fails the
generator-wise zero-sieve condition in general.

The border faithfully converts a boundary-current zero into cohomology, but
that cohomology is not stable under the action category.

## Smallest explicit hostile

Let

\[
G(q)=q(q-a).
\]

Then

\[
G(0)=G(a)=0,
\]

so

\[
b_G(a)=0.
\]

For \(b>0\),

\[
b_{S_aG}(b)
=
G(a+b)-G(a)
=
b(a+b)\ne0.
\]

Thus the present bordered complex has cohomology at displacement \(a\), while
the continued displacement \(a+b\) has nonzero readout.

This two-step polynomial witness rejects any claim that endpoint-current
bordering alone constructs the RH zero sieve.

## Logarithm versus determinant

For the finite logarithmic Euler operator,

\[
H_X=-\sum_{p\le X}\log(1-Q_p),
\]

the endpoint current is

\[
J_X=(\partial E_0)(H_X).
\]

It belongs to the logarithm of the relative determinant packet. The
multiplicative totalization has schematic form

\[
\Delta_X
=
\exp(J_X^{(1)}+J_X^{(2)})
\Delta_{3,X}^{\mathrm{conn}}
\Delta_X^{\mathrm{seam}}
\Delta_X^\infty.
\]

A zero of \(J_X\) is not a zero of \(\Delta_X\). Indeed, exponentiated
counterterms are nowhere zero wherever they are defined.

The possible zeros lie in the connected or coupled determinant section, after
all relative lines have been totalized. Bordering the logarithmic counterterm
creates an auxiliary zero locus with no direct \(\Xi\) authority.

## Correct bordered object

The RH-bearing border must be built from a source-derived transported state
\(u_\Delta(s)\) and observer \(y_\Delta(s)\) whose ordered readout is the full
multiplicative section:

\[
y_\Delta(s)u_\Delta(s)
=
\Delta(s).
\]

Only then does

\[
D_\Delta(s)
=
\begin{pmatrix}
I&u_\Delta(s)\\
y_\Delta(s)&0
\end{pmatrix}
\]

satisfy

\[
\det D_\Delta(s)=-\Delta(s),
\]

and only an independently derived invertible line comparison

\[
\Delta(s)=v(s)\Xi(s)
\]

turns its cohomology into a zero of \(\Xi\).

The endpoint-current border is one boundary component needed to construct
\(u_\Delta\) and \(y_\Delta\); it is not their substitute.

## Role retained by the current border

The additive border remains useful in four precise ways:

1. it proves that the endpoint current is a genuine operative port;
2. it preserves the primitive, square, and connected boundary columns;
3. it exposes which triangular eliminations fail under completion;
4. it supplies a finite domain witness for seam and endpoint anomalies.

These are provenance and completion functions, not zero-confinement.

## Correct zero-sieve target

Let \(\Delta\) be the fully totalized determinant readout. For every source
generator \(g\), categorical RH requires a nonzero line map \(\lambda_g\) with

\[
\Delta(F(g)x)=\lambda_g\Delta(x).
\]

The additive components may transform by cocycles, but their exponentials and
the connected determinant anomaly must combine so that the total
\(\lambda_g\) is multiplicative and relation-coherent.

Thus the zero sieve belongs to \(\Delta\), not to any one logarithmic
countercurrent.

## Completion consequence

Primitive and square endpoint currents may diverge, yet their anomaly lines
can still admit a relative trivialization. Since their exponentials are
nonvanishing line factors, their role is to normalize and orient the connected
determinant, not to contribute scalar zeros independently.

A completion theorem must therefore avoid asking for scalar convergence of
\(J^{(1)}\) and \(J^{(2)}\). It should ask for:

- convergence of their transition functions as anomaly-line data;
- cancellation with seam and archimedean transition functions;
- a completed connected determinant section;
- compatibility of the total product under cutoff inclusion.

This is weaker than scalar current convergence and stronger than discarding
the currents.

## Hostile tests

1. A proposed zero sieve for \(J\) fails on \(G(q)=q(q-a)\).
2. Identifying \(J=0\) with \(\Delta=0\) confuses a logarithm with its
   exponential totalization.
3. Bordering a fitted scalar current does not establish the \(\Xi\) bridge.
4. Deleting the current border because it is not RH-bearing loses boundary
   provenance.
5. Assuming scalar convergence of divergent countercurrents imposes the wrong
   completion target.
6. A total multiplier assembled from generator factors must be checked on all
   action relations.

## Revised frontier

The finite programme now has two distinct borders:

- \(D_J\): the additive endpoint-current border, already constructed;
- \(D_\Delta\): the multiplicative completed-determinant border, still missing.

The next source constructor must assemble the transported state and observer
for \(D_\Delta\) from the connected determinant carrier together with the
primitive, square, seam, and Gaussian anomaly lines.

Only the second border can enter the pointed zero-sieve pullback used by the
categorical RH certificate.

## Verdict

The endpoint-current border is exact but not RH-bearing. Its zeros do not
propagate because endpoint incidence is additive. Categorical RH requires the
border of the fully totalized multiplicative determinant section, with the
current border retained as typed boundary provenance inside that construction.
