# A Zero Lift Must Reflect the Common Source Domain

## Question

Grothendieck's primitive two-sector construction has a common regulated domain

\[
D_\varepsilon=\{z:|\operatorname{Re}z|<\varepsilon/2\},
\]

whose regulator limit is the critical seam. The completed scalar transform is
entire. What kind of zero-to-state map can transmit the smaller source domain
to the scalar divisor?

## The aggregate lift fails

At a scalar zero, the aggregate tail is superexponentially decaying at both
ends. It therefore exists in every opposite exponential rigging for every
value of \(\operatorname{Re}z\). This lift factors through label summation
before testing primitive admissibility.

Consequently it cannot distinguish a seam zero from a hostile off-seam zero.
It proves regularity of an aggregated representative, not membership in the
common primitive domain.

## Required categorical shape

Let \(Z\) be the scalar zero locus, \(D\) the scalar continuation domain,
\(A_\varepsilon\) the common labelled source-state object, and
\(j_\varepsilon:A_\varepsilon\to D\) its parameter map. A useful bridge must
provide a map

\[
\ell_\varepsilon:Z\longrightarrow A_\varepsilon
\]

such that

\[
j_\varepsilon\ell_\varepsilon=i_Z.
\]

This is a lifting problem against the source inclusion, not merely a map from
zeros to an ambient completed state space. The square forces every zero in the
scope of the lift to have a common labelled representative.

The regulator-compatible family must also commute with restriction:

\[
r_{\varepsilon,\delta}\ell_\varepsilon=\ell_\delta
\qquad (0<\delta<\varepsilon).
\]

Only then does the inverse-limit source domain constrain the zero parameter.

## Noncircularity gate

Domain reflection cannot be obtained by declaring the lift undefined away
from the seam. The object \(A_\varepsilon\), its norm, its primitive comb
incidences, and every restriction map must be constructed without inspecting
the scalar divisor.

The proof obligation is therefore:

1. construct the labelled lift before aggregation;
2. prove compatibility with every primitive comb current;
3. prove regulator naturality;
4. prove that every scalar zero supplies a nonzero lifted state;
5. derive seam confinement from the independently computed inverse-limit
   domain.

## Finite hostile model

Take one seam parameter and one off-seam parameter, both declared scalar
zeros. An ambient aggregate lift sends both to regular states. It passes
zero-to-state existence and is still useless.

A typed common-source carrier containing only the seam point is
domain-reflecting, but the off-seam zero has no lift. Thus it exposes the exact
unproved claim instead of proving it by vocabulary.

## Decisive outcome

The next RH-bearing theorem is not "a zero gives a state." It is:

> Every scalar zero has a regulator-natural, label-faithful lift through the
> independently constructed common primitive source domain.

The aggregate tail theorem falsifies every bridge that performs label
summation first. If no pre-aggregation lift exists, the two-sector completion
explains the critical seam but does not confine the zeros to it.
