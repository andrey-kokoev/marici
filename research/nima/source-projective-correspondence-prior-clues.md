## 1. A compatibility law that survives rank loss

`the-Cayley-real-projective-matrix-monoid-provides-the-rank-one-extension-of-reciprocal-unitarity.md` uses J0=i[[0,1],[-1,0]] and the polynomial identity

    M^T J0 M=det(M) J0

for real 2-by-2 M, including singular matrices. The row-action version is M J0 M^T=det(M) J0. No inverse or determinant normalization is needed. At rank one the pulled-back form vanishes: image lines are isotropic, rather than incorrectly declared invertibly unitary.

This is directly relevant to our actual real-fixture prefix matrices. It preserves a meaningful relation at precisely the singularities that destroy the four-point cross-ratio. But it is an identity for every real 2-by-2 matrix; verifying it cannot by itself derive a preferred interval metric, continuum action or reality condition for arbitrary complex source data.

Two qualifications are necessary when reusing the old note:

- Nonzero matrices modulo scale do not form a total matrix-product monoid without handling zero products. A=diag(1,0), B=diag(0,1) are nonzero but AB=0, whose projective class is undefined. Keep affine matrices including zero, adjoin an absorbing zero class, or explicitly restrict composition. The word 'monoid' alone does not supply that choice.
- On the real rank-two stratum, determinant sign matters. Positive determinant permits order-preserving PSL normalization; negative determinant reverses real projective order and gives a negative form multiplier. It must not silently be called the same positive-multiplier unitary action.

The old note's arithmetic/prime comparison is unrelated to the present AdS/dS target and is not imported.
