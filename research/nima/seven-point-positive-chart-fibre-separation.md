# Seven-point positive chart: a local image and fibre-separation test

The previous transfer audit stopped prematurely and missed the existing six-rank-table proposal. A later fibre test (`seven-point-positroid-compiler-has-three-collapsed-image-cells.md`) invalidates three proposed cells as eight-dimensional image charts; comparisons against them below remain exact comparisons with those source rank tables, not with certified history cells. Using its history-zero cell (vanishing cyclic minors 23 and 56), `check_seven_point_positive_chart.py` constructs a genuinely positive eight-parameter rank-two source chart. For the positive moment-curve external data `Z_j=(1,j,j²,j³,j⁴,j⁵)`, its target affine-chart Jacobian at a rational interior point is `102400/194481`, so its image has nonempty interior in the eight-dimensional target.

The next exact fibre test uses the one-dimensional kernel of the external-data matrix, spanned by `(1,-6,15,-20,15,-6,1)`. Every source matrix with the same `CZ` plane can be represented, after an invertible row change, by adding an independent multiple of this vector to each of the two source rows. For each of the other five compiled seven-point rank tables, the checker solves its two prescribed vanishing-minor conditions in those two fibre variables. At two rational interior history-zero samples the system has a unique solution in each rival cell, but each solution has both positive and negative ordered minors. Thus none belongs to that positive rank-two cell, even allowing reversal of row orientation. In particular, by continuity and nonsingularity of the constraint systems, these samples have neighborhoods in the history-zero image disjoint from all five rival chart images locally.

This is **not** a global triangulation theorem; three of the rival source tables have since been shown to have collapsed image dimension. Other fibre solutions could enter elsewhere; global coverage, common-refinement intersection loci, and equality of the source pushforward canonical form with the matched parity-dual history form are not shown. The five rival forms were matched to histories at a rational kinematic fixture, which does not identify this positive moment-curve chart's pushforward form. The next test should compute the chart's canonical form in eight positive variables and its rational pushforward on the `CZ` image, then compare normalized residues and forms rather than only dimensions or rank tables.
