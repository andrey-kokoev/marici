# Carrier polarity is not intrinsically time orientation

## Exact character test

The \(m=3\) Carrier comparison proves that both road reflection and
polarity/core exchange negate the one-dimensional relation/polarity line.

Spacetime has distinct involutions:

- a transverse spatial reflection preserves the future time-orientation line;
- time reversal negates the future time-orientation line.

A nonzero equivariant map between one-dimensional character lines exists only
when their characters agree.

Let \(a:L_{\rm pol}\to L_{\rm time}\). If Carrier road reflection is compared
with a spatial reflection, equivariance requires

\[
(+1)a=a(-1),
\]

hence

\[
2a=0.
\]

Over characteristic zero, \(a=0\). Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
L_{\rm pol}\not\cong L_{\rm time}
\quad\text{under the natural reflection comparison}.
}
\]

This is the same type of obstruction previously found between time
orientation and helicity orientation: a spatial reflection can preserve
future direction while reversing a different orientation line.

## Surviving comparison

Carrier polarity/core exchange and physical time reversal both have character
\(-1\). Their one-dimensional character lines are therefore algebraically
compatible:

\[
(-1)a=a(-1).
\]

But this does not construct a physical comparison. It requires declaring that
a particular Carrier core-exchange involution represents spacetime time
reversal and fixing a nonzero normalization. Neither is currently
source-derived.

Thus the exact result is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Carrier polarity cannot supply time orientation without a typed
involution comparison.}
}
\]
