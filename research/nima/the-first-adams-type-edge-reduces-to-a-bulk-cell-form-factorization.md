# The first Adams type edge reduces to a bulk-cell form factorization

## Source gap after extraction

The primitive-to-square source data currently provide the Gaussian bulk cell
\[
\alpha_{p,1}=W_{2\log p}-W_{\log p}.
\]
They do not provide the incidence relation
\[
\Gamma_{2;p,1}
\]
or an Adams edge
\[
A_{2;p,1}:F_{p,1}\to F_{p,2}.
\]

The missing construction can now be stated exactly: the bulk cell must induce a mixed sesquilinear form on the primitive and square defect spaces, and that form must factor through their positive energies.

## Defect-space setup

Let \(D_1,D_2\) be source cores for primitive and square sectors. Let
\[
c_1[x],\qquad c_2[y]
\]
be their source-derived nonnegative defect forms, with quotient-completion spaces
\[
\mathcal K_1,\qquad\mathcal K_2.
\]

The bulk cell must define a mixed form
\[
b_{\alpha,p}(x,y),
\qquad x\in D_1,\ y\in D_2.
\]

This definition is not yet present in the source records. It must come from a declared pairing of \(\alpha_{p,1}\) with primitive and square boundary currents.

## Radical gate

Before completion, require
\[
c_1[x]=0
\Longrightarrow
b_{\alpha,p}(x,y)=0
\quad\forall y,
\]
and
\[
c_2[y]=0
\Longrightarrow
b_{\alpha,p}(x,y)=0
\quad\forall x.
\]

Otherwise the mixed form does not descend to the gauge-reduced defect spaces.

This is the first exact test. No operator or relation can be extracted before it passes.

## Domination gate

Seek
\[
|b_{\alpha,p}(x,y)|^2
\le
c_1[x]\,c_2[y].
\]

More generally, a constant \(\eta_p\) gives
\[
|b_{\alpha,p}(x,y)|
\le
\eta_p\,c_1[x]^{1/2}c_2[y]^{1/2}.
\]

The contractive sewing theorem requires
\[
\eta_p\le1.
\]

A cutoff-independent strict margin would require
\[
\eta_p\le1-\varepsilon
\]
in the region where strict positivity is claimed.

## Riesz factorization

If radical annihilation and domination hold, the mixed form descends and there exists a unique contraction
\[
C_{2;p,1}:\mathcal K_2\to\mathcal K_1
\]
such that
\[
b_{\alpha,p}(x,y)
=
\langle[x],C_{2;p,1}[y]\rangle_{\mathcal K_1}
\]
after the canonical identifications of the form completions.

In operator coordinates, if the diagonal forms are represented by \(C_1,C_2\) and the mixed block by \(K\), this is
\[
K=C_1^{1/2}C_{2;p,1}C_2^{1/2}.
\]

This contraction is the first constructible type-edge candidate. It is derived from the mixed form, not identified directly with \(\alpha_{p,1}\).

## Relation versus map

The contraction acts on defect-space completions. To obtain a relation between the original grade fibers, pull it back through the quotient maps:
\[
q_1:D_1\to\mathcal K_1,
\qquad
q_2:D_2\to\mathcal K_2.
\]

Define the graph relation
\[
\Gamma_{2;p,1}
=
\{(x,y):q_1x=C_{2;p,1}q_2y\}
\]
or its directionally correct mate, according to the source boundary convention.

The orientation must be fixed from the graph boundary identity. The factorization alone does not decide whether the Adams edge points from primitive to square, square to primitive, or between their dual defect spaces.

## Closability

If \(\Gamma_{2;p,1}\) is represented on the original rigged spaces, prove its graph is closable.

The existing criterion requires a dense adjoint domain. In abstract form:
\[
\overline{\operatorname{dom}\Gamma_{2;p,1}^*}
=
\text{target space}.
\]

A bounded contraction on defect quotients does not automatically produce a closable relation on unreduced source spaces if the quotient maps or embeddings are unbounded.

## Graph boundary identity

The target identity is
\[
q_{\mathrm{completed}}
=
q_1\oplus q_2+\partial_\Gamma J.
\]

The source derivation must verify that the boundary contribution generated by \(\Gamma\) has mixed polarization exactly
\[
b_{\alpha,p}.
\]

This is the authority bridge:

1. \(\alpha_{p,1}\) defines the mixed form;
2. the mixed form factors through defect energies;
3. the factor defines \(C_{2;p,1}\);
4. \(C_{2;p,1}\) pulls back to \(\Gamma_{2;p,1}\);
5. \(\Gamma\) produces the graph boundary term.

Skipping any arrow fits a sewing map rather than deriving it.

## Minimal falsifiers

### Bulk cell without a pairing

The formula for \(\alpha_{p,1}\) exists, but no source pairing maps it to \(b_{\alpha,p}\). The edge remains undefined.

### Radical failure

The mixed form sees a zero-energy primitive or square vector. It cannot descend to the defect quotient.

### Domination failure

The sharp constant satisfies
\[
\eta_p>1.
\]
The completed block form is indefinite. The reported prime-five hostile is of this type for the naive same-carrier incidence.

### Nonclosable pullback

The defect contraction exists, but its pulled-back graph relation has nondense adjoint domain.

### Wrong boundary orientation

A contraction is inserted with the wrong mate, producing the desired scalar cross term but the wrong graph boundary sign or reciprocal behavior.

## Source-native finite calculation

At cutoff \(X\), the first executable calculation must provide:

1. finite primitive and square source cores;
2. diagonal Gram matrices \(C_{1,X},C_{2,X}\);
3. a mixed matrix \(K_{\alpha,p,X}\) derived directly from \(\alpha_{p,1}\);
4. kernel inclusions
   \[
   \ker C_{1,X}\subseteq\ker K_{\alpha,p,X}^*,
   \qquad
   \ker C_{2,X}\subseteq\ker K_{\alpha,p,X};
   \]
5. the normalized contraction
   \[
   W_X=C_{1,X}^{\dagger/2}K_{\alpha,p,X}C_{2,X}^{\dagger/2};
   \]
6. the sharp norm \(\|W_X\|\);
7. the induced graph relation and boundary sign;
8. cutoff and reciprocal compatibility.

Pseudoinverses act only on gauge-reduced supports.
