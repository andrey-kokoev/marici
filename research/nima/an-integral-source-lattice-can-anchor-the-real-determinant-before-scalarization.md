# An Integral Source Lattice Can Anchor the Real Determinant Before Scalarization

## Integral determinant line

Let \(C_{\mathbb Z}^\bullet\) be a finite free complex generated by ordered,
typed source labels. Its determinant line is a rank-one integral lattice
formed from the top exterior powers of the chain groups, with the usual
alternating duals by degree.

The ordered source basis supplies a primitive generator

\[
\omega_{\mathbb Z}\in\det C_{\mathbb Z}^\bullet.
\]

After extension to \(\mathbb R\) or \(\mathbb C\), this generator selects an
orientation of the Real determinant line. The orientation is established
before scalar completion rather than inferred from a scalar vacuum overlap.

## Transition law

An integral change of source presentation acts on the determinant generator
by its determinant.

- A unimodular transition with determinant \(+1\) preserves the anchor.
- A unimodular transition with determinant \(-1\) reverses it.
- A nonunimodular transition rescales the primitive lattice and is not an
  orientation-preserving equivalence.

Therefore presentation invariance needs an oriented integral transition cell,
not merely equality after tensoring with \(\mathbb C\).

## Saturation gate

Relations must be saturated before quotient formation. If a relation
submodule is not saturated, the quotient contains torsion and the image of
the determinant generator is nonprimitive.

For example, the relation generated by \(2e_1\) inside \(\mathbb Z^2\) gives
a quotient with a \(\mathbb Z/2\) component. Saturating the relation replaces
it by the primitive submodule generated by \(e_1\), leaving a free quotient
with a primitive determinant line.

This is the determinant-frame analogue of Benincasa's barcode lift: form the
integral source-label lattice and saturated exact relations before field
reduction.

## Candidate theta chain

The orientation route should now attempt:

`labelled valuation/Fock lattice -> saturated boundary complex -> integral determinant line -> Real completion`

The Gaussian or Fock vacuum can anchor the final line if it is the image of
the primitive integral generator under this chain. Positivity then confirms
the inherited orientation; it does not create it.

## Completion gate

For a cutoff system \(C_{\mathbb Z,X}^\bullet\), transition maps must:

1. preserve the ordered source-label orientation;
2. carry primitive determinant generators to primitive generators;
3. commute with the Real Klein-four action;
4. remain compatible with saturated relation submodules;
5. define a nonvanishing determinant-line limit.

A single determinant-negative transition creates sign holonomy. An index-two
embedding destroys primitivity even if it becomes invertible over
\(\mathbb Q\) or \(\mathbb C\).

## Finite falsifiers

- Swap two source labels. The scalar span is unchanged, but the determinant
  orientation reverses.
- Replace a primitive relation by twice itself. The rational relation space
  is unchanged, but the integral quotient gains torsion.
- Allow cutoff determinant generators to scale by \(1/N\) after
  complexification. Every finite line is nonzero while the completed anchor
  collapses.

## Decisive gain

The frame problem is not necessarily an analytic positivity theorem. It may
be an integral provenance theorem. The next calculation is to identify the
actual ordered theta/Tate source lattice and determine whether all boundary
and reciprocal transition maps preserve its primitive determinant generator.
