# Regular homogeneous leakage closure is equivalent to zero-freeness

## Status

Exact local analytic no-go and compiler correction. For a nonzero holomorphic
line section, existence of a regular homogeneous first-order equation is
equivalent to absence of zeros. Therefore homogeneous leakage closure can prove
zero exclusion only when its coefficient is independently constructed from the
source. Recovering the coefficient by division merely restates the desired
conclusion.

Pointwise kernel inclusion is weaker and insufficient. The correct finite
condition is ideal membership, equivalently a full jet-order test at every
candidate zero.

## Local setup

Let \(D\) be a holomorphic line connection on a one-complex-dimensional domain
\(\Omega\). In a local frame write

\[
D=d+A,
\]

where \(A\) is a holomorphic one-form. Let \(\sigma\) be a holomorphic scalar
section not identically zero.

The proposed leakage closure is

\[
D\sigma=\kappa\sigma
\]

with \(\kappa\) holomorphic.

## Zero-order obstruction

Suppose \(\sigma\) has a zero of order \(m\geq1\) at \(z_0\). Then

\[
\sigma(z)
=(z-z_0)^m g(z),
\qquad
g(z_0)\neq0.
\]

Its derivative has leading term

\[
d\sigma
=m(z-z_0)^{m-1}g(z_0)\,dz
+O((z-z_0)^m).
\]

The connection contribution \(A\sigma\) vanishes to order at least \(m\).
Therefore

\[
\operatorname{ord}_{z_0}(D\sigma)=m-1.
\]

But a regular product \(\kappa\sigma\) vanishes to order at least \(m\). Hence

\[
D\sigma=\kappa\sigma
\]

is impossible near any zero of a nontrivial section.

## Converse

If \(\sigma\) is zero-free on \(\Omega\), then

\[
\kappa=\frac{D\sigma}{\sigma}
\]

is holomorphic and supplies a homogeneous equation.

Thus, for \(\sigma\not\equiv0\), zero-freeness is equivalent to

\[
D\sigma\in(\sigma),
\]

where \((\sigma)\) is the principal ideal generated by \(\sigma\).

The forward implication is algebraic division by a unit. The reverse
implication is the zero-order obstruction above.

## Why source derivation matters

The equivalence does not make homogeneous closure useless. It makes its
authority requirement exact.

If source constructors independently produce a regular \(\kappa\) and prove

\[
D\sigma=\kappa\sigma,
\]

then zero-freeness follows by uniqueness of the first-order initial-value
problem. This would be a genuine explanation.

If \(\kappa\) is defined as \(D\sigma/\sigma\), regularity of \(\kappa\) is
already equivalent to the desired zero-freeness. That route is circular.

## Kernel inclusion is not enough

A pointwise test might require only

\[
\sigma(z_0)=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
D\sigma(z_0)=0.
\]

Even this fails for ordinary derivatives of nontrivial one-variable zeros. In
higher-rank or discretized systems, however, both sides can vanish at a point
while regular divisibility still fails.

The elementary jet hostile is

\[
\sigma(z)=z^2,
\qquad
\eta(z)=z.
\]

At \(z=0\), both \(\sigma\) and \(\eta\) vanish, so the pointwise kernel test
passes. But

\[
\eta\notin(\sigma)
\]

because the vanishing order of \(\eta\) is smaller.

Therefore a compiler must test jet-order domination or ideal membership, not
only equality of zero fibers.

## Operator-valued correction

At finite cutoff, let

\[
s_X=P_Xb_X,
\qquad
\eta_X=P_XdP_X(I-P_X)b_X.
\]

The proposed factorization

\[
\eta_X=K_Xs_X
\]

requires more than

\[
\ker s_X\subseteq\ker\eta_X.
\]

As the spectral parameter varies, the germ of \(\eta_X\) must lie in the
module generated by the germ of \(s_X\). For a line output this is ordinary
ideal membership. For several output components it is a syzygy or module-
membership problem.

Uniform completion additionally requires the multipliers \(K_X\) to remain
regular and controlled across cutoffs.

## Relation to the RH boundary

The completed scalar theta section is holomorphic. Consequently any regular
homogeneous scalar transport law for it is already equivalent to its
zero-freeness on the chosen domain unless the coefficient law is derived
independently before the completed scalar section is formed.

The source-local opportunity remains at the operator level: primitive, square,
seam, and archimedean incidence might construct \(K_X\) directly and prove the
factorization before projection. But the existence of some \(K_X\) cannot be
advertised as progress without that construction.

## Finite falsifiers

Two minimal hostile families are required:

1. Transverse zero:

   \[
   \sigma(z)=z,
   \qquad
   D\sigma=1.
   \]

   Leakage is nonzero at the zero fiber.

2. False kernel success:

   \[
   \sigma(z)=z^2,
   \qquad
   \eta(z)=z.
   \]

   Both vanish at the origin, but no regular multiplier exists.

Any homogeneous-closure compiler that accepts the second family is checking
only pointwise kernels and is unsound.

## Decisive conclusion

Regular homogeneous closure is the correct form of a source-derived
zero-exclusion law, but as a bare existence statement it is exactly equivalent
to zero-freeness. The research burden is not to posit a multiplier. It is to
construct that multiplier from labelled boundary incidence without dividing by
the scalar section.

The next theta calculation must therefore output an explicit finite-cutoff
operator \(K_X\), its source provenance, an exact factorization, and uniform
completion control. Anything less returns to the RH-equivalent scalar problem.
