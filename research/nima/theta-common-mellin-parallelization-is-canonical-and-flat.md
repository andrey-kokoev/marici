# The common Mellin parallelization is canonical and flat

## Status

Exact connection theorem on the labelled arithmetic module. There is a single
natural moving frame that cancels every Mellin phase at once: the connection
generated by logarithmic label. It is source-derived and compatible with
multiplicative transport, but it is flat. It therefore carries no
zero-confining curvature.

Nontrivial curvature can arise only after a parameter-dependent compression to
a source-selected boundary or port subspace. Constructing that compression is
the unresolved ordered-incidence problem.

## Label generator

Let \(\mathcal C_{\rm fin}\) be the finite labelled module with basis
\(e_n\), \(n\geq1\). Define

\[
Qe_n=(\log n)e_n.
\]

Mellin translation by spectral height \(t\) is

\[
U_t=e^{-itQ},
\qquad
U_te_n=n^{-it}e_n.
\]

This is one operator on the full label module. Pairwise Fourier rotation rates
are its eigenvalue differences:

\[
\log m-\log n=\log(m/n).
\]

The many pair-dependent rotations are therefore shadows of one common
generator.

## Canonical connection

On parameter-dependent coefficient packets \(c(t)\), define

\[
\nabla_t=\partial_t+iQ.
\]

Then every transported packet

\[
c(t)=U_tc_0
\]

is horizontal:

\[
\nabla_tc(t)=0.
\]

The parallel transport from \(t_0\) to \(t_1\) is exactly

\[
U_{t_1-t_0}=e^{-i(t_1-t_0)Q}.
\]

Thus the common moving frame demanded by the fixed-cone no-go is already
present. It is Mellin covariance itself.

## Multiplicative source compatibility

Let \(T_k\) transport labels by multiplication:

\[
T_ke_n=e_{kn}.
\]

Then

\[
QT_k-T_kQ=(\log k)T_k.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
U_tT_kU_t^{-1}=k^{-it}T_k.
\]

This is the exact source cocycle for multiplication. The connection does not
erase arithmetic typing: it records the logarithmic degree of every
multiplicative constructor.

## Two-parameter completion coordinate

Introduce a normal parameter \(a\) and write

\[
U_{a,t}=e^{-(a+it)Q}.
\]

The natural connection components are

\[
\nabla_a=\partial_a+Q,
\qquad
\nabla_t=\partial_t+iQ.
\]

Their curvature is

\[
[\nabla_a,\nabla_t]=0.
\]

The full Mellin connection is flat because both directions are generated by
the same operator \(Q\).

Reciprocal reflection changes the sign of the normal coordinate around the
critical seam, but it does not create curvature in the uncompressed label
bundle.

## Uniqueness under diagonal label naturality

Suppose a common connection has the diagonal form

\[
D_t=\partial_t+iB
\]

and makes every Mellin character \(n^{-it}e_n\) horizontal. Then

\[
Be_n=(\log n)e_n
\]

for every \(n\). Hence

\[
B=Q
\]

on \(\mathcal C_{\rm fin}\).

There is no alternative diagonal source-natural parallelization carrying an
extra orientation term.

## Where curvature can enter

Let \(P(a,t)\) project onto an admissible boundary, seam, or ordered-port
subspace. The compressed connection is

\[
\nabla^P=P\nabla P.
\]

Even when \(\nabla\) is flat, its compressed curvature can contain

\[
P[dP,dP]P.
\]

This is the familiar mechanism by which a moving subspace acquires Berry-type
curvature inside a flat ambient bundle.

But \(P\) must be constructed from source incidence. Choosing it from the
zero set, from a desired sign, or from a successful Pluecker chart is circular.

For the theta/Tate programme, the missing data are precisely the incidence
maps from the labelled valuation/Fock module into:

- the endpoint port;
- the retained seam;
- the primitive and square boundary currents;
- the archimedean completion channel.

Without those maps there is no authorized projection \(P\), hence no
source-derived compressed curvature to calculate.

## Relation to time

The same operator \(Q\) has two readings:

\[
Qe_n=(\log n)e_n
\]

is arithmetic scale, while

\[
U_t=e^{-itQ}
\]

is spectral-time evolution. This makes precise the earlier speculation that
time appears when multiplicative structure is represented as additive flow.

That insight explains the genesis of the common connection. It does not orient
the completed readout because the resulting ambient evolution is flat.

## Finite falsifier for curvature claims

At any finite cutoff, represent \(Q\) by the diagonal matrix

\[
Q_N=\operatorname{diag}(\log1,\ldots,\log N).
\]

Then

\[
[Q_N,iQ_N]=0.
\]

Any proposed nonzero ambient Mellin curvature at finite cutoff contradicts this
exact matrix identity. Nonzero curvature must identify the parameter-dependent
compression or boundary incidence that produces it.
