# RH needs fixed-point reflection, not equivariant germ faithfulness

## Categorical correction

Let \(B\) be the spectral base with seam involution \(I\), let \(L\) be the
completed determinant or readout line, and let

\[
\sigma:B\longrightarrow L
\]

be the source-derived completed section.

Reciprocal sewing proves that \(\sigma\) and its full boundary germ are
equivariant and faithful. This transports every zero to its reciprocal mate. It
does not force the zero to be fixed.

RH requires the stronger statement that the zero equalizer factors through the
fixed-point equalizer:

\[
\operatorname{Eq}(\sigma,0)
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Eq}(\operatorname{id}_B,I)
\longrightarrow B.
\]

Thus the missing property is fixed-point reflection.

## Strong source certificate

Let \(\delta_I\) denote horizontal displacement from the fixed seam. A strong
constructor-level certificate would be a source-derived factorization

\[
\delta_I=H\circ\sigma
\]

or its correctly typed multi-channel analogue.

Then \(\sigma=0\) implies \(\delta_I=0\), hence the base point lies on the
seam.

This factorization must be constructed without dividing by \(\sigma\). A
definition such as \(H=\delta_I/\sigma\) is circular and singular at the
points being studied.

## Algebraic version

If the section has several real coordinates, fixed-point reflection asks that
the horizontal-displacement ideal lie in the radical of the source-section
ideal. A stronger finite certificate is direct ideal membership through an
explicit source syzygy.

This connects the RH lane to Strominger's Fitting-stratum work:

- rank equality detects whether one current lies in an existing image;
- a maximal minor gives a finite membership certificate;
- here the required membership is displacement in the source-section ideal;
- hostile off-seam zeros are precisely points where that membership fails.

## Hostile model

On a real line with involution \(x\mapsto-x\), the odd section

\[
\sigma(x)=x
\]

reflects fixed points and gives the direct factorization \(x=\sigma(x)\).

The equivariant even section

\[
\sigma(x)=x^2-1
\]

has zeros at \(x=\pm1\), away from the fixed point. The displacement \(x\)
does not lie in the ideal generated by \(x^2-1\). Equivariance and complete
germ knowledge do not repair this.

## Next source test

Construct the complete theta/Tate section ideal before scalar compression and
ask whether horizontal displacement admits:

1. an exact source-derived syzygy;
2. a Fitting-minor membership certificate;
3. a positive-real transfer identity whose real part supplies the same
   factorization;
4. or only an RH-equivalent division by the completed section.

The finite falsifier is one source-local specialization where the section
coordinates vanish but the displacement coordinate does not.
