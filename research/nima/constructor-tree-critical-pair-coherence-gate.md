# Constructor-tree critical pairs and the coherence gate

## Problem

Typed, source-authorized constructor trees can admit several authorized
reductions or refactorizations. Equal result bytes do not imply that the
resulting authority proofs are interchangeable. The compiler needs a finite
test for factorization independence.

Orient admitted comparison cells as typed rewrite rules on constructor
trees. A rule may fire only when its signature, support, resource, temporal,
and fault predicates hold. A critical pair is a tree \(t\) with two
simultaneously admitted one-step rewrites

\[
t\to t_L,\qquad t\to t_R.
\]

The pair is filled when there are admitted rewrite paths

\[
t_L\to^*n,\qquad t_R\to^*n
\]

with compatible accumulated support semantics.

## Finite theorem

For a terminating, finitely presented typed constructor rewrite system,
factorization independence at the level of constructor-tree equality follows
from local confluence on every defined critical overlap. This is the typed,
partial form of the terminating rewrite diamond criterion.

Termination plus filled critical pairs yields a unique normal form for each
well-typed constructor tree. If a critical diamond is unfilled, its source
tree and the two irreconcilable branches are a finite falsifier.

This theorem concerns equality generated by the admitted one-dimensional
rewrites. If the system treats comparison cells themselves as operative
data, higher coherence laws between fillings remain additional obligations;
the diamond test must not be advertised as automatically proving all higher
categorical coherence.

## Smallest falsifier

Let one well-typed tree \(T\) admit

\[
T\xrightarrow{r_L}L,\qquad T\xrightarrow{r_R}R.
\]

Suppose \(L\to N\) is admitted but no rule or coherence cell joins \(R\) to
\(N\). Even if the evaluator assigns the same output bytes to \(L,R,N\),
the rewrite system has two normal forms \(N\) and \(R\). The compiler must
return

\[
\texttt{constructor\_tree\_coherence\_failure}
\]

at \(T\), naming \(r_L,r_R\) and the missing join.

Adding the authorized cell \(R\to N\) fills the diamond and restores a
unique normal form.

## Compiler algorithm

For each well-typed overlap:

1. enumerate the two immediate authorized branches;
2. normalize both under a terminating rule order;
3. compare normal forms together with accumulated support contracts,
   resource equations, epoch ranges, and fault domains;
4. accept only if they agree through an admitted comparison cell;
5. otherwise report the first source tree, branch rules, divergent normal
   forms, and missing cell.

The comparison must be provenance-sensitive. A byte-level equality oracle is
not a coherence oracle.

## Cross-sector readings

- **Strominger.** This is the finite coherence pass for the DPC
  constructor-tree compiler. Locally valid nodes do not authorize flattening
  when two compilation trees lack a filled authority diamond.
- **Benincasa.** Rank ten for the integrated period jet is evidence before
  quotient coherence. The three exact source relations must be transported
  into one common frame; a certified rank-seven minor then tests the
  quotient carrier. Comparing ranks across incompatible frames would be an
  unfilled interface diamond.
- **Kitaev.** The three-\(T\) controlled-\(S\) reduction is a valid local
  rewrite, but the cubic mod-eight obstruction shows that it does not close
  the controlled Wilson branches C/F/G/H in the ancilla-free phase-polynomial
  system. Those branches are explicit nonjoinability witnesses, not evidence
  for a universal \(T\)-factory reduction.
- **Arithmetic/RH.** Euler transport, endpoint augmentation, gamma
  completion, and scalar projection can be compared only through explicit
  coherence cells. The canonical equality recovering the Weil/Pick kernel
  fills a provenance diamond but supplies no positive orientation cell for
  \(C_Y\).

## Durable boundary

> A finite constructor compiler may identify alternative trees only after
> their typed critical pairs are joined by source-authorized coherence.
> Termination and local joinability give unique normal forms; matching output
> values do not.
