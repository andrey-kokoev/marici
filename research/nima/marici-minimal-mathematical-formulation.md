# Marici — Minimal Mathematical Formulation

## 1. Source

Let

\[
\Sigma
\]

be an involutive typed higher-cell signature.

It generates admissible cell shapes

\[
D_c
\]

with boundaries

\[
\partial D_c
\]

and orientation reversal

\[
(-)^{op},
\qquad
(x^{op})^{op}=x.
\]

Let

\[
X
\]

denote the higher structure retained so far.

---

## 2. Boundary and filler

An admissible boundary of shape \(c\) is

\[
\beta:\partial D_c\to X.
\]

Its filler type is

\[
\operatorname{Fill}_c(\beta)
:=
\operatorname{fib}_{\beta}
\left(
\operatorname{Map}(D_c,X)
\to
\operatorname{Map}(\partial D_c,X)
\right).
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\operatorname{Fill}_c(\beta)
=
\sum_{p:D_c\to X}
\left(
p|_{\partial D_c}=\beta
\right).
\]

A proof is an inhabitant

\[
p:\operatorname{Fill}_c(\beta).
\]

Thus

\[
\text{question}=\beta,
\]

\[
\text{answer type}=\operatorname{Fill}_c(\beta),
\]

\[
\text{proof}=p:\operatorname{Fill}_c(\beta).
\]

---

## 3. Recursive coherence

Let

\[
p,q:\operatorname{Fill}(K)
\]

be two admissible fillers of the same boundary \(K\).

Glue them with opposite orientation:

\[
\delta(p,q)
:=
p\cup_Kq^{op}.
\]

This is a closed higher boundary.

Define

\[
\operatorname{Coh}(p,q)
:=
\operatorname{Fill}\bigl(\delta(p,q)\bigr).
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\operatorname{Coh}(p,q)
\simeq
\operatorname{Id}_{\operatorname{Fill}(K)}(p,q).
\]

Hence

\[
p,q:A
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{Id}_A(p,q).
\]

An inhabitant

\[
h:\operatorname{Coh}(p,q)
\]

is retained as a new higher object.

The same construction then applies to

\[
h_1,h_2:\operatorname{Coh}(p,q).
\]

Therefore

\[
K
\mapsto
\operatorname{Fill}(K)
\mapsto
\operatorname{Id}
\mapsto
\operatorname{Id}
\mapsto\cdots
\]

is the native recursion.

---

## 4. Objectification

A proof becomes retained structure by cell attachment.

For

\[
\beta:\partial D_c\to X,
\]

define

\[
X[\beta]
:=
X\coprod_{\partial D_c}D_c.
\]

The pushout

\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
\partial D_c &\longrightarrow&D_c\\
\downarrow\beta&&\downarrow\\
X&\longrightarrow&X[\beta]
\end{array}
\]

objectifies the proof.

Its universal property is

\[
\operatorname{Map}_{X/}(X[\beta],Y)
\simeq
\operatorname{Fill}^{Y}_c(\beta).
\]

Thus proof and objectification are distinct:

\[
p:\operatorname{Fill}(\beta)
\]

is a proof,

while

\[
X[\beta]
\]

is the structure obtained by retaining such a proof.

Recursive objectification gives

\[
X_0\to X_1\to X_2\to\cdots
\]

and

\[
X_\infty
=
\operatorname*{colim}_nX_n.
\]

---

## 5. Shared kernel

For two local constructions with supports

\[
L_1\to X,
\qquad
L_2\to X,
\]

their shared kernel is

\[
K
=
L_1\times_XL_2.
\]

Their union is

\[
U
=
L_1\coprod_KL_2.
\]

For a minimal overlap,

\[
U\simeq X_{\mathrm{local}}.
\]

The kernel determines the compatibility boundary generated by the two extensions.

It does not by itself determine the filler.

Thus

\[
K
\longmapsto
\delta
\longmapsto
\operatorname{Fill}(\delta).
\]

If

\[
\operatorname{Fill}(\delta)=\varnothing,
\]

the two realizations are incompatible.

If

\[
\operatorname{Fill}(\delta)\simeq *,
\]

their higher compatibility is forced.

If

\[
\operatorname{Fill}(\delta)
\]

is noncontractible, independent higher coherence remains.

Critical-branching machinery is a minimal presentation method for finding which such comparisons require explicit generators. It is not primitive Marici structure.

---

## 6. Additive realization

After linearization,

\[
\partial p=\partial q=K.
\]

Define

\[
\delta=p-q.
\]

Then

\[
\partial\delta=0.
\]

A higher proof \(h\) satisfies

\[
\partial h=\delta.
\]

Therefore

\[
\partial h=p-q.
\]

The ambiguity between fillers is measured by homology:

\[
H_n
=
\frac{\ker\partial_n}
{\operatorname{im}\partial_{n+1}}.
\]

The comparison is fillable exactly when

\[
[\delta]=0.
\]

---

## 7. Stable obstruction realization

Define

\[
Z_n
:=
\operatorname{fib}(\partial_n).
\]

With the specified null-homotopy of

\[
\partial_n\partial_{n+1}\simeq0
\]

provided by the coherent chain realization, there is an induced factorization

\[
C_{n+1}
\xrightarrow{\widetilde{\partial}_{n+1}}
Z_n.
\]

Define

\[
O_n
:=
\operatorname{cofib}
\left(
C_{n+1}
\xrightarrow{\widetilde{\partial}_{n+1}}
Z_n
\right).
\]

This gives the canonical obstruction map

\[
q_n:Z_n\to O_n.
\]

### Stable realization theorem

Let \(\mathcal C\) be a stable \(\infty\)-category. By construction,

\[
C_{n+1}\xrightarrow{\widetilde\partial}Z_n
\xrightarrow{q_n}O_n
\]

is a cofiber sequence. Stability makes it a fiber sequence as well:

\[
C_{n+1}\simeq\operatorname{fib}(q_n).
\]

Fix a test object \(\mathbb 1\) (the unit when a monoidal structure is supplied) and a closed comparison

\[
\delta:\mathbb 1\to Z_n.
\]

Define its realized filler space as the lift space

\[
\operatorname{Fill}_{\mathcal C}(\delta)
:=\operatorname{fib}_{\delta}
\left(\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal C}(\mathbb 1,C_{n+1})
\to\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal C}(\mathbb 1,Z_n)\right).
\]

Mapping out of \(\mathbb 1\) preserves limits. Consequently the fiber-sequence identification gives a canonical equivalence

\[
\operatorname{Fill}_{\mathcal C}(\delta)
\simeq
\operatorname{Path}_{\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal C}(\mathbb 1,O_n)}
(q_n\circ\delta,0).
\]

Therefore

\[
\operatorname{Fill}_{\mathcal C}(\delta)\neq\varnothing
\iff q_n\circ\delta\simeq0.
\]

This is a theorem of stability, not an additional Marici axiom. The obstruction map is derived from the realized filler structure; it is not primitive. Identifying this lift space with a native coherence type requires the realization comparison in Section 11.

---

## 8. Positive realization

Let the stable realization carry a dagger

\[
(-)^\dagger
\]

with

\[
(x^\dagger)^\dagger=x.
\]

The positive defect of a comparison is

\[
d(\delta)
:=
q_n(\delta)^\dagger q_n(\delta).
\]

If the positive pairing is definite,

\[
x^\dagger x=0
\iff
x\simeq0,
\]

then

\[
d(\delta)=0
\iff
q_n(\delta)\simeq0
\iff
\operatorname{Fill}(\delta)\neq\varnothing.
\]

Thus positivity is generated by reversal and pairing:

\[
q_n(\delta)
\longmapsto
q_n(\delta)^\dagger q_n(\delta).
\]

Here \(q_n(\delta)\) means the map \(q_n\circ\delta:\mathbb 1\to O_n\), and its dagger square lies in \(\operatorname{End}(\mathbb 1)\). Definiteness is assumed for these maps out of \(\mathbb 1\); equality to zero is interpreted in the specified dagger realization (homotopy classes when appropriate).

Combining definiteness with the stable realization theorem yields

\[
\operatorname{Fill}_{\mathcal C}(\delta)\neq\varnothing
\iff q_n\circ\delta\simeq0
\iff(q_n\circ\delta)^\dagger(q_n\circ\delta)=0.
\]

Once the cofiber/lift presentation is supplied, the equivalence requires stability and dagger definiteness, not a separate positivity-closure theorem. Neither a definite dagger nor a suitable pairing follows from stability or orientation reversal alone.

No independent positive-closure predicate is required.

The raw comparison need not vanish:

\[
\delta\neq0
\]

may coexist with

\[
q_n(\delta)=0.
\]

This means distinct lower realizations can be connected by a higher proof.

---

## 9. Spectral realization

In a finite-dimensional chain realization with a positive-definite inner product, define

\[
\Delta_n
=
\partial_n^\dagger\partial_n
+
\partial_{n+1}\partial_{n+1}^\dagger.
\]

Then

\[
\ker\Delta_n
\simeq
H_n.
\]

Therefore unresolved coherence in this linear realization appears as harmonic zero modes.

### 9.1. Concrete cellular linearization

For an oriented cellular presentation with compatible incidence boundaries, form real cellular chains. Choose one orientation of each generating cell as a basis; the opposite orientation is its negative, not a second independent basis vector:

\[
R_\Sigma(x^{op})=-R_\Sigma(x),\qquad
R_\Sigma(\partial x)=\partial R_\Sigma(x),\qquad
\partial^2=0.
\]

These equations require an oriented cellular realization of the typed signature; an arbitrary involutive signature alone does not supply incidence coefficients or prove that its boundary squares to zero. At finite stages with finitely many cells in each relevant degree, the resulting complexes lie in \(\mathsf{Ch}_{\mathrm{fd}}(\mathbb R)\). Write the resulting linearization schematically as

\[
R_\Sigma:\mathsf{Marici}(\Sigma)\longrightarrow
\mathsf{Ch}(\mathbb R).
\]

Finite-dimensional Hodge theory applies to the finite-dimensional stages, or to a realization proved finite-dimensional in each relevant degree. An unrestricted recursive colimit need not remain in that finite-dimensional class.

For two realized fillers of the same boundary,

\[
p,q:\operatorname{Fill}(K),\qquad
\delta=R_\Sigma(p)-R_\Sigma(q),
\]

boundary compatibility implies

\[
\partial\delta=0.
\]

Oriented gluing is sent to the signed chain comparison:

\[
R_\Sigma(p\cup_Kq^{op})=R_\Sigma(p)-R_\Sigma(q).
\]

Thus this cellular linearization preserves the central gluing operation at the chain level.

### 9.2. Linear fillers and recursive attachment

The concrete linear filler set is

\[
\operatorname{Fill}_{\mathrm{lin}}(\delta)
:=\{h\in C_{n+1}\mid\partial h=\delta\}.
\]

If a solution \(h_0\) exists, then

\[
\operatorname{Fill}_{\mathrm{lin}}(\delta)
=h_0+\ker\partial_{n+1}.
\]

This is an affine solution space. It is the linear shadow of the filler problem, not by itself an equivalence with the native higher filler type. Real linear combinations can solve an equation without corresponding to an admissible native cell.

Given a closed comparison \(\delta\in C_n\), attachment of one new \((n+1)\)-cell has the explicit chain realization

\[
C'_{n+1}=C_{n+1}\oplus\mathbb R e_\delta,
\qquad \partial'e_\delta=\delta.
\]

All other graded components are unchanged; old differentials are retained, with the inclusion into the enlarged component where needed. Since \(\partial\delta=0\), this defines a chain complex. For a compatible cellular pushout,

\[
X'=X\coprod_{\partial D^{n+1}}D^{n+1},
\]

this is its cellular-chain attachment. The new generator is retained and can enter subsequent comparisons:

\[
p,q\longmapsto\delta=p-q
\longmapsto(\partial'e_\delta=\delta)
\longmapsto e_\delta\text{ retained}.
\]

Attachment freely adds a filler in the enlarged structure. It does not prove that a filler existed in the old structure. This distinguishes adjoining a proof generator from exhibiting an already available proof.

### 9.3. Hodge obstruction and positive defect

Choose positive-definite inner products in each degree of a finite-dimensional chain realization. The adjoints determine the Laplacian above and the orthogonal Hodge decomposition

\[
C_n=\operatorname{im}\partial_{n+1}
\oplus\ker\Delta_n
\oplus\operatorname{im}\partial_n^\dagger.
\]

For a cycle \(\delta\), the last component vanishes. Therefore

\[
\delta=\partial h+\delta_H,\qquad
\delta_H=\Pi_H\delta\in\ker\Delta_n,
\]

where \(\Pi_H\) is orthogonal harmonic projection. The restriction

\[
q_H=\Pi_H|_{Z_n}:Z_n\to\ker\Delta_n
\]

represents the homology quotient \(Z_n\to H_n\) via the Hodge isomorphism. Harmonic representatives and their norms depend on the inner products; vanishing of the class does not.

Define

\[
d(\delta)=\langle\Pi_H\delta,\Pi_H\delta\rangle
=\|\Pi_H\delta\|^2\ge0.
\]

Positive definiteness and Hodge decomposition give the exact equivalences

\[
\operatorname{Fill}_{\mathrm{lin}}(\delta)\neq\varnothing
\iff[\delta]=0
\iff\Pi_H\delta=0
\iff\|\Pi_H\delta\|^2=0.
\]

The complete finite-dimensional linear sequence is therefore

\[
p,q\longmapsto\delta=p-q\in Z_n
\longmapsto[\delta]\in H_n
\longmapsto\Pi_H\delta\in\ker\Delta_n
\longmapsto d(\delta)\ge0.
\]

No further positivity-closure assumption is needed for this linear statement. Identifying it with existence of a native Marici proof still requires the filler comparison or reflection theorem of Section 11.

This chain-level result should not be conflated with constructing a stable definite dagger realization. Inner products give adjoints of graded linear maps; the adjoint of a chain map need not itself be a chain map for the original differentials. Moreover, the ordinary homology quotient represented by \(q_H\) is not automatically the full stable cofiber obstruction object of Section 7. A comparison with that construction must specify the stable enhancement and its lift spaces.

### 9.4. Two attachment examples

For the \(4+1\) DG coherence datum, with degree-zero closed maps \(d:A\to B\) and \(r:B\to A\), assume

\[
\partial u=rd-1_A,\qquad
\partial v=dr-1_B.
\]

Then

\[
\delta=du-vd,\qquad
\partial\delta=d(rd-1_A)-(dr-1_B)d=0.
\]

Adjoining a degree-two generator \(W\) with

\[
\partial W=du-vd
\]

is the corresponding attachment in the underlying chain fragment. Extending the entire DG algebra also requires its products and the Leibniz rule; the single chain summand describes only the chain-level attachment.

For compatibly oriented tetrahedral faces, the alternating boundary is

\[
\delta=\alpha_{123}-\alpha_{023}
+\alpha_{013}-\alpha_{012}.
\]

The face boundaries cancel, so \(\partial\delta=0\). A degree-three generator \(T\) with

\[
\partial T=\delta
\]

is the analogous tetrahedral attachment.

Both instantiate the graded extension

\[
C'=C\oplus\mathbb R e_\delta,\qquad
\partial'e_\delta=\delta.
\]

### 9.5. What attachment does to homology

At the additive level, Marici can recursively adjoin higher generators whose boundaries are selected comparison cycles. Each such attachment makes the selected class a boundary:

\[
H_n(C')\cong H_n(C)/\langle[\delta]\rangle.
\]

If \([\delta]\neq0\), this removes one homology direction in degree \(n\), and hence one harmonic obstruction after choosing inner products on the enlarged complex. If \([\delta]=0\) already, attachment does not remove another degree-\(n\) class; instead it adds a degree-\(n+1\) homology direction, represented by \(e_\delta-h_0\) for any old filler \(h_0\).

Thus attachment converts a selected obstruction into a boundary, while retaining a new generator that can itself support further coherence. It is not a claim that every attachment strictly reduces total homology, or that every native identity type is automatically inhabited.

## 10. Boolean realization

Forget all structure except inhabitation.

For a boundary \(\beta\), retain

\[
\left\|
\operatorname{Fill}(\beta)
\right\|.
\]

The Boolean shadow records only

\[
\operatorname{Fill}(\beta)=\varnothing
\]

or

\[
\operatorname{Fill}(\beta)\neq\varnothing.
\]

Boolean truth is therefore the propositional truncation of higher fillability.

---

## 11. Minimal definition

The native Marici construction is:

\[
\mathsf{Marici}_\Sigma
=
\operatorname{RecIdFill}(\Sigma).
\]

Its generating rule is

\[
p,q:\operatorname{Fill}(K)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{Coh}(p,q)
=
\operatorname{Fill}(p\cup_Kq^{op}).
\]

Equivalently,

\[
p,q:A
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{Id}_A(p,q).
\]

Every inhabitant of the resulting coherence type is retained and becomes available for the same operation at the next dimension. The rule forms the identity **type**; it does not assert that every such type has an inhabitant.

### Recursive completeness

Schematically, retaining the dependent typing, put

\[
\mathsf M_0=\Sigma,
\qquad
\mathsf M_{n+1}=\mathsf M_n\cup
\left\{\operatorname{Id}_A(p,q)\mid
A\text{ is a type in }\mathsf M_n,\ p,q:A\text{ are admissible}\right\},
\]

\[
\mathsf{Marici}(\Sigma)=\operatorname*{colim}_n\mathsf M_n.
\]

Here the signature is understood through its generated typed syntax, including the initial admissible filler types. The stage construction retains inhabitants when supplied or derived, without freely asserting proofs between arbitrary pairs. Appropriate universe/size conventions are implicit in this schematic presentation.

If the rule applies to every admissible pair at every generated level, every finite iterated coherence question occurs at a finite stage: its lower-depth data occur at a common finite stage by induction, and its identity type occurs at the next stage. This finite-depth recursive completeness is built into the construction. It is not a theorem that all questions are fillable, decidable, or obtainable from a finite list of generators.

### Compression is a separate presentation theorem

Replacing all admissible pairs by a smaller critical-branching family would require a comparison of the form

\[
\operatorname{Closure}\bigl(\operatorname{Crit}_\Sigma+
\operatorname{Interchange}\bigr)
\simeq\operatorname{RecIdFill}(\Sigma).
\]

This is a presentation/compression claim, not an obligation for the all-pairs definition. Squier-type theorems can supply appropriate dimensional versions under suitable convergent polygraphic hypotheses; a full recursively coherent equivalence requires its own precise hypotheses and higher-dimensional comparison.

### Remaining model-dependent realization task

Exhibit a concrete realization

\[
R:\mathsf{Marici}(\Sigma)\to\mathcal C
\]

into a stable category with the required definite dagger structure. Verify that its typed gluing and identity comparisons identify native coherence spaces with the realized lift spaces:

\[
\operatorname{Id}_A(p,q)
\simeq\operatorname{Fill}_{\mathcal C}(\delta_R),
\]

where \(\delta_R\) is the realized closed comparison. A functor merely sending native proofs to realized fillers need not reflect fillability; the reverse implication requires this comparison or an appropriate reflection theorem.

With that identification, stability and definiteness give

\[
\operatorname{Id}_A(p,q)\text{ inhabited}
\iff q_n\circ\delta_R\simeq0
\iff(q_n\circ\delta_R)^\dagger(q_n\circ\delta_R)=0.
\]

No unresolved all-pairs closure condition remains in the abstract finite-depth construction. Existence and compatibility of a concrete realization remain model-dependent, as do any proposed compressed presentations.

The derived realizations are

\[
\operatorname{Id}_A(p,q)
\]

in higher type theory,

\[
\partial h=p-q
\]

in the additive realization,

\[
q(\delta)=0
\]

in the stable obstruction realization,

\[
q(\delta)^\dagger q(\delta)=0
\]

in the positive realization,

and

\[
\left\|
\operatorname{Fill}(\delta)
\right\|
\]

in the Boolean realization.

## 12. Final statement

**Marici is the recursively closed higher-type calculus in which alternative admissible fillers of a common boundary generate, by orientation-reversed gluing, a new coherence boundary; fillers of that boundary are retained as higher objects and recursively undergo the same construction.**

Its stable realization converts the coherence boundary into a canonical obstruction.

Its positive realization converts that obstruction into

\[
q(\delta)^\dagger q(\delta).
\]

Therefore

\[
\text{proof exists}
\]

\[
\Longleftrightarrow
\]

\[
\text{canonical obstruction vanishes}
\]

\[
\Longleftrightarrow
\]

\[
\text{positive obstruction square vanishes},
\]

subject to stability and definiteness in the corresponding realizations, and to the native-to-realized filler comparison when “proof exists” refers to a native proof rather than a realized lift.

The primitive content is the typed source

\[
\Sigma
\]

together with orientation reversal and recursive retention.

Boundary, filler, identity, objectification, obstruction, homology, spectral zero modes, positivity, and Boolean truth are derived structures or realizations.
