# The graph-augmented linking determinant isolates the analytic residual Schur complement

## Graph-stable source block

Let \(V_X\) be the finite arithmetic carrier, \(H_X\) the full typed analytic
boundary carrier, and

\[
U_X:V_X\longrightarrow H_X
\]

the weighted synthesis map.

Weighted synthesis is not bounded below at completion. The graph carrier
repairs this by retaining the arithmetic state as an identity channel.

Let \(B_X(s):H_X\to H_X\) be the still-to-be-constructed analytic boundary
operator containing tail, seam, endpoint, connected determinant, Gaussian, and
reciprocal data.

Define the graph-augmented linking operator

\[
\mathbb T_X(s)
=
\begin{pmatrix}
I_{V_X}&U_X^*\\
U_X&B_X(s)
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Both cross arrows are source-derived from synthesis. The arithmetic diagonal
is the provenance identity, not the RH readout.

## Exact Schur reduction

Since the arithmetic block is \(I_{V_X}\),

\[
\det\mathbb T_X(s)
=
\det\!\left(B_X(s)-U_XU_X^*\right).
\]

Define the analytic residual

\[
R_X(s)
=
B_X(s)-U_XU_X^*.
\]

Then

\[
\det\mathbb T_X(s)=\det R_X(s).
\]

The arithmetic identity contributes no nontrivial scalar factor. It stabilizes
the linking geometry while the entire zero locus lies in the residual Schur
complement.

This is the precise repair promised by graph augmentation.

## Kernel correspondence

If

\[
R_X(s)h=0,
\]

then

\[
\mathbb T_X(s)
\binom{-U_X^*h}{h}
=
0.
\]

Conversely, if

\[
\mathbb T_X(s)
\binom{v}{h}=0,
\]

the first block equation gives

\[
v=-U_X^*h,
\]

and the second gives

\[
R_X(s)h=0.
\]

Thus

\[
\ker\mathbb T_X(s)
\cong
\ker R_X(s)
\]

by a canonical graph lift. Singular residual states retain their arithmetic
component rather than disappearing in analytic completion.

## Why \(B_X\) cannot be arbitrary

Choosing

\[
B_X=I+U_XU_X^*
\]

would make \(R_X=I\) and force a vacuous nowhere-zero determinant. Choosing

\[
B_X=U_XU_X^*+f_X(s)I
\]

would install the desired scalar \(f_X\) by hand.

Therefore \(B_X\) must be derived independently from source operations before
forming the Schur complement. Its allowed ingredients are:

- tail-flow and Fourier--Poisson operators;
- moving-seam transport;
- the relative endpoint complex;
- primitive and square anomaly lines;
- the connected \(S_3\) determinant carrier;
- the Gaussian Mellin line;
- reciprocal sector sewing.

The subtraction \(B_X-U_XU_X^*\) is authorized only after both terms act on
the same typed carrier.

## Relation to source matching

The positive operator

\[
U_XU_X^*
\]

is the analytic frame operator of source synthesis. It measures the component
of an analytic state generated by arithmetic incidence.

The residual equation

\[
B_Xh=U_XU_X^*h
\]

says that analytic boundary transport balances the source-generated frame
action. It is not the graph matching equation \(Uv=h\); rather, it is the
Schur feedback law obtained after retaining and eliminating the arithmetic
identity block.

This is the first nontrivial operator equation whose kernel can survive graph
augmentation.

## Completion behavior

Although \(U_X\) is not bounded below, the operators \(U_XU_X^*\) can converge
in compact or Schatten topologies because Euler weighting makes synthesis
smoothing.

Therefore graph augmentation separates two requirements:

- lower control is supplied by the retained identity source channel;
- determinant compactness is supplied by the weighted analytic frame
  operator.

This is compatible rather than contradictory. The same Euler decay that
destroyed inverse synthesis can improve the determinant class of the residual.

The remaining completion theorem must prove that \(R_X(s)\) converges in the
relative determinant topology and that its kernels do not appear only at the
limit.

## Third-order typing

Decompose the source domain orthogonally by prime-power grade and write

\[
U_XU_X^*
=
F_X^{(1)}+F_X^{(2)}+F_X^{(\ge3)}.
\]

There are no cross terms in this frame-operator sum; cross-grade correlations live instead in the source Gram operator \(U_X^*U_X\). Primitive and square frame components require anomaly-line treatment, while the connected component has the stronger ideal class.

The analytic operator \(B_X\) must carry the same grading. Subtracting an
untyped scalar frame from a typed boundary operator would erase the
third-order normalization.

The relative determinant of \(R_X\) must therefore be formed only after
primitive and square counterterms and their mixed anomaly are included.

## Reciprocal doubling

For both half-planes, use residuals

\[
R_{+,X}(s)
=
B_{+,X}(s)-U_{+,X}U_{+,X}^*
\]

and

\[
R_{-,X}(s)
=
B_{-,X}(s)-U_{-,X}U_{-,X}^*.
\]

Source reciprocal transport must compare

\[
R_{-,X}(s)
\longleftrightarrow
R_{+,X}(1-s)^\dagger.
\]

This comparison must occur before taking determinants. Equality of the final
scalar sections is only its shadow.

## Determinant-line target

The finite multiplicative readout is

\[
\Delta_X(s)=\det R_X(s)
\]

or its third-order relative version. The top-exterior border constructed
previously applies directly to \(R_X\).

The completed \(\Xi\) bridge requires an invertible line comparison

\[
\det_{\mathrm{rel}}R(s)
=

\]

This is now the only legitimate scalar identification. The identity arithmetic
block has been eliminated exactly and cannot contaminate the zero locus.

## Observability target

A residual zero produces the canonical graph state

\[
(-U^*h,h).
\]

A source-derived observer family must be jointly faithful on these states. It
is enough to separate \(h\in\ker R(s)\), because the arithmetic component is
then forced.

Hence behavioral monicity may be tested on the residual analytic kernel rather
than on the entire graph carrier. This sharply reduces the observer problem.

## Hostile tests

1. Choosing \(B_X\) to make \(R_X\) invertible is circular.
2. Omitting \(U_X^*\) destroys the Schur residual.
3. Projecting away the arithmetic component before solving the kernel loses

4. Untyped subtraction of primitive, square, and connected blocks invalidates
   the relative determinant.
5. Finite residual invertibility with collapsing minimum singular value permits
   a completion kernel.
6. Scalar reciprocal equality without operator comparison does not orient the
   determinant line.
7. An observer that sees the arithmetic identity but not \(h\) does not prove
   residual monicity.
