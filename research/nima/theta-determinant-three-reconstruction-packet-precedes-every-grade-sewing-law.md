# The determinant-three reconstruction packet precedes every grade-sewing law

## One source operator, three regularity levels

Let \(A\) be the source-derived operator at finite cutoff. The relevant
regularized determinant packet is

\[
\mathfrak D_3(A)
=
\left(
\tau_1(A),\,
\tau_2(A),\,
\delta_3(A)
\right),
\]

where

\[
\tau_1(A)=\operatorname{Tr}A,
\qquad
\tau_2(A)=\frac12\operatorname{Tr}A^2,
\qquad
\delta_3(A)=\det_3(I-A).
\]

When all terms are defined, reconstruction is

\[
\det(I-A)
=
\delta_3(A)
\exp\left(
-\tau_1(A)-\tau_2(A)
\right).
\]

The primitive and square currents are the first two cyclic cumulants of the
same \(A\). The connected tail belongs to its order-three determinant. These
are filtered evaluations of one operator object, not three operators to be
added on a common carrier.

## Consequence for the earlier mixed-grade falsifier

The negative \(k=1,2\) self-commutator minor correctly rejects the operation
that identifies the two cumulant grades as unilateral operators and adds
them. But that operation is not source-native.

Its durable conclusion is a typing theorem:

\[
\text{cumulant decomposition}
\ne
\text{operator direct sum}.
\]

The prime-five minor is therefore a compiler falsifier for an invalid
coercion, not evidence that the actual determinant packet has a negative
boundary form.

## The archimedean action must come first

Let \(\mathcal A_\infty\) denote the source-derived archimedean operation. The
next object to construct is

\[
\mathfrak D_3(A)
\longmapsto
\mathfrak D_3(\mathcal A_\infty A),
\]

or, if the action changes domains or adds a boundary relation, the
corresponding relative packet.

No mixed primitive-square form may be introduced before this action is
specified. The source action may produce:

- a similarity, leaving all three components invariant;
- a block extension, producing a relative determinant;
- an additive perturbation, producing cyclic mixed traces;
- a compression, producing a boundary anomaly;
- an unbounded relation, requiring graph-domain reconstruction.

These cases have different coherence laws.

## Additive perturbation formula

If the source actually gives an additive perturbation

\[
A\longmapsto A+B,
\]

then

\[
\tau_1(A+B)
=
\tau_1(A)+\tau_1(B),
\]

while

\[
\tau_2(A+B)
=
\tau_2(A)+\tau_2(B)+\operatorname{Tr}(AB),
\]

assuming cyclicity is authorized on the common domain.

The term \(\operatorname{Tr}(AB)\) is a genuine source-derived mixed
cumulant. It is not a freely chosen bilinear form between \(\tau_1\) and
\(\tau_2\).

The order-three component must be handled by a relative determinant identity,
not by fitting the scalar remainder.

## Block-extension formula

If the archimedean operation constructs

\[
\widetilde A
=
\begin{pmatrix}
A&C\\
R&E
\end{pmatrix},
\]

then the correct transformation is obtained by Schur elimination on the
declared domains. The first two relative cumulants and the relative
\(\det_3\) must be computed from that block system.

This is the likely form for endpoint, seam, and gamma incidence. But the block
entries must be derived before their determinant packet is evaluated.

## Conditional contraction gate

Only if the derived transformation law produces a mixed boundary form

\[
b_{12}
\]

between completed defect spaces does the contraction condition apply:

\[
|b_{12}(x,y)|^2
\le
q_1(x)q_2(y).
\]

If no such form appears, testing a contraction constant is meaningless and
would manufacture a constructor.

Likewise, closability is tested on the actual relation produced by
\(\mathcal A_\infty\), not on a convenient Hankel–Volterra candidate selected
afterward.

## Finite compiler test

At each cutoff, require the proposed archimedean constructor to declare one
of the admissible action types. Then verify:

1. its source incidence and domain;
2. the induced transformation of \(\tau_1\);
3. the induced transformation of \(\tau_2\), including every cyclic cross
   term;
4. the relative order-three determinant;
5. reconstruction of the full finite determinant;
6. compatibility under prime deletion;
7. reciprocal transformation;
8. whether any mixed boundary form is actually generated.

A proposal that starts directly from corrected scalar cumulants or a desired
positive block form fails derivational order.

## Current target

The finite archimedean action is an independent determinant-line tensor
extension and creates no mixed arithmetic trace. Global Fourier–Poisson sewing
is a separate correspondence on the full restricted adelic source; it has no
source-authorized finite-Euler action.

The immediate theta/Tate problem is therefore to construct the global
restricted-product correspondence carrying

\[
\left(
\operatorname{Tr}A,\,
\frac12\operatorname{Tr}A^2,\,
\det_3(I-A)
\right)
\]

together with the archimedean line. Only boundary forms actually emitted by
that global constructor acquire downstream closability, contractivity, and
completion-stability obligations.

## Disposition

The determinant-three packet preserves both source unity and regularity
typing. It prevents two opposite errors:

- scalarizing the primitive and square currents through \(\log\zeta\);
- treating those cumulants as independent operators and adding them.

The RH lane now waits on one concrete constructor: the source-derived
archimedean action on the full regularized determinant packet.
