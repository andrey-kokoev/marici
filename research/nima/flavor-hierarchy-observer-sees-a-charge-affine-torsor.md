# Flavor Hierarchy Observer Sees a Charge Affine Torsor

## Result

The WP614 hierarchy exponents do not determine the absolute charge vector
\((3,2,0)\). They determine its labelled distance matrix.

Consequently, the early hierarchy observer is unsigned in two senses:

- it is invariant under common translation of every charge;
- it is invariant under reflection of the charge line.

The charge vector therefore lives in an affine orientation torsor above the
observed hierarchy.

## Distance observation

For charges \(q_i\), the hierarchy records

\[
D_{ij}=|q_i-q_j|.
\]

For \(q=(3,2,0)\),

\[
D=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1&3\\
1&0&2\\
3&2&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Every translated vector

\[
q+c(1,1,1)
\]

has the same matrix. Every reflected and translated vector

\[
-q+c(1,1,1)
\]

also has the same matrix.

## Exact quotient census

The checker exhausts all integer triples in a bounded cube. After quotienting
translations by setting the third labelled charge to zero, exactly two classes
remain:

\[
(3,2,0),
\qquad
(-3,-2,0).
\]

If one instead chooses a nonnegative presentation, the two representatives are

\[
(3,2,0),
\qquad
(0,1,3).
\]

Thus even a conventional choice of origin does not derive the charge-line
orientation.

## Later signed observer

An anomaly or representation constraint can depend on signed, absolute
charges rather than only their differences. Such a constraint belongs at a
later stage because it consumes a lift of the affine torsor.

The checker uses the illustrative affine condition

\[
q_1+q_2+q_3=5.
\]

Within the enumerated solution family it selects \((3,2,0)\) uniquely. This is
not asserted to be the physical anomaly equation. It demonstrates the type of
additional source relation required.

Using a fitted condition that was chosen because it selects \((3,2,0)\) would
be circular. The actual signed constraint must follow from independently
specified representations, anomaly cancellation, mediator content, or another
source operation.

## Staged reconciliation

The two observers have different domains:

1. the hierarchy observer acts on the affine quotient and returns \(D\);
2. the signed source observer acts on charge lifts and returns anomaly or
   representation data;
3. terminal reconciliation retains only lifts whose distance matrix and signed
   constraints belong to the same messenger preparation.

The later observer does not modify the earlier distances. It selects or rejects
lifts above them.

## WP614 interpretation

WP614 shows that the distance matrix organizes one-, two-, and three-insertion
messenger paths and the overall order of the Jarlskog invariant. This is a real
constructive gain.

It does not explain:

- the absolute charge origin;
- orientation of the charge line;
- Wilson coefficients;
- the CP phase;
- why the charge lift should be \((3,2,0)\) rather than another affine lift.

The first two are precisely the affine-torsor coordinates isolated here. The
last two require still later preparation structure.

## Finite falsifiers

A proposed derivation of the charge vector from hierarchy powers fails if:

- it assigns different hierarchy data to a common charge translation;
- it distinguishes reflected charge lifts without an independent signed
  source relation;
- its anomaly equation was fitted after inspecting the target charges;
- it joins a signed constraint from one messenger preparation to distances
  measured in another.

## Status

The observer-stage intuition now applies one level earlier than CP. The
hierarchy observer supplies unsigned charge geometry. A later independently
derived anomaly or representation observer must select an affine lift. Only
then can the preparation programme address Wilson coefficients and CP.
