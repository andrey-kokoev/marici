# The Total-Energy Cusp Is a Paired Picard--Lefschetz Problem

For the infinity quartic

\[
F(t)=x^2t^4-(x^2+y^2-z^2)t^2+y^2,
\qquad z=E-x-y,
\]

exact specialization gives

\[
F(t)|_{E=0}=(xt^2+y)^2.
\]

On the generic locus \(xy(x+y)\ne0\), the equation \(xt^2+y=0\) has two
simple roots exchanged by \(t\mapsto-t\). At either root,

\[
\left.\partial_EF\right|_{E=0}=\frac{2y(x+y)}x\ne0.
\]

Thus the width-two elliptic cusp is locally a pair of exchanged ordinary
Picard--Lefschetz nodes with primitive first-order smoothing. This supplies a
geometric explanation for two equal Dehn-twist contributions.

It does **not** yet determine the integral extension class. The remaining
global calculation is to identify the two ambient thimbles in the integral
Picard lattice of the degree-two del Pezzo surface. Their common/difference
root modulo two must then be expressed in the source-supported plane
\((e_6,v_{\rm alg})\). That result selects one of Entry 1152's four classes.

No inference from the rational residue matrix alone is authorized.
