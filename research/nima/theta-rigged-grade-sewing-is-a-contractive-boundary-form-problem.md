# Rigged grade sewing is a contractive boundary-form problem

## Derivational-order correction

This criterion is conditional. The primitive and square terms are cyclic
cumulants of one source operator \(A\), reconstructed together with
\(\det_3\). They are not natively independent operators, and no mixed form
\(b_{12}\) may be presumed.

The source must first define the archimedean action on the full determinant
packet

\[
\left(
\operatorname{Tr}A,\,
\frac12\operatorname{Tr}A^2,\,
\det_3(I-A)
\right).
\]

Only if that action produces a bilinear cross-grade boundary form do the
closability and contraction tests below become applicable.

## Separate positive defect forms

On the right half-strip, each source grade has its own unilateral
self-commutator form. Write

\[
q_1(x)=\langle C_1x,x\rangle
\]

for the primitive defect form on its Mellin-analytic or Laplace rigging, and

\[
q_2(y)=\langle C_2y,y\rangle
\]

for the square defect form on its Hilbert or tempered carrier.

Both forms are nonnegative before the grades are identified.

There is no canonical continuous inclusion from the primitive analytic dual
into the square Hilbert space. Therefore the expression \(T_1+T_2\) on one
Hilbert carrier is not merely nonpositive; it is source-ill-typed.

## Mixed sewing form

A legitimate archimedean or reciprocal sewing must define a sesquilinear
boundary form

\[
b_{12}(x,y)
\]

on a declared dense graph domain. The completed defect form is

\[
q(x,y)
=
q_1(x)+q_2(y)+2\Re b_{12}(x,y).
\]

This form is nonnegative for all admissible pairs precisely when

\[
|b_{12}(x,y)|^2
\le
q_1(x)q_2(y)
\]

after quotienting the null spaces and completing the two form domains.

This is the exact grade-wise Cauchy–Schwarz gate.

## Contractive factorization

Let \(\mathcal K_1\) and \(\mathcal K_2\) be the Hilbert completions generated
by \(q_1\) and \(q_2\). The mixed form is positive exactly when there is a
contraction

\[
W:\mathcal K_2\longrightarrow\mathcal K_1,
\qquad
\|W\|\le1,
\]

such that

\[
b_{12}(x,y)
=
\langle C_1^{1/2}x,\,
W C_2^{1/2}y\rangle.
\]

At finite cutoff this is the standard positive block-matrix criterion

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
C_1&K\\
K^*&C_2
\end{pmatrix}
\ge0.
\]

The infinite problem is harder only because the primitive and square form
domains have different topologies and the sewing may be a relation rather
than an everywhere-defined operator.

## Interpretation of the prime-five failure

The forbidden same-carrier addition induces a mixed form whose normalized
coupling exceeds one for prime \(5\) near the seam. Its negative determinant
is exactly the finite statement

\[
\|W_{\mathrm{naive}}\|>1.
\]

Across all sufficiently large primes in \(0<\Re z<1/6\), the violation occurs
in infinitely many independent directions.

Thus the finite falsifier already measures the required repair: the source
sewing must replace the naive identification by a different contraction on
the completed defect spaces.

## Closability gate

Before asking whether \(W\) is contractive, the source relation must be
closable. If \(\Gamma_0\) denotes the proposed primitive-to-square or
primitive-to-seam incidence, its adjoint domain must be dense.

For the existing Hankel–Volterra candidate, the known condition is

\[
\overline{
\{y:H^*y\in\operatorname{Ran}G\}
}
=
Y.
\]

Failure of this density condition means no completed mixed form exists.
Passing finite cutoff tests cannot repair nonclosability.

## Source authority gate

Even a perfect contraction can be fitted after seeing the negative part.
That is rejected. The map or relation must be derived from:

- theta or Poisson transport;
- the archimedean endpoint incidence;
- reciprocal reflection;
- the labelled primitive and square vacuum cocycles;
- a declared graph boundary identity.

The source derivation must precede evaluation of the mixed commutator.

## Exact graph boundary identity

The target should have the form

\[
q_{\mathrm{completed}}
=
q_1\oplus q_2
+
\partial_{\Gamma}J,
\]

where the boundary term generated by the closable incidence relation produces
\(b_{12}\). The calculation must show, rather than assume, that the normalized
boundary coupling has norm at most one.

On the opposite half-strip, reciprocal reflection must reverse the signed
defect law while preserving the contraction bound.

## Finite falsifiers

At each cutoff:

1. compute the positive forms \(C_{1,X}\) and \(C_{2,X}\) separately;
2. derive the mixed matrix \(K_X\) from the proposed incidence;
3. check the two kernel inclusions required for block positivity;
4. compute the sharp normalized coupling norm;
5. reject if that norm exceeds one;
6. test for a cutoff-independent bound;
7. delete the incidence and recover the prime-five negative minor.

The first cutoff where the norm exceeds one closes the proposed sewing law.

## Disposition

The orientation problem has become a precise operator-extension question.
The individual source grades already carry the correct signed index. Their
completion is positive exactly when the source supplies a closable,
reciprocal-compatible, contractive boundary relation between their defect
spaces.

This criterion is independent of the scalar zeta divisor. It is therefore a
legitimate RH-bearing gate rather than a reformulation of completed scalar
positivity.
