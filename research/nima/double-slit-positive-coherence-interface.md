# Double-slit visibility from a positive coherence interface

## Frozen two-path interface

Take two alternatives \(L,R\) and their normalized record states
\(e_L,e_R\) in one source-generated positive pairing.  The only relative
datum is

\[
\gamma=\langle e_R,e_L\rangle.
\]

Their Gram object is

\[
G=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&\gamma\\
\bar\gamma&1
\end{pmatrix}
\succeq0,
\qquad
\det G=1-|\gamma|^2\ge0.
\]

No path probability or complementarity inequality has yet been assumed.

## Interference readout

For balanced alternatives with controlled relative phase \(\theta\), the
normalized screen intensity is

\[
I(\theta)=1+\operatorname{Re}(e^{i\theta}\gamma).
\]

Therefore

\[
I_{\max}=1+|\gamma|,
\qquad
I_{\min}=1-|\gamma|,
\]

and fringe visibility is

\[
V=\frac{I_{\max}-I_{\min}}{I_{\max}+I_{\min}}=|\gamma|.
\]

## Which-path readout

The same positive pairing realizes the two records canonically in their
two-dimensional span.  Their rank-one record projectors differ by a traceless
operator whose determinant is

\[
-(1-|\gamma|^2).
\]

Its eigenvalues are therefore

\[
\pm\sqrt{1-|\gamma|^2}.
\]

For the authorized optimal binary separator, the normalized path
distinguishability is

\[
D=\sqrt{1-|\gamma|^2}.
\]

Hence the pure-record interface forces

\[
\boxed{V^2+D^2=1.}
\]

The two quantities are not independent policies.  They are respectively the
off-diagonal overlap and determinant reserve of the same positive Gram object.

## What has and has not been derived

The equality follows from four declared ingredients:

1. two normalized alternatives;
2. one conjugate-positive coherence pairing;
3. balanced phase recombination for the interference readout;
4. the optimal positive binary separator for the path readout.

Thus the calculation derives complementarity from a source-indexed interface
once those operations are admitted.  It does not yet derive those operations
from the bare Carrier, nor does it derive the Born frequency rule.

For mixed records, unequal priors, loss, or unobserved environmental degrees
of freedom, the equality becomes

\[
V^2+D^2\le1
\]

only after proving that the admitted coarse-graining is a positive contraction
for both the coherence and discrimination probes.  Importing that monotonicity
from standard quantum information would reproduce the theorem but would not
complete the Marici explanation.

### Source-labelled mixtures

One nontrivial coarse-graining class already closes without the full operator
theory.  Suppose an admitted source label \(k\) resolves pure path-record
interfaces with weights \(p_k\) and

\[
(V_k,D_k)=(|\gamma_k|,\sqrt{1-|\gamma_k|^2}).
\]

Forgetting the label gives the operational bounds

\[
V\le\sum_kp_kV_k,
\qquad
D\le\sum_kp_kD_k.
\]

The triangle inequality in \(\mathbb R^2\) then gives

\[
\sqrt{V^2+D^2}
\le
\left\|\sum_kp_k(V_k,D_k)\right\|
\le
\sum_kp_k\sqrt{V_k^2+D_k^2}
=1.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{V^2+D^2\le1}
\]

for every source-labelled convex mixture, provided the two displayed
operational contraction bounds are themselves generated by the forgetting
map.  The checker supplies an exact rational census of this contraction.

This identifies the remaining generality gap precisely: arbitrary quantum
records need not arrive with a common classical source label.  Their theorem
requires a source derivation of the positive contraction/partial-trace
operation, not merely convex mixing after a preferred decomposition.
