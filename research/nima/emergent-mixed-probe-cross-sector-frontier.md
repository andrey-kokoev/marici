# Emergent mixed probes across the current sector frontiers

## Question

For each active sector, do two source probes genuinely meet to construct a new
relational probe, or do we currently possess only a desired coherence shadow?

## Common typing

The cross-sector pattern is

\[
A\xleftarrow{p_A}X,
\qquad
Y\xrightarrow{p_B}B,
\qquad
\mu:A\times B\longrightarrow M,
\qquad
r:M\longrightarrow K.
\]

Here \(\mu\) is the mixed constructor, (M) is the emergent relational probe,
and (K) is a coherencer shadow or readout. The arrow (r) may forget
information. A proposed (K) does not establish either \(\mu\) or (M).

### Control

- Mixed probe: the response matrix and its disturbance-response subspace.
- Coherencer shadow: rank, observability, and common-mode versus differential

- Status: constructed in the finite models. Mere record duplication does not

### Optics

- Incoming probes: source amplitude and loop/reference response.
- Meeting carrier: the interferometer or cavity field.
- Mixed probe: the complex interference amplitude or cavity state.
- Coherencer shadow: relative phase, fringe record, or steady-state scalar.
- Status: constructed but commonly nonfaithful. A cavity scalar sees mirror
  return and internal survival only through their product, and common-loop
  gain amplifies signal and loop-reference error together. Ideal ringdown
  repeats the same product and does not raise factorization rank, although it
  can detect nonexponential model faults. A calibrated mirror-leakage port with
  a source-derived lossless relation, or a separately rooted phase cycle, is
  required to add an independent factorization probe.

Optics therefore demonstrates that a genuine mixed probe may still cast an
insufficient shadow.

## Cross-sector verdicts

The sectors occupy four different states:

1. **Constructed and informative:** flavor's positive mixed pairing; finite
   control response matrices; finite authority constructor models.
2. **Constructed but nonfaithfully projected:** cavity and phase readouts.
3. **Constructed qualitatively but not fault-closed:** dense topological
   control.
4. **Mixed constructor missing:** RH reverse incidence, cross-encoding anyon
   transport, and the all-orders connected-source decoration law.

## Transfer rule

For every frontier, ask in order:

1. What two typed probes enter?
2. Which source operation makes them interact?
3. What new carrier exists only because of that interaction?
4. Which readout of that carrier is called coherence?
5. Is that readout invariant?
6. Is it faithful on the intended quotient?
7. What faults can corrupt either the carrier or the coherence readout?

Failure at an earlier question blocks every later interpretation.

## Disposition

The emergent-probe pattern transfers cleanly, but it is diagnostic rather than
automatically explanatory. It identifies exactly where each sector stands and
prevents a desired coherence equation from being mistaken for the mixed
constructor that would explain it.
