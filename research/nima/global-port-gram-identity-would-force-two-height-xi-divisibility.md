# Global port Gram identity would force two-height Xi divisibility

Let `A` be the complete endpoint-plus-difference port map and let `B` be the complete sewn input/sum port map, both defined on the same source-generated history carrier. The noncircular source theorem to seek is the operator identity

$$
A^*A=B^*B
$$

on the common rapid/graph core, extended by closure.

This identity is independent of Xi zeros. It can be tested on arbitrary source histories and finite shell packets.

For the stable histories, use

$$
u_-(z)-u_+(z)=\tau(z)e_z.
$$

Define the polarized route defect

$$
\mathfrak D(w,z)
=
\langle Au_+(w),Au_+(z)\rangle
-
\langle Bu_-(w),Bu_-(z)\rangle.
$$

If `A*A=B*B=:G`, substitute `u_-=u_++tau e` in both variables. Expansion gives

$$
\mathfrak D(w,z)
=-\tau(z)\langle Gu_+(w),e_z\rangle
-\overline{\tau(w)}\langle Ge_w,u_+(z)\rangle
-\overline{\tau(w)}\tau(z)\langle Ge_w,e_z\rangle,
$$

up to the fixed choice of which stable route is taken as the reference. Hence

$$
\mathfrak D
\in(\tau(z),\overline{\tau(w)}).
$$

At a pair of Xi zeros, the defect vanishes automatically. On the diagonal this combines with the exact Green identity

$$
\mathfrak D(z,z)=2\operatorname{Re}z\,K_{\rm dbl}(z,z)
$$

to force seam confinement for a nonzero state.

Thus the missing two-height Xi divisibility would follow from one source-global Gram equality, rather than being imposed on the Xi divisor. The required finite test is the matrix identity `A_X^*A_X=B_X^*B_X` on each labelled source packet, including wall, reciprocal, linking, and Tate-line coordinates.
