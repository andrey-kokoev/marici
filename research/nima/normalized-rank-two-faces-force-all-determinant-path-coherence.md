# Normalized rank-two faces force all determinant-path coherence

## The apparent higher gate

Three prime additions admit two reduced adjacent-swap words related by the braid relation. It is tempting to introduce an independent three-dimensional determinant anomaly after checking all two-prime squares.

For an ordinary determinant line with a common basepoint trivialization, that extra anomaly does not exist.

## The theorem

Let finite labelled cutoffs form a Boolean lattice, or more generally the ideal lattice of a finite dependency poset. Associate a one-dimensional determinant line to every cutoff and a nonzero increment to every authorized addition arrow. Assume:

1. all increments use one source-authorized basepoint trivialization;
2. logarithmic connection increments agree around every reachable rank-two face;
3. basepoint holonomy is \(1\) around every such face;
4. the source and target domains of each compared path are identical.

Then every rank-two comparison is the identity scalar. Any two legal addition orders are connected by adjacent swaps of incomparable additions. Replacing one adjacent pair at a time leaves the path product unchanged. Therefore every complete path has the same determinant increment.

The braid and distant-commutation relations follow automatically because both sides are composites of identity comparison cells. No separately fitted higher determinant cell is permitted or needed.

## Why this is special to the determinant line

The collapse uses three facts:

- the comparison carrier is one-dimensional;
- a common trivialization turns every comparison into a scalar;
- connection flatness plus basepoint normalization makes that scalar exactly \(1\).

A genuinely higher anomaly can survive only if one of these facts fails. Examples include operator intertwiners, projective implementations without a common frame, domain-changing unbounded relations, or determinant-gerbe data. Such a lift must be typed explicitly; it cannot be inferred from scalar determinant increments.

## Coverage is the actual finite danger

Checking one two-prime square at the empty cutoff is insufficient. Every reachable rank-two face must be checked because an edge anomaly may depend on the retained cutoff.

A three-prime hostile can make all base-level pairings appear valid while inserting a phase only after one label has already been added. The upper face then fails, and two complete orders disagree. This is not a higher cocycle beyond squares. It is an untested square.

## Compiler consequence

The determinant compiler needs:

1. enumeration of every reachable cutoff object;
2. enumeration of every authorized rank-two face;
3. connection-curvature and basepoint-holonomy tests on each face;
4. verification that compared paths have equal domains and authority roots;
5. path enumeration only as a regression check, not as new authority.

If all rank-two faces pass, higher scalar path coherence is a theorem. If path coherence still fails, the carrier was not truly a commonly trivialized determinant line or the face enumeration was incomplete.
