# Unitarity of ray restriction and half-density closes the bivariate history graph bounds

## Unitary transport

Let

\[
g(q)=e^{-\pi q^2},
\qquad
g_t^\pm(q)=g(q\pm t).
\]

Translation is unitary on \(L^2(\mathbb R,dq)\). The two-ray restriction

\[
\mathcal R_2f=(f|_{\mathbb R_+},f(-\cdot)|_{\mathbb R_+})
\]

is unitary onto the doubled positive-ray space, and each half-density map
\(\mathcal M_n\) is unitary onto its label-\(n\) theta fiber.

Therefore

\[
F_{n,\pm}(t)
=
\mathcal M_n\mathcal R_2g_t^\pm
\]

satisfies

\[
\|F_{n,\pm}(t)\|=\|g\|
\]

for every \(n\) and \(t\).

## Path derivative

Since

\[
\partial_tg_t^\pm=\pm g'(\cdot\pm t),
\]

unitarity also gives

\[
\|\partial_tF_{n,\pm}(t)\|=\|g'\|.
\]

Hence, on \(t\in[L,2L]\),

\[
\int_L^{2L}
\left(
\|F_{n,\pm}(t)\|^2
+
\|\partial_tF_{n,\pm}(t)\|^2
\right)\,dt
=
L\left(\|g\|^2+\|g'\|^2\right).
\]

The first-order bivariate history norm is therefore exactly
\(O(\sqrt L)\), uniformly in the theta label.

This closes the basic graph-domain and endpoint-trace bounds for the
translated history.

## Scale derivative

Let

\[
A=q\partial_q+\frac12
\]

be the half-density dilation generator. Under \(\mathcal M_n\), differentiation
in \(u\) is intertwined with \(A\).

For translated Gaussians,

\[
A(g(\cdot\pm t))
=
T_{\mp t}Ag
\mp
t\,T_{\mp t}g',
\]

with the sign fixed by the translation convention. Consequently,

\[
\|\partial_uF_{n,\pm}(t)\|
\le
\|Ag\|+|t|\,\|g'\|.
\]

On \([L,2L]\),

\[
\int_L^{2L}
\|\partial_uF_{n,\pm}(t)\|^2\,dt
\le
2L\|Ag\|^2
+
\frac{14}{3}L^3\|g'\|^2.
\]

Thus adding one \(u\)-derivative gives at most \(O(L^{3/2})\) graph growth.

Every fixed finite \(u\)-jet gives only polynomial growth in \(L=\log p\).

## Four-grade closure

The prime-two packet uses \(f_j(q)=q^jg(q)\) for \(0\le j\le3\).
Translations and the half-density map preserve the finite Schwartz core
generated by these polynomial Gaussians.

For each fixed \(j\),

\[
\partial_tT_tf_j=T_tf_j',
\]

and

\[
AT_tf_j
=
T_tAf_j-tT_tf_j'.
\]

Hence the four-grade packet lies in the common bivariate graph core and has
only polynomial \(L\)-growth.

No additional analytic completion is required to define its endpoint traces
or its polarized \(H^1_t\) Green form.

## Polarized Stokes identity

For two histories \(F,G\) in the bivariate core,

\[
\int_L^{2L}
\left(
\langle\partial_tF,G\rangle
+
\langle F,\partial_tG\rangle
\right)\,dt
=
\langle F(2L),G(2L)\rangle
-
\langle F(L),G(L)\rangle.
\]

Because every transport before this identity is unitary, the same polarized
boundary form is obtained in:

- the full-line translated-front carrier;
- the two-ray carrier;
- every half-density theta label.

Thus the local two-lift Green/Stokes identity is analytically closed on the
bivariate Schwartz core.

What remains is not closability. It is the source comparison from this
endpoint boundary to the completed theta/Wronskian/tail quotient.

## Euler-weighted prime assembly

The primitive-square mixed coefficient is

\[
\frac12p^{-3/2-\sigma}.
\]

If a fixed \(r\)-jet bivariate graph norm grows as
\(O((\log p)^{m_r})\), the diagonal majorant is

\[
\sum_p
\frac{(\log p)^{m_r}}{p^{3/2+\sigma}},
\]

which converges uniformly for \(\sigma\ge0\).

Therefore every fixed finite bivariate jet required by the four-grade packet
assembles absolutely through the seam.

## Prime labels

The transports act inside each external prime and theta-label fiber. They do
not mix valuation idempotents. Consequently the global bivariate history is a
prime-diagonal direct sum before theta label aggregation.

Cross-prime estimates become necessary only if the later completion or sewing
map fails to intertwine these idempotents.

## Exact remaining arrow

Let

\[
\mathcal B_{p}^{\mathrm{bi}}
\]

be the oriented endpoint boundary of the bivariate translated history, and
let

\[
\mathcal B_{p}^{\mathrm{comp}}
\]

be the wall, Wronskian, and tail/PV boundary in the completed theta carrier.

The unresolved theorem is a source comparison

\[
Q_p:
\mathcal B_{p}^{\mathrm{bi}}
\longrightarrow
\mathcal B_{p}^{\mathrm{comp}}
\]

such that

\[
Q_p\mathcal B_{p}^{\mathrm{bi}}
=
\mathcal B_{p}^{\mathrm{comp}}Q_p
\]

in the appropriately typed commuting-square sense, with full polarization.

All graph norms, traces, finite jets, and diagonal prime sums on the source
side are now controlled.

## Hostiles

1. A comparison preserving endpoint differences but not the polarized
   boundary form.
2. A map valid on each theta label whose norm grows with the label.
3. A completion map that collapses the fourth Gaussian grade.
4. A scalar boundary equality with a nonzero Green-quotient residual.
5. A label sum that smears prime idempotents.

## Verdict

The bivariate translated-theta carrier is analytically well posed. Unitarity
of translation, ray restriction, and half-density transport gives exact
first-order history bounds; fixed \(u\)-jets add only logarithmic polynomial
growth, safely absorbed by Euler coefficients.

The earliest missing constructor is now solely the polarized comparison
\(Q_p\) from the bivariate endpoint boundary to the completed
theta--Wronskian--tail boundary.
