# The moving-center ray split gives the canonical window-to-theta comparison domain

## Exact fiberwise decomposition

Let \(f_0(x)=e^{-\pi x^2}\). The two moving Gaussian fronts in the
adjacent-window history are
\[
g_t^-(q)=f_0(q+t),
\qquad
g_t^+(q)=f_0(q-t).
\]

For a general \(g\in L^2(\mathbb R,dq)\), define the two moving-center ray
splittings
\[
(\mathcal R_t^-g)(x)
=
\begin{pmatrix}
g(x-t)\\
g(-x-t)
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
(\mathcal R_t^+g)(x)
=
\begin{pmatrix}
g(x+t)\\
g(-x+t)
\end{pmatrix},
\quad x>0.
\]
Each map is unitary from \(L^2(\mathbb R)\) onto
\(L^2(\mathbb R_+)\oplus L^2(\mathbb R_+)\), because it is exactly the
decomposition of the real line about the moving centers \(-t\) and \(+t\).

For the source fronts,
\[
\mathcal R_t^-g_t^-
=
\mathcal R_t^+g_t^+
=
\begin{pmatrix}f_0(x)\\f_0(-x)\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Thus both translated fronts become the same fixed two-ray Gaussian in their
own comoving frames. No fitted substitution \(q\pm t=ne^u\) is required.

## The connection cannot be discarded

Differentiating the moving trivializations gives
\[
\partial_t(\mathcal R_t^-g_t^-)
=
\mathcal R_t^-(\partial_t-\partial_q)g_t^-,
\]
and
\[
\partial_t(\mathcal R_t^+g_t^+)
=
\mathcal R_t^+(\partial_t+\partial_q)g_t^+.
\]
The actual source fronts are horizontal:
\[
(\partial_t-\partial_q)g_t^-=0,
\qquad
(\partial_t+\partial_q)g_t^+=0.
\]

Therefore the positive/negative ray split is compatible with the common
window history only as a bundle with the two opposite comoving connections.
Applying independent ray transforms and forgetting these connection signs
would erase the oriented interval geometry.

Reflection exchanges the two ray coordinates and the two connection signs.
Hence the reciprocal character is already source-typed at this stage.

## Half-density transport

Apply the exact label transport on each ray,
\[
(\mathcal M_n f)(u)=\sqrt n\,e^{u/2}f(ne^u).
\]
The composite
\[
\mathcal J_{n,t}^{\pm}
=
(\mathcal M_n\oplus\mathcal M_n)\mathcal R_t^\pm
\]
is an isometry on every fiber. On the moving source fronts it produces the
fixed reciprocal theta precursor pair. Moreover,
\[
\left(\partial_u^2-\frac14\right)\mathcal M_n
=
\mathcal M_n A_x(A_x+1),
\qquad A_x=x\partial_x.
\]

This constructs the previously missing fiberwise map from translated fronts
to the domain of theta completion.

## What remains unresolved

Fiberwise incidence is not yet the Adams edge. The scale-path derivative
\(\partial_t\) becomes the opposite comoving connections above, whereas theta
completion is generated by the dilation polynomial \(A_x(A_x+1)\). A source
comparison must still relate:

- oriented transport in \(t\in[L,2L]\);
- Mellin dilation in \(x\);
- the Lagrange boundary map of
  \(\mathcal C=\partial_u^2-\frac14\).

The missing identity is therefore no longer a map of carrier spaces. It is a
connection-intertwining theorem.

## Exact next square

Let \(\nabla_t^\mp=\partial_t\mp\partial_q\). The next theorem must prove that
the assembled two-front history satisfies a Green comparison of the form
\[
\partial_{\mathcal C}\,
\mathcal C\,
\mathcal J_{n,t}^{\pm}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{boundary transport generated by }\nabla_t^\mp,
\]
after integration over \([L,2L]\), with endpoint weights
\(p^{-1/2}\) and \(\frac12p^{-1}\) left on their declared ports.

It must also show that summing the two ray branches introduces no untyped
interface trace at \(x=0\). For the Gaussian the two values agree there, but
the Green domain must prove cancellation of the moving-cut boundary terms,
not infer it from pointwise symmetry.

## Consequence

The window-to-theta carrier map exists canonically and is fiberwise isometric.
The earliest missing arrow has contracted to the compatibility between its
comoving \(t\)-connection and the theta dilation/Lagrange Green structure.
