# A Degenerate Lens Can Select a Quotient

At coincident pointer records,

\[
E=
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
G=E^\dagger E=
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The pullback is not positive definite on the two-occurrence object, but this
is not inconsistency. Its radical is exactly the record kernel:

\[
\ker G=\ker E=\langle(1,-1)\rangle.
\]

The metric descends canonically to the one-dimensional quotient

\[
\mathcal O/\ker E,
\]

where it is positive. Operationally, the two occurrences have become
indistinguishable to the pointer: visibility is one and distinguishability is
zero.

Therefore an empty positive-lens fiber must not immediately falsify a sector.
First test whether the source supplies a semidefinite lift whose radical
generates a canonical quotient, support restriction, or derived replacement.
