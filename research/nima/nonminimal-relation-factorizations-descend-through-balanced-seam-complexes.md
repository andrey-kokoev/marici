# Nonminimal relation factorizations descend through balanced seam complexes

## Result

The first seven-event test gives a genuine obstruction to the old unbalanced target. The relation

`(a e) tensor b tensor c - a tensor (e b) tensor c`

lies in the multiplication kernel, but its two joint-seam images occupy distinct cut-labelled summands and are nonzero. Here a,b,c are two-event diamond relations and e is the extra marked event.

The repair is balancing, not a chosen preferred factorization. For the finite free source S, its record ideal I, and B=S/I, there are canonical isomorphisms

`I tensor_S ... tensor_S I = I^r`,

`(I/I^2) tensor_B ... tensor_B (I/I^2) = I^r/I^(r+1)`.

The path derivative then gives an injective bottom-cycle map from each associated layer into a **balanced iterated seam complex**. A coefficientwise embedding transports this map to the function-valued analytical refinement at a common finite capacity. This proves faithful factorization descent for these finite associated-layer maps, not merely consistency of their dimensions.

For seven events, the root-to-terminal multiplication presentation has dimensions

`55440 --quotient by a 20160-dimensional kernel--> 35280`.

Thus the root-to-terminal corner of I^3 has dimension 35280. It is not the 720-dimensional critical six-event corner, nor a dimension of the full derived analytical receiver.

## 1. Source and variance

Let D be the vertex-idempotent algebra, V the two-mark arrow bimodule, and S=T_D(V) the finite acyclic free path algebra. Let rho be the full typed terminal recorder, I=ker rho, B=S/I, C=I/I^2, and G^r=I^r/I^(r+1).

All tensor products retain their indicated algebra and endpoint types. Tensoring over D is different from tensoring over S or B. The first operation retains independent path factorizations; the latter operations impose the appropriate action-balancing relations.

The free source S is hereditary. Each ideal power I^k, being a left and right submodule of S, is projective on both sides. This fact is used below; the assertions are not made for arbitrary quotient sources with additional relations.

## 2. The entire multiplication kernel is balancing

Tensor the exact sequence of right source modules

`0 -> I -> S -> B -> 0`

with the flat left module I^(r-1). It follows that multiplication

`I tensor_S I^(r-1) -> I^(r-1)`

is injective, with image I^r. Induction identifies the r-fold tensor of I over S with I^r.

Therefore the kernel of

`I tensor_D ... tensor_D I -> I^r`

is precisely the subspace generated by the balancing relations

`... tensor (a s) tensor b tensor ...
 - ... tensor a tensor (s b) tensor ...`.

There is no additional multiplication kernel after these S-actions have been balanced.

Now quotient each factor I by I^2. Under the multiplication isomorphism, the images of tensors containing I^2 in any factor are exactly I^(r+1). By right exactness of tensor products,

`C tensor_S ... tensor_S C = G^r`.

Since I acts by zero on both sides of C, these tensors are the same as tensors over B. In particular the associated graded algebra is canonically the tensor algebra of the conormal bimodule over B, with its actual endpoint supports. In a finite packet its sufficiently high tensor powers vanish because each relation consumes at least two events.

C is projective on both sides over B: it is B tensor_S I on the left and I tensor_S B on the right, and extension of scalars preserves projectives.

## 3. The seven-event multiplication kernel

A relation has event length at least two. At seven events a three-factor product can have only the length patterns

`(3,2,2)`, `(2,3,2)`, `(2,2,3)`.

A two-event relation space has dimension 2. A three-event relation space has dimension 22: the checker independently obtains terminal rank 26 out of 48 source paths, and verifies that the 24 one-arrow contexts of local diamond relations span a rank-22 kernel.

These local computations use independent vertex potentials. Event coefficients are u_y-u_x; this is a faithful change of coordinates on the actual local chamber source, not a choice of spectral sample values.

For each length pattern there are

`7!/(3! 2! 2!)=210`

ordered endpoint partitions. Thus the unbalanced factorization space has dimension

`3 * 210 * 22 * 2 * 2 = 55440`.

At this total event length every nontrivial balancing element s must be a single elementary marked arrow: three relation factors already consume six events. Balancing over D has already accounted for vertex identities. The two possible internal interfaces give

`(a e) tensor b tensor c - a tensor (e b) tensor c`,

`a tensor b tensor (e c) - a tensor (b e) tensor c`.

Each family has dimension

`[7!/(2! 1! 2! 2!)] * 2^3 * 2 = 10080`.

The checker constructs all 20160 generators and a selected-coordinate identity minor. The first family is detected in the (3,2,2) component, where the second family is absent. The second is detected in the (2,2,3) component, where the first is absent. Within each family, the unique path cuts and fixed local route/mark probes provide the identity minor.

Section 2 proves these generators span the full kernel. Hence its dimension is exactly 20160, and

`dim e_root I^3 e_terminal = 55440-20160 = 35280`.

No global 645120-row rank computation is being reported. The dimension follows from local exact ranks, the exact kernel minor, and the flatness theorem. Also I^4=0 at seven events, so this corner of G^3 equals that of I^3.

## 4. Why the external joint map does not descend

For the first balancing relation, the product rule gives

`D(a e)=D(a) rho(e)`,

`D(e b)=rho(e) D(b)`.

In an unbalanced external tensor with distinct block-cut labels, the two resulting vectors lie in different summands. In the all-forgotten example both are nonzero: changing the first cut from event three to event two changes its label even though rho(e) emits a vacuum record.

The same issue occurs at the second internal boundary. The checker verifies raw failure for all 16 choices of local relation types and the extra event mark, at both interfaces.

This is not fixed by a Koszul phase. The target must implement

`D(a) rho(e) tensor D(b) = D(a) tensor rho(e) D(b)`

with the indicated composable vertex types. Simply retaining every possible factorization label as an independent summand is incompatible with descent.

## 5. The source-balanced seam target

Use the standard two-term source seam complex

`T_B^(-1)=B tensor_D V tensor_D B`,

`T_B^0=B tensor_D B`,

`d(p tensor v tensor q)=p rho(v) tensor q-p tensor rho(v) q`.

It computes B tensor_S^L B. The path derivative identifies

`C = ker(d:T_B^(-1)->T_B^0)`.

Write j1 for that inclusion. Each term of T_B is projective as a left and as a right B-module, since D is a finite product of copies of the scalar field.

Define

`J_(B,r)=T_B tensor_B ... tensor_B T_B`.

Ordinary tensor computes the derived tensor here because the displayed complexes are bounded and termwise flat on both sides. This is an **iterated** derived seam object, not a claim that Tor_r^S(B,B) is nonzero.

Tensoring j1 gives

`G^r[r] = C^(tensor_B r)[r] -> J_(B,r)`.

It is injective into the bottom term: both C and T_B^(-1) are flat on both sides, so tensoring the inclusions successively preserves monomorphisms. Its image consists of cycles. No boundaries enter degree -r, since the complex has no lower term. Thus the associated layer is detected in bottom homology.

This argument proves descent and noncollapse for every finite r at the fixed free source. It does not require the product factorizations to be minimal or unique.

## 6. A faithful function-valued analytical target

Use the existing analytical arrow space K_A, the common capacity N at least the packet path length, and the typed memory coefficient algebra

`R=direct_sum_(x<=y) E_(x,y) R_N(W)`.

The function-valued Clark theorem embeds B into R. The marked arrow map V->K_A is injective: the forgotten generator occupies the vacuum seam direction and the retained generator its nonzero feature direction in its actual edge block.

The analytical seam complex is

`T_R^(-1)=R tensor_D K_A tensor_D R`,

`T_R^0=R tensor_D R`,

with its admitted balancing differential. Define

`J_(R,r)=T_R tensor_R ... tensor_R T_R`.

The terms are projective on both sides over R, so again this ordinary tensor is a derived model. The coefficient and arrow embeddings give a chain map

`J_(B,r) -> J_(R,r)`.

It is degreewise injective. To see this, multiply only the adjacent outer coefficient factors at the tensor-over-B or tensor-over-R interfaces. Each resulting summand is a tensor over D of independent coefficient factors and the selected arrow factors. For example, the bottom term at r=3 is

`B tensor_D V tensor_D B tensor_D V tensor_D B tensor_D V tensor_D B`

on the source side, and the same expression with R and K_A analytically. Each factor map is injective; tensoring over the semisimple D and taking the labelled sums preserves injectivity.

Consequently

`G^r[r] -> J_(R,r)`

is an injective bottom-cycle map. In particular all 35280 seven-event root-to-terminal I^3 directions survive in this balanced analytical target. This uses the function-valued feature embedding, not a finite pointwise rank test.

## 7. What the normal form retains

For three seams the balanced normal form retains:

- three actual typed seam edges and their marked letters;
- four coefficient buffers: initial, two between seams, and final;
- the endpoints of every coefficient buffer, determined by those seams and the outer endpoints.

A factorization cut inside an intervening record is no longer an independent orthogonal label. Instead its two coefficient pieces compose in B or R. Moving that artificial cut produces the balancing relation, rather than an equality obtained by forgetting all source types.

The checker compares the two normal forms literally, including their retained words, and verifies their balanced differential is zero. All 32 representative raw failures become identical nonzero balanced cycles.

The parenthesization coherence now comes from associativity of the indicated balanced tensor products and of the coefficient multiplication. The previously fixed cochain tensor and opposite-reversal signs remain in force.

## 8. Attachment and pairing scope

The uniform derived quotient model remains

`B tensor_S^L G^(r-1) = G^(r-1) direct-sum G^r[1]`.

At the coefficient level its connecting projection can now be followed by the balanced cycle map, shifted as

`G^r[1] -> J_(R,r)[1-r]`.

For seven events, r=3, the target shift is [-2], exactly as at the critical six-event stage. The new issue was factorization descent, not a new cohomological shift.

The dimensions above refer to the root-to-terminal coefficient corner. Unlike the exactly-six-event case, the full seven-event I^3 is not supported only at the root: it also has six-event endpoint corners. Consequently its full receiver image must not be replaced, without a module calculation, by 35280 copies of the root carrier.

Nor is the passage from external seam records to balanced normal forms a Green isometry. The previously prescribed tensor form on an unbalanced external tensor need not descend through the balancing quotient. The normal-form coefficient and arrow factors have their existing carrier pairings; comparing the associated multiplication map uses its Green mate, not an inverse or a fitted quotient metric.

This work is separate from Voevodsky's ordinary-sector paired comparison. It supplies the algebraic target and faithful associated-layer descent needed beyond minimal products. It does not assert an equivalence of entire coarse block complexes, a completed packet limit, or uniform analytical estimates.
