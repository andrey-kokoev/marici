# Identifying the retained base edge turns the five-dimensional law into the cone on the presentation prism

## Based normalization

The retained corrected state is the same source coordinate in both phases:

\[
B_-=B_+=b_z.
\]

Thus the vertical base comparison is an identity degeneracy. Quotienting

\[
\Delta^1\times\Delta^4
\]

by this base edge identifies its two endpoints.

Since

\[
\Delta^4=B\star\Delta^3_{SACG},
\]

the normalized quotient is

\[
\boxed{
B\star(\Delta^1\times\Delta^3_{SACG})
}.
\]

It has nine vertices: one retained base and eight phase-presentation vertices.
Its dimension is five.

## Triangulation

The presentation prism has four staircase `4`-simplices:

\[
\begin{aligned}
 &[S_-,S_+,A_+,C_+,G_+],\\
 &[S_-,A_-,A_+,C_+,G_+],\\
 &[S_-,A_-,C_-,C_+,G_+],\\
 &[S_-,A_-,C_-,G_-,G_+].
\end{aligned}
\]

Coning each simplex with `B` gives four `5`-simplices. These are exactly the
nondegenerate simplices left from the staircase triangulation of
`Delta^1 x Delta^4` after `B_-=B_+` is imposed. The fifth original simplex,
which contains both base endpoints and only the positive presentation end,
becomes degenerate.

## Boundary law

For the oriented presentation prism `P`,

\[
\partial(B\star P)
=P-B\star\partial P
\]

up to the fixed global orientation convention. Hence the five-dimensional
based law has:

- the unbased presentation prism `P` as one boundary facet;
- cones on all six boundary components of `P` as based side facets.

Using

\[
\partial P
=T_+-T_--H_{ACG}+H_{SCG}-H_{SAG}+H_{SAC},
\]

the cone side data are precisely the based transports of the two phase laws
and four triangle homotopies.

## Meaning of the residual

The rung-four residual lives on the unbased prism facet `P`, but its attempted
filler is based at `b_z` through the cone. This explains simultaneously:

- why the residual is a scalar boundary evaluation;
- why the nonzero base must be retained at every lower face;
- why eight visible presentation nodes did not account for the full higher
  dimension;
- why the next law is a homotopy rather than a ninth independent response.

The nine-vertex object is not an eight-simplex. It is a five-dimensional cone
on a four-dimensional prism.

## Closure gate

A source-derived cone filler requires compatible based extensions of every
component of `partial P`. If those coned side facets sew, the remaining base
facet is the prism residual. The top cone law then gives its null-homotopy.
Without those extensions, contractibility of the cone supplies only an
unauthorized formal filler.
