# Two-doublet misalignment breaks generalized CP conditionally

## Question

WP596 proves that every angular extremum of one faithful dihedral doublet lies on a reflection axis. Can two such sectors remove every common generalized CP without reintroducing a fitted continuous angle?

## Exact D4 model

Measure angles in units of \(\pi/4\). A faithful square doublet carries the common transformations

\[
r:t\mapsto t+2,
\qquad
c_k:t\mapsto 2k-t,
\]

with arithmetic modulo 8 and \(k=0,1,2,3\).

Each individual direction is fixed by at least one reflection. This reproduces the single-doublet obstruction.

Now take two doublets. Opposite signs of their quartic angular anisotropies pin one to the even axes and the other to the odd diagonal axes. The checker enumerates all 16 resulting vacuum pairs. None is fixed by a common reflection. Hence residual-reflection misalignment is sufficient to remove generalized CP in this minimal representation.

The conclusion is invariant under the admitted common D4 action. Common rotations and reflections preserve the odd relative parity, so a misaligned pair is not gauge-related to an aligned pair.

## Hostile sign audit

D4 symmetry permits both signs of each quartic anisotropy. With equal signs, the vacuum set contains aligned pairs with a surviving common reflection. With opposite signs, every pair is misaligned and no common reflection survives.

Therefore the construction supplies a conditional CP-breaking architecture:

- two doublets are enough;
- a continuously fitted misalignment angle is unnecessary;
- the relative anisotropy sign is load-bearing;
- D4 representation theory alone does not select that sign.

Changing the unexplained coefficient to an unexplained binary choice is real progress, but it is not yet a source explanation.

## Next source gate

Derive the relative sign from an independently frozen constructor, such as a mediator exchange, anomaly, positivity condition, or ultraviolet matching calculation. The calculation must produce both anisotropies in one convention and one renormalization scheme. Choosing the sign after inspecting CP or flavor data merely relocates the fit.

A decisive finite falsifier is any source-compatible completion that preserves all declared field representations while flipping one anisotropy sign. If such completions exist and no additional source law distinguishes them, the two-doublet model predicts only a conditional branch.

## Scope

This packet establishes the residual-symmetry census for the minimal common D4 action. It does not claim that the present Flavor source already contains the second doublet, derives the anisotropy coefficients, or survives the full 23-coordinate completion debt identified by WP593.
