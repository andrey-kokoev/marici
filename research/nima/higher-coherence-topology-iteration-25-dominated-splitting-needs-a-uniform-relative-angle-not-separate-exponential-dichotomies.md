# Higher-coherence topology iteration 25: dominated splitting needs a uniform relative angle, not separate exponential dichotomies

## Candidate topology

Place the two stable Evans bundles in a Grassmannian with the gap or principal-
angle topology. A dominated graph transform would preserve a cone of transverse
pairs and provide a cutoff-uniform lower bound

\[
\sin\angle(E_+(z),E_-(z))\ge\varepsilon>0
\]

throughout each open half-plane. This directly keeps the state in the Schubert
big cell.

## Separate dichotomy is insufficient

Take

\[
D=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
E_+=\operatorname{span}(e_1),
\]

and, for any reciprocal-symmetric polynomial `p(z)`,

\[
R(z)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\p(z)&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
A_-(z)=R(z)DR(z)^{-1}.
\]

The eigenvalues remain exactly `-1,+1`, so both sectors have perfect uniform
exponential dichotomies. But

\[
E_-(z)=\operatorname{span}(1,p(z))^T
\]

and their Evans determinant is `p(z)`. Its zeros can be placed off seam without
changing either sector's spectral gap.

Thus domination inside each bundle does not control their relative position.

## Required relative graph transform

Write the second stable bundle as a graph over the first complement. The
relative coordinate `P(z)` must satisfy a source-derived Riccati/graph-transform
law with a strict invariant domain, for example

\[
\|P(z)^{-1}\|^{-1}\ge\varepsilon_K
\]

on every compact open-half-plane set, with completion-compatible constants.
The off-diagonal shear `p(z)` in the hostile must be forbidden by this law.

Higher cone systems could contribute missing state variables to this Riccati
flow, but they must preserve the same relative cone rather than merely improve
the separate dichotomy constants.

## Completion topology

At finite cutoff, trivial intersection is automatic in many models. The needed
completed statement is a positive Friedrichs angle. Without it, comparison
maps have inverse norms tending to infinity, determinant-line units can die at
completion, and normalized alternating packets become invisible.

A graph norm chosen to penalize every approaching intersection would enforce
an angle, but is admissible only if generated by source observers. Defining the
norm from the inverse sewing map is circular.

## Relation to the Haar residual

On the local two-ray line, the principal angle between the global neutral line
and the valuation line collapses exactly when their metric comparison becomes
singular. A uniform angle theorem strong enough to identify the two lines
isometrically would force

\[
(1-p^{-2\operatorname{Re}z})E_p(b_z)=0.
\]

Hence the relative-angle estimate must arise before this local metric
identification, not from it.

## Verdict for topology 25

Dominated-splitting topology gives the right quantitative notion of safe
completion: a cutoff-uniform relative angle between the two Evans bundles. It
cannot be inferred from separate exponential dichotomies, reciprocal symmetry,
or finite transversality.

The remaining executable object is the source-derived relative Riccati
generator and its invariant cone. No such uniform relative-frame law is yet
available.

The next nonredundant topology to test is a reproducing-kernel/de Branges
Hilbert topology, where transversality may be encoded by positivity of a
canonical kernel rather than by direct bundle-angle estimates.
