# Oriented boundary factorization is the source route to rank-one odd support

## Wall involution

Let the face involution exchange the two comoving charts:

\[
We_{\mathrm{in}}=e_{\mathrm{out}},
\qquad
We_{\mathrm{out}}=e_{\mathrm{in}}.
\]

Its eigenspaces are

\[
E_s=\operatorname{span}\{s\},
\qquad
s=e_{\mathrm{in}}+e_{\mathrm{out}},
\]

and

\[
E_a=\operatorname{span}\{a\},
\qquad
a=e_{\mathrm{out}}-e_{\mathrm{in}}.
\]

The projectors are

\[
P_s=\frac{I+W}{2},
\qquad
P_a=\frac{I-W}{2}.
\]

## The source cell contains both characters

The raw two-chart cell does not have a single wall character. Its two canonical readouts are

\[

\[

Therefore no selection theorem can be inferred from the existence of the cell alone. The complete relative cell genuinely carries both \(E_s\) and \(E_a\).

## Hostiles

### Raw-cell pairing

A pairing uses \(I\) and \(O\) without first applying oriented boundary incidence. Both \(s\) and \(a\) survive, so no rank-one theorem exists.

### Fitted antisymmetrization

One replaces \(B\) by

\[

### Endpoint sign reversal

The boundary is taken as \(e_{\mathrm{in}}-e_{\mathrm{out}}\). Rank remains one, but the seam character frame reverses.

### Completion leakage

Finite blocks obey the character law, but cutoff comparison maps fail to intertwine \(W\), allowing symmetric leakage in the limit.
