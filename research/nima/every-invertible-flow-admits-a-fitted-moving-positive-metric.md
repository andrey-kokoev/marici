# Every invertible flow admits a fitted moving positive metric

## Moving-metric no-go

Let \(\Phi(t)\) be any invertible finite-dimensional flow and let \(Q_0>0\).
Define

\[
Q(t)=\Phi(t)^{-*}Q_0\Phi(t)^{-1}.
\]

Then

\[
\Phi(t)^*Q(t)\Phi(t)=Q_0.
\]

Thus every invertible flow is isometric in a suitable moving positive metric.
The construction works for elliptic, parabolic, and hyperbolic dynamics.

Existence of a moving metric therefore supplies no confinement law by itself.

## Differential form

If

\[
\Phi'=H\Phi,
\]

then the fitted metric satisfies

\[
Q'+H^*Q+QH=0.
\]

This identity is automatic because \(Q\) was reconstructed from the
propagator. It cannot explain why a source divisor is confined.

## Hyperbolic hostile

Take

\[
H=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0\\
0&-1
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\Phi(t)=
\begin{pmatrix}
e^t&0\\
0&e^{-t}
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The flow has strict expansion and contraction in the ordinary Euclidean
metric. Yet

\[
Q(t)=
\begin{pmatrix}
e^{-2t}&0\\
0&e^{2t}
\end{pmatrix}
\]

is positive and makes the flow exactly isometric.

Hence a fitted moving metric can hide the very instability it is supposed to
explain.

## Source-derived gate

A theta/Tate moving metric is admissible only if:

1. it is constructed from source boundary, reciprocal, or measure data before
   solving the Evans flow;
2. its definition does not use \(\Phi^{-1}\), the zero set, or the completed
   scalar readout;
3. its normalization is fixed by source authority;
4. its completion is continuous and nondegenerate;
5. it yields an inequality or signature law stronger than automatic
   transported conservation.

The same warning applies to a Maslov crossing form. Pulling a reference form
along a known path can assign crossings after the fact; the crossing
orientation must come from an independently defined boundary polarization.

## Finite falsifier

For any proposed moving metric, test whether it equals

\[
\Phi^{-*}Q_0\Phi^{-1}
\]

for an arbitrary fitted \(Q_0\). If so, the metric adds no information beyond
the flow it was meant to constrain.
