# The forced heat-amplitude connection is exact and endpoint-regular

The polarized pole match forces

\[
c(a)^2
=
\frac{\pi}
{a(1-a)\zeta(1+a)\zeta(2-a)},
\qquad 0<a<1.
\]

This initially appears to introduce a new moving-seam connection obstruction.
It does not.

Define

\[
\omega(a)=d\log c(a).
\]

Because \(c(a)\) is a globally defined positive scalar on the open seam,
\(\omega\) is exact. Hence its curvature vanishes:

\[
d\omega=0,
\]

and every closed-loop holonomy inside the seam interval is trivial. Reciprocal
reflection acts by

\[
c(1-a)=c(a),
\qquad
\omega(1-a)=-\omega(a).
\]

Thus the connection has precisely the required reciprocal character.

## Endpoint cancellation

The apparent singularities in

\[
\frac{c'(a)}{c(a)}
=
-\frac12
\left[
\frac1a-\frac1{1-a}
+\frac{\zeta'(1+a)}{\zeta(1+a)}
-\frac{\zeta'(2-a)}{\zeta(2-a)}
\right]
\]

cancel. As \(a\downarrow0\),

\[
\zeta(1+a)=\frac1a+\gamma+O(a),
\qquad
\frac{\zeta'(1+a)}{\zeta(1+a)}
=
-\frac1a+\gamma+O(a).
\]

Therefore

\[
\lim_{a\downarrow0}c(a)^2
=
\frac{\pi}{\zeta(2)}
=
\frac6\pi,
\]

and, by reflection, the same limit holds as \(a\uparrow1\). Moreover,

\[
\lim_{a\downarrow0}\frac{c'(a)}{c(a)}
=
\frac12
\left[
1-\gamma+\frac{\zeta'(2)}{\zeta(2)}
\right],
\]

with the negative value at the reciprocal endpoint.

Consequently \(c\) extends to a positive \(C^1\) field on the closed seam.
By compactness,

\[
0<\inf_{0\le a\le1}c(a)
\le
\sup_{0\le a\le1}c(a)
<\infty.
\]

The amplitude transport and its inverse are therefore uniformly bounded, not
merely compact-off-seam bounded.

## Constructor consequence

The tangential amplitude does not create a new curvature or completion
margin. Once its source authority is established, it is a globally
bi-bounded exact metric retyping.

The unresolved content is now isolated in the finite defect

\[
\Delta(a)
=
\operatorname{FP}_{a}G_E
+
\frac{\pi}
{12a(1-a)\zeta(1+a)\zeta(2-a)}.
\]

No connection holonomy can explain this defect: the forced amplitude
connection has trivial holonomy. It must be supplied by a genuine finite
Todd, endpoint, archimedean, or discrete-remainder return.

A useful hostile is a proposed correction obtained by altering the amplitude
connection. Such a change can shift local formulas, but if it changes
\(\omega=d\log c\), it also breaks the already-forced polarized pole match.
The finite defect cannot be gauged away while retaining the pole metric.
