# Relative composition of two WG specializations

`agda/NestedWitnessSpecialization.agda` tests a bounded composition law. Given an initial compatible pair `(D,G)`, its first specialized WG has states `(s, admitted₁(s))` and retains the original relation witness. A second domain must use **that very first generator** as its base and supply its own compatibility and admission-closure proofs. Under these explicit conditions, nested admission states `( (s,ok₁),ok₂ )` are isomorphic to the single dependent presentation `(s,ok₁,ok₂)`; both inverse laws are `refl`. `same-base-transition` confirms the second wrapper does not change the first transition, and `original-witness` recovers the original relation witness after both layers. `recovered-second-input` retrieves the exact second compatible pair.

The file now additionally defines a **direct flattened WG** from the first generator plus the second domain's supplied compatibility and closure. `direct-vs-nested` compares its output with two-stage specialization; `direct-witness-vs-nested` compares the actual retained proof payload; `step-iso` identifies their entire one-step endpoint-and-witness types in both directions. This supplies a bounded one-step-vs-two-step constructor comparison, not merely a carrier reassociation.

The concrete fixture composes the trace-admitted certified-selection WG with a further (unit-admitted) layer. Its projected witness still has the original selection-witness type. The second admission policy is deliberately trivial; it is a structural regression, not an independent domain theory. The general theorem covers **two** compatible specializations; no associativity/coherence law for arbitrary chains of views or application in the original twelve rules follows.
