# The Residual T7 Quotient Does Not Remove the Leading UV Counterterm

The residual quotient has one detector equal to the full \(e_1^\vee\)
summand. The source large-loop audit gives

\[
\int^{\Lambda}e_1\sim c_4\Lambda^4+O(\Lambda^3),
\qquad c_4>0,
\]

The algebraic generator \(v_{\rm alg}\) generically also has a
\(\Lambda^4\) contribution through its \(e_8,e_9\) tail. Hence the leading
counterterm vector in the ordered basis

\[
(e_1,e_2,e_3,e_4,e_5,e_6,v_{\rm alg})
\]

has the form

\[
c_{\rm UV}^{(4)}=(1,0,0,0,0,0,\kappa),
\]

where \(\kappa\) is frame- and kinematics-dependent and is generically
nonzero. Its first component is fixed to be nonzero by positivity of the
literal \(e_1\) integrand.

Its residual pairing is

\[
e_1^\vee(c_{\rm UV}^{(4)})=1.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
T_7/(\operatorname{im}_{\log}+\operatorname{im}_{\rm Cut})
\text{ does not annihilate the source UV counterterm space.}
}
\]

The hoped-for shortcut—pairing directly with the residual quotient before
renormalizing—fails already in the leading asymptotic grade. Any physical
activation of the residual mixture containing the odd--odd \(e_1\) line
requires a source-authorized
renormalized bulk functional. Connection transport, deck projection, and
supported localization maps cannot manufacture it.

The next valid object is thus a renormalized Betti packet: analytic
continuation in the source dimension/regulator together with a declared
finite-part map and counterterm covariance. Only after proving that the
resulting residual rank is scheme invariant can the original rank-two
falsifier be run.
