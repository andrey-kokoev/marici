## The apparently conflicting relative-fibre conclusions

They refer to different maps or coefficient degrees; they must not be substituted for each other.

| Restriction | Generic/detector homotopy | Matching independently framed normalized fibre |
|---|---|---|
| Auxiliary node/norm detector | pi0 = Z/2 plus Z/3; pi1 = Z/2 | two contractible components |
| Actual augmentation/Q, unit multidegree | pi0 of generic part = Z/3; higher pi vanish | contractible |
| Actual augmentation/Q, three-long-normal multidegree gamma | pi0 of generic part = Z/3; pi1 = Z/2 | two contractible components |

Incompatible prescribed components give empty fibres. The two components are comparison paths for the same coherent unit, not two arithmetic unit classes or an assigned physical parity.

At unit degree, dT is the facet norm; lifting the detached parity loop would require an even reflection coefficient to be odd. At gamma, the three legal marked terms cancel that norm and yield the closed sign line omega. This restores the loop in the actual target, with an attached primitive 18-term supported boundary. Multiplication by U_L gives a commuting cube between these degrees; it is not an equivalence of sectors.

If the gamma comparison is required to be the specified U_L-multiple of the unit-degree comparison, only one component is inherited. Independently prescribing it permits two. The external Cartier/Rees Tor generator has not been proved to be a U_L-multiple, so module-linearity alone cannot select a physical component.

The absent closed degree-three lift of omega and the existence of a coherent degree-one unit with generic comparison data are different mapping problems. Their conclusions are compatible. Likewise, the three-long-normal principal-parts obstruction in relative-physical-admissibility and the six-short-normal connecting obstruction in filtered-q-comparision are different exact sequences and classes, not two answers for the same annihilator. Neither is integer-prime torsion.

- Carrier versus supported-dual endpoint cells, ordinary versus dual variance, and the specified tensor base.
