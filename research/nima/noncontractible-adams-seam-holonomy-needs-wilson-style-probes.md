# Noncontractible Adams-seam holonomy needs Wilson-style probes

## Local versus global metric compatibility

The seam Lyapunov equation
\[
\partial_sG+A_s^*G+GA_s=0
\]
tests local parallel transport of the metric.

Elementary Adams-seam plaquettes test mixed local compatibility. Contractible products of these plaquettes test local curvature integration.

None of these tests determines holonomy around a noncontractible cycle of the parameter complex.

The correct distinction is
\[
\text{local plaquette coherence}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{global logical-sector coherence}.
\]

## Parameter complex

Let \(\mathfrak P_X\) be the typed parameter complex at cutoff \(X\). Its cells may include:

- continuous seam charts;
- discrete Adams/grade directions;
- reciprocal-sheet identifications;
- Real involution edges;
- endpoint and archimedean attachments;
- cutoff inclusions and any independently authorized returns;
- associator and clutching cells.

Elementary two-cells impose local relations. The remaining global sectors are represented by
\[
\pi_1(\mathfrak P_X)
\]
or the appropriate groupoid when basepoints and fiber types vary.

## Wilson-style holonomy probes

For each source-authorized generator
\[
[\gamma]\in\pi_1(\mathfrak P_X,s_0),
\]
compute the ordered holonomy
\[
W_\gamma
=
\mathcal P\!\prod_{e\in\gamma}T_e.
\]

Metric compatibility requires
\[
W_\gamma^*G_{s_0}W_\gamma=G_{s_0}.
\]

These probes are independent of local curvature whenever \([\gamma]\) is not generated by contractible plaquette boundaries.

The probe must retain the full ordered matrix. Determinant and scalar traces are only projections.

## Flat scalar hostile on a circle

Take a seam circle with coordinate \(s\in[0,2\pi]\) and constant scalar generator
\[
A=a\in\mathbb R.
\]
The local metric equation has solution
\[
G(s)=e^{-2as}G(0).
\]
Local compatibility holds everywhere and curvature vanishes.

But periodicity requires
\[
G(2\pi)=G(0),
\]
while
\[
G(2\pi)=e^{-4\pi a}G(0).
\]
For \(a\neq0\), no positive periodic metric exists.

With the transport convention
\[
T_{0\to2\pi}=e^{2\pi a},
\]
the global monodromy satisfies
\[
|T_{0\to2\pi}|\neq1.
\]

This is a flat local connection with globally nonunitarizable holonomy.

## Five-stage audit

### Local seam compatibility

Solve or verify the Lyapunov equation in each seam chart.

### Elementary mixed plaquettes

Test one Adams step against one seam displacement, including the traceless Hermitian curvature.

### Contractible loops

Verify products of elementary plaquettes around boundaries of admitted two-cells. This tests integrated local relations and detects singular curvature missed infinitesimally.

### Fundamental cycles

Choose independent noncontractible cycle generators and compute full ordered holonomies.

### Completion control

Require the resulting common metrics, Wilson holonomies, and conjugators to remain uniformly controlled as cutoff grows and over compact off-seam regions.

No stage subsumes a later one without a topology theorem.

## Simply connected reduction

If the admitted parameter complex is simply connected and its two-cells generate all loop relations, local plaquette coherence plus regularity can determine all holonomies.

This reduction requires proof of:

- simple connectivity of the typed complex;
- completeness of the two-cell presentation;
- absence of singular or removed strata;
- compatibility of fiber typing across cells.

It cannot be assumed from a simply connected coordinate chart.

## Candidate noncontractible directions

The RH constructor may contain global cycles from:

### Seam periodicity

A periodic seam coordinate directly yields circle holonomy.

### Reciprocal-sheet identification

Composing transport from \(s\) to \(1-s\) and back may define a noncontractible sheet cycle, especially around removed seam or branch loci.

### Real/reciprocal mixed loops

Real involution and reciprocal transport can form a global square whose boundary is not contractible in the typed quotient.

### Cutoff topology

Cutoff inclusions alone are directed and create no cycles. A cycle arises only if a retraction or comparison return is independently authorized.

### Endpoint and archimedean clutching

Attaching completion cells can create new global loops or kill existing ones. Their effect must be computed after source authorization.

## Toric-code analogy

Elementary plaquettes constrain local flux. Wilson loops around noncontractible cycles label logical sectors not fixed by local stabilizers.

Here:

- local Adams-seam curvature is the plaquette flux;
- common metric transport is the local stabilizer condition;
- noncontractible ordered holonomy is the logical-sector observable;
- determinant modulus is only an abelian shadow of that logical operator.

A locally flat constructor can therefore occupy a globally amplifying sector.

## Gauge invariance

Changing the base frame by \(S\) conjugates
\[
W_\gamma\mapsto SW_\gamma S^{-1}.
\]
Thus the following are gauge invariant:

- determinant character;
- spectrum and Jordan type;
- similarity class;
- existence of one common positive invariant metric for all Wilson probes.

Raw matrix entries are not.

## Global simultaneous test

Let \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{loc}}\) be generated by local plaquette holonomies and \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}}\) by fundamental cycles. The full holonomy group is generated by both.

A metric is admissible only when
\[
H^*GH=G
\]
for every element of
\[
\langle\Gamma_{\mathrm{loc}},\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}}\rangle.
\]

Testing the two families with separate metrics is insufficient. Their products must preserve one common metric.

## Completion hostile

Let each finite parameter complex be a circle with unit-modulus holonomy, but conjugate the holonomy through
\[
S_X=
\begin{pmatrix}
X&0\\
0&X^{-1}
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Every finite global holonomy is unitarizable. The local curvature can remain zero. Yet the invariant metrics are not uniformly equivalent to the source metric.

Thus global cycle success at finite cutoff still requires the operator-log completion bound.
