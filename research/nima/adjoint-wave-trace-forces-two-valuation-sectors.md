# Adjoint wave-trace symmetry forces two valuation sectors

## The adjoint law

Let \(H\) be self-adjoint, let

\[
U(q)=e^{iqH},
\]

and let \(\tau\) be a fixed functional satisfying

\[
\tau(A^*)=\overline{\tau(A)}.
\]

Since \(U(q)^*=U(-q)\), its wave trace obeys

\[
W(-q)=\overline{W(q)}.
\]

This condition survives signed or distributional traces. It does not require positivity.

## One-sided prime current cannot be the full trace

The primitive and prime-power return events initially occur at positive logarithmic lengths

\[
q=k\log p>0.
\]

A nonzero distribution supported only at positive lengths cannot satisfy the adjoint wave-trace law. Every positive event must have a negative reflected event with conjugate coefficient.

Thus a self-adjoint trace formula forces a doubled source:

\[
\nu_{\mathrm{wave}}
=\nu_+ + \mathcal R\nu_+ + \nu_{\mathrm{arch}} + \nu_0,
\]

where \(\mathcal R\) reflects \(q\mapsto-q\), \(\nu_{\mathrm{arch}}\) is the adjoint-symmetric archimedean kernel, and \(\nu_0\) contains only terms actually supported at the fixed locus \(q=0\).

## Interpretation of the sectors

The two valuation cones are not duplicated presentations of one Euler packet. They carry the positive- and negative-length halves required by adjoint compatibility. Reciprocal sewing identifies their coefficients through the local Tate transition.

The fixed locus at \(q=0\) can carry genuinely zero-length boundary terms. The archimedean contribution is not generally confined there: the gamma channel has an extended reflection-symmetric kernel in the wave parameter, possibly together with a singular zero-length part. It must remain an independent typed reservoir.

This gives an exact operator reason for the two-sector architecture:

- the positive valuation cone supplies forward logarithmic return lengths;
- the negative valuation cone supplies their adjoint reflections;
- the archimedean channel supplies an extended adjoint-symmetric comparison kernel;
- the fixed-locus channel supplies only genuinely zero-length boundary data.

No physical-time interpretation is used. The variable \(q\) is logarithmic scale and wave-group parameter.

## Coefficient gate

Support symmetry is insufficient. For every labelled event, the negative-sector coefficient must be the adjoint transport of the positive-sector coefficient. If \(c_{p,k}^+\) is the positive coefficient, the source must derive

\[
c_{p,k}^- = \overline{c_{p,k}^+}
\]

in a common wave-trace frame, or the corresponding Tate-twisted relation before trivialization.

An arbitrary symmetric duplication would manufacture the adjoint law without deriving the reciprocal comparison.

## Finite compiler

At each cutoff:

1. list every positive labelled length and coefficient;
2. list every negative labelled length and coefficient;
3. verify bijective reflection of labels and multiplicities;
4. verify coefficient conjugacy after the declared Tate transport;
5. separate the extended archimedean kernel from genuinely zero-length boundary terms;
6. verify the completed trace satisfies \(W(-q)=\overline{W(q)}\);
7. reject any scale-dependent regularization that repairs the equality afterward.

The smallest falsifier is one unmatched length or one reflected coefficient with the wrong phase.

## Scope

Adjoint doubling explains why a self-adjoint wave-trace programme needs two reflected arithmetic sectors. It does not reduce the archimedean channel to the fixed locus, construct \(H\), prove the trace identity, or confine zeros. Those remain in the fixed-functional and source-realization gates.
