# The Tate gamma ratio is the canonical sewing-modulus candidate

Event 10273 reduced the amplitude factorization to the modulus \(s(a)\) of
reciprocal sewing. The standard completed-zeta archimedean factor supplies a
canonical candidate.

Let

\[
g(a)=\pi^{-a/2}\Gamma(a/2).
\]

The reciprocal Tate ratio is

\[
s_{\infty}(a)
=
\frac{g(1-a)}{g(a)}
=
\pi^{a-\frac12}
\frac{\Gamma((1-a)/2)}{\Gamma(a/2)}.
\]

It satisfies the exact involution law

\[
s_{\infty}(1-a)s_{\infty}(a)=1
\]

and

\[
s_{\infty}(1/2)=1.
\]

Thus it has exactly the algebraic type required of the sewing modular
cocycle.

If this ratio is the source-authorized sewing modulus, the two amplitudes are

\[
c_P(a)=c_0(a)s_{\infty}(a)^{-1/2},
\qquad
c_Q(1-a)=c_0(a)s_{\infty}(a)^{1/2}.
\]

The relative connection is then the explicit archimedean one-form

\[
-\frac12d\log s_{\infty}(a),
\]

where

\[
\frac{d}{da}\log s_{\infty}(a)
=
\log\pi
-\frac12\psi((1-a)/2)
-\frac12\psi(a/2).
\]

This is reciprocal-even as a derivative, while \(\log s_{\infty}\) is
reciprocal-odd.

## Endpoint behavior

The gamma ratio is not uniformly bi-bounded on the closed seam. As
\(a\downarrow0\),

\[
s_{\infty}(a)\sim\frac a2,
\]

and as \(a\uparrow1\),

\[
s_{\infty}(a)\sim\frac2{1-a}.
\]

Since the common pole metric \(c_0(a)\) has finite nonzero endpoint limits,
one typed amplitude diverges like \(a^{-1/2}\) while the other collapses like
\(a^{1/2}\), with reciprocal behavior at the opposite endpoint.

Therefore the gamma sewing candidate supports exactly the weaker topology
already suggested by the source riggings:

- uniform bi-boundedness on compact subsets of the open seam;
- separate distributional endpoint attachment;
- no global closed-seam Hilbert equivalence between primitive and square
  amplitude lines.

## Authority warning

The functional equation makes \(s_{\infty}\) the canonical candidate; it does
not by itself prove that the constructor sewing arrow has this modulus.
That requires a Tate/Fourier sewing theorem in the source metrics.

There are now two sharply distinct possibilities:

1. Source-unitary sewing: \(s=1\), giving the even square-root frame and
   closed-seam metric equivalence.
2. Tate-modular sewing: \(s=s_{\infty}\), giving compact-open equivalence and
   singular endpoint typing.

A hybrid choice is not authorized merely because it satisfies
\(s(1-a)s(a)=1\).

This candidate also offers a falsifiable link to the finite defect
\(\Delta(a)\): differentiate its gamma modular potential and test whether the
archimedean return generated by that connection supplies the non-Todd portion
of the polarized Euler finite part.
