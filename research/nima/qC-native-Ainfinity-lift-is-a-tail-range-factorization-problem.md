# The native q-C A-infinity lift is a tail range factorization problem

Let

$$
T_X=(I-P_X)\mathcal H
$$

be the retained tail carrier and

$$
d_X=A_X=P_X\mathcal F(I-P_X):T_X\to V_X
$$

its boundary map into the visible carrier.

Let

$$
M_2:A\widehat\otimes A\to V_X
$$

be the completed bilinear operator realizing the q-C multiplicativity defect

$$
M_2(a,b)=f_1(ab)-f_1(a)f_1(b).
$$

A native degree-one component exists exactly when there is a continuous map

$$
h_2:A\widehat\otimes A\to T_X
$$

such that

$$
A_Xh_2=M_2.
$$

Thus the native A-infinity gate is

$$
\operatorname{Ran}M_2
\subseteq
\operatorname{Ran}A_X.
$$

For bounded Hilbert-space operators, Douglas factorization gives the equivalent criterion

$$
M_2M_2^*
\le
\lambda A_XA_X^*
$$

for some finite constant

$$
\lambda.
$$

When the range is closed, the minimal lift is

$$
h_2=A_X^\dagger M_2.
$$

For nonclosed range, the same formula may be unbounded. The graph formulation then requires closability and a declared domain.

Each secondary q-C-a component has an analogous factorization problem. If its visible boundary defect is

$$
M_{3,a},
$$

then the required tail filler satisfies

$$
d_{3,a}K_{qCa}=M_{3,a}.
$$

The current source supplies the tail boundary operator and the formal defect law. A completed bilinear operator

$$
M_2:A\widehat\otimes A\to V_X
$$

on the same retained graph has not yet been serialized. Until that map is fixed, the range inclusion cannot be evaluated.

The next analytical datum is therefore the graph-bounded q-C defect operator, including its visible target coordinates. Once supplied, the Douglas test decides whether the retained tail realizes the native A-infinity component.
