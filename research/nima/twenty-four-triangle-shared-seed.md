# Two bodies from a shared seed: the 24-triangle construction

## Result

One seed generates two distinct, congruent tetrahedral bodies, each represented by twelve centroid-edge triangles. They share an explicitly constructed octahedral core. A seed-derived exchange connects their transported eigenlines, leaving one common phase identity.

The entire spatial assembly has a positive cell construction. It uses eight tetrahedra for the core and four additional tetrahedral tips per body. Every intermediate cell attachment has positive volume, compatible seams and a closed oriented boundary.

There are two different 24-triangle descriptions:

- the two complete twelve-triangle body surfaces, which intersect;
- the external boundary of their union, containing 24 corner triangles.

Resolving the shared core refines each body's boundary into sixteen triangles. The construction therefore does not identify 24 as a count of all independent lowest-level faces.

## Seed and construction rule

The existing directed seed contains

    AB, BC, CA, AD, DB, BA.

Its closed walks include (ABC), (ADB), and the two-cycle (AB). The two three-cycles generate A4, with twelve elements. Including the two-cycle generates S4, with twenty-four elements. This uses an existing seed relation rather than supplying a second independent body.

The new construction rule is explicit: act on oriented area packets, using the exterior-square representation of the three-dimensional contrast space. For an orthogonal state permutation matrix R on that space, its area action is

    R_area = det(R) R.

Every R_area has determinant +1. Even permutations retain the previous action. Odd permutations combine their reflection with central inversion and become proper rotations. This is the cofactor/exterior-square action; it is a proposed packet-realization rule, not a proven unique consequence of the seed.

The counting-pairing contrast space admits the familiar coordinate realization

    A=(1,1,1), B=(1,-1,-1), C=(-1,1,-1), D=(-1,-1,1).

The coordinate scale follows the existing twelve-triangle fixture. No independent placement of the second body is provided.

## The two geometric branches

Let T be the convex hull of these four points. Even permutations preserve T. Odd area actions carry it to -T, the complementary tetrahedron of the same cube.

The orbit of the reference triangle (F_ABC,A,B), where F_ABC is its parent face centroid, has twenty-four members:

- twelve cover the boundary of T;
- twelve cover the boundary of -T.

Each body's boundary cancels exactly and encloses volume 8/3. Their vertex sets are distinct. The proper rotation generated by (AB) exchanges the bodies and fixes the axis A-B.

## Shared core, derived from the same relations

The two tetrahedra have inequalities

    T: 1+v_i dot x >= 0,
    -T: 1-v_i dot x >= 0.

Their intersection is

    O = {|x|+|y|+|z| <= 1}.

Its six vertices are exactly the six edge midpoints of the original four-state tetrahedron. Thus the shared octahedron is constructed from existing endpoint comparisons.

Its volume is 4/3. The union has volume

    8/3 + 8/3 - 4/3 = 4.

The bodies overlap in this positive three-dimensional core. Their shared identity is represented by a constant mode on the core; the geometric intersection is larger than a line.

## Stagewise positive assembly

Construct O from eight origin-to-face tetrahedra, each of volume 1/6, in a face-connected order. Then attach the eight tips, four belonging to each body, each of volume 1/3.

For sign triple sigma, a tip has vertices

    sigma,
    (sigma_x,0,0), (0,sigma_y,0), (0,0,sigma_z).

Its interior lies in its corresponding octant and satisfies sigma dot x > 1. The core satisfies sigma dot x <= 1. Distinct tips lie in distinct octants. These facts certify that top-dimensional cell interiors are disjoint.

Every attachment glues along existing complete faces. The checker verifies, after each of the sixteen additions:

- positive oriented cell volume;
- cancellation of shared faces;
- zero boundary-of-boundary;
- two incident boundary faces per boundary edge;
- boundary Euler characteristic two.

After twelve additions, one complete tetrahedral body has formed around the shared core. The remaining four additions complete the second branch. The final union has 24 external triangles and volume four.

## Eigenline compatibility and shared identity

For each orbit triangle g, its vertex matrix satisfies X_g=R_g X_0. For the selected cyclic coefficient mode c=(1,omega,omega²),

    v_g=X_g c=R_g v_0,
    R_g^T v_g=v_0.

The seed exchange transports every positive-branch line to its corresponding negative-branch line. The equality is between transported lines in compatible frames.

On the twenty-four orbit positions, form the undirected comparison graph generated by the two three-cycles and the exchange. Its Laplacian has rank 23, so its kernel is one common identity line. Each twelve-position body graph has rank eleven and its own constant identity. Removing the cross-exchange edges gives two independent identities.

There are exactly twelve undirected cross-exchange pairs, hence twenty-four directed arrows. The [endpoint ledger](pair-arrow-ledger.md) counts these separately from geometric cell edges and the earlier dense coefficient protocol. A positive transition generated by these moves is

    M=I-L/6.

It is nonnegative and row-stochastic. The common mode is fixed. The relative mode, constant +1 on one body and -1 on the other, is multiplied by 2/3 per step. Thus this comparison operation aligns the branch amplitudes while retaining their separate geometric supports.

The geometric cell adjacency graphs give a compatible check: the eight-cell core, each twelve-cell body, and the sixteen-cell union each have a one-dimensional constant kernel. Both body sections restrict to the same constant section on their common core.

These facts establish coherent identity sharing and idempotent common-mode selection. They do not establish a mechanical binding force or identify the two congruent bodies as different particle species.
