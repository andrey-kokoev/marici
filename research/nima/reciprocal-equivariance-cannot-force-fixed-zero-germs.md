# Reciprocal Equivariance Cannot Force Fixed Zero Germs

## Minimal categorical obstruction

Let \(J\) be the reciprocal involution on the spectral parameter,

\[
J(s)=1-s.
\]

The functional equation and Fourier–Tate sewing make the scalar zero locus a
\(C_2\)-object: if \(s\) is a zero, then \(J(s)\) is a zero. This proves
closure under reciprocal transport. It does not prove that every orbit is a
fixed point.

There are two kinds of \(C_2\)-orbit:

- a fixed orbit on the critical seam;
- a free two-element orbit away from the seam.

Equivariance, naturality, common adelic height, and preservation of the total
complementary grade all admit both kinds.

## Free-orbit hostile model

Take two points \(u\) and \(v\), with \(J(u)=v\) and \(J(v)=u\). Give them
complementary grades \(a\) and \(1-a\). Declare the scalar null predicate true
on both points.

This model has:

- exact reciprocal symmetry;
- an involutive sewing map;
- one shared cutoff;
- conserved total grade;
- scalar nullity on the whole orbit;
- no fixed point when \(a\ne1/2\).

It is the categorical skeleton of a hostile off-seam zero pair.

## No-go theorem

Any proposed proof using only properties invariant under equivariant maps of
\(C_2\)-objects cannot distinguish a free orbit from two presentations of a
fixed orbit. In particular, reciprocity plus scalar nullity cannot force the
zero-germ map through the fixed-point equalizer.

The missing source law must be fixed-point sensitive. It must forbid free
null orbits by information not already contained in:

- the scalar functional equation;
- reciprocal transport;
- the product formula and common height;
- complementary-grade conservation;
- finite-part equality.

## Consequence for the current route

The search for a generic categorical coherencer now closes. An equalizer,
pullback, naturality square, or involutive transport constructed solely from
the existing reciprocal data cannot prove RH. Each admits the free-orbit
hostile model.

A surviving advance requires a new source operation whose action differs on
fixed and free reciprocal orbits. Examples of the required type include an
independently positive fixed-point index, a parity-sensitive boundary charge,
or a source-derived obstruction to transporting a null event around a free
orbit. Naming the fixed-point object is insufficient.

## Finite falsifier

For every proposed law, instantiate it on the two-point free orbit. If all
declared equations commute and the null predicate remains true while the
fixed-point set is empty, the law has no confinement content.

This falsifier is prior to zero computation and does not inspect the actual
Riemann divisor.
