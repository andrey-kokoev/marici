# Source-section rigidity leaves one global conservation DPC

## Canonical section is fixed

The Euler presentation and the theta/Tate presentation evaluate the same
labelled source and agree on the open half-plane

\[
\Re s>1.
\]

If two global analytic sections have the same source normalization there, the
identity theorem makes them equal on their common analytic continuation.

Therefore a nontrivial analytic multiplier cannot be another presentation of
the same source. Hostile even multipliers remain useful as tests of proposed
laws, but they no longer represent ambiguity in the canonical section.

This closes:

- exponential presentation gauge;
- divisor insertion by coordinate change;
- alternative analytic continuation with the same Euler normalization;
- finite reciprocal transition ambiguity.

It does not constrain the zeros of the now-fixed section.

## Required conservation identity

The target identity has the form

\[
2\Re(z)\,\mathcal E(\Psi_z)
=
-\partial_q\mathcal J(\Psi_z)
+
\mathcal R(\Psi_z),
\]

where:

- \(\mathcal E\) is a source-derived completed energy;
- \(\mathcal J\) is the total boundary flux;
- \(\mathcal R\) is a typed residual that must vanish or be absorbed into the
  energy by an exact source identity.

If the completed boundary conditions imply zero total flux and

\[
\mathcal E(\Psi)>0
\]

for every nonzero admissible state, then any zero-state forces

\[
\Re z=0.
\]

This is the desired zero-confinement theorem. It does not divide by the
completed section, inspect its zeros, or restate scalar Herglotz positivity.
