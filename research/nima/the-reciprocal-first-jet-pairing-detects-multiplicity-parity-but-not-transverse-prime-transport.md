# The reciprocal first-jet pairing detects multiplicity parity, but not transverse prime transport

## Test

Use the centered coordinate `z=s-1/2`, so the completed functional equation is

\[
\Xi(z)=\Xi(-z),
\qquad
\Xi(\bar z)=\overline{\Xi(z)}.
\]

Let `z0` be a zero of exact multiplicity `m` and put

\[
\tau_m(z_0)=\frac{\Xi^{(m)}(z_0)}{m!}\ne0.
\]

Differentiating the functional equation gives

\[
\tau_m(-z_0)=(-1)^m\tau_m(z_0),
\]

and therefore

\[
\tau_m(-\bar z_0)=(-1)^m\overline{\tau_m(z_0)}.
\]

A reciprocal two-sheet Hermitian pairing consequently reduces, up to its fixed
sheet convention, to

\[
(-1)^m|\tau_m(z_0)|^2.
\]

It records the parity of the zero multiplicity and remains nonzero.

## Comparison with the rung-four residual

The required placewise factor is

\[
1-p^{-2\operatorname{Re}z_0}.
\]

No such factor appears in the reciprocal jet identity. The functional equation
relates the two global jets by parity and conjugation; it does not compare the
local prime transport amplitudes at `z0` and `-bar z0` separately.

Thus no source-derived coefficient can identify the reciprocal jet pairing
with

\[
\bigl(1-p^{-2\operatorname{Re}z_0}\bigr)E_p(b_{z_0})
\]

without adding the missing local transport relation by hand.

## Disposition

The reciprocal first-jet pairing is a nonvanishing based boundary probe. It
supplies multiplicity parity and phase orientation. It does not supply the
transverse prime factor required for rung-four closure.

A viable probe must retain both the first nonzero global jet and a local
valuation-transport coordinate. The missing identity is their compatibility,
not either coordinate separately.
