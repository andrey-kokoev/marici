# Positive collocated Xi realization is the RH-bearing gate

Let `A=A*` on a Hilbert space and let a positive conservative realization have one collocated port `b`, with transfer

$$
F(z)=\langle b,(A-zI)^{-1}b\rangle
$$

up to a fixed positive scalar and the declared sign convention. For `Im z != 0`, the resolvent identity gives

$$
\operatorname{Im}F(z)
=(\operatorname{Im}z)
\|(A-zI)^{-1}b\|^2
$$

with the corresponding overall sign if the opposite resolvent convention is used.

Hence `F` is a Herglotz/Nevanlinna function on each half-plane and cannot vanish there unless the port is trivial. Therefore every zero of a nontrivial collocated transfer lies on the self-adjoint spectral seam.

If a source-derived positive conservative realization has transfer

$$
F(z)=u(z)\tau(z),
$$

where `u` is a nowhere-zero authorized unit and `tau=Xi_centered`, then all Xi zeros lie on the seam. Multiplicity is preserved when `u` is nonvanishing.

Thus constructing the missing positive colocation metric is not routine bialgebraic bookkeeping. It is exactly the RH-bearing confinement theorem. The already established Hopf/groupoid coherence transports such a metric if it exists, but cannot create its positivity.

The native Xi pencil avoids circularity by using distinct forcing and endpoint ports. Identifying them through a positive metric is precisely the unresolved source theorem. An indefinite metric yields reciprocal symmetry only and permits nonreal conjugate zeros.
