# Higher-coherence topology iteration 46: analytic wavefront topology detects the Xi conormal sector, but positivity does not exclude it

## Candidate topology

Realify the spectral parameter and realize the codiagonal cokernel in analytic
distributions or hyperfunctions. Equip it with the analytic wavefront topology.
A class supported on the Xi divisor

\[
Z=V(\tau)
\]

has wavefront contained in, and generically equal to, the conormal bundle
`N^*Z`.

The torsion class from iterations 42--45 would therefore appear as a
microlocal boundary sector rather than as an invisible algebraic kernel.

## Detection

Labelled theta coefficients and their normally convergent synthesis are
analytic away from declared endpoint singularities, hence have empty analytic
wavefront there. A nonzero class in

\[
\operatorname{Tor}_1(Q,O_Z)
\]

must acquire a conormal singularity after passage to the quotient or boundary
realization.

This gives a concrete falsifier: construct a distributional representative of
`[H_border]` and test whether its analytic wavefront contains `(z_0,d tau)`.

## Positivity does not remove conormals

Positive distributions may be concentrated on a hypersurface: a positive
multiple of a delta measure is the basic example. Therefore positivity of the
Haar energy does not imply an empty conormal wavefront and cannot by itself
exclude the Xi-supported torsion sector.

Likewise, Hermitian positivity controls coefficient signs, not cotangent
support.

## Reciprocal propagation

Reciprocal symmetry transports wavefront covectors by the cotangent lift of
`z -> 1-conjugate(z)` (or the chosen realified involution). It pairs or fixes
conormal directions but does not force them to disappear. A symmetric sum of
two conormal delta sectors is still nonzero and positive.

At an isolated Xi zero in a one-complex-dimensional base, the real conormal
fiber is already large, so reciprocal invariance imposes especially little
microlocal restriction.

## Unique continuation

Holmgren-type uniqueness would exclude a solution supported on one side of a
noncharacteristic analytic hypersurface. But invoking it requires exactly the
principal-symbol noncharacteristicity left open in iteration 45. Wavefront
language does not supply that estimate automatically.

A propagation theorem can move an existing singularity along bicharacteristics;
it cannot show that its initial conormal amplitude is zero.

## Strictness issue

A conormal quotient class can emerge from loss of closed range even when every
source vector is analytic. Thus proving empty wavefront at each finite cutoff
is insufficient. One needs a uniform microlocal estimate that makes the
codiagonal range closed in the Köthe limit and controls conormal amplitudes.

## Verdict for topology 46

Analytic-wavefront topology makes the missing Xi-torsion observable and offers
a finite-cutoff symbol test. Neither positivity nor reciprocal symmetry
excludes a divisor-supported conormal sector. The decisive condition remains a
uniform noncharacteristic estimate.

The next nonredundant topology to test is a microlocal energy or positive-
commutator topology, asking whether an independently sourced escape function
can prove such a uniform noncharacteristic estimate at the Xi divisor.
