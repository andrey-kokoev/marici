# Flavor Observer Count Is Signed Character Rank

## Answer

Flavor probably needs one unsigned observer plus one or two signed observers.
The exact number of signed observers is determined by how many independent
sign torsors remain after source coherence is imposed.

An unsigned observer reconstructs projective shape: spectral weights, moments,
cyclicity magnitude, and other ray-level data. Signed observers compare that
shape against orientation frames.

## Two-torsor hostile

Let

\[
f\in\{-1,1\}
\]

denote the ordered-family frame and let

\[
v\in\{-1,1\}
\]

denote the volume-reference frame. Suppose the first signed observer sees only

\[
s_1=fv.
\]

Then

\[
(f,v)
\quad\hbox{and}\quad
(-f,-v)
\]

produce the same value. One signed observer distinguishes two classes but
leaves a two-element fiber.

This remains true no matter how complete the unsigned observer is, because
unsigned channels are invariant under both flips.

## Two possible repairs

### Second signed observer

Add an independent signed character, for example

\[
s_2=f.
\]

The pair \((s_1,s_2)\) reconstructs both signs:

\[
f=s_2,
\qquad
v=s_1s_2.
\]

Thus two independent signed observers separate all four torsor states.

### Source coherence

Alternatively, a source law may tie one frame to the other or fix one torsor.
If the reachable locus satisfies

\[
v=1,
\]

then \(s_1=f\), and one signed observer is faithful on that restricted locus.

The coherence relation must be constructed before observation. Fitting it from
the desired sign is circular.

## Character-rank rule

Let the surviving discrete frame group be

\[
G\cong C_2^r.
\]

Every signed observer carries a character of \(G\). Collect those characters
as rows of a matrix over the two-element field. The observers distinguish all
frame states exactly when that character matrix has rank \(r\).

Therefore:

- rank-zero observers are unsigned;
- one independent signed observer resolves one sign direction;
- two independent signed observers resolve two sign directions;
- redundant signed observers with the same character add no information.

## Flavor interpretation

The current programme has at least two candidate frame coordinates:

1. ordering and cyclic alignment of the two Flavor families;
2. the phase or volume reference needed to descend a signed Krylov observer.

If a microscopic mediator derives a coherence relation joining these frames,
then the minimal readout is:

- one complete unsigned moment observer;
- one signed relational observer.

If the frames remain independent, the minimal readout is:

- one complete unsigned moment observer;
- two signed observers carrying independent characters.

The second signed observer cannot be another copy of the first measurement. It
must respond differently to at least one frame reversal.

## Staged observer architecture

The observers should not necessarily be parallel measurements of one finished
object. Their types naturally belong to different construction stages.

### Projective stage

Before any orientation frame is chosen, an unsigned observer records the
moment tower, spectral weights, and positive cyclicity. Its output is invariant
under seed rephasing and family reversal. This observer establishes the shape
that later comparisons must share.

### Frame-construction stage

After the mediator has aligned the two Flavor families, a first signed observer
may record the family-orientation character. It is undefined before alignment;
assigning it earlier would turn chart labels into physical data.

### Reference-comparison stage

After a volume or phase reference has been constructed, a later signed observer
compares the Krylov history with that reference. It is undefined before the
reference exists.

### Terminal reconciliation

The final node does not average or overwrite the observer outputs. It forms
their compatibility pullback: all records must refer to the same preparation,
evolution, family alignment, epoch, and detector frame. Only then may the
unsigned shape and signed characters be combined into one Flavor statement.

This explains how two observers can be enough without being interchangeable.
If source coherence has already joined the family and volume frames, the early
unsigned observer and one late signed observer suffice. If the frames are
constructed independently, their two signed stage records must both survive to
the terminal reconciliation.

Deleting an intermediate signed record cannot be repaired by recomputing it
from the final scalar. The scalar is downstream of the very distinction that
was erased.

## Physical boundary

Not every mathematical frame sign should become observable. If a sign is pure
gauge, the correct operation is quotienting it out, not adding an instrument to
measure it. Signed observer counting begins only after the physical source has
declared which relative orientations survive as operative distinctions.

For the CKM-like problem, the unsigned moment tower can reconstruct the
projective seed weights. A signed CP-sensitive invariant could then select a
conjugate sheet only if its reference frame is source-calibrated. If family and
volume frames are separately operative, a second independent comparison is
needed.

## Exact falsifier

The checker enumerates the four states of \(C_2\times C_2\). One signed product
has two-element fibers. Two independent signed characters give singleton
fibers. Restricting by one source coherence relation also makes one signed
observer faithful.
