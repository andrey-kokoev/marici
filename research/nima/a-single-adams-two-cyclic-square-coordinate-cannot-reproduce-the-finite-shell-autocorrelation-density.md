# A single Adams-two cyclic-square coordinate cannot reproduce the finite-shell autocorrelation density

## Question

Can the first Adams-two diagonal coordinate, with its matching \(p^{-2}\)
order, supply the exact completed-theta shell correction rather than only its
leading asymptotic coefficient?

## Claim boundary

No. The finite-shell correction is the Laplace transform of a nontrivial bulk
density. A single cyclic-square scalar or a finite endpoint multiple cannot
retain that density. The grade-two coordinate can match one asymptotic
coefficient only. Exact cancellation requires a function-valued history/linking
response or an infinite source identity whose Laplace density equals the shell
autocorrelation.

## Completed-theta density

For shell \([a,b]\),

\[
 I_{a,b}^{(0)}(z)
 =-\int_0^\infty e^{-zt}\rho_{a,b}(t)\,dt,
\]

where

\[
 \rho_{a,b}(t)
 =\int_a^b\Phi(x)\Phi(x+t)\,dx.
\]

Label by label, \(\rho_{a,b}\) is the explicit incomplete-gamma ordered-pair
kernel. It varies with the full separation coordinate \(t\), both shell
endpoints, and all retained theta labels.

## One cyclic-square coordinate

The first balanced Adams-two coefficient is

\[
 w_{p,2}^2=\frac14p^{-2}.
\]

This is one scalar coordinate on the Euler cyclic-square carrier. Multiplying
it by a fixed endpoint column or the proposed scalar \(-1/\pi\) changes only
its normalization. It does not create the separation density
\(\rho_{a,b}(t)\).

A finite endpoint realization has density in a finite span of

\[
 t^j\Phi(a+t),
 \qquad
 t^j\Phi(b+t).
\]

The shell autocorrelation instead integrates translated forcing over every
interior point \(x\in[a,b]\).

## Transform injectivity

On a right half-plane, the Laplace transform is injective on these rapidly
decaying densities. Therefore an exact identity for all \(z\) in an open set
would force equality of source densities.

Matching

\[
 -\frac1{4\pi}p^{-2}
\]

in the late-shell expansion does not imply such equality. It fixes one Watson
coefficient and discards the remaining density.

## Jet consequence

A scalar grade-two match may reproduce a selected value or leading jet
asymptotic. Candidate one requires every jet below each Xi multiplicity:

\[
 \partial_z^jI_{a,b}^{(0)}(z)
 =(-1)^{j+1}\int_0^\infty
 t^je^{-zt}\rho_{a,b}(t)\,dt.
\]

All moments of the density enter. A single cyclic-square coordinate cannot
supply this moment family unless a prior source theorem reconstructs
\(\rho_{a,b}\) from it, which would contradict the known bulk-versus-endpoint
separation without additional nonlocal data.

## What remains viable

Three Adams-related architectures remain logically possible:

1. an infinite even-grade response whose summed density equals
   \(\rho_{a,b}\);
2. an Adams-two carrier tensored with a retained separation-history module;
3. a comparison from the completed-theta pair density into a nonlocal linking
   port, with the cyclic-square coordinate recording only its leading
   arithmetic grade.

Each requires an explicit density-level map. The scalar coefficient match
alone authorizes none of them.

## Hostile

Take two finite-shell densities with the same leading Watson coefficient but
different first separation moments. They agree at the grade-two asymptotic
coordinate and disagree in the first parameter derivative of their Laplace
transforms. This rejects any scalar-only comparison.

## Disposition

The Adams-two grade-two coordinate is demoted from a possible complete
diagonal response to a leading asymptotic coordinate. Exact candidate-one
cancellation still requires the full autocorrelation density, or a
source-equivalent nonlocal response retaining all its Laplace moments. No RH
conclusion is authorized.
