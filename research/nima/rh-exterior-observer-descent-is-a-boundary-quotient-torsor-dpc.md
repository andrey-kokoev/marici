# RH exterior-observer descent is a boundary-quotient torsor DPC

## The categorical object

Let (i:H\to B) embed the admitted interior state object into the completed
boundary-bearing carrier. Let (ell:H\to\mathbb C) be the ordinary observer
in its convergence chamber. An exterior observer is an extension

\[
\beta:B\to\mathbb C,
\qquad
\beta i=\ell.
\]

If one extension exists, all extensions form an affine space over

\[
\operatorname{Ann}(iH)
\cong
(B/iH)^*.
\]

This is the deeper DPC. The ambiguity is not an arbitrary normalization
constant. It is a covector on the entire retained boundary quotient.

## Pathwise form

Suppose source-authorized boundary approaches are indexed by paths (p), and
each constructs an extension \(\beta_p\). For two paths define

\[
\delta_{pq}=\beta_q-\beta_p.
\]

Because both paths agree with \(\ell\) on the interior, \(\delta_{pq}\)
annihilates (iH) and therefore descends to ((B/iH)^*\). For three paths,

\[
\delta_{pq}+\delta_{qr}=\delta_{pr}.
\]

The comparison residues automatically form a Čech one-cocycle. This
telescoping law does not prove that the cocycle vanishes. It merely types the
remaining disagreement.

## The three DPC verdicts

Let the source laws impose linear constraints on the boundary quotient.
Exactly three outcomes are possible.

1. Unique descent: the source constraints separate every quotient direction,
   so the admissible extension fiber has dimension zero.
2. Residual boundary torsor: the constraints leave a nonzero quotient-dual
   direction. Different approaches remain observationally indistinguishable
   on the interior but disagree at the boundary.
3. Inconsistent boundary laws: no extension satisfies all source constraints.
   This is detected when the augmented constraint rank exceeds the coefficient
   rank.

Scalar analytic continuation tests at most the scalar section it is given. It
cannot establish unique descent unless that section separates the complete
boundary quotient. In the finite checker, one scalar constraint on a
three-dimensional quotient leaves a two-dimensional torsor.

## What a coherencer must do

A coherencer is not a declaration that two scalar limits agree. It is a
source-derived comparison cell whose action on every retained wall direction
is known. Operationally, it must make the restriction map from admissible
exterior observers to the interior observer injective.

For the RH carrier, the separating family must include the actual independent
boundary directions generated by:

- the constant overlap and its delta incidence;
- primitive and square conormal grades;
- the archimedean heat boundary;
- reciprocal Fourier–Tate sewing and any mixed-seam residual that survives
  the declared relations.

This list names source roles, not an asserted quotient dimension. Relations
among them must be computed before counting independent probes.

## Hostile tests

The DPC has four immediate falsifiers.

1. Interior alias: two extensions agree throughout the convergence chamber
   but differ on one retained boundary wall.
2. Scalar alias: two extensions give the same completed scalar section but
   different Green currents.
3. Loop residue: a source-authorized comparison loop has nonzero
   \(\delta\in(B/iH)^*\).
4. Inconsistency: two source laws demand different values on the same boundary
   direction.

Any one blocks canonical observer descent. Conversely, vanishing of these
finite residuals at each cutoff is still insufficient without continuity and
closedness through the authorized completion.

## Scope boundary

Unique exterior-observer descent would establish provenance and remove path
ambiguity. It would not prove RH. A separate mixed Green identity must show
that a zero of the descended observer forces the positive interval energy to
be multiplied by the horizontal displacement with no uncontrolled boundary
residue. This separation avoids turning coherence into another disguised
positivity assertion.

## Next finite calculation

Construct the boundary quotient at one labelled theta cutoff, reduce the five
source roles by their proved relations, and form the matrix whose rows are the
source-authorized observer constraints. Compute:

- coefficient rank;
- augmented rank;
- the nullspace of residual boundary covectors;
- the comparison cocycle for at least two independent approaches.

The first nonzero null vector is the finite exterior-observer ambiguity. Full
rank with consistent values advances the programme to uniform completion and
the mixed Green orientation theorem.
