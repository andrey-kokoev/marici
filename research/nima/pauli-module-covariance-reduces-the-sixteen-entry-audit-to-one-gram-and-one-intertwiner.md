# Pauli module covariance reduces the sixteen-entry audit to one Gram and one intertwiner

## Algebraic redundancy

Let

\[
O_X=JX,
\qquad
O_Y=JY.
\]

Because \(X^2=I\),

\[
O_Y=O_XU,
\qquad
U=XY.
\]

For the frozen matrices,

\[
U=
\begin{pmatrix}
-i&0\\
0&i
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
U^*U=I.
\]

Hence the full output linking Gram is generated by the single block

\[
H=O_X^*O_X
\]

and the right module action of \(U\):

\[
O_X^*O_Y=HU,
\]

\[
O_Y^*O_X=U^*H,
\]

\[
O_Y^*O_Y=U^*HU.
\]

Thus sixteen scalar endpoint/output entries are not independent.

## Correct minimal theorem

Let \(\widetilde O_X\) and \(\widetilde O_Y\) be the completed
theta-history outputs. It is sufficient to prove:

1. the polarized \(X\)-channel Gram identity
   \[
   \widetilde O_X^*\widetilde O_X=O_X^*O_X;
   \]
2. the Pauli module covariance
   \[
   \widetilde O_Y=\widetilde O_XU.
   \]

The remaining three output-channel blocks then follow algebraically.

The first statement is four endpoint matrix-unit identities. The second is a
linear intertwining theorem fixing the relative output phase. Together they
replace the raw sixteen-entry audit.

## Why the covariance is not optional

If only the two diagonal output Grams are matched, one may replace

\[
\widetilde O_Y
\longmapsto
e^{i\varphi}\widetilde O_Y
\]

without changing either diagonal block. The mixed blocks rotate, and the
reciprocal orientation can reverse.

The module law forbids this free phase because \(U=XY\) is fixed by the
source Pauli action. It is therefore the constructor-level authority for the
relative channel phase.

This also clarifies the division of labor:

- the \(X\)-channel Gram carries the complete raw polarization;
- the module intertwiner fixes how the \(Y\) output is generated;
- the diagonal trace of the two channels supplies uniform coercivity.

## Completed-domain requirement

The identity

\[
\widetilde O_Y=\widetilde O_XU
\]

must hold as an equality of closed graphs, not merely on finite endpoint
coordinates. In particular:

- \(U\) preserves the rapid prime core;
- \(U\) respects wall and odd typing;
- the common core remains a core for both closed output maps;
- radical quotients commute with the right \(U\)-action;
- reciprocal sewing uses the same sign convention;
- twisted Mellin prime projections commute with \(U\).

If the equality holds only before closure, the mixed linking blocks need not
survive completion.

## Coercivity without raw inversion

The analysis operator is

\[
\mathcal O=
\begin{pmatrix}
O_X\\
O_Y
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its frame operator is

\[
\mathcal O^*\mathcal O
=
H+U^*HU
=
2\operatorname{diag}(b,a).
\]

Uniform endpoint lower bounds therefore give a uniform lower frame bound,
even though \(H=XGX\) itself inherits the soft raw disagreement.

No inverse of \(H\) or \(G\) is required.

## Minimal hostiles

1. Match the \(X\)-channel Gram but choose
   \(\widetilde O_Y=e^{i\varphi}\widetilde O_XU\).
2. Prove module covariance on the algebraic core but lose it after closure.
3. Let the wall quotient fail to be invariant under \(U\).
4. Preserve the diagonal trace while replacing \(U\) by \(U^*\).
5. Use a prime-dependent phase \(e^{i\varphi_p}\), invisible to local frame
   bounds but incompatible with global sewing.

## Verdict

The full Pauli linking Gram is the faithful quadratic target, but its finite
audit has a smaller source-native presentation. One polarized channel Gram
plus the fixed Pauli right-module covariance generates every output pairing.

The earliest remaining Adams gate can therefore be attacked as two sharply
typed statements:

\[
\text{one quadratic Gram identity}
\quad+quad
\text{one linear module intertwiner}.
\]

This is strictly stronger than a frame estimate and substantially smaller
than sixteen unrelated scalar checks.
