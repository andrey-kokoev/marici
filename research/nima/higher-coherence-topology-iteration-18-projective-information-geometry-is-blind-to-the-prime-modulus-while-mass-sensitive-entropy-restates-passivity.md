# Higher-coherence topology iteration 18: projective information geometry is blind to the prime modulus, while mass-sensitive entropy restates passivity

## Candidate topology

Represent positive route states by positive functionals or density operators and
compare them using relative entropy, Fisher information, or projective cone
metrics. Data-processing inequalities could then make higher coherence
contractive without requiring equality of vectors.

## Normalized-state geometry

The two prime routes differ by a positive scalar:

\[
\rho_+=p^{-2a}\rho,
\qquad
\rho_-=p^{2a}\rho,
\qquad a=\operatorname{Re}z.
\]

After normalization to trace one,

\[
\frac{\rho_+}{\operatorname{Tr}\rho_+}
=
\frac{\rho}{\operatorname{Tr}\rho}
=
\frac{\rho_-}{\operatorname{Tr}\rho_-}.
\]

Therefore every information geometry defined on normalized rays—Fisher,
Bures, Fubini--Study, or ordinary state relative entropy—assigns zero distance
to the two routes for every `a`. It discards exactly the total-mass coordinate
that carries the Haar residual.

The Hilbert projective metric on a positive cone has the same scalar
invariance and is likewise blind.

## Mass-sensitive divergences

For unnormalized positive functionals, an extended relative entropy includes a
mass term. In the scalar one-ray case, its asymmetry detects the factors
`p^(plus-or-minus 2a)`. But data processing holds under a common
trace-preserving or trace-nonincreasing channel. Establishing that hypothesis
for both reciprocal routes is the passivity theorem from iterations 16--17.

Thus mass-sensitive entropy can measure the residual but does not force it to
vanish.

## Thompson cone metric

Unlike the projective metric, the Thompson metric retains scale. On one ray,

\[
d_T(c\rho,d\rho)=|\log(c/d)|.
\]

Hence

\[
d_T(\rho_+,\rho_-)
=4|a|\log p.
\]

This gives an exact geometric size for the reciprocal mismatch. Requiring the
two routes to have zero Thompson distance is equivalent to `a=0`; topology has
repackaged, not proved, confinement.

## Repeated-cone behavior

Monotone information metrics contract under admitted stochastic/CP maps.
Repeated higher fillers could drive the route distance to zero if they formed a
common primitive or quantum Markov semigroup with a strict contraction
coefficient. But the forward and reciprocal scalar channels have inverse gains.
No common strict contraction coefficient exists off seam without separate
renormalization.

Separate normalization collapses the projective distance immediately and
again loses the Haar mass.

## Verdict for topology 18

Information-geometric topologies split into two classes:

- projective/normalized geometries erase the prime modulus and cannot detect
  confinement;
- mass-sensitive geometries detect it exactly but require common passivity or
  zero route distance, which is the missing theorem.

No data-processing shortcut forces the reciprocal gains to agree.

The next nonredundant topology to test is an optimal-transport/Wasserstein
geometry on logarithmic prime-position measures, where reciprocal dilation
becomes translation and higher coherence might impose barycentric balance.
