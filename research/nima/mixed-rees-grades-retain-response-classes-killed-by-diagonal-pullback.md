# Mixed Rees grades retain response classes killed by diagonal pullback

## Claim boundary

Inclusive diagonal pullback removes branch-odd observables. It does not prove that their source-derived response classes are absent. Those classes may survive in the conormal grade, and interactions between temporal completion and branch difference may first appear in a mixed Rees grade invisible to either separate first-jet audit.

This packet gives the local algebraic theorem and finite falsifier. It does not establish that a particular cosmological coefficient occupies the mixed grade.

## Diagonal ideal

Let the doubled branch coordinates be \(\phi_+\) and \(\phi_-\). Define

\[
\Delta=\phi_+-\phi_-
\]

and let \(I_\Delta=(\Delta)\) be the diagonal ideal.

Inclusive closure is the pullback

\[
R\longrightarrow R/I_\Delta.
\]

Every branch-odd function in \(I_\Delta\) vanishes under this map. The first conormal grade

\[
I_\Delta/I_\Delta^2
\]

retains its coefficient of \(\Delta\).

Thus value-level inclusive closure and difference-source response are different functors.

## Two independent normals

Let \(E\) be a temporal-boundary or decompactification normal coordinate. The local bifiltration is

\[
F^{p,q}=(E^p)\cap(I_\Delta^q).
\]

Its associated bigraded object records coefficients by temporal order \(p\) and branch-difference order \(q\).

The operations of setting \(E=0\), pulling back \(\Delta=0\), and taking either normal derivative need not detect a class supported only in bidegree \((1,1)\).

## Smallest mixed-grade hostile

Take

\[
f(E,\Delta)=E\Delta.
\]

Then:

\[
f(E,0)=0,
\qquad
f(0,\Delta)=0.
\]

Taking an \(E\)-derivative after diagonal pullback gives zero, and taking a \(\Delta\)-derivative after temporal specialization also gives zero:

\[
\partial_E f(E,0)=0,
\qquad
\partial_\Delta f(0,\Delta)=0.
\]

But the mixed derivative is

\[
\partial_E\partial_\Delta f=1.
\]

Therefore two separate value-plus-first-jet audits do not reconstruct the bifiltered jet.

## Order of operations

For a general local expansion

\[
f(E,\Delta)=
a_{00}+a_{10}E+a_{01}\Delta+a_{11}E\Delta+
O(E^2,\Delta^2),
\]

ordinary inclusive pullback sees \(a_{00}+a_{10}E\). The branch conormal jet at fixed \(E\) sees \(a_{01}+a_{11}E\). If temporal specialization is performed before retaining that jet, \(a_{11}\) is lost.

The coefficient \(a_{11}\) is not a correction to either axis. It is independent mixed incidence data.

## Source-authority gate

A mixed-grade response claim is admitted only when:

1. both normal coordinates are source-derived;
2. the doubled branch carrier exists before diagonal pullback;
3. difference-source differentiation is an authorized operation;
4. the temporal filtration and its specialization are independently typed;
5. the two operations act on a common domain;
6. the mixed coefficient is computed before either lossy quotient;
7. a finite deletion test removes one normal operation and destroys the class.

An arbitrary formal thickening introduced after the inclusive observable vanishes would be circular.

## Relation to event-measure jets

The flavor support-zero hostile has the same logical shape at one normal coordinate: a central value and first derivative vanish while a second-order support term survives. The doubled cosmological construction adds a second normal coordinate, so the first missing datum may instead be a mixed coefficient.

The common rule is:

> Quotient values do not determine the normal or mixed jets of the source family. Those jets require source-derived thickening directions and must be taken before the quotient that kills them.
