# The global Fourier boundary retract closes first on the observer cylinder

## The typing correction

The local Fourier exchange theorem acts canonically on boundary observers, not
on an endpoint state plane. At a place \(v\), define

\[
\epsilon_v(f)=f(0),
\qquad
\mu_v(f)=\int f.
\]

With self-dual Haar normalization,

\[
(\epsilon_v,\mu_v)\mathcal F_v
=
(\mu_v,\epsilon_v).
\]

Thus the canonical boundary object is

\[
E_{\partial,v}^*
=
\operatorname{span}\{\epsilon_v,\mu_v\},
\]

and Fourier acts there by

\[
B=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

A state-valued endpoint retract requires an additional source metric or
adjunction. It cannot be inferred from the observer square alone.

At every unramified place,

\[
\epsilon_p(1_{\mathbb Z_p})
=

This is precisely what fails for finite Euler truncation: omitting infinitely
many Euler factors changes infinitely many local source vectors.

## Closed extension

Let \(P_\partial\) denote the complete observer or state boundary projection.
On the cylinder core,

\[
P_\partial\mathcal F_{\mathbb A}
=
\mathcal F_{\partial,\mathbb A}P_\partial.
\]

Both Fourier operators are unitary. If \(P_\partial\) is bounded in the
source-completed graph norm, this identity passes to the closure.

The boundedness is already supplied locally by the retained theta endpoint
metric. Globally it requires the graph norm to contain the restricted
boundary metric rather than only scalar Tate synthesis.

Under that declared norm, the reciprocal attachment preserves:

- the split boundary plane;
- its wall and jump characters;
- zero radical;
- the endpoint coercivity margin;
- and the archimedean line extension.

## What remains unproved

The observer-cylinder theorem does not identify the completed arithmetic
prime-current graph with this adelic boundary state. Prime currents arise
downstream through determinant logarithms and are not Fourier-stable source
states.

The remaining comparison is therefore not global Poisson sewing itself. It is
the constructor map

\[
\text{adelic boundary observer/state cylinder}
\longrightarrow
\text{theta--Adams endpoint graph}
\]

and the proof that this map preserves the split endpoint metric before scalar
connected readout.

In particular, one must not demand an endomorphism of the primitive
\(\ell^2(\mathbb P)\) port.

## Hostiles

1. Treat \(\epsilon_v\) and \(\mu_v\) as endpoint states without a Riesz or
   adjunction map. The Fourier square is then type-correct only in the dual.
2. Choose a local endpoint metric not invariant under \(B\). Observer exchange
   survives while state sewing ceases to be unitary.
3. Use a restricted-product vacuum not fixed by local Fourier. The global
   exchange creates infinitely many excitations.
4. Complete only in scalar Tate norm. The endpoint projection can become
   unbounded even though scalar Poisson summation remains valid.
5. Apply Fourier directly to the prime-labelled Hilbert port. Additive support
   leaves the prime labels.

## Verdict

Global Fourier--Poisson sewing preserves the restricted boundary-observer
cylinder exactly because the unramified observer vacuum is Fourier fixed.
The source metric \(2I\) converts the local observer exchange into a unitary
wall--jump state exchange, and the identity extends globally whenever the
completed graph norm retains the boundary metric.

The earliest remaining arrow is now the metric-compatible comparison from the
adelic boundary cylinder to the theta--Adams endpoint graph. Scalar functional
equation agreement cannot replace that comparison.
