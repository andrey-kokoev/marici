# Dual variance removes the internal helicity-lift ambiguity

## Retyping the Cut

Entry 1575 exhibited two lifts after representing both Cut occurrences as
helicity ket spaces. The physical polarization trace is instead the canonical
coevaluation

\[
\omega=e_+\otimes e^+ + e_-\otimes e^-
\in V\otimes V^*.
\]

Under an arbitrary frame change \(U\in GL(V)\), the dual occurrence transforms
contragrediently:

\[
U\otimes U^{-T}.
\]

The exact identity is

\[
(U\otimes U^{-T})\omega=\omega.
\]

This includes the helicity swap. Swapping only one occurrence changes
\(\omega\), exactly as Entry 1575 found; swapping the state and dual frames
together leaves it fixed.

## Consequence

The two-lift fiber is real in a mistyped \(V\otimes V\) presentation but is
gauge in the correctly typed internal Cut \(V\otimes V^*\). Thus no
source-chosen helicity-preserving-versus-exchanging convention is needed for
an unmeasured internal edge. Entry 45's trace-strict theorem already has the
right invariant target once its variance is made explicit.

This does not trivialize Bell settings. Alice and Bob's analyzers act on two
external output spaces, not on a state/dual Cut pair. Holding an analyzer
fixed while changing only its state frame changes the physical experiment;
co-transporting the analyzer is merely a change of description.

\[

\]

That map—not helicity naming on an internal Cut—is now the sharpest Bell
