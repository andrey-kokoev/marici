# A Positive Vacuum Anchors the Real Determinant Only Through a Normalized Incidence

## Anchor data

Let \(H\) be a source state space with Real involution \(c_H\), and let
\(L\) be the Real determinant line with involution \(c_L\). Suppose the source
supplies a distinguished nonzero fixed vacuum

\[
c_H\Omega=\Omega.
\]

A map

\[
a:H\longrightarrow L
\]

can orient the fixed real line of \(L\) only when:

1. \(a\) is a named source-authorized incidence;
2. \(a c_H=c_L a\);
3. \(a(\Omega)\ne0\);
4. its normalization is fixed, not merely projective;
5. the image remains nonzero through completion.

Then \(a(\Omega)\) is a nonzero fixed vector and its positive real multiples
select an orientation of \(L^{c_L}\).

## Projective ambiguity

If the incidence is known only up to nonzero scalar, the orientation is not
defined. Real equivariance reduces the ambiguity on the fixed line to a real
scalar, but both \(a\) and \(-a\) remain admissible. They induce opposite
orientations while preserving rank, norm, equivariance, and determinant
magnitude.

Thus an unoriented determinant isomorphism or a projective vacuum transport
cannot anchor the Burnside fixed mark.

## Nonvanishing gate

Even a normalized incidence fails if \(a(\Omega)=0\). This is the determinant
analogue of an unobservable state: the source vacuum exists but does not reach
the orientation port.

Cutoffwise nonvanishing is also insufficient. A sequence of incidence values
may approach zero in completion, causing the orientation frame to disappear
at infinity. The completed graph norm must provide a uniform nonvanishing or
a protected one-dimensional isomorphism.

## Candidate source anchors

The positive Gaussian and primitive Fock vacuum are plausible source vectors.
Neither is yet an anchor for the zero determinant line. The missing artifact
is an exact incidence chain:

`source vacuum -> tail-seam boundary object -> Real determinant line`

Each arrow must carry its normalization and completion law.

## Finite falsifiers

- Replace \(a\) by \(-a\). If every declared test still passes, the frame was
  not source-fixed.
- Choose a nonzero vacuum in the kernel of \(a\). If the construction still
  claims an orientation, it confused source positivity with determinant
  observability.
- Use \(a_N(\Omega)=1/N\). Every finite cutoff is oriented, but the completed
  anchor vanishes.

## Decisive status

The Gaussian/Fock proposal is not a positivity problem. It is a normalized
incidence and completion-stability problem. Once those maps are constructed,
the vacuum does canonically orient the Real determinant line. Before then,
calling it positive does not transfer authority.
