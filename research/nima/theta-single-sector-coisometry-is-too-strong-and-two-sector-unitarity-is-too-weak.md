# Single-sector coisometry is too strong and two-sector unitarity is too weak

## Scalar Hardy classification

Let \(\phi\) be a bounded analytic scalar symbol on a Hardy domain. Multiplication defines

\[
T_\phi h=\phi h.
\]

If \(\phi\) is inner, then

\[
T_\phi^*T_\phi=I.
\]

Thus \(T_\phi\) is an isometry. It is coisometric exactly when

\[
T_\phi T_\phi^*=I,
\]

which is equivalent to

\[
\phi H^2=H^2.
\]

For a scalar inner function, this happens only when \(\phi\) is a constant unimodular function. Any nonconstant Blaschke or singular inner factor has a nonzero model space

\[
K_\phi=H^2\ominus\phi H^2.
\]

Therefore single-sector coisometry would remove not only hostile off-sector zeros but every nontrivial scalar inner transport. It is generally too strong to serve as the source law for a nontrivial scattering or sewing symbol.

## Two-sector completion

For a contraction \(T\), define the defect operators

\[
D_T=(I-T^*T)^{1/2},
\qquad
D_{T^*}=(I-TT^*)^{1/2}.
\]

The Julia or unitary-colligation construction adjoins defect channels and produces a unitary two-sector operator from \(T\), \(D_T\), \(D_{T^*}\), and \(T^*\), with the appropriate source and target defect spaces.

The key point does not depend on one block convention:

> Every contraction admits a conservative unitary dilation after its defect spaces are retained.

For an inner multiplier \(T_B\),

\[
D_{T_B}=0,
\]

while

\[
D_{T_B^*}=P_{K_B}.
\]

The zero model space is exactly the additional channel needed to complete the one-sided isometry into a conservative two-sector system.

## Consequence

Global two-sector unitarity does not forbid an inner divisor. It routes the divisor's defect states through the complementary sector.

This produces a sharp trilemma:

1. Single-sector coisometry excludes the divisor but also collapses every nontrivial scalar inner transport.
2. Single-sector isometry permits the divisor and records it as a cokernel.
3. Two-sector unitarity restores conservation for every contraction and therefore imposes no zero restriction by itself.

The missing RH law is neither positivity nor conservation.

## Correct two-sector question

The actual question is where the defect spaces are source-authorized to live.

A source-derived Ubersector must type:

- the two open half-sector state spaces;
- the seam carrier;
- the defect input and output channels;
- the incidence of prime, square, archimedean, and seam data;
- the support on which a defect channel is allowed to appear.

RH-strength confinement would follow from a theorem that off-seam model-space defects have no authorized target channel, while seam defects do.

That theorem cannot be replaced by constructing a unitary dilation. The dilation freely creates precisely the channel whose source authority is in question.

## Authority correction

The categorical distinction is exact: the existence of a defect space does not imply that the source supplies a defect port.

Ordinary operator theory permits the Julia constructor. Source physics or arithmetic must authorize its complementary port and its support.

This is the same distinction found in the authority compiler: mathematical completion of a partial operation does not grant operative authority to the added state.

## Finite falsifier

Take a degree-one Blaschke factor \(B_a\). Its Toeplitz operator has a one-dimensional cokernel \(K_{B_a}\), yet it admits a two-sector unitary colligation after that one-dimensional defect channel is adjoined.

Therefore global two-sector unitarity does not imply the absence of interior zeros.

A surviving theorem must reject the adjoined defect port through a source typing or support rule before the zero location is inspected.

## Relation to the critical seam

The seam remains distinguished because reciprocal Fourier–Tate sewing is unitary there. But seam unitarity alone does not say that every defect state is seam-supported.

The missing localization theorem must show that the source incidence functor sends all admissible defect channels to the seam carrier and supplies no defect object in either open half-sector.

This is stronger than a scalar functional equation and more specific than global conservation.

## Disposition

Coisometry is not the right one-sector law for a nontrivial theta transport. Unitary two-sector dilation is universal and therefore nonexplanatory.

The live RH gate is a source-local defect-support theorem:

> The labelled theta/Tate Ubersector authorizes complementary defect channels only on the critical seam.

Until that support law is constructed independently of the divisor, the two-sector operator architecture geometrizes zeros without confining them.
