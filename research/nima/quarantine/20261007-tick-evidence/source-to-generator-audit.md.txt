# Source-to-generator audit: three distinct selection stages

**Question:** Does the source—at any retained level—select the continuous Hamiltonian generator?

**Answer:** The selection occurs in three stages. The source selects the first incompletely; the second is a policy; the third is a module. Treating them as one conflates structural boundaries with open targets.

## Stage A: Operations

| What | Source constructors |
|---|---|
| **expandLeft, expandRight** | `ObserverCoherenceCube.Geometry` for any `e : O ≃ R`. Generic—works for any equivalence. Not Bool-specific; the Bool instantiation is `f = notEquiv`. |
| **Involutions L,R** | L² = R² = I on the 4-element torsor (Bool²). Both commute (LR = RL). This is a faithful (Z/2)² action. |
| **RetainedReturn** from `RetainedSquareReversal` | Adds the backward section, start/end laws, source/target pairing. Record structure, not a dynamical constraint. |

**Constraint on generator:** L and R must be realized as linear operators. Their commutation forces the generator's eigenspaces to split along the two coordinate axes:  
`H = a·P_L + b·P_R` for some \(a,b\).

*The source operations select the projector decomposition but **not** the scalar weights \(a,b\).*

## Stage B: Algebra policy

| What | Prior result (Voevodsky, `source-algebra-selection-boundary.md`) |
|---|---|
| **Trivial phase** | L(a,b)=(¬a,b), R(a,b)=(a,¬b) as commuting bit flips. Four states. |
| **Clifford phase** | L⟶e1 (left mult), R⟶e2 (left mult). Anticommute: e1·e2 = -e2·e1. Uses 2×2 implementation, non-commuting, extra phase cocycle. |
| **Identical source projections** | Both produce the same word histories and pointwise readouts. The source does not select between them. |

The **Bool square uses the trivial phase** (commuting operations). The Clifford phase is a different algebra policy.

The Hamiltonian construction `H = a·P_L + b·P_R` is WITHIN the trivial-phase policy. The policy does not select \((a,b)\). The time-evolution unitary remains
\[
\exp(-i\theta H) = \exp(-i\theta a P_L)\exp(-i\theta b P_R)
\]
for ANY \(a,b\). At \(\theta=\pi\), \(L\) and \(R\) appear when \(a,b\) are odd; at \(\theta=2\pi\) identity requires \(a,b\) integers.

**The Tick's schedule pair and return live at Stage A. They do not migrate to Stage B.** Both left-first and right-first schedules use the same L,R. The route-homotopy certifies endpoint equality, which holds for any commuting P_L,P_R regardless of weight scaling. The return section reverses the combined path, which is also weight-independent.

**Algebra policy says whether L,R come from a commutative or non-commutative algebra. It alone does not fix their generator's continuous weighting.**

## Stage C: Defining module

| What | Rotor precedent | Current status |
|---|---|---|
| **Rotor case** | Finite group samples fix \(k\equiv1\pmod4\). The **defining module** (R² with \(U(\theta)w=e^{-i\theta}w\)) selects \(k=1\) at all \(\theta\). |
| **Square case** | Tick data fix \(a,b\) odd. No module yet supplied to select \((1,1)\) over \((3,1),(1,3),(3,3),\ldots\) |

A defining module for the Bool square would be an explicit declaration of what the continuous path \(|\psi(\theta)\rangle = \exp(-i\theta H)|\psi_0\rangle\) should look like for ALL \(\theta\), not just at the discrete schedule angles.

The **natural** module (tensor product of two independent 2-state systems with unit spectral gap) gives \(a=b=1\). But it is not forced by the finite Tick data—it is an additional declared input, exactly as the complex structure on R² was additional in the rotor case.

---

## What is needed to resolve the ambiguity

**Option A: Declare the natural module.** Accept that the Bool states factor as two independent binary subsystems and that the generator should assign unit weight to each projector. This gives \(H_0 = P_L + P_R\) uniquely.

Cost: this is an input, not a deduction. The Tick does not record "independence of the two bits"—it records that L and R commute and both are involutions. Many coupled generalizations also commute.

**Option B: Find a source-constructed selection principle.** Search the existing Agda modules for any construction that explicitly couples the two axes in a weight-selective way, e.g. a second-order constraint, a primitive return invariant, or a period-counting witness that forces the gap.

**Option C: Accept the ambiguity.** Document that \(a,b\) are unconstrained by the discrete retained process data, just as the rotor's \(k\) modulo 4 was unresolved by the finite samples. The defining module is then a separate forward decision.

## Concrete next executable

The rotor case resolved its \(k\equiv1\pmod4\) ambiguity via the **explicit matrix identity** \(U(\theta) = \cos\theta + J\sin\theta\) evaluated at \(\theta=\pi/4\). The Bool-square analog would be:

Pick a specific non-corner angle \(\theta\) (say \(\pi/2\), the midpoint of each half-square leg). The left-first path visits the intermediate state \(L(s)\) at the half-square. The continuous evolution under \(H\) from \(s\) at \(\theta=\pi/2\) should also reach \(L(s)\)—otherwise the schedule is not a continuous realization of the leg.

**Check:**  
For \(H = aP_L + bP_R\), is \(\exp(-i\pi a P_L / 2)|s\rangle = L(s)\)?  
- Yes if \(a\) is odd (since \(\exp(-i\pi P_L/2) = I\) restricted to P_L=0, \(i\) on P_L=1, but we need the full flip to happen at \(\theta=\pi\) not \(\pi/2\)).  

Actually at \(\theta=\pi/2\):  
\(\exp(-i\pi P_L/4)\) is not L. The midpoint of the LEFT-LEG evolution is \(i^{-1/2}\) on the flipped subspace, not L. So requiring the schedule leg's midpoint doesn't select a,b either.

**Correct test:** The leg's FULL DURATION is parameterized from 0 to \(\pi\) (half-square under P_L). This duration is FIXED by requiring that the flip completes at \(\theta=\pi\). But this is satisfied for ANY odd \(a\). The rate of evolution (how fast the phase winds) differs, but the Tick does not record rate—only that the flip completed.

The Tick records: "L was applied starting from s, and the result was L(s)." This is a single discrete transition, not a record of how long it took or how the phase interpolated. So the timetable is not fixed by the Tick.

This means the Tick's schedule structure does NOT determine the evolution rate, just as the rotor's finite samples didn't determine k=1 uniquely. The distinction between H0 and 3*H0 is a rate/weight distinction invisible to the discrete process record.

**Decision point:** Do we accept that the discrete retained process record underspecifies the continuous evolution rate, and move forward with a declared module (like the rotor did)? Or do we search for a different kind of witness that carries rate information?
