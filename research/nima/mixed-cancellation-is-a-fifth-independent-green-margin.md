# Mixed cancellation is a fifth independent Green margin

## Fifth gate

Let the completed joint state split as
\[
H=H_{\mathrm{coh}}\oplus H_{\mathrm{dis}},
\]
and let the Green Gram operator be
\[
J^*J=
\begin{pmatrix}
A&C\\
C^*&B
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Positive lower bounds for \(A\) and \(B\) establish faithfulness on each sector separately. They do not prevent the two observed outputs from cancelling.

Assume the reduced diagonal blocks are positive and invertible after authorized gauge removal. Define the normalized mixed coupling
\[
R=A^{-1/2}CB^{-1/2}.
\]
The fifth margin is
\[
\delta_{\mathrm{mix}}=1-\|R\|.
\]
A completion-valid theorem requires
\[
\|R\|\le\rho<1
\]
uniformly in cutoff and on compact \(s\)-sets.

## Schur-complement equivalence

The Green Gram is strictly positive exactly when
\[
A>0
\quad\text{and}\quad
B-C^*A^{-1}C>0,
\]
or symmetrically,
\[
B>0
\quad\text{and}\quad
A-CB^{-1}C^*>0.
\]
After normalization,
\[
B-C^*A^{-1}C
=
B^{1/2}(I-R^*R)B^{1/2}.
\]
Hence \(\|R\|<1\) is the strict mixed-cancellation condition.

If
\[
A\ge aI,\qquad B\ge bI,\qquad \|R\|\le\rho<1,
\]
then the assembled quadratic form has a lower bound controlled by
\[
(1-\rho)\min(a,b),
\]
up to the fixed norm-equivalence constants of the chosen sector decomposition.

This bound is independent evidence. It cannot be reconstructed from \(a\) and \(b\) alone.

## Output-angle interpretation

Let
\[
Y_c=\overline{J(H_{\mathrm{coh}})},
\qquad
Y_d=\overline{J(H_{\mathrm{dis}})}.
\]
The mixed margin measures the output angle between these two Green images after normalization by their internal Gramians.

This angle is distinct from the Friedrichs angle used for seam transversality:

- \(\delta_{\mathrm{glue}}\) measures the angle between arithmetic and analytic incidence images in the seam object;
- \(\delta_{\mathrm{mix}}\) measures the angle between coherent and disagreement images at the final Green evaluator.

The first stabilizes comparison. The second prevents terminal annihilation.

## Minimal hostile

Take
\[
H_{\mathrm{coh}}=H_{\mathrm{dis}}=\mathbb C
\]
and
\[
J(c,d)=c+d.
\]
Each restriction is an isometry:
\[
\|J(c,0)\|=|c|,
\qquad
\|J(0,d)\|=|d|.
\]
Thus
\[
A=B=1.
\]
But
\[
J(1,-1)=0.
\]
Here \(C=1\), \(R=1\), and both Schur complements vanish. The coherent and disagreement margins are perfect separately while the full observer has a kernel.

## Near-cancellation family

Let
\[
J_X(c,d)=c+\rho_Xd
\]
with \(\rho_X\uparrow1\). Both restrictions remain uniformly bounded below when \(\rho_X\) stays away from zero, but
\[
\delta_{\mathrm{mix},X}=1-\rho_X\to0.
\]
The normalized antisymmetric direction becomes asymptotically invisible. No sector kernel exists at any finite cutoff.

This is the exact completion escape that separate diagonal estimates miss.

## Interaction-net reading

Two continuation residues may each remain visible under separate evaluation yet carry opposite relative phase or sign at a shared terminal evaluator. Their energies do not encode this relative datum.

Therefore the complete interaction net must retain:

- sector type;
- orientation;
- phase or sign;
- terminal port;
- clutching order;
- observer-theory version.

A rewrite that preserves separate energies while forgetting relative phase can pass diagonal observer tests and still create a mixed kernel.

## Birman–Schwinger block pencil

The normalized incidence defect must be block-decomposed before spectral exclusion. If the Birman–Schwinger operator is
\[
K=
\begin{pmatrix}
K_{cc}&K_{cd}\\
K_{dc}&K_{dd}
\end{pmatrix},
\]
then an eigenvalue-one state may be generated by cross-sector cancellation even when neither diagonal block reaches one.

The spectral theorem must control the assembled block pencil, not only sector radii. A Schur reduction can transfer the problem to one sector only after proving the relevant inverse and mixed norm bounds uniformly.

## Rewrite-certificate interface

The arithmetic-analytic rewrite extension must preserve relative terminal phase as part of the external interface. In particular, critical joins between moving-seam transport and Adams weighting must agree not only in scalar coefficient but in the typed terminal evaluator.

This links the fifth analytic margin to the convergent interaction-net certificate: confluence in a coarse scalar representation does not prevent mixed cancellation in the complete observer.
