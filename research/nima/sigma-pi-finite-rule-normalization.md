# A finite complete normalization rule for the binary-index model

## Setting and notation

P(X)=Pi(i:Bool).X and S(X)=Sigma(j:Bool).X. Words list operations innermost first. Therefore the string SP denotes P(S(X)), while the named paired constructor SP in the earlier checker denotes S(P(X)); the expansion table explicitly accounts for that convention. Full expanded words and every rewriting step are stored.

## One generating equivalence

Use the oriented rule

SP -> PSS.

With all binders exposed:

Pi(i:Bool). Sigma(j:Bool). X
 ~=
Sigma(j0:Bool). Sigma(j1:Bool). Pi(i:Bool). X.

A value ((j0,x0),(j1,x1)) maps to (j0,(j1,(x0,x1))). The inverse restores the two tagged entries. The two choice indices survive separately. The constructor variable X can itself contain any preceding complete expression. The rule can also be applied inside subsequent constructors by their action on equivalences.

## Normal form and termination

Each word has a signature (a,b), starting from (0,0), with

S: (a,b)->(a+1,b),
P: (a,b)->(2a,b+1).

The normal form is the chronological word P^b S^a, whose type is

Sigma(bits:Bool^a). Pi(position:Fin(2^b)). Q.

For a word with b occurrences of P, assign the b-tuple counting how many S occur before each successive P. Rewriting one SP to PSS decreases the count at the affected P by one, leaves earlier counts unchanged, and may increase later counts. Thus the tuple strictly decreases lexicographically. Its length b stays fixed, and lexicographic order on a fixed finite power of natural numbers is well-founded. Every rewrite sequence terminates.

An irreducible word has no SP and hence consists of all P followed by all S. The rule preserves (a,b), so this irreducible word is uniquely determined. This establishes normalization independently of finite tests.

Distinct SP occurrences cannot overlap: the suffix P of SP differs from its prefix S. Replacements at disjoint occurrences commute, with their positions shifted appropriately. The resulting local diamonds are consistent with the unique normal form. At the value level the generating equivalence is just an invertible rearrangement of tagged entries; canonical normalization gives coherent comparison maps through the same retained coordinates.

## Completeness and its precise extent

Two words with the same signature reduce to the same normal form. Following one reduction chain and the inverse of the other supplies a finite equivalence chain. Every use of the rule preserves the signature. Hence the single reversible relation generates exactly equality of binary-container signatures on the expanded P,S language.

For the associated functors on finite sets, distinct signatures cannot be naturally equivalent: evaluating at Q=Unit recovers 2^a; evaluating at a two-element Q then recovers 2^(a+2^b), determining b. Thus this signature classification is also complete for existence of natural equivalence between these finite-container functors. It does not classify all natural automorphisms or higher comparison witnesses.

This gives a finite presentation for the expanded P,S model. The original three paired constructors are embedded in that language; intermediate rewriting stages can have odd length and additional S blocks. A finite presentation using only those three generators remains a distinct question. The general variable-dependent I,J,K,L grammar also requires its own formulation.

## Concrete closure result

The previously found depth-four chains

SP, PS, SP, PP
PS, PP, SP, PS

where these are the original paired-constructor names, both normalize to five P operations followed by 36 S operations. Both complete reduction routes are in the result artifact. The first depth-four equivalence is therefore generated by the single elementary distributivity rule, rather than requiring a new independent depth-four axiom in this model.
