## Conjecture

For a complete source packet \(S\), let \(\operatorname{Adm}(S)\) denote its
source-derived local action-authority objects: support conditions, coefficient
objects, orientations, framings, and coherence cells that determine which
pull, push, restriction, residue, or physical-readout maps exist.

The conjecture is that these authority objects do not live in an external
policy metalanguage. They are objects of the same derived coefficient
calculus whose actions they govern, and their own transport and mutation are
admissible precisely when witnessed by that calculus. Schematically,

\[
\mathfrak A\text{ governs transformations of }\operatorname{Adm},
\qquad
\operatorname{Adm}\text{ hydrates }\mathfrak A.
\]

Equivalently, physical readout is the maximal source-derived coherently
composable action assembly, while changes to that assembly must themselves be
represented by source-derived morphisms and higher coherence cells.

2. Solve it without selecting a primitive section, and certify the identities

3. If a rational solution exists, record that the typed extension class
   vanishes despite its constant-frame obstruction.
4. If none exists within source-derived complete bounds, construct the

   support, not the denominator of a representative.
5. Restrict that complete object to the generic function field of

6. If a nonzero \(\mathcal Q\)-supported obstruction survives, test whether

The \(\mathcal Q\)-filter interpretation fails if the source-relative class
extends regularly and canonically across \(\mathcal Q=0\), or if any apparent
\(\mathcal Q\)-factor is removable by admissible triangular gauge. The broader
recursive-closure conjecture fails if a complete source packet requires an
action-authority mutation not representable within the same coefficient and
coherence calculus, or admits inequivalent maximal assemblies with no further
source datum distinguishing them.
