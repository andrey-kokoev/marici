# A source-horizontal determinant line is the first C2 law that rejects central multipliers

## Surviving strengthened theorem

Let `U` be one open half-plane. Suppose the labelled theta/Tate source
constructs all of the following without using the completed scalar divisor:

1. a holomorphic line bundle `L` over `U`;
2. a regular holomorphic connection

\[
\nabla=d-A(s)\,ds;
\]

3. a distinguished section `sigma` generated by the source;
4. the horizontality law

\[
\nabla\sigma=0;
\]

5. one source normalization `sigma(s_*)` that is nonzero.

Then `sigma` has no zero in the connected component of `U` containing `s_*`.

In a local frame, horizontality is the linear equation

\[
\sigma'(s)=A(s)\sigma(s).
\]

A solution that vanishes at one point is identically zero by uniqueness for a
regular first-order holomorphic equation. The nonzero normalization excludes
that solution.

## Why this defeats the central multiplier

Let `h` be a nonconstant holomorphic central multiplier. If `sigma` is
horizontal, then

\[
\nabla(h\sigma)=h'\sigma\,ds.
\]

Thus `h sigma` is horizontal for the same source connection only when `h` is
constant. The reciprocal-symmetric hostile multiplier from the preceding
packet therefore fails the differential law even though it preserves every
homogeneous static coherence diagram and every finite scalar normalization.

This is the first candidate fifth-tower law in the current programme that
actually detects central divisor insertion.

## Naturality attack on a scalar connection

A connection chosen only for the distinguished scalar risks hiding the same
circularity in a different place. Requiring one scalar connection to be
natural over the source category immediately produces an obstruction.

Consider the two elementary positive source atoms at zero and one. Their
Laplace sections are

\[
F_0(s)=1,
\qquad
F_1(s)=e^s.
\]

If one scalar connection coefficient `A(s)` made both sections horizontal,
then the first equation would give

\[
0=F_0'=A F_0,
\]

so `A=0`. The second would require

\[
e^s=F_1'=A F_1=0,
\]

which is impossible.

More generally, two nonzero sections can be horizontal for the same line
connection only when their ratio is locally constant. A source category with
more than one independent transform therefore cannot carry a universal
rank-one horizontalization of every scalar readout.

This falsifies the cheapest implementation of the strengthened theorem. The
surviving architecture is larger:

- an operator-valued connection on the full labelled source-state bundle;
- a source-derived determinant-line functor;
- an induced connection on that determinant line;
- one distinguished determinant section proven horizontal by the induced
  construction.

The determinant connection must inherit its coefficient from the operator
connection, such as through a trace, supertrace, boundary anomaly, or index
construction. It cannot be fitted directly to the distinguished scalar.

## Immediate circular repair

For any nonzero scalar section one can formally set

\[
A=\frac{\sigma'}{\sigma}.
\]

This makes the section horizontal by construction. It is prohibited for two
reasons:

- it divides by the section whose zeros are under investigation;
- at every zero it creates a pole in the connection.

The regularity of a connection manufactured this way is exactly equivalent to
zero exclusion. It supplies no explanation.

## Exact hostile comparison

Start with the trivial connection `nabla=d` and horizontal normalized section
`sigma=1`. Multiply by

\[
h(s)=1+\frac{256}{3}s(s-1)(s-1/2)^2.
\]

The multiplier preserves reciprocal symmetry, equals one at zero, one half,
and one, and vanishes at one quarter and three quarters. But

\[
d(h\sigma)=h'(s)\,ds
\]

is not zero. To restore horizontality one must replace the source connection
by `d-d log h`, which is singular at the inserted zeros.

## DPC

The route passes only if all of these are established independently:

- `L` is derived from the labelled source before scalar projection;
- `A(s)` is derived without division by `sigma` or inspection of its zeros;
- `A(s)` descends naturally from an operator-valued source connection rather
  than being selected only for the distinguished scalar section;
- the connection extends regularly through restricted-product completion on
  each open half-plane;
- the actual distinguished section, not merely a transported vacuum line, is
  horizontal;
- the normalization is source-authorized;
- reciprocal sewing relates the two regular half-plane connections;
- primitive, square, seam, and archimedean currents occur in the connection
  law with their source-fixed coefficients.

Reject immediately if:

- the connection is defined as a logarithmic derivative of the completed
  scalar;
- poles are permitted exactly at the unknown divisor;
- only projective horizontality is proved, leaving a scalar gauge free;
- the connection acts on a line different from the Evans determinant line;
- finite-cutoff regularity has no completion-stable domain theorem.

## Relation to the fifth tower

Static two-cell coherence compares completed routes. A connection is its
infinitesimal, path-sensitive refinement. Horizontality says that the
distinguished section is generated by those comparisons at every infinitesimal
step. The non-homogeneous derivative term prevents a common nonconstant scalar
from passing unnoticed.

The fifth tower can therefore prove zero exclusion only if its complete family
of coherence cells differentiates to a source-regular connection on the actual
determinant line. Ordinary horn filling is too weak; source horizontality is
strong enough, but remains entirely unconstructed for theta/Tate.

## Verdict

The strengthened theorem is mathematically valid and survives the central
multiplier hostile. A universal scalar implementation fails the two-atom
naturality test. Its unresolved content is therefore sharper: construct a
regular operator-valued source connection, derive the determinant-line
connection from it, and prove horizontality without recovering either object
backward from the completed scalar section.
