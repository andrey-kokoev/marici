# Xi leading coefficient is the first positive colocation test

For a bounded source-authorized compression, the theta cross transfer has expansion

$$
F(z)=b_f^*(A-zI)^{-1}b_0
=-\frac{\langle b_f,b_0\rangle}{z}+O(z^{-2})
$$

under the `A-zI` convention. Equivalently, with `zI-A`, the leading coefficient is `+<b_f,b_0>`.

If a positive conservative metric `G` collocates the ports,

$$
Gb_0=j b_f,
\qquad j>0,
$$

then

$$
\langle b_f,b_0\rangle
=j^{-1}\langle Gb_0,b_0\rangle>0.
$$

Thus positivity forces the leading cross-transfer coefficient to be real and to have the orientation-prescribed sign. Nonvanishing alone is insufficient.

Prior source work establishes

$$
\langle b_f,b_0\rangle\ne0
$$

for the native theta source-endpoint incidence, but the inspected records do not establish its reality and positive sign in the conservative orientation. Therefore the first executable positive-symmetrizer test is

$$
\operatorname{Im}\langle b_f,b_0\rangle=0,
\qquad
\operatorname{Re}\langle b_f,b_0\rangle>0.
$$

Passing this scalar test is necessary, not sufficient: the full spectral port ratio must still be positive almost everywhere or satisfy the higher-multiplicity fiber criterion.
