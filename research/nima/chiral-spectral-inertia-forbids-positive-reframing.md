# Chiral spectral inertia forbids positive reframing

The chiral spectral coefficient is

$$
\mathcal P=
\begin{pmatrix}0&P^*\\P&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

For each nonzero singular pair of `P`, choose vectors `u,v` with

$$
Pv=\sigma u,
\qquad
P^*u=\sigma v,
\qquad \sigma>0.
$$

Then

$$
\mathcal P\binom{v}{u}=\sigma\binom{v}{u},
\qquad
\mathcal P\binom{v}{-u}=-\sigma\binom{v}{-u}.
$$

Thus every active singular channel contributes one positive and one negative direction.

For any bounded invertible frame change `S`, the transformed pencil coefficient is obtained by congruence,

$$
\mathcal P_S=S^*\mathcal P S.
$$

Sylvester inertia is invariant under such congruence. Therefore no invertible coordinate change, bounded metric reweighting, or anomaly-line scalar can make `Pcal` positive on the full active chiral carrier.

A positive coefficient can arise only by a noninvertible operation:

1. select a positive graph/subspace;
2. quotient a negative sector;
3. use a Schur/Feshbach reduction;
4. construct a different one-leg-linear dilation.

Any such operation must prove that the Xi kernel and root-chain multiplicities survive. Merely diagonalizing the chiral double or changing its metric cannot close the gate.

Status: positive reframing of the canonical chiral double ruled out; divisor-faithful positive reduction is the remaining constructor.
