# RH Three-Observer DPC Requires Function-Valued Instruments

## Result

The three-observer proposal is the minimal linearly complete RH carrier when
its ports retain the full function-valued data:

\[
(P,Q,M).
\]

Here \(P\) and \(Q\) are reciprocal tail instruments and \(M\) is the
straddling-seam instrument.

Replacing the two tails by their completed sum loses one full antisymmetric
function channel. No fixed finite tower of tangent moments can recover it.

## Finite rank core

At one fixed finite coordinate, the primitive observation matrix is the
identity on \((P,Q,M)\), so it has rank three.

The completed scalar and seam observations are

\[
A=P+Q+M,
\qquad
M=M.
\]

Their observation matrix has rank two and kernel

\[
\operatorname{span}\{(1,-1,0)\}.
\]

This is exactly the reciprocal-odd tail direction \(P-Q\).

At finite dimension, adding the odd row restores rank:

\[
P+Q,
\qquad
P-Q,
\qquad
M.
\]

But this finite calculation must not be promoted to the completed functional
carrier.

## Function-valued obstruction

Grothendieck's exact finite-difference theorem shows that for every moment
depth \(N\), two strictly positive separation measures can agree on all signed
moments through \(N\) while giving opposite cosine-transform signs later. The
first mismatch occurs at moment \(N+1\).

Therefore no fixed finite list of odd moments is faithful on the full
reciprocal tail channel.

The surviving choices are:

1. retain \(P\) and \(Q\) separately as function-valued instruments;
2. retain the complete function-valued odd channel \(P-Q\);
3. retain an infinite represented moment tower with a declared completion;
4. derive a source recurrence proving that a finite state determines the whole
   odd channel.

Without the fourth theorem, a finite tangent packet is only a truncation.

## Seam observer

The seam port \(M\) is independently necessary because it records the cell
straddling the reciprocal interface. It is even under sector exchange and
cannot replace \(P-Q\).

Thus three observers do not mean three arbitrary scalar readouts. They mean
three typed state channels:

- plus tail;
- minus tail;
- shared seam.

Their terminal scalar projections may be finite, but their retained state need
not be.

## Instrument correction

Scalar effects are still insufficient. Two instruments can emit identical
present \((P,Q,M)\) records while preparing different continuation states.
Later reciprocal transport can distinguish those states.

Each observer must therefore return a record-labelled update:

\[
\mathcal I_alpha:
x\longmapsto
(r_alpha,x'_alpha).
\]

Terminal reconciliation must compare both the records and the update lineage.
The Clifford decomposition of present effects is only the visible shadow of
this instrument system.

## DPC

The proposed RH carrier is admitted only if all gates hold:

1. \(P,Q,M\) are derived from labelled theta/Tate source operations;
2. each carries its complete function domain and boundary trace;
3. reciprocal exchange swaps \(P,Q\) and fixes \(M\);
4. cut and completion preserve their record-labelled updates;
5. terminal sewing forms a lineage pullback;
6. the source Green law controls the complete odd channel;
7. common scalar zero plus that law forces the odd current to vanish;
8. vanishing on the reachable source family forces the critical seam.

The first five construct the carrier. The last three contain the unresolved RH
force.

## Falsifiers

The architecture fails if:

- it retains only \((A,M)\);
- it replaces \(P-Q\) by a fixed finite moment list without a recurrence;
- it retains equal present records but discards different update states;
- its odd law is reconstructed from the scalar zero set;
- hostile symmetric sources satisfy the declared instrument laws while keeping
  an off-seam zero.

## Status

The operator's three-observer proposal is correct at the carrier level. The
precise formulation is three function-valued instruments, not three scalar
effects. This prevents both antisymmetric-tail erasure and seam erasure. It
still does not prove RH until a source-derived dynamical law controls the full
odd channel.
