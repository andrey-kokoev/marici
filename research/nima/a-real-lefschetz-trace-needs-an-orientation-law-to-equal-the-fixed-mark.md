# A Real Lefschetz Trace Needs an Orientation Law to Equal the Fixed Mark

## Two fixed-point readouts

For a finite zero packet with reciprocal-conjugation involution \(I\), the
Burnside mark is the unsigned count

\[
m_I(X)=|X^I|.
\]

A source-derived Real complex would more naturally provide a Lefschetz-type
trace. Such a trace is a sum of local contributions:

\[
L_I=\sum_{x\in X^I}\epsilon_x m_x,
\]

where \(m_x\) is the positive zero multiplicity and \(\epsilon_x\) records
the action on the local orientation, determinant, or coefficient line.

These quantities agree only when every local sign is source-fixed to
\(+1\).

## Cancellation hostile

Two fixed seam zeros with opposite local signs contribute two to the Burnside
fixed mark and zero to the signed Lefschetz trace. A free reciprocal orbit
also contributes zero to the trace because its two points are exchanged.

Therefore a signed trace without an orientation theorem cannot distinguish
"two fixed points with cancellation" from "no fixed points." It is not the
second Burnside observer.

## Semilinear fiber warning

The issue is sharper for anti-linear symmetry. On a one-dimensional complex
line, ordinary conjugation has real eigenvalues \(+1\) and \(-1\) after
realification, hence real trace zero. The divisor permutation line at a fixed
label instead has trace one.

Transferring a Real analytic complex to the divisor mark therefore requires a
specified local coefficient functor. One cannot identify the two traces by
calling both of them fixed-point indices.

## Required orientation theorem

A valid source-side realization of \(m_I\) must prove:

1. the zero-complex cohomology transfers to divisor multiplicity;
2. reciprocal conjugation fixes each seam local class rather than merely its
   label;
3. the induced action on the determinant or orientation line is \(+1\);
4. completion preserves that sign frame;
5. hostile symmetric multipliers lack authority to inherit the frame.

Only then does the Real Lefschetz trace equal the unsigned fixed mark.

## Relation to prior sectors

This is the same torsor-origin problem found in the Wilson-sign and
quadrature audits. A symmetry may preserve the carrier while reversing the
frame used by the readout. The tensor unit fixed the Wilson frame there. The
theta/Tate sector still needs its corresponding source normalization.

## Finite falsifier

Given any proposed fixed-point trace, evaluate two one-dimensional fixed
local classes with actions \(+1\) and \(-1\). If the trace cancels while the
claimed fixed count is two, the construction is signed and cannot establish
RH without an additional positivity theorem.

## Revised frontier

The source programme needs both:

- a Real-equivariant zero complex;
- a canonical positive orientation of its \(I\)-fixed local coefficient
  lines.

The second requirement is not cosmetic. It is what converts a character
trace into the Burnside mark that distinguishes seam-fixed zeros from free
orbits.
