# Contextual faithfulness: theorem, tautology, and falsifier

Owner: `marici.Nima`

## Claim under attack

The tempting strong claim is:

> Hidden coherence residues are precisely the data required to make
> source-authorized contextual composition faithful.

This is not true without a separate source-separation theorem.

Let `X` be a state object, `R` a readout, and `C` the monoid generated by the
authorized constructors.  Define

\[
x\sim_C y
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
R(c x)=R(c y)
\quad\text{for every }c\in C.
\]

The quotient `X/~_C` is canonical and the contextual readout is faithful on
that quotient by construction.  But this is a quotient theorem, not a proof
that the original source states are separated.

## Hostile model 1: delayed separation

Two initial states have the same present and one-step readouts.  A legal
constructor advances each through an intermediate state, and the readouts
separate only after the second composition.  Therefore a one-step successor
census is insufficient; legal composition closure is essential.

## Hostile model 2: permanent aliasing

Take four states labelled by two bits.  The only readout is the first bit and
the only constructor toggles the second bit.  Every generated context
preserves the first bit.  Consequently `(0,0)` and `(0,1)` remain
indistinguishable under the entire context monoid, as do `(1,0)` and `(1,1)`.

No retained source-generated residue repairs this.  Adding the second-bit
probe would make the semantics faithful, but that is a new constructor, not
a deduction from the old operational language.

## Correct theorem

The restricted contextual profile determines exactly the operational
quotient

\[
X\longrightarrow X/{\sim_C}.
\]

It need not determine `X`.  The quotient has the universal property that
every authorized contextual readout factors through it, and any other
quotient with that property factors through the contextual quotient.

Thus:

- contextual equivalence is canonical relative to the constructor language;
- a coordinate representation of its classes is not canonical;
- faithfulness on the quotient is automatic and explanatory only when the
  source independently explains why this is the physically relevant
  constructor language;
- faithfulness on the original source requires a separate jointly-separating
  probe theorem.

## Corrected Deutsch--Popperian conjecture

The physical identity presented by a sector is its class in the quotient by
all source-authorized contexts.  A residue is operative only when it refines
that quotient under an authorized enlargement or composition of contexts.
No residue should be claimed to restore source-level identity without an
independent jointly-faithful probe theorem.

## Falsifiers

- A pair declared contextually equivalent is separated by a generated legal
  context.
- A claimed finite-depth closure changes when the same generators are
  composed further.
- A claimed faithful source semantics has a non-singleton contextual class.
- A proposed residue separates states only after adding an unauthorized
  probe.
