# Flavor Orientation Can Only Be Relative to Another Sector

## Result

The zero-anomaly no-go does not eliminate orientation physics. It changes the
target from absolute orientation to relative orientation between two sectors.

Let \(Q\) be a reflection-odd quark charge cubic and \(R\) a reflection-odd
reference-sector cubic. Their product

\[
QR
\]

is invariant under simultaneous reflection. A source-derived interaction can
therefore select aligned or anti-aligned relative orientation without choosing
an absolute sign.

## Minimal interaction

Consider

\[
V_{\mathrm{rel}}=-\lambda QR,
\qquad
\lambda>0.
\]

The energy is lower when \(Q\) and \(R\) have the same sign. If \(\lambda<0\),
it instead prefers opposite signs.

Under simultaneous reflection,

\[
(Q,R)
\longmapsto
(-Q,-R),
\]

and the energy is unchanged. Thus the interaction selects one relative class
while retaining the global reflection pair.

That remaining twofold presentation is expected. Without an external oriented
frame, simultaneous reflection is gauge-equivalent rather than an unresolved
physical choice.

## Exact quotient census

There are four raw sign assignments:

\[
(+,+),
\quad
(+,-),
\quad
(-,+),
\quad
(-,-).
\]

Quotienting simultaneous reflection leaves two classes:

- aligned signs;
- anti-aligned signs.

The single relational character

\[
\chi=\operatorname{sgn}(QR)
\]

distinguishes those two classes faithfully. This is a case where one signed
observer is enough because the source has reduced the physical sign rank to
one.

## Candidate reference sectors

The nearest Flavor candidate is a separately derived lepton or mediator charge
sector. Its orientation cannot be fitted from the quark target. It must have:

- independently specified representations or charges;
- a nonzero signed cubic or equivalent odd character;
- a source-derived cross-coupling to the quark charge sector;
- an independent experimental criticism.

WP616's vector-resonance width ratios provide one possible criticism of a
joint quark/lepton charge architecture. They do not derive the architecture or
the sign of the coupling.

## Why anomaly cancellation is different

Separate equations

\[
F_Q(Q)=0,
\qquad
F_R(R)=0
\]

do not compare the two orientations. A joint zero equation may correlate them
only if its source structure contains both sectors and its cross-term survives.

The interaction \(QR\) is not an anomaly equation retrofitted with a nonzero
target. It is a new constructor joining two signed ports. Its coefficient,
resource scale, and stability must be derived.

## Staged observer architecture

The resulting stages are:

1. unsigned hierarchy observers reconstruct quark and reference charge
   geometries;
2. origin constraints select affine lifts in each sector;
3. signed cubic observers expose each relative-orientation port;
4. the cross-sector interaction constructs \(QR\);
5. one terminal signed observer reads the relative character;
6. physical width and mixing measurements criticize the joined model.

The signed observers at stage three need not individually define absolute
signs. Their product at stage four is the gauge-invariant relational object.

## Finite falsifiers

A proposed relative selector fails if:

- either sector's signed port vanishes;
- the cross-coupling coefficient is fitted from the desired relative sign;
- the interaction changes under simultaneous reflection;
- the reference sector is merely copied from the quark target;
- no independent experiment can criticize the reference-sector realization.

## Status

The minimal orientation programme now has a plausible form: one unsigned
observer for projective hierarchy and one signed relational observer for the
relative orientation of two independently derived sectors. Two signed
observers are needed only before their ports have been joined by the
source-derived cross-coupling.
