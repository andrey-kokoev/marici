# One primitive seam line has the exact minimal zero-fiber rank

## Status

Exact finite-rank compatibility theorem. The boundary-control kernel at a fixed
scalar zero is one complex dimensional. Although the Clark observation Gram
can have rank two or higher on the ambient state presentation, its restriction
to the zero-state fiber has rank one. Therefore one surviving complex primitive
seam line has exactly the minimal rank capable of cancelling the positive Clark
supply on that fiber.

This does not prove cancellation. It removes a rank obstruction and reduces the
finite gate to exact coefficient and phase matching on the constrained solution
module.

## One-dimensional zero-state fiber

At fixed \(z\), the boundary-control equation is

\[
(\partial_q+z)G=-cf,
\qquad
G(\infty)=0.
\]

For each scalar \(c\), decay at infinity determines the unique solution

\[
G=cG_z.
\]

If the scalar transform vanishes at \(z=z_0\), then

\[
G_z(0)=0.
\]

Hence every \(c\) gives a two-endpoint zero-state, and the kernel is

\[
\mathcal Z(z_0)
=
\{c(G_{z_0},1):c\in\mathbb C\}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\dim_{\mathbb C}\mathcal Z(z_0)=1.
\]

## Restriction of the Clark Gram

Let \(J\) collect any finite source-authorized Clark rows and put

\[
Q_J=J^*J.
\]

Choose the canonical zero-state generator

\[
x_0=(G_{z_0},1).
\]

On the zero fiber,

\[
\mathcal Q_J(cx_0)
=
|c|^2\lVert Jx_0\rVert^2.
\]

If the Clark packet is faithful, then

\[
\lVert Jx_0\rVert^2>0.
\]

Thus the restricted positive form has complex rank one, regardless of the
ambient rank of \(Q_J\).

## Minimal negative repair rank

Let \(r\) be one complex seam readout. Its signed supply is

\[
-|r x|^2.
\]

Restricted to the zero fiber,

\[
-|r(cx_0)|^2
=
-|c|^2|rx_0|^2.
\]

It cancels the Clark supply exactly when

\[
|rx_0|^2
=
\lVert Jx_0\rVert^2.
\]

One complex negative line is therefore sufficient in rank and necessary when
the restricted Clark supply is nonzero.

## Ambient rank is the wrong test

If \(Q_J\) is positive definite on a two-coordinate presentation, no rank-one
form can satisfy

\[
Q_J=r^*r
\]

on the whole ambient state space. Demanding that identity would falsely reject
the only repair rank supplied by bilateral sewing.

The correct equality is in the quotient by the source equation and endpoint
constraint. Equivalently, it is equality after restriction to
\(\mathcal Z(z_0)\).

This is another instance where relationship energy behaves differently before
and after quotienting: rank can collapse when the equations identify all
admissible zero-states with one source amplitude.

## Constraint-module formulation

The cancellation should not be verified by first locating \(z_0\). Let
\(L_z\) denote the source equation and \(C_z\) the endpoint condition. The
desired finite identity is

\[
Q_J-r^*r
\]

equal to a Hermitian combination of the declared constraints. In a finite
matrix realization, one seeks multipliers \(Y_z\) such that

\[
Q_J-r^*r
=
Y_z^*\mathcal L_z
+\mathcal L_z^*Y_z,
\]

where \(\mathcal L_z\) stacks the dynamical and endpoint constraints.

Then the difference vanishes on every constrained zero-state without using a
zero location. This is the finite algebraic form of equality in the solution
module.

The multipliers must be source-derived and regular. Solving them only after a
zero is supplied would lose authority.

## Relation to bilateral sewing

The continuous Green audit leaves one complex primitive seam line after the
common forcing-norm defect cancels under reflection. Independently, the
arithmetic fold supplies a positive logarithmic cocycle.

The rank calculation shows that the one surviving primitive seam line is not
too small to cancel the Clark supply on the zero fiber. It is exactly the
minimal possible signed repair port.

The positive logarithmic cocycle cannot itself cancel positive Clark supply as
an independently positive port. Its role must be to alter the source-normalized
coefficient of the primitive line, enter the normal-factor energy, or
participate through oriented cross terms before scalarization.

## Finite falsifier

For a canonical zero-state generator \(x_0\), compute

\[
\delta_X(z)
=
\lVert J_Xx_0\rVert^2
-|r_Xx_0|^2.
\]

Any nonzero \(\delta_X\) disproves exact cancellation at that cutoff. A
stronger source-local test is failure to represent

\[
Q_{J,X}-r_X^*r_X
\]

in the Hermitian module generated by the finite source and endpoint
constraints.

## Completion gate

Rank compatibility at each cutoff does not control coefficient convergence.
The ratio between primitive seam supply and Clark supply may tend to zero,
infinity, or a path-dependent value. The primitive and prime-square currents
must remain typed boundary partners in the relative completion.

The completed theorem requires convergence of the constrained form identity,
not merely convergence of its scalar value on separately normalized states.

## Decisive conclusion

The signed-balance route passes its first structural rank test. One complex
primitive seam line is exactly large enough to cancel the positive Clark
supply because the actual zero-state fiber is one complex dimensional.

The decisive finite question is now coefficient-level: does Fourier--Tate
sewing produce the exact constrained form identity between the Clark Gram and
the primitive seam line, with the logarithmic cocycle and remaining currents
correctly typed? The first mismatch closes the route.
