# Three observers generate a transport triangle

## Question

What new object appears when a third unpointed observer is added to the
two-observer relational system?

## Claim boundary

The third observer generates a composition test for relative transports. The
resulting coherencer is triangle holonomy. Exact source-derived transports have
identity holonomy; projected scalar readouts may miss a nontrivial defect.

## Relative transports

Let three observer frames be \(g_0,g_1,g_2\) in a group \(G\). Define the
ordered relative transport

\[
t_{ij}=g_i g_j^{-1}.
\]

Then

\[
t_{ij}t_{jk}=t_{ik}
\]

and the triangle holonomy is

\[
h_{ijk}=t_{ij}t_{jk}t_{ki}=1.
\]

The coherence cell is not an additional fitted equality. It is generated by
the common source frames and cancellation of the middle frames.

## Why the third observer matters

With two observers there is one relative coordinate but no independent
composition path. Three observers provide a direct edge \(t_{02}\) and the
two-step path \(t_{01}t_{12}\). Their comparison is the first finite witness
of whether relational probes share one frame system.

A nonidentity residual

\[
\delta=t_{01}t_{12}t_{20}
\]

means at least one edge was measured in an incompatible frame, transported by
an unauthorized map, or projected through a lossy readout.

## Ordered-port typing

For noncommutative \(G\), reversing an edge replaces \(t_{ij}\) by
\(t_{ji}=t_{ij}^{-1}\), and changing multiplication order changes the
comparison. Ordered ports are therefore part of the type, not presentation
metadata.

A common right action \(g_i\mapsto g_i a\) leaves every \(t_{ij}\) invariant.
A common left action \(g_i\mapsto ag_i\) conjugates every transport and hence
conjugates holonomy. Identity holonomy is invariant under both conventions,
but a nonidentity defect retains only a conjugacy class unless a frame is
pointed.

## Projection can hide the defect

Let \(\chi:G\to A\) be a nonfaithful scalar character. It is possible that
\(\chi(\delta)=1\) while \(\delta\neq1\). Then all scalar triangle tests pass
even though the full transport triangle fails.

The exact \(S_3\) hostile uses an even three-cycle as the holonomy defect. The
sign character sends it to \(+1\), so parity readout declares false coherence.

## Categorical interpretation

Two observers generate a morphism object. Three generate a composable pair, a
direct comparison, and a coherence cell between them. The coherencer is the
residual of comparing those two paths. It vanishes when all edges descend from
one source-framed torsor.

Thus the relationship probe has its own next-rung falsifier:

1. observer frames generate ordered edges;
2. ordered edges generate path comparisons;
3. path comparison generates holonomy;
4. faithful observation of holonomy tests the coherencer.

## Cross-sector gate

- Flavor requires three presentation or acquisition frames before relative
  phase transports can be checked for path independence.
- Optical phase references require a triangle or independently pointed frame
  to detect a miscalibrated relational edge.
- Boundary-line exchanges require full transport comparison; determinant or
  sign projections can miss an even residual.

## DPC

For a three-observer proposal:

1. type every ordered edge and its inverse;
2. derive edges from source frames rather than fitting pairwise values;
3. compare the direct and two-step paths;
4. compute the full group- or operator-valued holonomy;
5. reject scalar coherence when the readout is not faithful on possible
   defects;
6. retain the exact residual and its conjugacy behavior.

## Disposition

The third observer does not merely add another measurement. It creates the
first coherence square in relational transport. The decisive finite falsifier
is nonidentity triangle holonomy evaluated before lossy scalar projection.
