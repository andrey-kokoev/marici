# The RH Three-Observer Object Is a Coalgebra, Not a Finite Readout

## Decisive correction

The three primitive channels are the two reciprocal tails and their common seam:

\[
(P,Q,M).
\]

Their finite observation rank is three. That statement concerns only the present
record. It does not say that three scalar values determine every future record
generated by source-authorized transport.

The correct object carries both observation and continuation. Categorically, it
is a coalgebra for the source operations, with an observation map into the three
typed channels and update maps induced by dilation, reciprocal sewing, and
cutoff extension.

## Exact finite falsifier

After the common theta factor is removed from one label, native dilation acts on
the polynomial part by

\[
D(P)=2XP'+\left(\frac12-2X\right)P.
\]

Starting from the constant polynomial, the degree of \(D^k(P)\) is exactly
\(k\), and its leading coefficient is \((-2)^k\). Consequently, every
projection retaining polynomial degrees through \(N\) loses a new nonzero
direction after the next update.

This is stronger than failure of a convenient basis. It is a failure of every
finite degree-bounded present-record quotient to be closed under the native
continuation law.

## DPC

Candidate principle: three scalar observers determine the completed theta state.

Source premises:

- the two tail channels are retained separately;
- the seam channel is retained independently;
- native dilation is an admitted continuation;
- reciprocal sewing and cutoff extension remain typed operations.

Required consequence: equality of the three present observations must imply
equality of all future observations after every admitted word of source
operations.

Finite falsifier: choose any degree cutoff \(N\). The retained record contains
the orbit through degree \(N\), while one further dilation creates a nonzero
degree-\(N+1\) coefficient. The quotient identifies states that the next Task
separates.

Verdict: the finite-readout principle is false.

## Surviving architecture

The smallest presently authorized architecture is not three numbers. It is the
function-valued instrument

\[
(P,Q,M;D,R,I),
\]

where \(D\) is dilation, \(R\) is reciprocal sewing, and \(I\) is cutoff
extension. An admissible compression must be a coalgebra quotient: it must
preserve not just present outputs but every sequential record generated by
these operations.

There are only three live compression mechanisms:

1. retain the full function-valued channels;
2. retain an infinite represented orbit;
3. derive a nonlinear transformed-argument modular law that closes the orbit.

The third option is not yet constructed. A fitted recurrence, a finite moment
tower, or reconstruction from the completed scalar is not an admissible
substitute.

## Cross-sector meaning

This is the same distinction control theory makes between an output map and an
observable dynamical system. It is also Sontag's distinction between an effect
and an instrument: equal present statistics need not imply equal conditioned
continuations. A hidden environment can preserve the same current record and
be exposed by a later Task.

For RH, the useful question is therefore no longer how many scalar observers
exist. It is whether the labelled theta/Tate source supplies a faithful
three-port coalgebra, and whether reciprocal sewing forces its relative current
to vanish only on the seam.

## Boundary

This result does not prove zero confinement. It eliminates finite observer
compression and identifies the missing theorem more precisely: a source-derived
coalgebra law must connect a common scalar zero to vanishing of the full odd
continuation channel, after which an independent seam-injectivity theorem would
be required.
