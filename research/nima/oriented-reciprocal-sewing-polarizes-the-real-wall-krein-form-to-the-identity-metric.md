# Oriented reciprocal sewing polarizes the real wall Krein form to the identity metric

## Scope

The wall-plus-partner module has the real Green matrix

\[
J=
\begin{pmatrix}
-1&0\\
0&1
\end{pmatrix},
\]

and reciprocal reflection acts on the normalized real coefficient frame by

\[
M=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0\\
0&-1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

These two source-derived structures already determine a positive real polarization.

## Reflected Green pairing

Let \(x,y\in\mathbb R^2\) be primal boundary coefficient vectors. Sew the second vector to the reciprocal fiber by \(My\). The Green pairing pulled back to the primal frame is

\[
x^{T}JMy.
\]

But

\[
JM
=
\begin{pmatrix}
-1&0\\
0&-1
\end{pmatrix}
=
-I.
\]

The outward-minus-inward boundary orientation contributes the additional minus sign. Therefore define

\[
g_{\partial}(x,y)
=
-x^{T}JMy.
\]

Then

\[
g_{\partial}(x,y)=x^{T}y.
\]

Hence

\[
g_{\partial}(x,x)=\|x\|^2>0
\]

for every nonzero real boundary vector.

The polarized real wall metric is exactly the identity.

## Why reversal is decisive

Had reciprocal sewing preserved the chain orientation, its coefficient matrix would be \(I\). The same construction would give

\[
-J=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0\\
0&-1
\end{pmatrix},
\]

which remains indefinite.

Thus positivity is not obtained from the Green concomitant alone. It is produced by the combination

\[
\text{Krein boundary form}
+
\text{chain-reversing reciprocal sewing}
+
\text{boundary orientation}.
\]

Each factor is independently source-derived.

## Complexification

The preceding construction is canonical on the real boundary module generated by the real indicial modes \(1\) and \(x\).

Its standard complexification is the Hermitian metric

\[
\langle z,w\rangle_{\partial}
=
z^*w.
\]

This complexification is distinct from inserting a free complex phase into the Green form. It is the unique Hermitian extension of the positive real polarization once the real source frame is fixed.

If the project instead treats boundary coefficients as intrinsically complex before choosing a real structure, then the antiunitary sewing and sesquilinearity conventions must be written explicitly. One may not silently replace a bilinear pullback by a Hermitian one.

## Auxiliary block consequence

In the normalized boundary frame, the even auxiliary wall block has absolute scale one:

\[
S_{\partial}=I.
\]

Therefore:

- its reduced-support lower bound is \(s_0=1\);
- its inverse has norm one;
- there is no auxiliary soft mode;
- triangular shear estimates no longer suffer from an unfixed wall normalization.

This fixes the finite boundary scale. It does not yet control the bulk-to-boundary incidence \(C_p\) or the causal odd perturbation.

## Odd perturbation budget

Any Hermitian reciprocal-odd correction on this two-dimensional positive boundary space has the real-frame form

\[
i\tau
\begin{pmatrix}
0&-1\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The resulting auxiliary Hermitian block is positive exactly when

\[
|\tau|<1.
\]

Thus the source-normalized auxiliary contraction parameter is simply

\[
\kappa_{\partial}=|\tau|.
\]

The magnitude \(\tau\) must still come from causal versus anti-causal history. The present polarization fixes the denominator and the metric frame, not the numerator.

## Result

The positive wall metric is no longer hypothetical. On the real source boundary module,

\[
-JM=I.
\]

Reciprocal chain reversal converts the canonical Krein boundary pairing into the Euclidean metric with fixed scale. The remaining local auxiliary gate is now sharply isolated: derive the causal odd coefficient \(\tau\) and prove

\[
|\tau|<1
\]

uniformly under completion.
