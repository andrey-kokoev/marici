# Generic Conductor Helicity Selection

On either generic chiral lift of the six-scalar conductor, the alternating
pairing \((12)(34)(56)\) selects edges \(0,2,4\), while
\((23)(45)(61)\) selects edges \(1,3,5\).  Since the chiral word alternates,
each pairing sees a homogeneous triple and the two pairings see opposite ones:

\[
(+++;---)\quad\text{or}\quad(---;+++).
\]

The ordinary four-dimensional three-gluon Yang–Mills amplitude is supported on
the MHV and anti-MHV \(2+1\) sectors, and vanishes on both homogeneous triples.
Therefore the source-derived diagonal product-character line found on the
generic conductor has zero strict grade-zero Yang–Mills section.

This does not kill the alternating conductor symbol.  That symbol is a first
relative-normal class, whereas the helicity statement above concerns ordinary
restriction.  It does rule out a shortcut:

\[
\text{generic conductor restriction}
\not\to
\text{nonzero gravitational/BMS orientation class}.
\]

There is a further typing obstruction to interpreting the first-normal class
as a helicity flip.  Locally a fusion node is

\[
xy=0,
\]

where its two normalization branches are \(x=0\) and \(y=0\).  At a generic
point of the first branch, \(y\ne0\), a normal deformation changes \(xy\) and
leaves the fusion locus.  It does not infinitesimally move to the second
branch.  In the six-scalar packet, each coefficient of
\(\sigma_{\rm alt}\) therefore makes one complementary scalar pair off shell;
it does not turn an all-equal helicity triple into an MHV triple.

Thus the scalar-scaffolded three-point lane cannot activate the radiative
orientation bridge by restriction or by reinterpreting its ordinary conductor
derivative as a helicity change.  The remaining admissible operation is an
independently defined relative residue/Gysin map converting the off-shell
normal class into a physical coefficient insertion.  Without that map, this
lane is closed.
