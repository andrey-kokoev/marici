# Opposite Krein signatures confine symplectic boundary rank loss

## Symplectic zero bridge

Let \(V\) be a complexified two-dimensional boundary plane with bilinear
symplectic form

\[
\omega(u,v)=u_1v_2-u_2v_1.
\]

For two nonzero boundary traces \(u\) and \(v\),

\[
\omega(u,v)=0
\]

if and only if they are linearly dependent. Thus a symplectic cross zero is
genuine rank loss of the two-column boundary trace matrix.

This differs from a cross entry of a positive Weyl matrix, which can vanish
without any rank loss.

## Krein signature

The Real structure defines the Hermitian boundary form

\[
q(u)=i\omega(u,\overline u).
\]

It is real and scales by

\[
q(\alpha u)=|\alpha|^2q(u).
\]

Therefore two nonzero vectors with opposite signs of \(q\) cannot be
proportional.

It follows immediately that

\[
q(u)>0,
\qquad
q(v)<0
\]

implies

\[
\omega(u,v)\ne0.
\]

This is the finite symbolic confinement theorem: opposite boundary signatures
forbid a symplectic determinant zero.

## Seam mechanism

On the common seam, the sector signatures may become null:

\[
q(u)=q(v)=0.
\]

Then proportionality is no longer excluded, so a symplectic determinant zero
can occur.

This gives the desired two-sector architecture:

- one open sector carries positive boundary signature;
- the other carries negative boundary signature;
- the seam is the common null locus;
- a zero is intersection of the two boundary lines.

## Essential typing gate

The determinant statement uses the bilinear form \(u^TJv\). A Hermitian
cross entry \(u^*Jv\) is a different object; its vanishing need not mean
linear dependence. The theta/Tate audit must identify which pairing the Evans
readout actually uses.

## Source requirements

The route remains conditional until the source supplies:

1. a two-dimensional value–flux trace of full rank;
2. the Green boundary symplectic form;
3. the bilinear identity

\[
\Xi(s)=u(s)\omega(b_+(s),b_-(s)),
\]

with a source-derived nowhere-zero unit \(u\);
4. opposite Krein signatures in the two open half-planes;
5. null degeneration exactly on the critical seam;
6. stability of all statements under restricted-product completion.

The signature law must be derived from the Green identity and reciprocal
orientation. Assuming it from zero locations would be circular.

## Finite falsifiers

The route fails if:

- the trace space has dimension other than two;
- the readout is sesquilinear rather than the bilinear determinant;
- either sector contains a trace with the wrong or zero off-seam signature;
- completion admits a nonzero signature-null escape state off seam;
- the scalar readout differs from the symplectic determinant by a factor that
  can vanish.
