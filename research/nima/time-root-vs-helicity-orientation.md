# The time-root line does not determine helicity orientation

Entry 126's surviving global \(\mathbb Z_2\) is a time-root equalizer.  It
cannot by itself supply Entry 1583's transverse orientation.

The exact counterexample is the Lorentz transformation

\[
P=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,1,1).
\]

It preserves the future time vector and the null ray
\(k=(1,0,0,1)\), but has spacetime determinant \(-1\).  On
\(k^\perp/\langle k\rangle\) it reflects one transverse axis, so

\[
P_\perp J P_\perp^{-1}=-J
\]

and the two helicities are exchanged.

Therefore the Bell lens requires a second orientation character beyond the
Entry 126 time-root line: equivalently a spacetime orientation, or directly an
orientation of the transverse Ward plane.  Any comparison must preserve these
two types rather than identifying their abstract \(\mathbb Z_2\) groups.
