# The bilateral theta derivative supplies the missing reciprocal trace direction

## Bilateral completion packet

Let

\[
h(u)=\frac12 e^{u/2}\vartheta(e^{2u}),
\qquad
\Phi=\left(\partial_u^2-\frac14\right)h.
\]

The theta modular law gives

\[
h(-u)=h(u),
\]

so \(\Phi\) is even and rapidly decreasing on the full line. Define bilateral weighted moments

\[
M_-(g)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-u/2}g(u)\,du,
\qquad
M_+(g)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{u/2}g(u)\,du.
\]

Evenness gives

\[
M_-(\Phi)=M_+(\Phi)=m.
\]

The theta forcing therefore occupies the reciprocal-even trace ray

\[
v_0=m
\begin{pmatrix}
1\\1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

## Canonical odd companion

The bilateral derivative \(\Phi'\) is reciprocal-odd. Integration by parts is boundary-free because \(\Phi\) is rapidly decreasing:

\[
M_-(\Phi')
=
\frac12M_-(\Phi),
\]

\[
M_+(\Phi')
=
-\frac12M_+(\Phi).
\]

Thus

\[
v_1=
\frac m2
\begin{pmatrix}
1\\-1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The two columns are orthogonal in the unweighted trace frame, and

\[
\det(v_0,v_1)=-m^2.
\]

Provided \(m\ne0\), the bilateral packet \((\Phi,\Phi')\) gives an exact rank-two reciprocal trace cell. Unlike the half-line translate jet, the derivative changes reciprocal parity rather than merely differentiating a vanishing scalar tail.

## Source authority

This companion is not an arbitrary test bump. It is generated by the scale derivative on the completed bilateral theta packet, and

\[
\partial_u\mathcal C=\mathcal C\partial_u.
\]

Hence differentiation descends through the completion quotient. Reciprocal reflection acts by

\[
R\Phi=\Phi,
\qquad
R\Phi'=-\Phi'.
\]

The missing trace coordinate is precisely the odd representation of the reciprocal involution.

## Translation qualification

For a bilateral translate \(g_L(u)=g(u+L)\),

\[
M_-(g_L)=e^{L/2}M_-(g),
\qquad
M_+(g_L)=e^{-L/2}M_+(g).
\]

Consequently the translated even and odd columns are

\[
v_0(L)=m
\begin{pmatrix}
e^{L/2}\\e^{-L/2}
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
v_1(L)=\frac m2
\begin{pmatrix}
e^{L/2}\\-e^{-L/2}
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Their determinant stays nonzero, but their Euclidean conditioning deteriorates as \(|L|\to\infty\). In the reciprocal half-density metric

\[
G_L=
\begin{pmatrix}
e^{-L}&0\\
0&e^L
\end{pmatrix},
\]

they remain orthogonal with fixed norms. Therefore uniformity is valid only if this moving metric is independently the source metric transported by the Mellin half-density. It cannot be introduced after the fact.
