# Green reduction must define a stable source carrier before spectral exclusion

## Carrier reduction, not post hoc deletion

Let \(H_X\) be the typed finite boundary carrier, with Green form \(B_X\ge0\),
arithmetic frame \(F_X\ge0\), and radical

\[
N_X=\ker B_X.
\]

If \(F_X\le c_XB_X\), every vector in \(N_X\) lies in the residual kernel.
Therefore a positive Green route can continue only after replacing \(H_X\) by
a source-authorized reduced carrier on which the Green form is nondegenerate.

This replacement must define the state object before any RH claim. Removing a
direction only because it obstructs nonvanishing is circular.

## Two authorized mechanisms

There are two distinct carrier reductions.

### Radical quotient

Set

\[
\overline H_X=H_X/N_X.
\]

This is authorized when:

1. every source constructor preserves \(N_X\);
2. every declared observer and determinant readout annihilates \(N_X\);
3. reciprocal transport carries \(N_{+,X}\) to \(N_{-,X}\);
4. cutoff maps descend to the quotients;
5. the quotient retains all arithmetic labels and boundary types.

Then the Green form descends to a positive definite form
\(\overline B_X\).

Calling \(N_X\) gauge is shorthand for these descent properties, not an
independent authorization.

### Reachable restriction

Let \(R_X\subset H_X\) be the source-defined reachable carrier generated by
arithmetic incidence and every admitted source action:

\[
R_X
=
\operatorname{span}
\left\{
F(w)U_Xv
\right\}_{w,v}.
\]

Restriction to \(R_X\) is authorized when:

1. \(R_X\) is invariant under the complete source category;
2. all required boundary maps and reciprocal operations restrict to \(R_X\);
3. \(R_X\cap N_X=0\);
4. the restricted Green form is nondegenerate;
5. \(R_X\) was declared as the actual state carrier independently of spectral
   outcomes.

Mere observational unreachability of \(N_X\) does not establish these
conditions.

## When quotient and restriction agree

Suppose

\[
H_X=R_X\oplus N_X
\]

as a source-natural direct sum. Then the quotient map restricts to an
isomorphism

\[
R_X\cong H_X/N_X.
\]

In that case gauge quotient and reachable restriction are two presentations of
one reduced carrier.

Without a source-natural splitting, they are different constructions.
Selecting an arbitrary complement to \(N_X\) introduces a frame choice and may
break reciprocal or cutoff coherence.

## Cutoff descent

Let

\[
j_{XY}:H_X\longrightarrow H_Y
\]

be a cutoff inclusion for \(X\le Y\).

A quotient system exists when

\[
j_{XY}N_X\subseteq N_Y.
\]

Exact radical compatibility requires

\[
N_Y\cap j_{XY}H_X
=
j_{XY}N_X.
\]

The first condition allows descent. The second prevents a previously positive
direction from becoming radical merely by embedding into the next cutoff.

For reachable restrictions, one needs

\[
j_{XY}R_X\subseteq R_Y
\]

and compatibility of every restricted constructor.

These are finite categorical cells and must be verified before taking a
limit.

## Stable reduced bundle

Let \(P_X\) be the support projection of \(B_X\), or the projection onto the
declared reachable carrier when restriction is used.

A stable reduced bundle over cutoffs and spectral parameters requires:

1. compatible reduced carriers under cutoff maps;
2. compact-local continuity of \(P_X(s)\) in the chosen topology;
3. reciprocal transport of the complete reduced pair;
4. constant local index or another controlled dimension law;
5. convergence of the reduced closed forms.

Pointwise existence of \(P_X(s)\) is insufficient. Rotating support
projections can destroy the limit bundle while leaving every finite spectrum
unchanged.

## Rotating-support hostile

In a two-dimensional ambient carrier, let

\[
B_n=P_n
\]

where \(P_n\) projects onto a line whose angle alternates between two distinct
values. Let

\[
F_n=\frac12P_n.
\]

On each finite support, the normalized operator is

\[
K_n=\frac12I
\]

and has a perfect gap from one.

Nevertheless, \(P_n\) does not converge. There is no stable reduced carrier
and no completed \(K\) on one fixed support.

Thus spectral gaps without carrier convergence have no completion authority.

## Collapsing-support hostile

Let

\[
B_n=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0\\
0&1/n
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
F_n=
\frac12B_n.
\]

Every finite \(B_n\) is positive definite and every normalized operator is
\(K_n=\frac12I\). But the second Green direction becomes null at the limit.

The finite normalized spectra again conceal a new completion radical.
Compact-local coercivity or convergence of closed forms is needed to detect
this collapse.

## Spectral-exactness cell

After the reduced carriers stabilize, define the reduced pencil

\[
\overline F_X-\lambda\overline B_X
\]

or normalized operator

\[
\overline K_X
=
\overline B_X^{-1/2}
\overline F_X
\overline B_X^{-1/2}.
\]

Strong convergence alone does not preserve absence of spectrum near one.

Sufficient typed packages include:

- norm-resolvent convergence of the reduced pencils in a neighborhood of one;
- norm convergence of \(\overline K_X\) in the relevant determinant ideal;
- collectively compact convergence with spectral exactness at one.

This cell is separate from support convergence. One controls the state bundle;
the other controls spectra on that bundle.

## Reciprocal carrier congruence

A reciprocal map \(J_X\) must carry the full data:

\[
J_X^*B_{-,X}J_X=B_{+,X},
\qquad
J_X^*F_{-,X}J_X=F_{+,X},
\]

together with

\[
J_XN_{+,X}=N_{-,X}
\]

for quotients, or

\[
J_XR_{+,X}=R_{-,X}
\]

for restrictions.

Only then does it descend to a congruence of reduced pencils and preserve the
generalized eigenvalue one.

Comparing scalar determinants or independently normalized \(K\) operators is
downstream and insufficient.

## Interaction with graph augmentation

The graph carrier retains an arithmetic identity component. A reduced analytic
carrier is authorized only if the graph matching residual descends with it.

For quotient reduction, the analytic radical must pair with a declared graph
subobject that leaves arithmetic provenance unchanged. For reachable
restriction, every retained arithmetic state must synthesize into the
restricted analytic carrier.

If either fails, the reduction severs the arithmetic-to-analytic incidence
whose determinant is supposed to represent \(\Xi\).

## Ordered completion theorem

A valid completion theorem has the following order:

1. classify the Green radical at every cutoff;
2. choose and authorize quotient or reachable restriction;
3. prove cutoff and reciprocal compatibility of reduced carriers;
4. prove compact-local support or closed-form convergence;
5. establish spectral exactness near one;
6. prove a compact-local gap from one;
7. identify the relative determinant of the reduced residual with \(\Xi\).

No later spectral estimate repairs a failure in an earlier carrier cell.

## Hostile tests

1. Removing \(N_X\) only because it creates residual cohomology is circular.
2. Unreachability without source invariance does not authorize restriction.
3. Arbitrary complements to \(N_X\) can rotate and break reciprocity.
4. Cutoff descent without exact radical compatibility permits new finite
   null directions.
5. Stable finite spectra on rotating supports do not define a limit operator.
6. Normalized spectra can hide collapsing Green coercivity.
7. Strong convergence alone permits spectral pollution at one.

## Consequence for categorical RH

The next theorem is not yet a spectral estimate. It is construction of a
stable reduced Green carrier. Only on that carrier does separation of the
generalized spectrum from one become an RH-bearing completion statement.

The source must decide whether the complete Green radical is a true gauge
subobject or whether the actual state object is a source-generated reachable
restriction. Either decision must commute with cutoff inclusion, reciprocal
transport, graph incidence, and determinant lines.

## Verdict

Green-radical removal is legitimate only as a source-authorized carrier
reduction made before RH. Quotient and reachable restriction are distinct
unless a natural splitting identifies them. Stable support and spectral
exactness are separate completion cells; both are prior to any uniform gap
from the generalized eigenvalue one.
