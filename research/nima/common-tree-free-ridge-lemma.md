# Free-ridge lemma for common-tree complexes

## Setup

Fix a cyclic order `alpha` on `n` labels. Let `A_alpha` be its simplicial triangulation complex:

- vertices are `alpha`-planar channels;
- facets are complete `alpha`-planar cubic trees, equivalently polygon triangulations;
- ridges are partial triangulations missing one diagonal.

Every ridge of `A_alpha` lies in exactly two facets. The two facets differ by flipping the missing diagonal in the unique quadrilateral left by the partial triangulation.

For a second cyclic order `beta`, let `K(alpha,beta)` be the subcomplex generated by facets whose channels are also `beta`-planar.

## Lemma

Let `F` be any nonempty proper subset of the facets of `A_alpha`. Then some facet in `F` contains a ridge incident to no other facet in `F`.

Consequently:

1. every proper nonempty common-tree complex has a free ridge;
2. after deleting any facet, every later nonempty proper remainder again has a free ridge;
3. any proper collection of common-tree facets can therefore be peeled down to one facet.

## Proof

Construct the dual flip graph `G_alpha`:

- one graph vertex for each facet of `A_alpha`;
- one graph edge for each ridge, joining its two incident facets.

The flip graph is connected. One proof is constructive: choose the fan triangulation based at vertex `1`. If a triangulation is not the fan, choose a triangle separating vertex `1` from a non-fan region and flip its separating diagonal so that the number of diagonals incident to `1` increases. Repetition terminates at the fan. Thus every triangulation is connected to the fan by flips, so every pair is connected.

Now let `F` be nonempty and proper. Choose `T in F` and `T' notin F`. A path in `G_alpha` from `T` to `T'` has a first edge leaving `F`. Write that edge as `U--V`, with `U in F` and `V notin F`. The edge corresponds to a ridge `R=U intersection V`. Since every ridge has exactly two incident facets in `A_alpha`, namely `U` and `V`, only `U` remains incident to `R` inside the facet set `F`. Hence `R` is a free ridge of `U` relative to `F`.

After deleting a facet from a proper set, the remainder is still proper. If it is nonempty, the same argument applies again. Induction on the number of remaining facets proves complete peeling.

## Application to common trees

If `K(alpha,beta)` is nonempty and not the full `A_alpha`, its facet set is a nonempty proper subset, so the lemma applies. If it equals `A_alpha`, it is the closed associahedral sphere and has no free ridge. Thus free-ridge existence and iterative peeling require no finite-multiplicity assumption and no special property of `beta` beyond selecting a facet subset.

## What this lemma does not prove

Free-ridge peeling alone does **not** imply that the reverse deletion order is a shelling. For reverse shellability one must additionally prove that, when a deleted facet is reattached, its intersection with the accumulated complex is a pure codimension-one subcomplex. Equivalently, every earlier lower-dimensional intersection must lie in an earlier shared ridge.

The executable checks establish this stronger condition through all order pairs at `n<=9` and sampled pairs at `n=10`, but the arbitrary-`n` proof of that stronger admissibility statement remains open.
