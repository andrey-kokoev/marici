# Physical quotient versus self-modeling completion: the flavor phase test

## Source-derived quotient

Figueiredo's exact flavor audit supplies two presentations related by a
weak-basis transformation. All tested physical invariants agree, while the
sparse zero pattern and its loop phase differ. Write

\[
p\sim_{\rm phys}q,
\qquad
\phi(p)\ne\phi(q).
\]

This gives a genuine quotient equation rather than another freely generated
constructor.

## Test

Lift the physical congruence through every constructor word:

\[
wp\sim wq,
\qquad w\in\{m,r,o,c\}^*.
\]

The checker compares:

1. complete the two-presentation system to depth \(n\), then quotient by the
   lifted congruence;
2. quotient the base system first, then complete the single physical class to
   depth \(n\).

Nodes, typed edges, and coherence 2-cells agree exactly through depth four.
The all-finite-word normal form proves the equality at omega:

\[
\boxed{
\mathsf V(X_\omega/\!\sim_{\rm phys})
=
\mathsf V(X_\omega)/\!\sim_{\rm phys}
=
X_\omega/\!\sim_{\rm phys}.
}
\]

## The readout obstruction

The phase label cannot be placed on the quotient class because its two source
representatives have unequal values. Thus the structural completion descends
while the chart readout does not:

\[
\boxed{
\text{self-model structure descends}
\quad\text{but}\quad
\phi\text{ does not}.
}
\]

This is the first source-derived quotient test of the omega construction. It
confirms that recursive closure does not automatically promote local labels to
physical observables.

## Scope

The result covers a congruence generated by a finite physical equivalence and
lifted functorially through a finitary signature. It does not establish
preservation of analytic completion, derived quotients with higher Tor, or
non-effective equivalence relations. Those remain the sharper failure modes.
