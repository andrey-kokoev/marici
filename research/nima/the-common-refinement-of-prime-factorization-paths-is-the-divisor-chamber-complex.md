# The Common Refinement of Prime-Factorization Paths Is the Divisor-Chamber Complex

## Ordered paths and their cuts

Let

\[
n=\prod_{i=1}^r p_i^{a_i}.
\]

Choose an ordered word of prime factors whose product is $n$. Its successive
moving-seam cuts occur at the logarithms of the prefix products. Thus one
factorization path gives one ordered partition of

\[
[0,\log n).
\]

Different orders have the same endpoint but generally different internal
cuts.

## Exact common-refinement theorem

Every proper divisor $d$ of $n$ can occur as a prefix product of some ordered
factorization word. Conversely, every prefix product is a divisor of $n$.
Therefore the union of the cut sets over all factorization orders is exactly

\[
\{\log d: d\mid n,\ 1<d<n\}.
\]

Sorting these points gives the minimal common refinement of every authorized
factorization path. Since $n$ has

\[
\tau(n)=\prod_{i=1}^r(a_i+1)
\]

positive divisors, the refined interval has exactly

\[
\tau(n)-1
\]

open chambers between consecutive divisor cuts, including the two endpoint
chambers.

For a squarefree product of $r$ distinct primes,

\[
\tau(n)-1=2^r-1.
\]

Thus the number of comparison chambers grows exponentially even though each
individual path has only $r$ stages.

## Exact recursive tower law

Let $N_r$ be the chamber count for $r$ distinct primes. Then

\[
N_1=1,
\qquad
N_{r+1}=2N_r+1.
\]

Indeed, adjoining a new prime doubles the divisor set:

\[
\mathcal D(np)
=
\mathcal D(n)\sqcup p\mathcal D(n),
\qquad
p\nmid n.
\]

After sorting their logarithmic images, the two transported copies require
one additional chamber to relate them. Hence

\[
1,\ 3,\ 7,\ 15,\ldots
\]

is not a numerological tower. It is the exact comparison complexity of
successively adjoining independent prime constructors.

Reciprocal complementation pairs $N_r-1$ chambers and leaves one central
chamber fixed as a set:

\[
N_r=2(2^{r-1}-1)+1.
\]

The final $+1$ is dynamic. Its endpoints are consecutive complementary
divisor cuts and move whenever the source prime packet changes. A static
inserted scalar cannot play this role.

## First cases

For two distinct primes, the divisor cuts are

\[
0,\ \log p,\ \log q,\ \log(pq)
\]

after ordering the middle pair. They produce three chambers. The middle
chamber has length

\[
|\log p-\log q|
=
\left|\log\frac pq\right|.
\]

For three distinct primes, the cuts are the logarithms of all eight divisors.
There are seven chambers. The six factorization paths each see only two
internal cuts; their common comparison object requires all six internal
divisor cuts.

For one prime power $p^k$, the divisor cuts are

\[
0,\ \log p,\ldots,k\log p,
\]

so there are $k$ chambers. Repetition grows linearly, while introducing new
independent primes doubles the divisor count.

## Reciprocal involution

Divisor complementation

\[
d\longmapsto\frac nd
\]

acts on cut coordinates by

\[
\log d\longmapsto\log n-\log d.
\]

Hence the chamber complex has an exact reflection about
$\frac12\log n$. Chambers occur in reciprocal pairs, together with one
central chamber fixed as a set. For squarefree $n>1$, no divisor lies exactly
at $\sqrt n$, so the central chamber has positive length and moves when the
prime data change.

The central component is therefore dynamic. It is fixed by reciprocal
reflection as a chamber, but it is not a static point or an independently
inserted law.

## Categorical interpretation

Factorization words are paths in the prime-constructor category. Adjacent
swaps generate the permutohedral comparison groupoid. The divisor-chamber
complex is the minimal common refinement on which every path partition can
be represented without losing a tagged intermediate distinction.

There are two separate structures:

- the permutohedron records transformations among ordered paths;
- the divisor complex records the observer strata needed to compare all
  those paths faithfully.

The second is not obtained by merely quotienting the first to its common
endpoint. It is generated by retaining every prefix distinction before the
quotient.

## Weighted divisor-lattice formulation

Let $\mathcal D(n)$ be the divisor lattice of $n$. For squarefree $n$ it is
the Boolean lattice on its prime set. For general $n$ it is the product of
chains

\[
\mathcal D(n)\cong\prod_i[0,a_i].
\]

The logarithmic valuation is a strictly order-preserving functor

\[
\ell_n:\mathcal D(n)\longrightarrow[0,\log n],
\qquad
\ell_n(d)=\log d.
\]

Maximal chains in $\mathcal D(n)$ are ordered prime-factorization paths.
Taking the union of their images under $\ell_n$ produces every divisor cut.
The divisor-chamber complex is therefore the interval stratification induced
by the image of the complete weighted lattice.

Complementation

\[
d^\vee=\frac nd
\]

is an order-reversing involution satisfying

\[
\ell_n(d^\vee)=\log n-\ell_n(d).
\]

Thus reciprocal sewing is the image of lattice duality under the valuation
functor.

This formulation distinguishes two kinds of order:

- divisibility is the source partial order;
- numerical size of $\log d$ is the geometric total order on the seam.

The coherencer records their interaction. Replacing the divisor lattice by
only the final product forgets the source order; replacing it by only the
sorted cut list forgets which constructor paths generated each cut.

## The interval shadow hides higher divisor topology

The sorted divisor chambers form a subdivision of one interval and are
therefore contractible. Their number and reciprocal pairing alone cannot
produce a topological obstruction.

The source divisor category contains more information. For squarefree $n$
with $r$ prime factors, remove the initial and terminal divisors from
$\mathcal D(n)$. The order complex of the remaining proper divisor poset is
the barycentric subdivision of the boundary of an $(r-1)$-simplex. Hence

\[
|\Delta(\mathcal D(n)\setminus\{1,n\})|
\simeq
S^{r-2}.
\]

Its top reduced homology is one-dimensional. Its reduced Euler
characteristic is

\[
(-1)^{r-2}=(-1)^r=\mu(n),
\]

the squarefree Möbius sign.

For three distinct primes, the proper divisor complex is a hexagonal circle.
Its six edges are the incidences between singleton-prime and two-prime
divisors. This is the first factorization packet with a genuine coherence
loop.

If one prime is repeated, the divisor lattice is a product containing a
longer chain and its Möbius value vanishes. The squarefree top class is no
longer present in the same form. Thus the higher divisor topology
distinguishes mixed primitive directions from prime-power repetition.

## Valuation pushforward loses the top class

The logarithmic functor

\[
\ell_n:\mathcal D(n)\longrightarrow[0,\log n]
\]

sends all higher simplices into a one-dimensional ordered target. It retains
their cut locations but forgets the top homology of the proper divisor
complex.

This gives a precise information-loss statement:

- the divisor category retains higher factorization coherence;
- the chamber observer retains every scalar interval distinction;
- scalar endpoint aggregation retains only the total interval.

Neither of the latter two reconstructs the squarefree top class without
additional coefficient data.

## First higher-coherence gate

For three distinct primes, attach the source-authorized comparison map to
each edge of the proper-divisor hexagon and compose around the loop before
applying the valuation or scalar-readout functor.

There are two decisive outcomes:

1. the product is the identity, so the coefficient system is flat and the
   higher divisor loop adds no orientation;
2. a source-derived residual remains, giving a typed three-prime coherence
   class that must then be compared with the order-three determinant anomaly
   and the moving-seam refinement.

A residual seen only after scalarization is inadmissible. A residual fitted
from a desired divisor is equally inadmissible.

This is the first finite place where a proposed higher tower can carry more
than path bookkeeping. Two-prime diamonds test commutation; the three-prime
hexagon tests genuine loop coherence.

## The two known coefficient systems are flat

The moving-window coefficient system factors every ordered path through the
same complete divisor-chamber refinement. Each edge comparison is induced by
restriction and reaggregation of that common interval object. Composing the
six edge maps around the three-prime hexagon therefore gives the identity.

The completed finite Euler coefficient system is also flat. The
order-three regularized determinant alone has a multiplicative anomaly, but
the source-derived primitive--square counterfactor has the opposite anomaly.
Their product is the ordinary Euler multiplier, which is strictly
multiplicative and independent of factorization order.

Thus the higher divisor topology does not automatically supply arithmetic
orientation. Both currently constructed shadows have trivial holonomy on the
first nontrivial loop.

The remaining possible information lies in a relative comparison between
the two flat systems. One needs an operator-level natural transformation
whose endpoint shadow is moving-window refinement and whose determinant
shadow is Euler multiplication. Its failure of naturality around the
three-prime hexagon would be a genuine mixed coherence residual.

If that relative hexagon also commutes, the divisor-topology tower adds
provenance and faithful bookkeeping but no zero-confining law. This is the
sharp finite falsifier for promoting the newly found sphere class into an RH
mechanism.

## The source-Gram tower is an exact coboundary

For each prime, define the Stein difference on operator forms by

\[
\Delta_p(H)=H-A_p^*HA_p.
\]

The source Gram satisfies

\[
\Delta_p(G)=B_p^*B_p.
\]

The valuation shifts commute. Their adjoints commute as well, so

\[
\Delta_p\Delta_q=\Delta_q\Delta_p.
\]

Consequently the mixed seam defects obey

\[
\Delta_q(B_p^*B_p)
=
\Delta_p(B_q^*B_q).
\]

All higher mixed cells are iterated coboundaries of the same global potential
$G$. Their values are independent of the order of the prime differences.
The three-prime hexagon therefore has identity holonomy in the source-Gram
system.

This is a finite no-go theorem. No incompatibility among finite coherence
towers can be extracted from the analytic Gram while it remains one globally
defined potential.

A nontrivial residual can appear only if one of the following occurs:

- the global Gram fails to extend through the restricted-product completion;
- the completion functor fails to commute with a prime difference;
- the determinant and Gram completions require inequivalent boundary
  counterterms;
- the archimedean sewing contributes a relative class not represented by the
  finite Gram potential.

Thus the tower calculation returns to a sharply typed completion-at-infinity
gate. The finite divisor topology identifies where a class could live, while
the exact Stein potential proves that the currently known finite analytic
coefficients do not populate it.

## Source observer

For consecutive divisors $d_j<d_{j+1}$, the corresponding chamber observer
is the source restriction

\[
\Phi\longmapsto
\Phi\big|_{[\log d_j,\log d_{j+1})}.
\]

The direct sum of all chamber observers reconstructs the complete moving
window on $[0,\log n)$. Coarser ordered-path observers are obtained by
authorized aggregation of adjacent chambers.

This makes the refinement source-realizable. It is not only a combinatorial
subdivision.

## Refinement conserves energy

Let $I_1,\ldots,I_{\tau(n)-1}$ be the divisor chambers. Restriction gives the
canonical orthogonal decomposition

\[
L^2(0,\log n)
\cong
\bigoplus_jL^2(I_j).
\]

Hence

\[
\|f\|_{L^2(0,\log n)}^2
=
\sum_j\|f|_{I_j}\|_{L^2(I_j)}^2.
\]

The exponential increase in relationship ports does not create exponential
physical energy. It resolves one fixed seam state into more source-typed
components. Energy is invariant under refinement; information is lost only
when chamber records are aggregated or scalarized.

## Chamber collapse toward completion

The total interval length is $\log n$, so at least one chamber has width no
larger than

\[
\frac{\log n}{\tau(n)-1}.
\]

For the product of $r$ distinct primes this is

\[
\frac{\log n}{2^r-1}.
\]

Along primorial packets, $\log n$ grows subexponentially in $r$, while the
number of chambers is $2^r-1$. The smallest chamber width therefore tends to
zero at least exponentially up to the logarithmic numerator.

Thus no fixed finite-resolution observer can retain every factorization
distinction uniformly through completion. The continuum seam is not an
unrelated extra carrier: it is the completion forced by increasingly fine
divisor-chamber refinements. The labelled divisor lattice must nevertheless
remain alongside that continuum, because the analytic interval alone forgets
divisibility type.

## Finite falsifier

Suppose a proposed comparison object omits one divisor chamber. Choose a
smooth source variation supported inside that chamber. Every retained
endpoint total can remain unchanged after compensating variations in its
coarse parent windows, while the omitted prefix distinction changes.

Therefore endpoint equality, path telescoping, and scalar determinant
agreement cannot certify faithful comparison unless every divisor chamber
required by the chosen factorization family is retained or proven
irrelevant by a source-derived descent relation.

## Scope

This theorem identifies the minimum finite observer refinement for all
factorization orders of one integer label. It explains the exponential growth
of comparison relationships and the origin of the reciprocal central
chamber. It does not prove that the completed divisor-chamber system has a
uniform frame bound, construct its adelic determinant, orient its scalar
section, or imply RH.
