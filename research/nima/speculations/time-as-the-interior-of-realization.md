# Speculation: time as the interior of realization

## Operator intuition

Time may be what it feels like when the prime domain is transformed into the
natural-number domain. More generally, time may be what is required to convert
a category of ideas into the physical world.

The durable core of this intuition is not that arithmetic literally flows in
physical time. It is the distinction between a completed relationship and an
execution history that realizes it.

## Arithmetic model

The multiplicative monoid of positive integers is the free commutative monoid
on the primes:

\[
\mathbb N_{>0}
\cong
\operatorname{FreeCMon}(\mathbb P).
\]

A completed integer records prime multiplicities but contains no preferred
order for applying its generators. For example, the endpoint

\[
30=2\cdot3\cdot5
\]

can be reached through distinct constructor paths such as

\[
1\longrightarrow2\longrightarrow6\longrightarrow30
\]

or

\[
1\longrightarrow5\longrightarrow15\longrightarrow30.
\]

The integer is timeless in this limited sense. A local realization of it has
intermediate states, an order, and a path. If the multiplications have resource
or dependency constraints, the path also has a causal partial order.

This yields the first conjectural principle:

> Time is carried by admissible realization paths, not by completed arithmetic
> objects alone.

## From ideas to physical processes

Let \(\mathcal C_{\rm idea}\) contain specifications, explanations, and desired
transformations. Let \(\mathcal C_{\rm physical}\) contain physical states and
physically possible transformations. A bare semantic assignment

\[
S:\mathcal C_{\rm idea}\longrightarrow\mathcal C_{\rm physical}
\]

can associate an idea with a physical endpoint without explaining how that
endpoint is produced.

Physical compilation requires a factorization through a process category:

\[
\mathcal C_{\rm idea}
\xrightarrow{F}
\mathcal C_{\rm process}
\xrightarrow{E}
\mathcal C_{\rm physical}.
\]

The middle category carries the structure absent from an all-at-once endpoint:

- causal precedence;
- resource consumption and release;
- admissible intermediate states;
- partiality and failure;
- coherence among alternative factorizations;
- and, when source-derived, clocks and durations.

On this formulation, time is not an arbitrary ordering imposed on ideas. It is
the internal structure required when a semantic relationship must be compiled
into locally executable physical morphisms.

## Relation to the theta/Tate lane

The completed functional equation presents a global two-sector relationship.
The recent Lagrangian and Cayley rotations replace a scalar endpoint with a
path of comparison operators. Differentiating the unitary path produces the
signed delay density

\[
\mathcal Q(\lambda)=iU(\lambda)^*U'(\lambda).
\]

This is time-like because it measures the internal response accumulated while
the spectral parameter changes. It does not by itself identify \(\lambda\)
with physical time. Such an identification would require a source-derived
clock or causal functor connecting spectral transport to physical evolution.

## Three distinct notions

The speculation must preserve three types:

1. Constructor depth counts stages in one chosen or authorized factorization.
2. Causal order records which physical operations may precede others.
3. Metric time assigns durations or clock readings to causal processes.

The first does not automatically produce the second, and the second does not
automatically produce the third. A valid theory must construct the comparison
maps rather than identifying them by metaphor.

## Falsifiers

The strong speculation fails if:

- arbitrary presentation order is mistaken for physical order;
- equivalent constructor paths yield incompatible elapsed times without a
  typed clock difference;
- physical duration varies while every proposed process invariant remains
  fixed;
- a supposedly timeless endpoint already contains a source-derived temporal
  orientation;
- or the theta/Tate delay density cannot be related to any causal or clocked
  physical process.

## Boundary of the claim

This is not a claim that time is prime factorization, computation, subjective
experience, or an illusion. It is a structural proposal: the distinction
between global specification and local realization may be where temporal
structure first becomes necessary.
