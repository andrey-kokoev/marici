# RH Fixed Points Use Reciprocal Conjugation, Not Holomorphic Reciprocity

## The typing error

Holomorphic reciprocity acts by

\[
J(s)=1-s.
\]

Its fixed locus is the single point \(s=1/2\), not the critical line. A
nonreal zero on the critical line is exchanged with its complex conjugate by
\(J\).

The involution whose fixed locus is the critical line is

\[
I(s)=1-\overline{s}.
\]

Therefore the Burnside fixed-point mark relevant to RH belongs to reciprocal
conjugation \(I\), not to holomorphic Fourier–Tate reciprocity \(J\) alone.

## Klein-four symmetry

Let

\[
K(s)=\overline{s}.
\]

Then \(I=JK=KJ\), and the completed zero locus is acted on by

\[
C_2^J\times C_2^K.
\]

A generic off-seam nonreal zero produces a four-point orbit:

\[
s,
\quad
1-s,
\quad
\overline{s},
\quad
1-\overline{s}.
\]

A nonreal seam zero is fixed by \(I\) but still paired by both \(J\) and
\(K\). Thus treating it as a fixed point of \(J\) misclassifies every known
kind of critical-line zero.

## Corrected Burnside criterion

For a finite zero packet stable under the Klein-four action, restrict the
packet to the subgroup generated by \(I\). The two relevant marks are

\[
m_{\rm total}=|X|,
\qquad
m_I=|X^I|.
\]

RH is equivalent to

\[
m_{\rm total}=m_I
\]

at every stable finite truncation. The earlier total-versus-fixed formula
remains valid only after replacing \(J\) by \(I\).

## Semilinear operator consequence

On analytic state spaces, \(K\) and \(I\) are anti-linear. Their character
decomposition is not an ordinary complex-linear eigenspace decomposition.
One must either:

1. work on the underlying real complex, where \(I\) is a real-linear
   involution;
2. use a Real or semilinear equivariant category;
3. retain the full Klein-four action on the divisor permutation module while
   separately typing the analytic conjugation structure.

The projector \((1-I)/2\) is valid on the underlying real module, not as a
complex-linear projector without additional typing.

## Effect on the local Tate comparison

There are now at least three distinct involutions:

- additive spatial parity \(P:x\mapsto-x\);
- holomorphic reciprocal transport \(J:s\mapsto1-s\);
- anti-holomorphic seam reflection \(I:s\mapsto1-\overline{s}\).

Local Tate parity exactness concerns \(P\). Fourier–Tate sewing supplies
\(J\). RH fixed-point sensitivity concerns \(I\), which additionally needs
the source real structure \(K\).

No contraction may be transported between these actions without explicit
intertwiners.

## Finite falsifier

Take \(s=1/2+it\) with nonzero real \(t\). Then

\[
J(s)=1/2-it\ne s,
\qquad
I(s)=s.
\]

Any proposed RH index that assigns this point to a free orbit because it uses
\(J\) rather than \(I\) has the wrong fixed-point type.

## Revised frontier

Rebuild the joint character audit using the source real structure and the
Klein-four action. The next source complex must be Real-equivariant, and the
acyclicity target is the anti-fixed component for \(I\) on its underlying
real zero cohomology.
