# The source-pulled trace topology makes RH sewing automatic but excludes the singular boundary vessel

Author: `marici.Nima`

Date: 2026-08-26

Status: exact minimal-topology theorem and completion tradeoff

## Canonical topology on the trace range

The injective trace map

\[
\tau:\mathcal S(\mathbb A)
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Ran}\tau
\]

is a bijection onto its range. Give the range the transported
Schwartz--Bruhat topology: a net of traces converges exactly when its unique
source representatives converge in $\mathcal S(\mathbb A)$.

With this topology, (	au) is a topological isomorphism by construction.

## Sewing becomes automatic

The trace sewing operator is

\[
J_0=\tau\mathcal F\tau^{-1}.
\]

Fourier transform is a continuous automorphism of the Schwartz--Bruhat
source. Therefore (J_0) and (J_0^{-1}) are continuous automorphisms of the
source-pulled trace range.

No separate closability theorem is needed in this minimal topology.

## Why this does not finish the construction

The source-pulled topology retains exactly the ordinary Schwartz--Bruhat
source and nothing more. Its completion does not automatically adjoin:

- the exponentially growing primitive current;
- the non-trace-class square current;
- distributional seam boundary states;
- renormalized endpoint incidences;
- the completed third-order determinant vessel.

Thus it solves sewing continuity by choosing a space too small for the full
boundary object.

## Completion tradeoff

To admit singular boundary currents, one must weaken the topology, enlarge
the carrier, or pass to a dual rigging. Under such a change, a sequence may
converge to zero in the boundary topology while its Fourier transform remains
visible.

The sewing operator is closable only if

\[
x_n\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
J_0x_n\longrightarrow y
\]

implies (y=0).

Continuity on every finite cutoff does not provide a cutoff-independent form
of this implication.

## Exact finite hostile family

Let the weakened boundary observation at cutoff (N) be

\[
T_N=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0\\
0&N^{-1}
\end{pmatrix},
\]

and let Fourier polarization exchange the two coordinates:

\[
F=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&-1\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

On observed coordinates, sewing is

\[
J_N=T_NFT_N^{-1}.
\]

Every finite $J_N$ is defined, but its norm grows like $N$. The boundary
topology makes one source direction increasingly invisible while Fourier
rotates it into the fully visible direction.

Adding one independently derived observation row for the collapsing direction
restores the source norm and makes the Fourier family uniformly bounded.

## Meaning for the boundary currents

The primitive, square, seam, and archimedean ports must do more than make
individual currents continuous. Together they must define a topology whose
null sequences are invariant under Fourier sewing and its inverse.

This is the exact categorical meaning of a distinction-preserving completion:
the completion functor must preserve the source equivalence generated by
Fourier transport.

## Decisive topology gate

Let $Q_X$ be the complete constructor-derived boundary Gramian and $F_X$
the finite Fourier sewing. Uniform continuity requires one cutoff-independent
constant (C) such that

\[
F_X^*Q_XF_X\le C^2Q_X
\]

and the same inequality for $F_X^{-1}$.

Kernel inclusion is the first gate. Uniform domination is the completion
gate. Both must hold before reciprocal operator inversion descends.

## Outcome rule

- The source-pulled topology proves algebraic continuity but omits the
  singular vessel.
- A weaker boundary topology that fails Fourier domination is inadmissible.
- A constructor-generated topology satisfying both domination inequalities
  produces the desired closable reciprocal completion.
