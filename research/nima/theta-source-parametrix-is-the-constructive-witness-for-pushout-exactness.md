# A source parametrix is the constructive witness for pushout exactness

## The closed-range gate needs a witness

Let

\[
R_s:\widehat S_s
\longrightarrow
\widehat B_{\mathrm{an},s}\oplus
\widehat B_{\mathrm{arith},s}
\]

be the completed oriented comparison relation defining the source pushout.

A bare assertion that \(\operatorname{Ran}R_s\) is closed is not yet explanatory. The source-level constructive witness is a continuous generalized inverse or parametrix

\[
H_s:
\widehat B_{\mathrm{an},s}\oplus
\widehat B_{\mathrm{arith},s}
\longrightarrow
\widehat S_s
\]

satisfying

\[
H_sR_s=1-P_{\ker,s},
\qquad
R_sH_s=P_{\operatorname{Ran},s},
\]

with continuous projections in the declared typed topology.

Such a parametrix proves that the range is complemented and closed. If both projections onto kernel and cokernel defects vanish, \(H_s\) is an actual inverse and supplies the contracting homotopy of the two-term comparison cone.

## Three distinct gates

The completed cone must pass three levels.

### Strictness

The range of \(R_s\) is closed and the quotient is Hausdorff. Equivalently, the comparison admits a continuous generalized inverse on its image.

Failure appears as an approximate-kernel sequence and is an essential completion defect.

### Supported finite cohomology

Even with closed range, finite kernel or cokernel may remain. Their support and Fourier character must be retained. A total index can hide reciprocal open-sector defects.

### Acyclicity

A source-authorized homotopy eliminates the supported kernel and cokernel:

\[
d_sh_s+h_sd_s=1.
\]

Only this level excludes off-seam cohomology.

Thus strictness is necessary for the pushout to survive completion, but it is not RH by itself.

## Exact hostile hierarchy

### Dense nonclosed range

For

\[
T(x_n)=(x_n/n),
\]

the finite inverses have norms growing like \(N\). The completed operator has no continuous generalized inverse. This fails strictness.

### Closed range with finite defect

The unilateral shift on \(\ell^2\),

\[
S(x_1,x_2,\ldots)=(0,x_1,x_2,\ldots),
\]

is an isometry with closed range and a bounded left inverse. Its cokernel is one-dimensional. The associated two-term complex is strict but not acyclic.

### Contractible model

The identity operator has a continuous inverse and no kernel or cokernel. Its two-term complex is contractible.

These three examples prevent the compiler from conflating bounded-below transport, Fredholmness, index zero, and full source contraction.

## Relation to the existing tail–seam problem

The Hankel–Volterra graph relation has the adjoint-domain gate

\[
\operatorname{Dom}\Gamma^*
=
\{y:H^*y\in\operatorname{Ran}G\}.
\]

Density establishes closability, not closed range or acyclicity. A source parametrix for the full analytic–arithmetic comparison would have to include:

- the tail Green operator;
- the independent seam component;
- endpoint boundary conditions;
- primitive and square boundary incidence;
- connected and archimedean reservoirs;
- reciprocal transport between supported sector copies.

No current construction supplies this full parametrix.

## Compact-sector requirement

For a holomorphic family \(R_s\), the useful theorem must hold uniformly for \(s\) in each compact subset of an open sector. The parametrices must form an equicontinuous family in the Fourier-saturated multigraph topology.

A family of finite inverses whose norms diverge with cutoff does not define a completed source contraction. The divergent component may be retained only if an independently derived boundary channel carries its exact principal part.

## DPC

A proposed completion proof passes only if:

1. it constructs \(R_s\) from the source pushout;
2. it supplies a continuous generalized inverse or an equivalent strictness witness;
3. kernel and cokernel are typed separately from range closure;
4. support and Fourier characters are retained;
5. an actual contraction is asserted only after supported defects vanish;
6. parametrices are cutoff-compatible and compact-sector equicontinuous;
7. divergent inverse components are carried by declared boundary channels;
8. no inverse is defined from the completed scalar section.

## Outcome

Pushout exactness and open-sector contraction are not separate programmes. A source parametrix is their common constructive object. The first analytic task is strictness of the completed comparison relation; the next is supported defect classification; the final task is a source-authorized contraction. The present theta/Tate programme has not yet constructed the comparison parametrix that would begin this sequence.
