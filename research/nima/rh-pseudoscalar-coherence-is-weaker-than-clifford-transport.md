# Pseudoscalar Coherence Is Weaker Than Clifford Transport

## Hostile correction

The four-port construction makes reciprocal sewing preserve the total
pseudoscalar. That is necessary orientation coherence, but it does not control
the scalar part of a Clifford product.

Consider the volume-preserving squeeze

\[
S=\operatorname{diag}\left(2,\frac12,1,1\right).
\]

Its determinant is one, so it preserves the four-dimensional pseudoscalar. For
the ordinary positive quadratic form and the vector

\[
x=(1,1,0,0),
\]

the scalar norm changes from

\[
x^{T}x=2
\]

to

\[
(Sx)^{T}(Sx)=\frac{17}{4}.
\]

Thus an exact top-grade invariant can coexist with a changed scalar channel.
Pseudoscalar preservation alone cannot constrain zeros of a scalar projection.

## Correct Clifford gate

Let \(K\) be the source-derived bilinear form on the four ports. A genuine
Clifford transport must satisfy

\[
S^{\dagger}KS=K.
\]

A conformal version may be sufficient if its scale is independently typed:

\[
S^{\dagger}KS=\lambda K,
\qquad
\lambda>0.
\]

This is exactly stronger than determinant preservation. It controls the scalar
quadratic channel and induces a compatible action on every Clifford grade.
The determinant-line law follows as a consequence, with dimension-dependent
power of \(\lambda\), rather than serving as the primary theorem.

after seeing the completed scalar, the construction is circular even when the
