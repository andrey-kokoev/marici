# Q-1 blinded replay: can the micro-operator request the missing experiment?

## Question

The finite closure test shows that \(\mathsf U_1\) can describe itself without
changing its physical subobject. That is not yet explanatory. This replay asks
whether it can use incomplete evidence to identify a missing, typed test rather
than prematurely closing the question.

## Evidence channels

For a proposed singular-support locus \(Z\), keep four channels separate:

1. **carrier/source:** rank loss, incidence, or a derived source divisor;
2. **coefficient:** support or nontrivial extension/coherence carried by the
   derived coefficient complex;
3. **transport:** loss of independence or nontrivial local connection data;
4. **physical readout:** monodromy or variation of the source-normalized
   relative cycle.

The operator is intentionally asymmetric:

- one positive source-derived intrinsic witness is enough to keep a locus
  live, subject to typing;
- negative results in only some channels are not enough to prove apparency;
- a generic apparency conclusion requires the governed carrier, transport,
  and physical-readout gates to close negatively in the declared scope;
- an unresolved coefficient-extension channel may retain presentation data,
  but cannot by itself manufacture physical singular support.

This is a decision protocol, not a numerical score.

## Historical mask

The blinded state supplies the results known before the simultaneous physical
resolution:

- no Carrier divisor or new incidence stratum;
- no pure elliptic discriminant;
- no generic rank loss;
- two labelled source transports remain independent on \(\mathcal Q=0\).

It withholds

\[
T_{\mathcal Q}=1,\qquad N_{\mathcal Q}=0,\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_{\mathcal Q}(\Gamma_{\rm phys})=0.
\]

The required output is therefore neither `intrinsic` nor `apparent`, but

\[
\boxed{\texttt{underdetermined: derive physical-cycle monodromy/variation}.}
\]

After the withheld result is restored, the output must become `apparent` in
the exact generic nonsoft homogeneous three-site scope.

## Why this is a useful test

The replay checks whether the micro-system preserves a missing experimental
interface. A generic database summary could collapse four negative-looking
facts into a conclusion. The typed operator must instead identify that those
facts all live upstream of physical readout.

Success is retrospective and therefore weak evidence. Its value is that it
fixes a prospective rule: future candidate loci must not be declared apparent
until the physical-readout gate appropriate to that sector is actually
derived.

## Falsifiers

- The masked packet is classified as apparent.
- The operator asks for another carrier-rank census instead of the missing
  physical-cycle test.
- Restoring trivial physical monodromy fails to close the generic case.
- A positive physical variation is classified as apparent.
- A result outside the declared scope silently modifies the generic verdict.
