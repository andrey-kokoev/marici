# The thirteenth theta quarter-turn rotates arithmetic curvature into completion anomaly

## Ordered-port correction

Completed descent must preserve the ordered endpoint/source flag and the
bordered transmission divisor. A convergent trivialization of the unframed
full Weyl family does not descend the theta observable. Every gauge,
homotopy, and boundary current in this packet is now required to act on framed
colligation fibers.

Filtered-base no-go theorem and completion-at-infinity DPC. The twelfth turn
places locally closed colligations over finite arithmetic cutoffs and types
their transport curvature. The thirteenth turn asks whether that curvature can
remain a finite combinatorial obstruction.

The base category of finite prime subsets is filtered and has contractible
nerve. Consequently ordinary finite-cutoff holonomy supplies no stable global
topological charge once all coherent comparison cells are retained. A genuine
obstruction can survive only through source typing or through failure of the
finite trivializations to remain bounded, continuous, and convergent under the
restricted-product completion.

## Filtered prime base

Let \(\mathcal F(\mathbb P)\) be the poset of finite subsets of primes, ordered
by inclusion. For any \(X,Y\), the union \(X\cup Y\) is a common upper bound.
Thus \(\mathcal F(\mathbb P)\) is filtered.

Its nerve is contractible. In particular, ordinary locally constant
cohomological twisting over the finite-cutoff base cannot by itself provide a
nontrivial global obstruction.

This does not make source coherence automatic. It says that a proposed
obstruction based only on the topology of the cutoff poset is empty.

## Exact scalar flat connection

Assign a unit phase \(g_p\in U(1)\) to each prime and define, for finite \(X\),

\[
G_X
=
\prod_{p\in X}g_p.
\]

The edge transport for adjoining \(p\notin X\) is

\[
V_{X,Xp}
=
G_{Xp}G_X^{-1}
=
g_p.
\]

For distinct \(p,q\),

\[
V_{Xp,Xpq}V_{X,Xp}
=
g_qg_p
=
g_pg_q
=
V_{Xq,Xpq}V_{X,Xq}.
\]

Every finite square is strictly flat, and \(G_X\) gives an explicit finite
trivialization.

## The obstruction moves to infinity

The infinite trivialization would require a meaningful limit

\[
G_\infty
=
\prod_pg_p.
\]

Finite flatness does not imply that this product converges. If

\[
g_p=e^{i\theta_p},
\]

then absolute product convergence is controlled by summability of the local
phase increments. A sequence can therefore be strictly flat at every finite
cutoff while having no admissible restricted-product gauge at infinity.

The anomaly is not finite curvature. It is failure of the trivializing section
to belong to the completed gauge class.

## Relation to the Tate current filtration

The local Tate phase already has a three-level summability structure:

\[
k=1,
\qquad
k=2,
\qquad
k\geq3.
\]

The primitive and square currents obstruct the absolute scalar product, while
the connected tail beginning at order three is trace-class. The thirteenth
turn gives this filtration a connection-theoretic interpretation:

- every finite Euler cutoff has a valid gauge;
- the order-three tail admits an ordinary determinant-level limit;
- the first two currents are the boundary data required to compare finite
  gauges with the completed object;
- deleting them would manufacture convergence by erasing the anomaly.

Thus completion failure, not finite curvature, is the natural location of the
global sewing obstruction.

## Operator-valued version

Let \(V_{X,Y}\) be the state and port transports of the local colligation
fibers. Even when every finite square is strictly or homotopy flat, a completed
trivialization requires a compatible family \(G_X\) satisfying uniform
analytic control.

Depending on the chosen completion, the gates include:

\[
\sup_X\lVert G_X\rVert<\infty,
\qquad
\sup_X\lVert G_X^{-1}\rVert<\infty,
\]

equicontinuity on the source rigging, convergence on a common core, and
continuity of every boundary-current incidence map.

Failure may appear as a projective-limit or derived-limit obstruction once the
actual completion functor is fixed. No such derived class should be named
before that functor and its topology are constructed.

## Strict versus homotopy-flat descent

If finite curvature is killed by source-labelled homotopies, those homotopies
must themselves form a uniformly controlled compatible family. Cutoffwise
identities

\[
\Omega_X
=
d_Xh_X+h_Xd_X
\]

do not descend if \(\lVert h_X\rVert\) diverges or if the domains fail to share
a completed graph topology.

Therefore three distinct verdicts must remain separate:

1. finite curvature is nonexact;
2. finite curvature is source-exact but its homotopies do not complete;
3. finite curvature is coherently exact with a completed bounded or rigged
   trivialization.

Only the third yields global descent.

## Why hostile factors remain dangerous

A hostile inner factor can be attached by a strictly flat scalar phase at
every finite cutoff. It may fail only because its infinite product or port
trivialization violates the source completion topology.

This gives the programme a real discriminator, but only if the topology is
independently derived. Choosing a topology specifically to reject the hostile
factor would be circular.

The actual prime, square, seam, and archimedean currents must determine the
admissible gauge class before any zero-related test is run.

## Finite and asymptotic falsifiers

The connection route fails immediately if a finite square has a residual
outside every authorized relation image. If finite coherence passes, the first
completion falsifiers are:

- an unbounded sequence of port gauges;
- inverse gauges with no uniform control;
- source-labelled homotopies whose graph norms diverge;
- primitive or square currents omitted from the completed boundary object;
- two cutoff cofinal sequences producing inequivalent limiting gauges;
- or a hostile factor whose gauges satisfy the same independently fixed
  completion laws as the genuine arithmetic source.

The scalar phase family \(g_p=e^{i\theta_p}\) with divergent phase product is
the minimal example showing that finite strict flatness does not imply global
descent.

## Meaning of the thirteenth rotation

The twelfth turn converted local gauge into arithmetic curvature. The
thirteenth converts coherently vanishing finite curvature into a question of
descent:

\[
\left(
\mathfrak C_X,
V_{X,Y},
h_X
\right)
\longrightarrow
\mathfrak C_\infty.
\]

The new datum is not another local operator. It is the topology and boundary
renormalization law under which the entire compatible family becomes one
global object.

## Decisive conclusion

Finite arithmetic holonomy cannot carry the full RH burden on a filtered,
contractible cutoff base. After local and higher coherence are supplied, the
only remaining obstruction is source authority or completion at infinity.
The primitive and square Tate currents are natural candidates for the boundary
anomaly that controls this descent. The next decisive theorem must construct
the completed port trivialization with those currents retained, or exhibit a
hostile flat family that survives the same source-derived topology.
