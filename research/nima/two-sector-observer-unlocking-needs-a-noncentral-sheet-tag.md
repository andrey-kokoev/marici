# Two-sector observer unlocking needs a noncentral sheet tag

## Repeated-sector model

Let \(\mathfrak g\) be a simple Lie algebra represented on two equivalent
source sectors.  Suppose the available operations initially act diagonally:

\[
\Delta\mathfrak g
=
\{(K,K):K\in\mathfrak g\}.
\]

This can be algebraically rich inside each sector while leaving the two
sectors dynamically locked.

Adjoining the central sheet label

\[
J=(I,-I)
\]

does not unlock them.  Since \(J\) commutes with every diagonal operation,
Lie brackets cannot form sector-selective noncentral controls.

Now adjoin a noncentral asymmetric tag

\[
H_-=(H,-H),
\qquad
0\ne H\in\mathfrak g.
\]

Bracketing with diagonal controls generates

\[
([K,H],-[K,H]).
\]

Simplicity implies that the ideal generated by \(H\) is all of
\(\mathfrak g\).  Hence both

\[
(X,X)
\quad\hbox{and}\quad
(X,-X)
\]

are generated for every \(X\in\mathfrak g\).  Their sums and differences
isolate

\[
(X,0)
\quad\hbox{and}\quad
(0,X).
\]

The two sectors are then independently controlled.

## Observer consequence

A second observer is not useful merely because it has the opposite sheet
label.  If it differs only through a central sign or a scalar normalization,
it adds classification without adding dynamical separation.

The useful extra observer must arise from a source operation whose projection
is noncentral in the internal sector algebra and unequal across the two
sectors.  This is the two-sector specialization of Kitaev's repeated-block
spectral-tag theorem.

## Theta typing

For reciprocal theta sheets, multiplication, differentiation, reflection,
and scalar evaluation must be decomposed by their two sheet projections.
The relevant question is not whether a dual observer pairs the identity
nontrivially.  The augmented constant channel already does that.

The finite gate is instead:

1. identify the internal simple or irreducible operator block carried by each
   sheet;
2. compute the projections of every source-authorized operator to both
   blocks;
3. determine whether all noncentral projections are diagonally locked;
4. find or rule out one noncentral operator with unequal sheet weights.

If every asymmetry is central, the two-sheet observer system cannot generate
an independent orientation through commutators.  If a noncentral asymmetric
tag exists, it unlocks the sectors algebraically, but a separate estimate is
still required to imply eventual compact exclusion of zeros.

## Cross-sector transfer

The same gate applies when two flavor observers or two staged measurements
share an internal response algebra.  Distinct readout labels do not imply
independent interventions.  The distinction is:

- central asymmetry classifies sectors;
- noncentral asymmetry controls sectors independently.

This also explains why associative generation can overstate operational
completeness: central projectors may isolate blocks under multiplication while
remaining unavailable through the Lie closure of executable operations.

## Falsifiers

The proposal fails if any of the following occurs:

1. the alleged tag is central;
2. its two noncentral projections have equal weights;
3. the sector representations are not equivalent and the repeated-block
   theorem is applied without retyping;
4. associative products are treated as executable brackets;
5. algebraic unlocking is promoted to positivity or zero confinement without
   an independent estimate.
