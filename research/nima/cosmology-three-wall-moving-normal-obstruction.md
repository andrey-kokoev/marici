# The physical three-wall family has a canonical moving-normal obstruction

On the physical residue surface use fiber coordinates \((a,b)\), external
kinematics \((x,y,z)\), and shared walls

\[
q_1=b-y-z,
\qquad
q_2=a-x-z,
\qquad
q_3=a+b+z.
\]

Their fiber-normal Jacobian is

\[
J=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1\\
1&0\\
1&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

It has rank two and left cokernel generated by

\[
\lambda=(-1,-1,1),
\qquad \lambda J=0.
\]

For an external variation \((dx,dy,dz)\), a fiber correction
\((da,db)\) keeping all three wall normals fixed would solve

\[
J\binom{da}{db}
=-
\begin{pmatrix}
-dy-dz\\-dx-dz\\dz
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The exact compatibility condition is therefore

\[
\boxed{dx+dy+3dz=0.}
\]

Generic kinematic motion violates it. In particular, pure total-energy
motion with \(z=E-x-y\) and fixed \(x,y\) has obstruction \(3dE\).

The displayed coefficient three is not promoted to a prime-three invariant.
Rescaling wall equations changes the representative \(\lambda\) and hence
the scalar coordinate on the cokernel line. The invariant datum is the
nonzero one-dimensional cokernel class and whether a given base tangent maps
to zero in it.
