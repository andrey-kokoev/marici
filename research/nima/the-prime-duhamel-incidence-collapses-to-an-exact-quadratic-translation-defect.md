# The prime Duhamel incidence collapses to an exact quadratic translation defect

## Translation transport

On one comoving ray, let

\[
(U_af)(x)=f(x+a),
\qquad

\]

Its generator is \(D=\partial_x\). Let

\[
A=xD,
\qquad
P=A(A+1).
\]

For the prime interval \([L,2L]\), the transport length is

\[

\]

## Conjugated dilation generator

A direct calculation gives

\[
U_a^{-1}AU_a
=
A-aD.
\]

Therefore

\[
U_a^{-1}PU_a
=
(A-aD)(A-aD+1).
\]

Using

\[
DA=(A+1)D,
\]

the difference expands exactly as

\[
U_a^{-1}PU_a-P
=
-2a(A+1)D+a^2D^2.
\]

Multiplying by \(U_a\) yields

\[
PU_a-U_aP
=
U_a
\left[
-2a(A+1)D+a^2D^2
\right].
\]

## Closed form of the Duhamel integral

The Duhamel curvature identity was

\[
PU_a-U_aP
=
\int_0^a
U(a,r)\Omega U(r,0)\,dr,
\qquad
\Omega=-2(A+1)D.
\]

Hence the integral evaluates exactly to

\[
\mathcal R(a)
=
U_a
\left[
a\Omega+a^2D^2
\right].
\]

At prime \(p\),

\[
\mathcal R_p
=
U_{\log p}
\left[
-2(\log p)(A+1)D
+

\right].
\]

No path-ordering ambiguity remains for the pure translation cell.

## Reciprocal ray

For the opposite translation \(U_{-a}\),

\[

The full comparison contains both sectors. Calling the entire Duhamel defect
an odd operator is too coarse.
