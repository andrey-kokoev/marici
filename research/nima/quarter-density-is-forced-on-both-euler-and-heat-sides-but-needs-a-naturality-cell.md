# Quarter-density is forced on both Euler and heat sides but still needs a naturality cell

The quarter-power suggested by the Euler--heat exponent match is not isolated.
The arithmetic staircase independently forces the same Hilbertization type.

A linear prime half-density current has coefficient

\[
p^{-k/2}.
\]

If positive curvature is produced by squaring a weighted shift
\(p^{-\alpha}T_p\), matching every depth requires

\[
p^{-2\alpha k}=p^{-k/2},
\qquad
\alpha=\frac14.
\]

Thus the arithmetic positive lift is uniquely quarter-dense:

\[
U_p=p^{-1/4}T_p.
\]

On the theta heat side, the raw energy amplitude

\[
A^{1/2}e^{-tA/2}\mathbf 1
\]

has boundary Gram order \((r+t)^{-3/2}\). Applying \(A^{-1/4}\) gives

\[
A^{1/4}e^{-tA/2}\mathbf 1,
\]

whose squared spectral weights are \(n\), and whose continuum boundary order
is \((r+t)^{-1}\), exactly the Euler Hankel exponent.

So the same rule appears independently:

\[
\text{linear half-density}
\longrightarrow
\text{positive quadratic energy}
\quad\text{through a quarter-density amplitude}.
\]

This is strong evidence for a common half-form functor, but not yet authority
for identifying the two lifts. The prime shift and heat generator live in
different source loci. Agreement of exponents does not supply a comparison
arrow.

The cell must commute on complete typed blocks, not only after scalar
