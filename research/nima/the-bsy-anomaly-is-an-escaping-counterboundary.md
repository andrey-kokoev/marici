# The BSY anomaly is an escaping counterboundary

## Synthesis

The finite-Euler divergence and the translated low-grade hostile are two views
of one completion mechanism. A finite commutator carries cancelling boundary
charges. A nonzero completed anomaly can appear only when the completion is
not cyclic for the relevant products and one counterboundary escapes.

## Exact shift model

Let \(S_N\) be the nilpotent unilateral shift on an \(N\)-dimensional basis:

\[
S_Ne_j=e_{j+1}\quad(0\leq j<N-1),
\qquad
S_Ne_{N-1}=0.
\]

Then

\[
[S_N^*,S_N]
=|e_0\rangle\langle e_0|-|e_{N-1}\rangle\langle e_{N-1}|.
\]

Its trace is zero at every finite cutoff. On every fixed leading coordinate
window, however, the second projector eventually disappears and the
commutator stabilizes to the positive rank-one boundary projector. The
opposite charge has not been cancelled; it has moved to the cutoff boundary.

For the completed unilateral shift \(S\),

\[
[S^*,S]=|e_0\rangle\langle e_0|.
\]

There is no contradiction with finite-dimensional trace cyclicity. Neither
product is trace class before subtraction, so the infinite commutator lies
outside the hypothesis under which the trace of a commutator must vanish.

## Consequence for the BSY route

Writing the desired residual schematically as

\[
\mathfrak A=[B,R](L_E)
\]

is informative only if the boundary domains and completion order are retained.
Any bounded finite-dimensional model followed by an ordinary cyclic trace
forces the scalar commutator anomaly to vanish. Any uniformly trace-norm
convergent family of trace-class commutators also has zero limiting trace.

Therefore a nonzero BSY residual must be carried by one of the following:

- an unbounded or partially defined boundary operation;
- a relative trace with a declared reference;
- a Toeplitz/Fredholm boundary index;
- a failure of uniform integrability;
- a counterboundary escaping through the cutoff completion.

These are not alternative presentations that may be forgotten afterward.
They are the type of the anomaly.

## Orientation gate

The RH-strength question is now more precise than the sign of a scalar:

Which source law determines which of the two finite counterboundaries is
retained by the completed boundary functor?

Choosing the leading projector rather than the receding projector is an
orientation choice. A fitted truncation convention can choose either one.
The choice is explanatory only if forced by the theta--Tate endpoint,
reciprocal sewing, and labelled Euler incidence together.

The required source construction must therefore provide:

1. the two finite boundary charges before cancellation;
2. their transport under cutoff refinement;
3. the completion functor and its domain;
4. the retained relative-trace or index functional;
5. a source-derived orientation of the surviving charge.

Without these data, the notation \([B,R]\) is only a schematic name for the
missing theorem.

## Finite falsifier

At every \(N\), the full trace of \([S_N^*,S_N]\) is zero. A checker that
reports only the first diagonal entry returns one; a checker that reports only
the last returns minus one. Both scalar answers arise from the same finite
commutator. Hence a proposed anomaly law is falsified if it does not declare
which boundary port its completion retains and why.

## Verdict

The live RH object is a typed relative boundary anomaly, not a finite-Euler
positive expectation and not an ordinary trace of a commutator. The next
source calculation is the refinement transport of both low-grade boundary
charges and the theta-derived rule selecting their relative orientation.
