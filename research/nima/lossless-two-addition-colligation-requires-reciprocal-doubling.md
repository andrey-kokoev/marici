# Lossless two-addition colligation requires reciprocal doubling

## From section identity to operator incidence

The two-addition scalar identity is the shadow of a simpler source operation:
cut the source into the swept seam and the untranslated remainder.

For a finite cut \(L\), write

\[
C_L^+f=\bigl(R_L^+f,T_L^+f\bigr),
\]

where \(R_L^+f=f|_{[0,L]}) and \(T_L^+f=f(L+\cdot)\). With the graph
energy split across the interface, \(C_L^+\) is an isometry onto its
incidence-constrained image.

For two lengths \(a,b>0\), the complete state is

\[
C_{a,b}^+f
=
\bigl(
f|_{[0,a]},
f|_{[a,a+b]},
f(a+b+\cdot)
\bigr).
\]

Cutting first at \(a\) or first at \(b\) gives different segmentations of
the same interval \([0,a+b]\). Concatenation followed by recutting is the
canonical unitary comparison. After forgetting the internal segmentation,
both paths are exactly \(C_{a+b}^+\).

The shared moving-seam corner is therefore the full interval state
\(f|_{[0,a+b]}\). Its scalar integral is a later readout.

## Why one sector cannot carry reciprocal dagger internally

Let \(J\) reverse source orientation. A positive prefix cut is not preserved:
reflection sends it to a negative or suffix cut. Define

\[
C_L^-f=\bigl(R_L^-f,T_L^-f\bigr)
\]

using the terminal interval and the complementary head. Componentwise
reflection gives the exact intertwining relation

\[
C_L^+J=J_L^{\partial}C_L^-.
\]

Thus reciprocal reflection does not act internally on the positive cut
colligation. It exchanges two differently typed boundary incidences.

The minimum reciprocal object is doubled:

\[
\mathcal H^+\oplus\mathcal H^-.
\]

Its involution exchanges sectors while reversing their coordinates. The
doubled cut

\[
C_L^+\oplus C_L^-
\]

is equivariant under that involution. Reciprocal dagger is therefore lawful
only after the second sector is retained.

## What the colligation realizes

This source-derived operator model provides:

- exact norm conservation;
- the complete shared seam state for two additions;
- canonical comparison of both segmentation orders;
- an explicit reciprocal exchange law;
- the undivided scalar section identity after endpoint readout.

It does not yet attach the Euler determinant line. In particular, the cyclic
grades \(\operatorname{Tr}(L^k)/k\) are not generated by cutting the source.
They require the separate cyclic/Fock chart and a comparison map into this
lossless boundary object.

## Completion gate

At finite cutoff the construction is unitary. Passing to the restricted
product requires the recutting comparisons and reciprocal exchange maps to be
compatible with the directed limit. Pointwise finite unitarity does not
control a parameterized resolvent or determinant line.

The next obstruction is therefore sharply located: construct the Euler cyclic
chart over the doubled cut colligation and prove that its comparison maps are
locally uniformly bounded on the completed parameter domain.

## DPC verdict

Resolved at finite source level:

- operator realization of the shared moving-seam corner;
- coherence of both addition orders;
- necessity and sufficiency of reciprocal doubling for the reflection
  intertwiner.

Withheld:

- Euler determinant attachment;
- infinite restricted-product compatibility;
- locally uniform resolvent control;
- the zero-state-to-flux bridge.

The finite falsifier for a one-sector reciprocal model is immediate:
reflection takes a prefix-supported state to a suffix-supported state, outside
the declared positive boundary port.
