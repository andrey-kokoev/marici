# One witness-bearing resolution operator

## Implemented object

The input signature has a type P of **complete packages**, one-premise rules U(p,q), and two-premise rules V(p,q,r). A rule inhabitant is its actual witness. P,U,V are arbitrary types, with no set or proposition restriction. A seed family S specifies the admitted initial packages, including its own evidence.

Resolve(S,p) is generated by:

* seed(s), for s:S(p);
* unary(u,d), for u:U(p,q) and d:Resolve(S,p);
* binary(v,d,e), for v:V(p,q,r), d:Resolve(S,p), e:Resolve(S,q).

PackageClosure(S) = Sigma(p:P). Resolve(S,p).

This retains each package and each finite admitted resolution history, including its rule witnesses and premise histories. It does not select a resolution order. Different legal orders remain separate derivations until a specified comparison relates them. There is no global algorithmic enumeration claim for arbitrary type-valued signatures.

The generic interface currently handles one- and two-premise rules. Higher-coherence generators and their boundaries must be supplied as actual typed rule data; this interface alone does not manufacture higher fillers or define the intended package-level E/P operations. Finitary rules of other arities require an extension or an explicit encoding.

## Self-application

Resolve(Resolve(S),p) is a resolution whose seeds are entire prior resolutions. flatten substitutes those histories into the outer resolution. Agda checks its left and right unit laws and associativity, together with identity and composition laws for seed mapping. All source steps and rule witnesses remain in the flattened derivation; the extra distinction of which closure layer introduced a step does not.

This gives the free-resolution, monad-style structure appropriate to retained histories.

## Correction to the proposed idempotence equation

The earlier unqualified C(C(Q)) ~= C(Q) was too strong for full resolution histories. A step introduced inside a seed and the same step introduced outside it give distinct nested histories with identical flattenings. `CoherenceResolutionClosureRegression.agda` proves this distinction and their identical flattening. In particular the canonical flattening is not generally an equivalence. This counterexample does not assert that the underlying types can never have some unrelated equivalence.

The separate reachability view

Reach(S,p) = propositional truncation of Resolve(S,p)

forgets the external derivation witness and asks which complete packages are admitted. Agda proves Reach(Reach(S),p) ~= Reach(S,p), and the corresponding equivalence of Sigma-types of admitted packages. Package data p are retained by that equivalence. Reach is a separate projection; it does not replace the witness-bearing PackageClosure object.

Thus the equations belong to two different views:

* histories: flatten : C(C(S)) -> C(S), with checked unit/associativity laws;
* membership: Reach(Reach(S)) ~= Reach(S).

An idempotent higher-categorical completion of the full structure would need an explicitly specified notion of equivalence between layered histories and its coherence laws. That construction is not supplied here.

## Concrete integration

`DependentSigmaPiResolutionClosure.agda` instantiates P as the sum of the complete SourceRecord and TargetRecord from the checked dependent Sigma/Pi chains. It supplies forward transport, inverse transport, and witnessed internal equality steps. The closed-route derivation retains the original package, its transported package, its inverse-transported package, and the full-chain recovery path. It can become the seed of another resolution and flatten back to its complete retained history.

This signature exercises the existing checked package comparison. It does not yet supply every intended E/P package constructor, or freely adjoin comparisons between arbitrary derivations.
